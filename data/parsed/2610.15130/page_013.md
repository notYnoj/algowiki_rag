<tbc>tenant recall-loss framework for the joint filtered, multi-tenant, streaming graph-ANN setting, a decomposition unconditional in three of its four terms, with the staleness term instantiated from a degradation law calibrated on this substrate.

_f) Benchmarks.:_ We compose existing benchmarks rather than propose one, drawing on the Big-ANN’23 \[29\] streaming track with filtered slices alongside CANDY \[30\], CANDOR- Bench \[1\], and the Filtered-ANN Unified Benchmark \[31\]. The substrate builds on Vamana/DiskANN \[32\], HNSW \[33\], NSG \[34\], and SPANN \[35\].

## VII. DISCUSSION

_a) The staleness half of the bound is semi-empirical.:_<br>
Its mechanism and functional form are proved and validated on two independent axes, while the degradation exponents (\(\theta\approx 1.46\), _b_≈0.5) are _calibrated_ rather than derived from first principles. Three properties bound what it establishes, all quantified in §III-A and §V-B. The fit is valid over \(0.05\le c/N\le 0.45\), with the smallest batch sizes falling under the 0.02 pp reporting floor. The exponent CI (_a_<sub>recall</sub>=1.28, 95% CI \[1.03,1.52\]) supports _a_-1_\>_0 but not its magnitude, so the design rests on the _sign_ \(a\ge 1\) rather than the value. And the horizon assembly is an upper bound loose by 2.3-2.7\(\times\), since \(\eta<1\). The measured monotonicity of _L_<sup>∗</sup>(_c_) rules out the one qualitative failure mode, leaving the deployed-regime magnitude weakly constrained but not the direction.

_b) The filtered term is a loss bound only under homogeneous local density.:_ Lemma 7(ii) is parameter-free (\(\beta\)=1 is forced by the engine contract) and predicts the recovery constant rather than fitting it. For spatially-clustered predicates the two-sided bound is validated by evaluating the query average \(\bar{h}_\tau\) on the archived per-query \(\sigma_\mathrm{loc}\) (§V-C), which absorbs the up-to-26 pp mean-substitution gap and holds as a _conservative_ lower bound on delivered recall (measured \(\ge\bar{h}_\tau\) in 14 of 15 clustered cells), loose by up to 14 pp at low \(\sigma_\mathrm{loc}\). Where per-query densities are unavailable, the assumption-free envelope Lemma 7(i) constrains the required search width but gives no per-tenant recall guarantee.

_d) Scale and measurement scope.:_ The SLA _mechanism_ is measured (ceiling enforcement drives tight-tier violations to zero under churn where a scheduler-less baseline cannot) and the shard rebuild-outage is measured to 100K/tenant, but the shard violation _rate_ is a duty-cycle model and the 1M-10M memory inversion is a projection from measured per-vector RSS. The topology-\(\Delta\) is a same-method _relative_ measurement with limited power to exclude sub-pp differences, and our absolute _p_<sub>99</sub> trails PipeANN’s by roughly an<tbc>

<tbc>order of magnitude at the configurations measured; external comparisons are preliminary (the PipeANN recall gap is on unmatched parameters; ACORN did not build within our memory ceiling). Results are on a single dataset family (SIFT and a filtered YFCC slice) and a single storage backend, so the measured constants may not transfer to other datasets or storage stacks without recalibration. Finally, the analysis presupposes a merge-based (out-of-place) substrate; direct- insert indexes have no _c/d_/\(\tau\) referent and lie outside our scope.

## VIII. CONCLUSION

We studied the intersection of graph-substrate, filtered, multi-tenant, streaming ANN with an analytical per-tenant recall@_k_ bound, which prior work has not jointly addressed. The organizing result is anti-amortization, and we observe it directly: over a fixed 60K-insert horizon and a \(20\times\) range of merge sizes, terminal recall falls monotonically as merges grow. On this substrate, batching is therefore not a free recall- amortization lever; degradation can be re-prioritized but not amortized away. The per-merge law behind it is super-linear in _c_ across the evaluated _c/N_ range, with an exponent estimated from that range rather than derived. That fact explains the design: a filter and tenant-aware staging cache that absorbs within-cycle staleness on the query path (C2), and a select- not-resize, SLA-aware scheduler that re-orders repair under a per-tenant max-staleness ceiling (C3), both binding to a bound we generalize to the filtered (local-density \(\sigma_\mathrm{loc}\)), multi- tenant (cadence-bounded), deletes/migration setting (C1). The filtered half of that bound is a parameter-free frontier-mass law (expected in-predicate frontier mass truncated at _k_) that predicts the measured recovery scale \(L_{95}=1.25k/\sigma_\mathrm{loc}\) and holds as a loss bound under homogeneous local density. On a FreshDiskANN substrate we measure that the scheduler’s re-ordering leaves terminal recall unchanged, that the per- tenant staleness ceiling eliminates tight-tier violations under churn where a scheduler-less baseline cannot, and that the design stays memory-bounded and recall-correct at mid-scale. PipeANN and OdinANN remain stronger on single-tenant recall and tail latency, and Curator on static filtered recall; our contribution targets the filtered, multi-tenant, streaming axes and the per-tenant freshness guarantee.
