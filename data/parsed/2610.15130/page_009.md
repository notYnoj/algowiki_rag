_d) (4) The clustered deficit is one-signed and carries the predicted curvature signature.:_ All 15 clustered cells sit _below h_ evaluated at their measured mean \(\hat{\sigma}_\mathrm{loc}\), never above, and the deficit is not monotone in _L_: for the _T_=96 clustered control it runs +6.1,+21.7,+26.0,+7.2,+1.1 pp across _L_=75_..._1000, peaking in the knee _x_≈1.4 and decaying to ≈1 pp at both ends. That shape is what Lemma 7(iii) requires and what a genuine breakdown of the frontier-mass form would not produce: the Jensen gap must vanish where \(\gamma\) is affine (linear at small _x_, flat at saturation) and peak where it is most curved. The one-signedness is the correct direction, since dispersion in \(\sigma_\mathrm{loc}\) across a tenant’s queries can only lower recall. We attribute the deficit to evaluating _h_ at the mean rather than averaging it over queries; the query average \(\bar{h}_\tau=\mathbb{E}_q[h(\sigma_\mathrm{loc}(q),L,k)]\), computable from the per-query \(\sigma_\mathrm{loc}\) the sweep records, closes the 26 pp gap (see the query-average result below).

_e) (5) Tenant count does not set_ \(\sigma_\mathrm{loc}\)_; label geometry does.:_ This distinction decides whether the law undermines or supports the multi-tenant thesis, so we state it explicitly. Read naively, the law looks self-defeating, since more tenants means smaller per-tenant selectivity \(\sigma\)=1_/T_, hence (via \(L_{95}=1.25k/\sigma_\mathrm{loc}\)) a search width growing linearly in _T_, so the very regime the system targets would be the regime it serves worst. That reading conflates \(\sigma_\mathrm{loc}\) with \(\sigma\), and the clustered controls refute it. Random-balanced labels make \(\hat{\sigma}_\mathrm{loc}\) equal 1_/T_ to within 0.5% at every _T_. They are the _worst case_ for locality, since a tenant’s points are scattered uniformly through the space. Spatially-coherent labels, which model workloads in which a tenant’s vectors exhibit domain-specific locality, give \(\hat{\sigma}_\mathrm{loc}\) of 0.511/0.168/0.047 against global \(\sigma\) of 0.167/0.042/0.010 at _T_=6/24/96, a 3-5\(\times\) locality premium that does not decay with _T_. The consequence at matched search cost is large. At _L_=75 the clustered controls recall 97.8/73.6/29.2 where the diffuse controls at the same tenant counts recall 95.4/31.4/7.8, a 2.3\(\times\) and 3.8\(\times\) improvement at _T_=24 and _T_=96, obtained purely from label geometry and _despite_ the clustered cells’ own dispersion deficit (finding 4). What the law penalizes is therefore predicate diffuseness, not multi-tenancy: a thousand tenants with coherent data are cheaper to serve than twenty-four with data sprayed uniformly. Real workloads need not sit at the clustered extreme; \(\hat{\sigma}_\mathrm{loc}\) is a measurable property of a deployment and the input the law needs.

_f) (6) The joint recall-latency feasibility test.:_ Because recovery costs _L_<sub>95</sub>=1.25\(k/\sigma_\mathrm{loc}\) and per-query cost grows with _L_, a tenant carrying both a recall floor \(\rho_\tau\) and a latency SLO \(\ell_\tau\) is servable on the shared substrate only if<tbc>

\(1.25k/\hat{\sigma}_\mathrm{loc}^{(\tau)}\le L_\mathrm{max}(\ell_\tau),\) (11)

<tbc>where \(L_\mathrm{max}(\ell_\tau)\) is the widest search meeting \(\ell_\tau\). This is an admission-control and provisioning test, computable before deployment from a quantity the operator can measure: the practical content of "recoverable at \(\Theta(k/\sigma_\mathrm{loc})\)". It has a hard consequence: at \(\hat{\sigma}_\mathrm{loc}=0.01\) the test demands _L_≈1250 (17\(\times\) our default _L_=75), which §V-D prices at ≈275 ms mean, outside any interactive budget, so such tenants cannot meet a 95% recall floor and an interactive SLO simultaneously on the shared substrate, and a per-tenant-navigable index is the right structure (the same boundary on which §V-F places Curator ahead, now a quantitative condition). Mean latency is linear in _L_ over the same ladder (§V-D), and since the search is label-blind the price of a recall target depends only on the _L_ that \(\hat{\sigma}_\mathrm{loc}\) demands, not on the predicate. The test is stated on mean latency and against a candidate SLO ladder, and §V-D measures the latency side directly.

_g) On the strength of the diffuse evidence.:_ Two limits apply. The diffuse \(\hat{\sigma}_\mathrm{loc}\) agrees with 1_/T_ to within 0.5% at every _T_, which validates the \(\sigma_\mathrm{loc}\) instrument but also means the seven diffuse controls form a single scaling family, so their mutual collapse is largely structural. And every measurement here is at _k_=10, leaving the law’s _k_-dependence (Remark 9) untested.
