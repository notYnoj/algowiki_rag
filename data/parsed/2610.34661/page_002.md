The point-charge singularities are usually removed by writing the potential as a singular part, known in closed form, plus one or two regular parts that solve interface problems with bounded data \[42, 11, 16, 9, 18\]. In this paper we use the decomposition of Xie \[34\],

\(u=G+\Psi+\widehat{\Phi},\)

where _G_ is the Coulomb potential of the solute charges, \(\Psi\) solves a linear interface problem that carries the boundary and interface conditions, and \(\widehat{\Phi}\) solves a semilinear interface problem with homogeneous interface and boundary conditions. The nonlinearity in the problem for \(\widehat{\Phi}\) comes from the ionic charges and acts only in the solvent region. Both \(\Psi\) and \(\widehat{\Phi}\) solve interface problems with bounded data, and the decomposition allows any number of ionic species. Xie combined it with a finite element method and a modified Newton minimization scheme in a FEniCS-based PBE solver \[34\], and the same splitting has been used in finite element solvers for the Poisson-Nernst-Planck equations \[35, 8, 41\].

In such solvers most of the work goes into the nonlinear algebraic system for the regular part \(\widehat{\Phi}\). It is usually solved by a damped Newton or Newton-type minimization method \[18, 34\], and every Newton step requires a large linear solve on the fine mesh. The two-grid method of Xu \[36, 37\], see also \[38, 39, 2, 27, 12, 20, 7\], reduces this cost. The nonlinear problem is solved only on a coarse mesh with mesh size _H_, and the solution on the fine mesh with mesh size \(h\checkmark H\) is obtained by one linearized (Newton) correction. The coarse mesh can be much coarser than the fine one, typically \(H\sim h^\gamma\) with \(\gamma<1\), without loss of asymptotic accuracy. Holst, Szypowski and Zhu \[19\] extended the analysis to semilinear interface problems with discontinuous coefficients, with the regularized PBE as their main example, and showed that _H∼h_<sup>2/5</sup> suffices in three dimensions under full regularity. Two-grid methods have also been used to decouple and accelerate finite element solvers for the Poisson-Nernst-Planck equations \[33, 41\].

Here we apply the two-grid method to Xie’s decomposition and give an a priori error analysis of the resulting scheme. Two points distinguish this setting from \[19\]. First, the nonlinear term in the problem for \(\widehat{\Phi}\) is<tbc>

\(\rho(U+\widehat{\Phi})\chi_{D_s},\rho(\xi)=-\beta\sum_{i=1}^nZ_i\bar{c}_ie^{-Z_i\xi},\)

<tbc>where \(U=G+\Psi\) is the potential of the solute in pure water, _Z<sub>i</sub>_ and \(\bar{c}_i\) are the charge numbers and bulk concentrations of the ionic species, and \(\chi_{D_s}\) is the indicator function of the solvent region. This term depends on _x_ through _U_, vanishes in the solute region, and in the discrete problem _U_ is replaced by the finite element approximation \(U_h=G+\Psi_h\), so the analysis has to account for this perturbation. Second, for nonlinearities of supercritical growth such as the exponential, the analysis in \[19\] relies on a discrete maximum principle, i.e., on a sign condition on the off-diagonal stiffness entries. For tetrahedral meshes generated from molecular surfaces this is a strong restriction. We do not need it. Instead we replace _e<sup>y</sup>_ by a _C_<sup>2</sup> function \(E_\tau(y)\) that equals _e<sup>y</sup>_ for \(y\le\tau\) and is quadratic for \(y>\tau\); this is a smooth version of the overflow safeguard ("blow-up" truncation) used in \[34\]. The truncated nonlinearity is monotone, grows quadratically and has a bounded second derivative. We prove the bound

\(\left\|\widehat{\Phi}\right\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}\le\left\|U\right\|_{L^\infty(D_s)}\)

for the solution of the original problem and conclude that, for every \(\tau\) above an explicit threshold, the truncated problem has the same unique solution. The error analysis can therefore be done for the truncated problem, where no pointwise bounds on discrete solutions are needed, and the results hold for the original problem. As a by-product we obtain a short proof of existence and uniqueness for \(\widehat{\Phi}\) that works in \(H_0^1(\Omega)\) rather than on the set H used in \[34\].
