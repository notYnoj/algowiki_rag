## Boxes:

Fix an index \(1\le i\le D\), and consider any tree \(T'\in\mathcal{T}_i\). Denote the number of leaves by _k_, and let _v_<sub>1</sub>_,...,v<sub>k</sub>_ be the leaves of \(T'\). If _v_ is the root vertex of the tree \(T'\), then we can view \(T'\) as defining a hierarchical partitioning scheme of the strip _S(v_), until we obtain the partition (_S(v_<sub>1</sub>)_,S(v_<sub>2</sub>)_,...,S(v<sub>k</sub>_)) of _S(v_). We define a collection of \(T'\)-boxes as follows. Let \(X'=X\cap S(v)\) be the set of all points lying in strip _S(v_). Assume that \(X'=\{p_1,p_2,\dots,p_{m^\prime}\}\), where the points are indexed in the increasing order of their _y_-coordinates.

We now iteratively partition the point set \(X'\) into boxes, where each box is a consecutive set of points of \(X'\). Let _v<sub>i</sub>_ be the leaf vertex of \(T'\), such that \(p_1\in S(v_i)\), and let _m_<sub>1</sub> be the largest index, such that all points _p_<sub>1</sub>_,...,p_<sub>_m_<sub>1</sub></sub> lie in _S(v<sub>i</sub>_). We then then define a box _B_<sub>1</sub>={_p_<sub>1</sub>_,p_<sub>2</sub>_,...,p_<sub>_m_<sub>1</sub></sub>}. We discard the points _p_<sub>1</sub>_,...,p_<sub>_m_<sub>1</sub></sub>, and continue this process to define _B_<sub>2</sub> (starting from point _p_<sub>_m_<sub>1</sub></sub>+1), and so on, until every point of \(X'\) belongs to some box.

We let \(\mathcal{B}(T')=\big\{B_1,B_2,\dots,B_{z(T^\prime)}\big\}\) be the resulting partition of the points in \(X'\), where we refer to each set _B<sub>i</sub>_ as a \(T'\)-box, or just a box. For each box \(B'\in\mathcal{B}(T')\), the lowest and the highest rows containing points of \(B'\) are denoted by \(\text{first}(B')\) and \(\text{last}(B')\) respectively.

## Projections of points:

Recall that the solution that our recursive algorithm returns is _T_-special, that is, the points that participate in the solution lie on the active rows of _X_ and on _T_-auxiliary columns. Let _Y_ be a feasible solution obtained by our algorithm RecursiveBST(_X_, _T_, \(\rho\)), where \(\rho=1\). Notice the every point in _Y_ is obtained by "projecting" some input point \(p\in X\) to the boundary of some strip _S(v_) for \(v\in V(T)\), where strip _S(v_) contains _p_. For each point \(p\in X\) and node \(v\in V(T)\) of the partitioning tree, such that \(p\in S(v)\), we define the set proj(_p,v_) to contain two points on the same row as _p_, that lie on the left and right boundaries of _S(v_), respectively. We denote by _Y<sub>p,v</sub>_ the set that contain the two points proj(_p,v_) if our algorithm adds these two points to the solution. Notice that, since all points of _X_ lie on distinct rows, for any two points \(p\neq p'\), for any pair \(v,v'\in V(T)\) of vertices, \(\mathsf{proj}(p,v)\cap\mathsf{proj}(p',v')=\emptyset\). Observe that we can now write the solution _Y_ as \(Y=\bigcup_{p\in X}\bigcup_{v\in V(T)}Y_{p,v}\). The following lemma characterizes the points of _Y_ in terms of boxes.

**Lemma 7.2** _For every point_ \(p\in X\) _and every node_ \(v\in V(T)\) _of the partitioning tree,_ |_Y<sub>p,v</sub>_|=2 _if and only if there is an index_ \(1\le i\le D\)_, and a subtree_ \(T'\in\mathcal{T}_i\)_, such that (i) p is the first or the last point of its_ \(T'\)_-box; (ii) v lies in the middle layer of_ \(T'\)_; and (iii) S(v_) _contains p._

**Proof:** Let \(1\le i\le D\) be an index, and let \(T'\in\mathcal{T}_i\) be the corresponding partitioning tree. We denote the corresponding point set by \(X'\). Let _v_<sub>1</sub>_,...,v<sub>k</sub>_ be the leaf vertices of \(T'\), and let _u_<sub>1</sub>_,...,u<sub>r</sub>_ be the vertices lying in the middle layer of \(T'\). The only points added to the solution when instance \(X'\) is processed are the following: for every \(1\le j\le r\), for every point \(p\in X'\cap S(u_j)\), we add the two copies of _p_ to the boundaries of _S(u<sub>j</sub>_). In subsequent, or in previous iterations, we may add points to boundaries of _S(u<sub>j</sub>_), but we will never add two copies of the same input point to both boundaries of _S(u<sub>j</sub>_). Therefore, if |_Y<sub>p,v</sub>_|=2, then there must be an index _i_, and a tree \(T'\in\mathcal{T}_i\), such that _v_ lies in the middle layer of \(T'\), and _S(v_) contains _p_. Observe that for every \(T'\)-box _B_, instance \(X'\) only contains the first and the last point of _B_; all remaining points are redundant for \(X'\), and such points are not projected to the boundaries of their strips. Therefore, _p_ must be the first or the last point of its \(T'\)-box. \(\square\)

## An equivalent view of our algorithm:

We can think of our algorithm as follows. First, we compute all families T<sub>0</sub>,T<sub>1</sub>_,...,_T_<sub>D</sub>_ of trees, and for each tree \(T'\) in each family T_<sub>i</sub>_, all \(T'\)-boxes.<tbc>
