The remainder of this section is dedicated to the proof of Theorem 3.6. For convenience, we denote the instances \(\{X_v^s\}_{v\in U}\) by {_X_<sub>1</sub>_,...,X<sub>N</sub>_}, where the instances are indexed in the natural left-to- right order of their corresponding strips, and we denote the instance _X<sup>c</sup>_ by \(\tilde{X}\). For each \(1\le i\le N\), we denote by B_<sub>i</sub>_ be the set of consecutive active columns containing the points of _X<sub>i</sub>_, and we refer to it as a _block_. For brevity, we also say "Wilber bound" to mean the strong WB-1 bound in this section.

## Forbidden Points.

For the sake of the proof, we need the notion of forbidden points. Let \(\hat{X}\) be some semi-permutation and \(\hat{\mathcal{L}}\) be the set of auxiliary columns for \(\hat{X}\). Let \(F\subseteq\hat{X}\) be a set of points that we refer to as _forbidden points_. We now define the strong WB-1 bound with respect to the forbidden points, \(\mathsf{WB}^F(\hat{X})\).

Consider any permutation \(\hat{\sigma}\) of the lines in \(\hat{\mathcal{L}}\). Intuitively, \(\mathsf{WB}_\hat{\sigma}^F(\hat{X})\) counts all the crossings con tributed to \(\mathsf{WB}_\hat{\sigma}(\hat{X})\) but excludes all crossing pairs \((p,p')\) where at least one of \(p,p'\) lie in _F_. Similar to WB(_X_), we define \(\mathsf{WB}^F(\hat{X})=max_\hat{\sigma}\mathsf{WB}_\hat{\sigma}^F(\hat{X})\), where the maximum is over all permutations \(\hat{\sigma}\) of the lines in \(\hat{\mathcal{L}}\).

Next, we define \(\mathsf{WB}_\hat{\sigma}^F(\hat{X})\) more formally. Let \(T=T(\hat{\sigma})\) be the partitioning tree associated with \(\hat{\sigma}\). For each vertex \(v\in V(T)\), let _L=L(v_) be the line that belongs to _v_, and let \(\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}(L)\) be the set of all crossings \((p,p')\) that contribute to cost(_L_); that is, _p_ and \(p'\) are two points that lie in the strip _S(v_) on two opposite sides of _L_, and no other point of \(\hat{X}\cap S(v)\) lies between the row of _p_ and the row of \(p'\). Let \(\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}=\bigcup_{L\in\hat{\mathcal{L}}}\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}(L)\). Observe that \(\mathsf{WB}_\hat{\sigma}(X)=|\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}|\) by definition. We say that a crossing \((p,p')\in\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}(L)\) is _forbidden_ iff at least one of \(p,p'\) lie in _F_; otherwise the crossing is _allowed_. We let \(\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}^F(L)\) be the set of crossings obtained from \(\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}(L)\) by discarding all forbidden crossings. We then let \(\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}^F=\bigcup_{L\in\hat{\mathcal{L}}}\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}^F(L)\), and \(\mathsf{WB}_\hat{\sigma}^F(\hat{X})=|\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}^F|\).

We emphasize that \(\mathsf{WB}^F(\hat{X})\) is not necessarily the same as \(\mathsf{WB}(\hat{X}\backslash F)\), as some crossings of the instance \(\hat{X}\backslash F\) may not correspond to allowed crossings of instance \(\hat{X}\).
