It is however possible that (_p_<sub>1</sub>_,p_<sub>2</sub>) is not a crossing of \(\mathrm{Cr}_{\hat{\sigma}^\prime}(L)\), since by moving the left boundary of _S_ to the left, we add more points to the strip, some of which may lie between the row of _p_<sub>1</sub> and the row of _p_<sub>2</sub>. Let _A_ be the set of all points that lie between the row of _p_<sub>1</sub> and the row of _p_<sub>2</sub> in \(S'\). Notice that the points of _A_ are not forbidden in \(\hat{F}'\). Let \(a\in A\) be the point of _a_ whose row is closest to the row of _p_<sub>2</sub>; if \(A=\emptyset\), then we set _a=p_<sub>1</sub>. Then (_a,p_<sub>2</sub>) defines a crossing in \(\mathrm{Cr}_{\hat{\sigma}^\prime}(L)\), and, since neither point lies in \(\hat{F}'\), \((a,p_2)\in\mathrm{Cr}_{\hat{\sigma}^\prime}^{\hat{F}^\prime}(L)\). In this way, we map every crossing \((p_1,p_2)\in\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}^{\hat{F}}(L)\) to some crossing \((p'_1,p'_2)\in\mathrm{Cr}_{\hat{\sigma}^\prime}^{\hat{F}^\prime}(L)\). It is easy to verify that no two crossings of \(\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}^{\hat{F}}(L)\) are mapped to the same crossing of \(\mathrm{Cr}_{\hat{\sigma}^\prime}^{\hat{F}^\prime}(L)\). We conclude that \(|\mathrm{Cr}_{\hat{\sigma}^\prime}^{\hat{F}^\prime}(L)|\ge|\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}^{\hat{F}}(L)|\).

Lastly, assume that _b<sub>i</sub>_=1. Recall that the set of all points of _X_ lying between \(L_i^*\) and \(L_i^{q_i+1}\) is forbidden in \(\hat{F}\) but not in \(\hat{F}'\), and that we have replaced \(L_i^*\) with the line \(L_i^{q_i+1}\), that lies to the right of \(L_i^*\). Therefore, the right boundary of _S_ remains the same, and the left boundary is pushed to the right. In order to prove that \(|\mathrm{Cr}_{\hat{\sigma}^\prime}^{\hat{F}^\prime}(L)|\ge|\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}^{\hat{F}}(L)|\), we show that every crossing \((p_1,p_2)\in\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}^{\hat{F}}(L)\) belongs to \(\mathrm{Cr}_{\hat{\sigma}^\prime}^{\hat{F}^\prime}(L)\). Indeed, consider any crossing \((p_1,p_2)\in\mathrm{Cr}_\hat{\sigma}^{\hat{F}}(L)\). We know that \(p_1,p_2\in S\), and they lie on opposite sides of _L_. We assume w.l.o.g. that _p_<sub>1</sub> lies to the left of _L_. Since _p_<sub>1</sub> cannot be a forbidden point, it must lie to the right of \(L_i^{q_i+1}\). Moreover, no point of \(X\cap S\) lies between the row of _p_<sub>1</sub> and the row of _p_<sub>2</sub>. It is now easy to verify that (_p_<sub>1</sub>_,p_<sub>2</sub>) is also a crossing in \(\mathrm{Cr}_{\hat{\sigma}^\prime}^{\hat{F}^\prime}(L)\).

## Case 5.

The fifth case happens when _L_ appears between \(L_i^*\) and \(L_i^{**}\) in the permutation \(\hat{\sigma}\), and the right boundary of _S_ belongs to L_<sub>i</sub>_. This case is symmetric to the fourth case and is analyzed similarly. \(\square\)

It now remains to analyze the crossings of the lines in L_<sub>i</sub>_. We do so in the following two claims. The first claim shows that switching \(L_i^*\) with \(L_i^1\) or \(L_i^{q_i+1}\) does not decrease the number of crossings.
