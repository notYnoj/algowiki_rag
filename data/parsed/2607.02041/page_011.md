The undirected Beckmann congestion equilibrium (signed flows; Wardrop’s path-nonnegativity relaxed, App. A) is the convex program (2.3) with \(\Phi_e(f)=\int_0^ft_e=t_e^0f+\frac{bt_e^0}{p+1}f^{p+1}/c_e^p\); its edge law is the inverse marginal cost \(\rho_e=t_e^{-1}\), with conductance \(\rho'_e=1/t'_e(f)=\big(t_e^0bp(f/c_e)^{p-1}/c_e\big)^{-1}>0\) at every finite flow: the cost rises but never caps the flow (the law does not saturate), so there is no feasibility limit and no fold.3 The Jacobian \(J=B\mathrm{diag}(\rho')B^\top\) is a well-conditioned Laplacian at every bounded-flow iterate, and NLF runs the fixed-load Algorithm 3.1.

**4.2. No combinatorial competitor; an interior-point baseline.** No combinatorial algorithm addresses the convex congestion program (2.3). Frank-Wolfe \[13\] converges only sublinearly on the Beckmann program: its O(1_/k_) rate (_k_=iteration count) drives the iteration count to even a 10<sup>-4</sup> duality gap up with problem size and cannot reach the high accuracy NLF attains--the classical slow-tail limitation of the method on traffic assignment \[11\]. The field’s high-accuracy standard, the bush-based family (Algorithm B \[38\], TAPAS \[39\]), solves the _directed_ user equilibrium (nonnegative arc flows, one-sided cost)--a different convex program from the undirected, signed-flow Beckmann relaxation NLF solves here (App. A), so a same- instance head-to-head is ill-posed. We therefore position bush methods qualitatively and benchmark quantitatively against Ipopt \[14\], a mature IPM solver on the identical program. This is conservative: Ipopt attains the same 10<sup>-10</sup>, whereas Frank-Wolfe and bush-based methods do not address the max-flow instance (§5). Ipopt’s per-step cost is a sparse-direct KKT factorization: cheap on planar graphs but fill-in grows superlinearly as separators worsen--exactly the axis on which NLF is immune.

**4.3. Real road data: NLF and Ipopt.** We use the Transportation Networks for Research benchmark \[12\], the field-standard repository of real metropolitan road-network topologies with real capacities, free-flow times and BPR parameters. Across instances spanning three decades of size, NLF and Ipopt reach the identical equilibrium of the undirected single-commodity Beckmann program ( 4.1): the Beckmann objective agrees to the solver tolerance and the equilibrium link flows to \(\|f_\mathrm{NLF}-f_\mathrm{Ipopt}\|/\|f_\mathrm{Ipopt}\|\sim 10^{-10}\), in ≈9 Newton steps with no size trend.
