Nevertheless, even with these simplified collision models, numerical simulation is still computationally demanding. In the past few decades, considerable effort has been devoted to developing efficient and accurate numerical methods for the Boltzmann equation and its simplified collision models; see, e.g., [5-10].

The moment method provides a hierarchy of macroscopic transport models capable of describing rarefaction effects beyond the NSF equations [11]. The method, originally proposed by Grad [12], approximates the distribution function by a Hermite expansion in velocity space with expansion parameters determined by the local physical quantities. When an appropriate closure ansatz is adopted [13, 14], the global hyperbolicity of moment systems of arbitrary order can be guaranteed. As the moment order increases, these systems often provide increasingly accurate approximations to the underlying Boltzmann equation [15, 16]. However, the rapidly increasing number of moment equations makes the direct formulation and implementation of high-order systems cumbersome. To address this issue, a unified numerical framework for high-order moment systems is developed in [7, 17, 18], where the full discretization can be formally obtained without explicitly writing out each equation. While this framework facilitates the practical implementation of high-order moment systems, efficiently computing their steady-state solutions remains challenging.

To accelerate the convergence of steady-state solutions of hyperbolic moment systems, we have previously developed two iterative solvers under the nonlinear multigrid (NMG) framework [19]: an NMG iteration using spatial coarse-grid corrections [20, 21] and a nonlinear multilevel moment (NMLM) iteration using lower-order model corrections [16, 22]. Both methods have shown significant improvement in convergence in comparison to the direct time-integration methods. However, the convergence of both methods still deteriorates significantly as _Kn_ decreases, leading to excessively high computational cost in the near-continuum flow regime. Moreover, integrating the two types of coarse-level corrections into a more efficient and robust multilevel solver remains nontrivial.

Motivated by the slow convergence of these methods in the near-continuum flow regime, we have developed a fast iterative moment (FIM) method in [23] by alternately solving the high-order moment system and the hydrodynamic equations with consistent constitutive relations and boundary conditions. Since the hydrodynamic equations constitute a subsystem of the moment system for \(M\ge 2\), they can efficiently predict the evolution of the primary macroscopic quantities at a much lower computational cost and thereby accelerate the overall iteration. This alternating framework can be further incorporated into the spatial NMG method, leading to an FIM-based multigrid solver that combines coarse-level corrections in the velocity and spatial spaces. From a broader perspective, the FIM method shares some conceptual similarities with several recently developed micro-macro acceleration methods, including the general synthetic iterative scheme (GSIS) [24], the unified gas-kinetic scheme (UGKS) [25], and the improved discrete velocity method (IDVM) [9, 26], although their formulations and implementations are fundamentally different.

Nevertheless, there is still room for improvement in the accuracy and efficiency of the FIM method. The original implementation is restricted to the Boltzmann-BGK equation and employs only first-order spatial discretization, which introduces considerable numerical dissipation and limits the spatial resolution. Moreover, the time-integration-based hydrodynamic solver in each alternating iteration usually requires a large number of iterations to sufficiently evolve the macroscopic quantities when _Kn_ is small. Its convergence may also slow down as the grid is refined, leading to increased computational cost for high-resolution simulations. Motivated by these limitations, in this work we extend the FIM solver to the Shakhov model, with the following main contributions.

• The spatial discretization is extended to second-order accuracy by employing a finite volume method with linear reconstruction for both the moment system and the consistent hydrodynamic equations.<tbc>

• An implicit-explicit (IMEX) scheme is adopted for the moment system, in which the Shakhov collision term is treated implicitly to improve numerical stability at small Knudsen numbers, as in the original FIM method for the BGK model.<tbc>

• A local Fourier analysis of a linearized one-dimensional BGK model is performed to explain the need for more hydrodynamic iterations at smaller Knudsen numbers and their slower convergence on refined grids.<tbc>

• A Newton-multigrid method equipped with a new regularization strategy is introduced for the hydrodynamic equations, replacing the time-integration-based methods used in [23].<tbc>
