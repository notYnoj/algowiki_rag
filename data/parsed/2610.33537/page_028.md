**Proposition 2.** _Under_ (C.1)_, when_ \(|k\Delta x|\le 2\pi/5\),

\(|\mathcal{G}_0^{(0)}(k)|\ge|\mathcal{G}_{\pm\sqrt{3}}^{(0)}(k)|.\) (C.21)

_More precisely, the inequality is strict for_ \(0<|k\Delta x|\le 2\pi/5\)_, whereas all three moduli are equal to one at k_=0.

_Proof of Proposition 1._ Set

\(r=\frac{\mu}{C_{M+1}},x=\mathrm{cos}\zeta,F_\alpha(x)=\frac{(1-\nu)^2+\frac{\nu^2\alpha^2}{4}+\nu(1-\nu)\alpha x}{1+\frac{\nu^2\alpha^2}{4}-\nu\alpha x}.\) (C.22)

Since \(|r|\le 1\) under (C.1), both 1-_r_ and 1+_r_ belong to [0,2]. Taking the modulus of (40) gives

\(|\mathcal{G}_\mu^{(0)}(k)|^2=F_{1-r}(x)F_{1+r}(x).\) (C.23)

For \(\alpha>0\),

\(\frac{dF_\alpha}{dx}=\frac{\nu\alpha(2-\nu)\left(1-\nu+\frac{\nu^2\alpha^2}{4}\right)}{\left(1+\frac{\nu^2\alpha^2}{4}-\nu\alpha x\right)^2}>0,\) (C.24)

whereas \(F_0(x)=(1-\nu)^2\) is independent of _x_. Therefore the right-hand side of (C.23) is nondecreasing in \(x=\mathrm{cos}\zeta\). Since \(\mathrm{cos}\zeta\) is even in \(\zeta\) and decreases as \(|\zeta|\) increases from 0 to \(\pi\), it follows that \(|\mathcal{G}_\mu^{(0)}(k)|\) is even in \(\zeta\) and nonincreasing in \(|\zeta|\). Finally, direct substitution into (40) gives

\(\mathcal{G}_\mu^{(0)}(0)=1,\) (C.25)

which proves (C.20).

_Proof of Proposition 2._ Replacing \(\mu\) by \(-\mu\) in (40) gives the complex conjugate after exchanging the two factors. Hence

\(|\mathcal{G}_{-\sqrt{3}}^{(0)}(k)|=|\mathcal{G}_{\sqrt{3}}^{(0)}(k)|.\) (C.26)

It remains to compare the branches \(\mu=0\) and \(\mu=\sqrt{3}\). Set \(q=3/C_{M+1}^2\) and \(t=1-\mathrm{cos}k\Delta x\). Then \(0<q\le 1\) and \(0\le t\le 2\). For \(0\le\alpha\le 2\), define

\(D_\alpha(t)=1+\frac{\nu^2\alpha^2}{4}-\nu\alpha(1-t)>0.\) (C.27)

A direct subtraction of the two squared moduli gives

\(|\mathcal{G}_0^{(0)}(k)|^2-|\mathcal{G}_{\sqrt{3}}^{(0)}(k)|^2=\frac{\nu^3q(2-\nu)t}{32[D_1(t)]^2D_{1-\sqrt{q}}(t)D_{1+\sqrt{q}}(t)}P_q(t),\) (C.28)

where<tbc>

\(P_q(t)=64(1-\nu)^2t^2+2\left[(2-\nu)^2(\nu^2+20\nu-20)-\nu^4q\right]t+(2-\nu)^2\left[5(2-\nu)^2-\nu^2q\right].\)

<tbc>Moreover,

\(\frac{\partial P_q(t)}{\partial q}=-2\nu^4t-\nu^2(2-\nu)^2<0,\) (C.29)

so \(q\le 1\) implies \(P_q(t)\ge P_1(t)\). Let

\(t_*=1-\mathrm{cos}\frac{2\pi}{5}=\frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{4}.\) (C.30)
