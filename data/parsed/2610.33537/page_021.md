## 5.3. Heat transfer in a bottom-heated cavity

The third example is a heat transfer problem for the gas confined in a bottom-heated cavity \[21, 40, 42\]. The computational domain and gas properties are the same as those in the lid-driven cavity problem (section 5.2). The initial conditions are also the same, except that the temperature is _T_<sup>0</sup>=300K. All walls are stationary, with the bottom wall maintained at 600K and the other three walls at 300K.

The temperature and heat-flux solutions obtained by the second-order FIM solver on a \(64\times 64\) grid are shown in Figure 10 and agree well with the DUGKS results generated by the dugksFoam solver. As _Kn_ increases, the temperature jumps near the bottom-left and bottom-right corners are more obvious due to the discontinuous boundary temperature \[43\].
