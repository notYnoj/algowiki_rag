This is done by induction on \(\ell\). When \(\ell=0\), (1.7) is just the very definition of _B<sub>k</sub>_. Assume next that (1.7) holds for \(\ell\in\{0,\dots,n-2\}\). Let \(k\in\{1,\dots,n-(\ell+1)\}\). Then,

\(D^{\ell+1}B_k=\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{i\in\mathfrak{S}_n}S(\Delta_{i_1,\dots,i_{\ell+1}}S)^{i_{\ell+2},\dots,i_{k+\ell}}-\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{i\in\mathfrak{S}_n}S(\Delta_{i_1,\dots,i_{\ell+1}}S)^{i_{\ell+2},\dots,i_{k+1+\ell}}\)<br>
\(=\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{i\in\mathfrak{S}_n}S(\Delta_{i_1,\dots,i_{\ell+1}}S)^{i_{\ell+3},\dots,i_{k+1+\ell}}-\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{i\in\mathfrak{S}_n}S(\Delta_{i_1,\dots,i_{\ell+1}}S)^{i_{\ell+2},\dots,i_{k+1+\ell}}\)<br>
\(=\mathbb{E}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{i\in\mathfrak{S}_n}S(\Delta_{i_1,\dots,i_{\ell+2}}S)^{i_{\ell+3},\dots,i_{k+1+\ell}},\)

where in getting the second equality, the terms are reindexed.

We wish now to study potential connections between the _B<sub>k</sub>_’s and jackknives operators _J<sub>k</sub>_ and _K<sub>k</sub>_ previously studied in \[4\]. For \(Y\in\sigma(X_1,\dots,X_n)\), i.e., Y measurable with respect to the \(\sigma\)-field generated by _X_<sub>1</sub>_,...,X<sub>n</sub>_ and \(i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\), let E<sup>(_i_)</sup>_Y_:=E(_Y|X_<sub>1</sub>_,...,X_<sub>_i_-1</sub>_,X_<sub>_i_+1</sub>_,...,X<sub>n</sub>_) (with the convention E<sup>(0)</sup>_Y_:=_Y_) and more generally for any subset \(\alpha\) of {1_,...,n_}, let<tbc>

\(\mathbb{E}^\alpha Y:=\mathbb{E}(Y|(X_j)_{j\notin\alpha}),\)

<tbc>with \(\mathbb{E}^\emptyset Y=Y\) , and E<sup>{1_,...,n_}</sup>_Y_=E_Y_ . Next, for \(i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\), let<tbc>

\(\mathrm{Var}^{(i)}Y:=\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Y^2-(\mathbb{E}^{(i)}Y)^2\)

<tbc>while iterating, for \(i\in\mathfrak{S}_n\), and \(k\ge 1\), let

\(\mathrm{Var}^{(i_1,\dots,i_k)}Y:=\mathbb{E}^{(i_1)}(\mathrm{Var}^{(i_2,\dots,i_k)}Y)-\mathrm{Var}^{(i_2,\dots,i_k)}(\mathbb{E}^{(i_1)}Y),\)

and so (using the commutation property of conditional expectations) for any \(\alpha=\{i_1,\dots,i_k\}\), \(\mathrm{Var}^\alpha Y\) is well defined.

For \(k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\), let<tbc>

\(J_k:=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_1\neq i_2\dots\neq i_k}\mathrm{Var}^{(i_1,\dots,i_k)}S,\)

<tbc>and

\(K_k:=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i_1\neq i_2\dots\neq i_k}\mathrm{Var}^{(i_1,\dots,i_k)}\mathbb{E}^\overline{(i_1,\dots,i_k)}S,\)

where <o>(_i_<sub>1</sub>_,...,i<sub>k</sub>_)</o>=(_i_<sub>_k_+1</sub>_,...,i<sub>n</sub>_). For ease of notation, set also \(J'_k:=J_k/k!\) and \(K'_k:=K_k/k!\). The next lemma provides relationships between these quantities and the _B<sub>k</sub>_’s, it allows to easily get, and in a unified fashion, many of the known expressions involving the variance, along with some new ones.

**Lemma 1.3.** _Let_ \(\alpha\neq\emptyset\) _and_ \(\beta\) _be two disjoint subsets of_ {1_,...,n}. Then_

\(\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{\textit{Var}}^\alpha\mathbb{E}^\beta S\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(S(\Delta_\alpha S)^\beta\right).\) (1.8)

_Proof._ If \(\beta=\emptyset\), then the result is clear. Next, the proof is done by induction on the cardinality of \(\alpha\). For the base case, let \(|\alpha|=1\), i.e., let \(\alpha=\{i\}\), for some \(i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\). Then,

\(\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Var}^{(i)}\mathbb{E}^\beta S\right)=\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}^{(i)}(\mathbb{E}^\beta S)^2-(\mathbb{E}^{(i)}\mathbb{E}^\beta S)^2\right)\)<br>
\(=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}^\beta S)^2-\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}^{\beta,i}S)^2\)<br>
\(=\mathbb{E}(SS^\beta)-\mathbb{E}(SS^{\beta,i})\)<br>
\(=\mathbb{E}(S(\Delta_iS)^\beta),\)

where to get the next to last equality we use \(\mathbb{E}(SS^\beta)=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}^\beta(SS^\beta))=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}^\beta S)^2\), which follows from the independence assumption. Now, let us assume the validity of the lemma for any set of cardinality _k_ and let \(\alpha=\{i_1,\dots,i_{k+1}\}\).
