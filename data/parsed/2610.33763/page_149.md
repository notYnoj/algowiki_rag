since \(\kappa,\xi\ge 0\) a.e. due to (9.4) and (9.5). Hence, we are again in the framework of Theorem 9.1. Therefore, by Theorem 9.1 we conclude that the sequence of geometric means satisfies the GLT relation<tbc>

\(\{G(A_{\mathbf{n},\varepsilon},B_\mathbf{n})\}_\mathbf{n}\sim_\mathrm{GLT}\left(\kappa_\varepsilon\xi\right)^\frac{1}{2}.\)

<tbc>Furthermore, we apply the full ∗-algebra framework of the GLT matrix sequences as in \[1, Section 2.9\]. More precisely, by third item of GLT 3 followed by GLT 6 with the function \(f(z)=z^\frac{1}{4}\), we obtain<tbc>

\(\{(A_{\mathbf{n},\varepsilon} B_\mathbf{n}^2A_{\mathbf{n},\varepsilon})^\frac{1}{4}\}_\mathbf{n}\sim_\mathrm{GLT}\left(\kappa_\varepsilon^2\xi^2\right)^\frac{1}{4}=(\kappa_\varepsilon\xi)^\frac{1}{2},\)

<tbc>which is the very same GLT symbol of \(\{G(A_{\mathbf{n},\varepsilon},B_\mathbf{n})\}_\mathbf{n}\). As a result, again by the second item of GLT 3, the difference between these sequences satisfies the following asymptotic GLT relation<tbc>

\(\{G(A_{\mathbf{n},\varepsilon},B_\mathbf{n})-(A_{\mathbf{n},\varepsilon} B_\mathbf{n}^2A_{\mathbf{n},\varepsilon})^\frac{1}{4}\}_\mathbf{n}\sim_\mathrm{GLT}0.\) (9.6)

<tbc>The previous relation is the key step since we have found a new GLT matrix sequence having the same GLT symbol as the geometric mean matrix sequence \(\{G(A_{\mathbf{n},\varepsilon},B_\mathbf{n})\}_\mathbf{n}\), but where no inversion is required. Now given the structure of the performed operations, by virtue of Definition 2.3, the class<tbc>

\(\{\{(A_{n,\epsilon_m}B_n^2A_{n,\epsilon_m})^\frac{1}{4}\}_n\}_m,\epsilon_m=\frac{1}{m},\)

<tbc>is obviously an a.c.s. for \(\{(A_\mathbf{n}B_\mathbf{n}^2A_\mathbf{n})^\frac{1}{4}\}_\mathbf{n}\). As a consequence, by invoking (9.6), also the class\(\{\{G(A_{n,\epsilon_m},B_n)\}_n\}_m\) is an a.c.s. for \(\{(A_\mathbf{n}B_\mathbf{n}^2A_\mathbf{n})^\frac{1}{4}\}_\mathbf{n}\), with all the in volved sequences being GLT matrix sequences, with symbols \((\kappa_\varepsilon\xi)^\frac{1}{2},(\kappa\xi)^\frac{1}{2}\), \(\varepsilon>0\). Therefore, since \(\exists\mathrm{lim}_{m\rightarrow 0}(\kappa_{\varepsilon_m}\xi)^\frac{1}{2}=(\kappa\xi)^\frac{1}{2}\), by using the powerful Theorem 2.1, we deduce that \(\exists\mathrm{lim}_{m\rightarrow 0}\) (a.c.s.) \(\{\{G(A_{\mathbf{n},\varepsilon_m},B_\mathbf{n})\}_\mathbf{n}\}_m=\{(A_\mathbf{n}B_\mathbf{n}^2A_\mathbf{n})^\frac{1}{4}\}_\mathbf{n}\sim_\mathrm{GLT}\left(\kappa\xi\right)^\frac{1}{2}\) and this limit coincides with {_G(A<sub>n</sub>,B<sub>n</sub>_)}<sub>**_n_**</sub> in the a.c.s. topology, that is, \(\{G(A_\mathbf{n},B_\mathbf{n})-(A_\mathbf{n}B_\mathbf{n}^2A_\mathbf{n})^\frac{1}{4}\}_\mathbf{n}\sim_\mathrm{GLT}0\). Finally \(\{(A_\mathbf{n}B_\mathbf{n}^2A_\mathbf{n})^\frac{1}{4}\}_\mathbf{n}\sim_\mathrm{GLT}\left(\kappa\xi\right)^\frac{1}{2}\). Hence<tbc>

\(\{G(A_\mathbf{n},B_\mathbf{n})\}_\mathbf{n}\sim_\mathrm{GLT}\left(\kappa\xi\right)^{1/2},\)

<tbc>so that, by GLT 1, we infer

\(\{G(A_\mathbf{n},B_\mathbf{n})\}_\mathbf{n}\sim_{\sigma,\lambda}\left(\kappa\xi\right)^{1/2}.\)

**Remark 9.1 (Intuition on the generalization)** _One of the basic but key ingredients of the previous proof is that scalar-valued functions commute. Hence, it is reasonable to expect that the same proof also works in the case of d-level r-block GLT matrix sequences, when assuming that the symbols commute. We collect the result in Theorem 9.4 whose proof follows verbatim that of Theorem 9.2, except for minimal changes. On the other hand, when both_ \(\kappa\) _and_ \(\xi\) _are not invertible almost everywhere (degenerate) and do not commute, the expression_ \(G(\kappa,\xi)\) _is not well defined. In fact, we could replace the inversion with the standard pseudoinversion, which is denoted as X_<sup>+</sup> _if X is any complex matrix. However, we stress that the expression_ \(\kappa^\frac{1}{2}([\kappa^\frac{1}{2}]^+\xi[\kappa^\frac{1}{2}]^+)^\frac{1}{2}\kappa^\frac{1}{2}\) _is not the same as_ \(\xi^\frac{1}{2}([\xi^\frac{1}{2}]^+\kappa[\xi^\frac{1}{2}]^+)^\frac{1}{2}\xi^\frac{1}{2}\) _in general and this is a serious indication that the commutation between the GLT symbols is essential, when the symbols are both degenerate._
