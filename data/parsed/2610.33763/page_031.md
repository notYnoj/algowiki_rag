## Chapter 3

### Structured matrices

Matrices arising from the discretization of differential equations frequently exhibit characteristic structural patterns that reflect the analytical properties of the underlying continuous operators. Identifying and exploiting such structures is a central theme in numerical linear algebra, as it often leads to significant reductions in computational cost and provides insight into the behavior of numerical schemes. Among the most prominent examples are Toeplitz matrices, which are defined by constant entries along each diagonal. This structure naturally emerges from the discretization of constant coefficient PDEs and FDEs, where translation invariance of the operator is preserved at the discrete level. In a one-dimensional setting, the use of uniform grids typically produces standard Toeplitz matrices, while higher-dimensional problems posed on rectangular domains give rise to multi-level Toeplitz structures. In this case, the discretization leads to block Toeplitz matrices whose blocks are themselves Toeplitz, with the number of recursive levels directly linked to the spatial dimension of the problem. Such structured matrices play a fundamental role in both theoretical analysis and algorithmic design, forming the basis for fast matrix- vector products, efficient solvers, and the spectral tools developed in the subsequent sections of this chapter.<br>
Throughout this chapter, all the structures are introduced directly in the _d_-level, _r_- block setting, that is, for \(n\in\mathbb{N}^d\) and \(r\in\mathbb{N}\) the matrices under consideration belong to \(\mathbb{C}^{N(n)r\times N(n)r}\) and their entries are \(r\times r\) matrices rather than scalars. This level of generality is required in the rest of the thesis, where blocks of size _r\>_1 arise from systems of FDEs, from vector-valued unknowns and from all-at-once discretizations. The scalar case treated in the classical literature is recovered by setting _r_=1, in which case all the statements below reduce verbatim to their scalar counterparts.<br>
This chapter is devoted to the systematic study of Toeplitz and related structured matrices. In Section 3.1, we introduce the formal definition of multi-level Toeplitz matrices and review their main algebraic and spectral properties, drawing on the extensive literature developed over the past decades. Unless stated otherwise, these results are collected from [42, Chapter 3]. In addition, we discuss several matrix classes that either arise as special cases of Toeplitz matrices or exhibit strong structural and spectral connections with them, which makes them particularly relevant in numerical applications. Circulant and \(\omega\)-circulant matrices are presented in Sections 3.2 and 3.3, respectively, while Sections 3.4 and 3.5 are devoted to \(\tau\) and Hankel matrices.
