In more general settings, such as variable coefficients, nonuniform grids, or non-Cartesian domains, the classical Toeplitz structure is typically lost. Nevertheless, many discretization matrices arising in practice belong to the broader class of GLT matrix sequences [124, 41, 42]. The GLT theory provides a unifying framework for describing the asymptotic spectral behavior of large structured and partially structured matrices. GLT sequences form a rich ∗-algebra that includes Toeplitz sequences, diagonal sampling matrices, and zero-distributed sequences as fundamental components. Each GLT sequence is uniquely associated with a symbol, and algebraic operations on matrix sequences correspond, almost everywhere, to pointwise operations on their symbols [41, 42]. This framework supports the design and analysis of numerical solvers, preconditioners, and multigrid methods, since the convergence of Krylov subspace and multigrid schemes depends strongly on conditioning and eigenvalue clustering, which can be predicted and optimized via GLT symbols [41, 27, 95]. Recent developments in symbol-based preconditioning for evolutionary PDEs include the block Toeplitz preconditioner for wave equations introduced by Hon and Serra-Capizzano [51], and the sine transform based preconditioned MINRES method for all-at-once systems developed by Hon, Fung, Dong, and Serra-Capizzano [53]. These works highlight the effectiveness of combining structural properties with fast transforms, and the present thesis extends these ideas to IPs and variable-coefficient FDEs.<br>
A particularly rich connection within the theory of GLT matrix sequences concerns the notion of geometric means of matrices. The geometric mean of Hermitian positive definite matrices was rigorously formalized by Ando, Li, and Mathias and has since become a fundamental concept in matrix analysis and operator theory. Given two Hermitian positive definite matrices _A_ and _B_, their geometric mean is defined by

\(G(A,B)=A^{1/2}\big(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\big)^{1/2}A^{1/2}=G(B,A),\)

a definition that preserves symmetry, positive definiteness, and key variational properties [3, 16]. Geometric means naturally arise in preconditioning strategies, operator interpolation, and the modeling of composite physical systems, where nonlinear matrix operations are unavoidable. When applied to sequences of Hermitian positive definite matrices, the geometric mean interacts in a subtle way with asymptotic spectral properties. Within the GLT framework, this interaction raises fundamental questions concerning symbol calculus, commutativity of matrix-valued symbols, degeneracy, and maximality of distribution results.

## Structure of the thesis

This thesis is structured as follows.<br>
**Chapter 1** introduces the analytical foundations required throughout the thesis. We review the main definitions of fractional calculus employed in our work, including the Riemann–Liouville, Caputo, Grünwald–Letnikov, and Riesz fractional operators, together with their relationships under suitable regularity assumptions. Generalized formulations in the form of tempered fractional derivatives are also discussed, due to their relevance in modeling finite-range diffusion processes. The chapter further presents Mittag–Leffler and generalized Mittag–Leffler type functions, along with their key analytical properties used in the study of FDEs and IPs.
