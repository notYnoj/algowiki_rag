## Chapter 4

### GLT matrix sequences and their algebraic structure

We are now in a position to introduce the theory of GLT matrix sequences, which provides the central spectral framework for the analysis carried out in this thesis. The GLT machinery offers a unified language for describing the asymptotic behavior of large structured matrices generated by the discretization of differential and nonlocal operators and, at the same time, supplies the theoretical tools required for the design of efficient numerical solvers.<br>
In the present setting we consider the multi-level block case, corresponding to _d_- level GLT matrix sequences with \(r\times r\) matrix-valued symbols, where \(d\ge 1\) denotes the number of structural levels and \(r\ge 1\) is the block size. For fixed _d_ and _r_, the associated GLT class is characterized by symbols given by measurable functions

\(\kappa:[0,1]^d\times[-\pi,\pi]^d\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{r\times r},\)

which encode the asymptotic spectral distribution of the matrix sequence. While the scalar case is thoroughly treated in the monographs [41, 42], the matrix-valued framework adopted here follows the more general and flexible approach developed in [11, 10]. This setting is essential for the study of block discretizations and coupled systems arising in PDEs and FDEs. Moreover, the theory continues to evolve, as witnessed by recent developments on reduced and rectangular GLT sequences [8, 9]. Rather than presenting the full constructive definition of the multi-level GLT class, we focus on the fundamental operative properties that uniquely characterize it and that are most relevant for numerical applications. These properties, commonly referred to as the _GLT axioms_, provide a complete algebraic and spectral description of the class and will be systematically used in the subsequent analysis. They are introduced and discussed in Section 4.6. In addition, we highlight how the GLT framework naturally guides the construction of effective preconditioning strategies, thereby establishing the link between asymptotic spectral theory and fast iterative solvers.

### 4.1 The GLT class

Let us first review some basic aspects to better understand GLT sequences. The theory of LT sequences provides a powerful framework for analyzing the asymptotic singular value and eigenvalue distributions of structured matrix sequences that arise in numerical analysis. In the context of PDE discretization, the matrices resulting from numerical approximations often fail to retain the classical Toeplitz structure due to the presence of variable coefficients.
