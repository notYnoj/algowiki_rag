We further demonstrated that the result is maximal: when the symbols do not commute and are simultaneously degenerate, the classical relation may fail and the geometric mean sequence may not coincide with the classical geometric mean of the symbols. Numerical experiments revealed new spectral phenomena explained by the theory of momentary GLT symbols, including secondary eigenvalue distributions and extremal spectral behavior connected to the order of vanishing of the associated GLT symbol. The numerical evidences open the door to further theoretical studies. Finally, the study of the spectral distribution when we consider the geometric mean of more than two matrix sequences is completely open and requires normwise error estimates for using the relevant tools.

## Future research directions

The same theoretical apparatus, including GLT theory, approximating classes of sequences, and non-Hermitian perturbations of Hermitian matrix sequences, could be applied to even more complex cases. For instance, high-order finite element approximations in _d_ spatial dimensions lead to _d_-level Toeplitz matrices with matrix- valued generating functions, corresponding to the GLT symbol [14]. Using similar approaches, we could also consider discontinuous Galerkin methods or isogeometric analysis. These cases lead to matrix-valued symbols in 2_d_ independent variables, as in the general GLT theory already available [8, 10, 11, 41, 42]. Finally, as a future task in this research direction, we will consider the design of optimized code in order to match the theoretical complexity derived in the present work for the preconditioning strategies proposed in the research chapters. The implementation of efficient algorithms that fully exploit the structure of the preconditioners, combined with careful optimization and parallelization, would further enhance the practical applicability of our approach.
