The numerical results reported in this chapter indicate that the eigenvalue distribution may not follow the GLT symbol in the current setting, even when _a_ is chosen as a constant function. This contrasts with the spatial discretization case, where the numerical evidence was consistent with the symbol. The distinction is important: for non- Hermitian matrices, the GLT symbol provides a rigorous description of the singular value distribution, but the eigenvalue distribution requires additional analysis. In this work, we focus on preconditioning strategies that ensure weak clustering at one for the preconditioned matrix sequences, which is sufficient to guarantee fast convergence of the GMRES method without requiring a full characterization of the eigenvalue distribution of the original coefficient matrices.

## 8.2 Preconditioning strategy: 1D space setting

Since the considered coefficient matrices are non-symmetric and exhibit a Toeplitz- like structure, Krylov subspace methods such as GMRES \[107\] are well-suited for solving the linear systems in (8.15), benefiting from efficient fast matrix-vector products. To further enhance the efficiency and robustness of these iterative solvers, the use of preconditioning techniques is an essential strategy \[15\].<br>
Here, we propose a block circulant preconditioner for _A<sub>N,M</sub>_, obtained by replacing the Toeplitz block \(\overline{G_{\alpha,N}}\) with a circulant counterpart \(C_{\alpha,N}\), while keeping \(Q_{\theta,M}\) unchanged. Since our constant term \(\gamma=\Delta t/(\Delta x)^\alpha\) is sufficiently large, we apply the circulant approximation only to \(\overline{G_{\alpha,N}}\) and retain \(Q_{\theta,M}\) as it is. In order to preserve the circulant structure, instead of using the diagonal matrix _D<sub>N</sub>(a_), we replace it with a constant scalar multiplier given by the average of the diffusion coefficients<tbc>

\(d=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^Nd_i,\)

<tbc>where _d<sub>i</sub>_ are the diagonal entries of _D<sub>N</sub>(a_). Of course the choice of the circulant counterpart is far from unique and several proposals are treated in three decades of relevant literature. From theoretical studies, we know that Strang-type approaches are better than Frobenius optimal approximations, especially for matching the small eigenvalues and especially when the generating functions are smooth. However, here we have lower Hessenberg structures and hence, together with the usual Strang approximation, we take the Strang-type matrix chosen taking into account the largest coefficients, i.e. those on the first column, except the last which is replaced by that on upper main diagonal. More precisely, we consider these two possible circulant approximations, where the first preconditioner is defined as<tbc>

\(\widetilde{P}_{\alpha,N,M}=\gamma d\widetilde{C}_{\alpha,N}\otimes I_M+I_N\otimes Q_{\theta,M}\) (8.35)

<tbc>where \(\widetilde{C}_{\alpha,N}\) is the standard Strang circulant approximation, while the latter is defined as
