_b_3) \(\{G_{\alpha,\mathbf{n}}\}_\mathbf{n}\sim_\lambda a(x,y)\left(\gamma_1^*f_{\alpha_1}(\xi_1)+\gamma_2^*f_{\alpha_2}(\xi_2)\right)\)?

As in the one-dimensional case, the eigenvalue distribution is not an automatic GLT consequence for non-Hermitian matrices, but it is partially supported by the numerical evidence presented; see Figures 8.3-8.4. In the two-dimensional case, we first consider the constant-coefficient matrix \(U_{\alpha,\mathbf{n}}\) in equation (8.22) and compare its eigenvalues with sampled values of the symbol \(f_{\alpha_1}(\xi_1)+f_{\alpha_2}(\xi_2)\) (Figure 8.3). We then examine the variable-coefficient case \(D_\mathbf{n}(a)U_{\alpha,\mathbf{n}}\) in equation (8.21), with _a(x,y_)=_x_<sup>2</sup>+_y_<sup>2</sup>+1, comparing the eigenvalues with sampled values of the symbol \(a(x,y)\left(f_{\alpha_1}(\xi_1)+f_{\alpha_2}(\xi_2)\right)\) (Figure 8.4). It is evident that the two-dimensional setting is more intricate and we are not sure that the tools from \[21\] are useful as in the one- dimensional setting. The question remains open and it is left for future researches.
