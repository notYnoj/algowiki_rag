## 9.2.2 Distribution results and momentary symbols

### Example 2

Let _d_=1 and define the matrix sequences {_A<sub>n</sub>}<sub>n</sub>_, {_B<sub>n</sub>}<sub>n</sub>_, with \(A_n=D_n(a)+\frac{1}{n^4}I_n\), \(C_n=T_n(3+2cos(\theta))\) and \(B_n=\left(D_n(1-a)+\frac{1}{n^4}I_n\right)C_n\left(D_n(1-a)+\frac{1}{n^4}I_n\right)\), where _D<sub>n</sub>(a_) is the diagonal matrix generated by the piecewise continuous function _a(x_) given in **Example 1**. Then, the geometric mean sequence explicitly satisfies the GLT relation:

\(\left\{G\left(A_n,B_n\right)\right\}_n\sim_\mathrm{GLT}\sqrt{a(x)(1-a(x))(3+2cos\theta)}\)<br> \(=a(x)(1-a(x))\sqrt{3+2cos\theta}=0\)

where

\(\kappa=a(x),\xi=(1-a(x))(3+2cos(\theta)).\)

The zero-valued symbol arises naturally due to the definition of the piecewise function _a(x_):

\(\bullet\) For \(x\in[0,\frac{1}{2})\), we have _a(x_)=0. Thus, the term _a(x_)(1-_a(x_)) becomes zero.

\(\bullet\) For \(x\in[\frac{1}{2},1]\), we have _a(x_)=1. In this interval, the factor 1-_a(x_) becomes zero, making _a(x_)(1-_a(x_))=0.

### Eigenvalue distribution

The eigenvalue distribution of {_G(A<sub>n</sub>,B<sub>n</sub>_)}_<sub>n</sub>_ is numerically analyzed for increasing matrix dimensions _n_. Since the GLT symbol for this example is zero, by Theorem 9.2 we expect the eigenvalues to concentrate along the zero line. Interestingly, numerical results reveal an additional spectral structure beyond Theorem 9.2. For smaller values of _n_, eigenvalues align closely with zero, as predicted by the GLT symbol. However, some eigenvalues appear above this level, forming a secondary symbol. This phenomenon is attributed to momentary symbols, as discussed in the introduction (see [23, 24, 7]). As _n_ increases, these elevated eigenvalues shift upwards reaching approximately 10<sup>-3</sup> for _n_=40 (shown in the Figure 9.2) and 10<sup>-4</sup> for _n_=80.
