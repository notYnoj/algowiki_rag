## Chapter 7

### Efficient Krylov solvers for inverse source problem in 2D space-time fractional diffusion equation

The motivation for this work stems from the increasing importance of space-time FDEs, which offer a versatile framework for modeling complex, multiscale phenomena in a rich variety of scientific disciplines. By incorporating fractional derivatives, FDEs can capture essential features such as memory effects, non-local interactions, and anomalous diffusion behaviors. These characteristics make FDEs ideal for modeling processes that involve complex transport phenomena, including anomalous diffusion, subdiffusion, and superdiffusion in heterogeneous media. In particular, the Caputo fractional derivative is widely used for modeling spatial heterogeneities, as it incorporates physically meaningful initial conditions and avoids singularities at the boundaries. Meanwhile, tempered fractional derivatives are effective in describing transient anomalous diffusion, where the diffusion initially follows a fractional power law and later transitions to normal diffusion [112].

In this chapter, based on the results presented in [68], we develop a fast algorithm to reconstruct the source term _f(x,y_) in the context of a two-dimensional space-time FDE, generalizing the work in [66] devoted to the uni-dimensional space version. Additional details, numerical experiments, and reformulations are included for completeness of the thesis presentation. Since IPs are often ill-posed in the sense of Hadamard, small perturbations in the input data can lead to significant errors in the solution [60, 70, 135, 61, 67]. To address this, we apply the quasi-boundary value method, a nonlocal regularization technique that has been successfully utilized to handle similar ill-posed problems in both parabolic and FDEs [29, 48]. The finite difference discretization of the regularized problem leads to a two-by-two block linear system. We design symbol-based approximations [66, 54] to the resulting coefficient matrices and construct an effective preconditioner for the GMRES method [107]. A detailed spectral analysis of the associated matrix sequences is carried out using the GLT theory, which provides a rigorous description of their asymptotic spectral behavior despite the presence of the variable coefficient _a(x,y_) that spoils the pure Toeplitz structure [41, 42, 10, 11]. Numerical experiments confirm the effectiveness of the proposed preconditioning strategy in reducing iteration counts and computational cost.
