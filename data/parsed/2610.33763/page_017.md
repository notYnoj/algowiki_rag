In **Chapter 2**, we introduce the foundational concepts related to matrix sequences that are essential for the subsequent spectral analysis. The chapter focuses on singular value and eigenvalue distributions in the asymptotic sense, approximation classes of matrix sequences, and multi-level structures. These topics provide the mathematical background required for understanding the spectral behavior of large structured matrices arising from discretized differential operators.<br>
In **Chapter 3**, we review several classes of classical structured matrices that play a central role in numerical linear algebra. In particular, we discuss Toeplitz, circulant, \(\omega\)-circulant, \(\tau\), and Hankel matrices, presenting their definitions together with their main algebraic and spectral properties. The importance of these matrix classes in both numerical computations and asymptotic linear algebra is highlighted, with particular emphasis on their spectral features and computational advantages.<br>
**Chapter 4** is devoted to the development of the theory of GLT matrix sequences, which constitutes the core theoretical framework for the spectral analysis carried out in this thesis. We introduce the notion of GLT sequences and present the structure of GLT algebras together with their fundamental properties. The chapter establishes the axioms and algebraic rules governing GLT sequences, distinguishing between matrix sequences with explicit and hidden asymptotic structures. Key concepts such as zero-distributed sequences, block Toeplitz and block diagonal sampling matrices, and the construction of _d_-level, _r_-block GLT ∗-algebras are also discussed in detail.<br>
**Chapter 5** is devoted to the study of IPs for a two-dimensional generalized diffusion equation involving memory-dependent time-fractional derivatives. The chapter considers the identification of both a space-dependent source term and a time-dependent coefficient from additional measurement data. The IPs are shown to be ill-posed in the sense of Hadamard, and existence and uniqueness of solutions are established under suitable over-specified and regularity assumptions. Special cases corresponding to particular choices of the memory kernel are analyzed in detail.<br>
In **Chapter 6**, we investigate an IP for a space-time FDE with variable coefficients. The ill-posedness of the problem is addressed through a quasi-boundary value regularization. A finite difference discretization yields large two-by-two block linear system. Its spectral behavior is analyzed within the framework of GLT, which provides guidance for the construction of an effective Krylov subspace preconditioner. Numerical experiments demonstrate the accuracy of the reconstruction and the efficiency of the proposed preconditioning strategy.<br>
**Chapter 7** investigates an IP for a two-dimensional space-time FDE involving a tempered time-fractional derivative and Caputo fractional derivatives in space. The ill-posed problem is regularized by a quasi-boundary value method. A finite difference discretization yields large two-by-two block structured linear systems with multilevel Toeplitz-like structure, for which we develop an efficient preconditioning strategy combined with GMRES. A comprehensive GLT analysis of the coefficient matrix sequences guides the construction of the preconditioner. Numerical experiments confirm the stability, accuracy, and computational efficiency of the proposed approach in reconstructing the source term from noisy data.<br>
