## 9.2.1 Validation of the distribution results

**Example 1**

Let _d_=1 and consider the following two matrix-sequences {_A<sub>n</sub>}<sub>n</sub>_, {_B<sub>n</sub>}<sub>n</sub>_, with \(A_n=D_n(a)+\frac{1}{n^4}I_n\) and \(B_n=T_n(3+2cos(\theta))\), where \(T_n(\cdot)\) denotes the Toeplitz operator for _d_=1, as introduced in Section 3.1, _D<sub>n</sub>(a_) represents the diagonal matrix generated by the continuous function<tbc>

\(a(x)=\left\{\begin{array}{ c c }
0, & x\in[0,\frac{1}{2}), \\
1, & x\in[\frac{1}{2},1].
\end{array}\right.\)

<tbc>Because of Theorem 9.2, the geometric mean of these two sequences, that is {_G(A<sub>n</sub>,B<sub>n</sub>_)}_<sub>n</sub>_, satisfies the GLT relation:

\(\left\{G\left(A_n,B_n\right)\right\}_n\sim_\mathrm{GLT}\sqrt{a(x)(3+2cos(\theta))},\)

where the corresponding GLT symbols are explicitly given by \(\kappa=a(x)\) and \(\xi=3+2cos(\theta)\).

**Eigenvalue distribution**

We numerically analyze the spectral behavior of the geometric mean _G(A<sub>n</sub>,B<sub>n</sub>_) from **Example 1**. The eigenvalues of this geometric mean are computed for various increasing dimensions _n_ and compared with the uniformly sampled points from the GLT symbol \(\sqrt{a(x)(3+2cos(\theta))}\). In Figure 9.1, numerical results strongly indicate that the GLT symbol accurately characterizes the eigenvalue distribution of the geometric mean. As _n_ grows, the eigenvalue distribution closely matches the GLT symbol, giving evidence of the theoretical findings in Theorem 9.2.
