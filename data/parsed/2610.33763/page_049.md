<tbc>resulting matrices exhibit a Toeplitz-like structure that varies smoothly along the diagonals as the matrix size increases, leading to the broader class of LT sequences \[134, 136\].<br>
A classical Toeplitz matrix generated by a function \(f:[-\pi,\pi]^d\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}\) belonging to \(L^1([-\pi,\pi]^d)\), where the constant diagonal entries correspond to the Fourier coefficients of _f_. However, for differential operators with non-constant coefficients, the resulting matrices do not maintain this global Toeplitz structure but instead belong to the LT class. Specifically, an LT matrix _A<sub>n</sub>_ is obtained by modulating the entries of a Toeplitz matrix using an almost everywhere (a.e.) continuous function \(a(x):[0,1]^d\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}\), scaling the entries along the same diagonal \(a\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)\) for **_i_**=**1**_,...,n_**. As \(n\rightarrow\infty\), the differences between consecutive diagonal elements vanish due to continuity, making the structure asymptotically Toeplitz-like. More precisely, this structure can be represented as the product of a Toeplitz matrix and a diagonal matrix, where the diagonal entries correspond to evaluations of the function _a(x_), such that _A<sub>n</sub>=D<sub>n</sub>(a)T<sub>n</sub>_, where _D<sub>n</sub>(a_) is a diagonal matrix whose entries are given by \(D_n(a)=\mathrm{diag}\left(a\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)\right)_{i=1}^n\). This formulation allows LT sequences to accurately capture local variations in variable-coefficient differential operators while preserving the structural properties of Toeplitz matrices. Formally, a LT sequence {_A_**<sub>n</sub>**}**<sub>n</sub>** can be expressed through the product of the weight function _a(x_) and the generating function \(f(\theta)\), leading to the LT symbol<tbc>

\(a\otimes f:[0,1]^d\times[-\pi,\pi]^d\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{r\times r},\)

<tbc>where

\((a\otimes f)(x,\theta):=a(x)f(\theta),(x,\theta)\in[0,1]^d\times[-\pi,\pi]^d.\)

Here the symbol "\(\otimes\)" in \(a\otimes f\) denotes the pointwise matrix product of the two matrix-valued functions, not the Kronecker product of the two matrices. Since \(a(x),f(\theta)\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}\), the product is well defined and belongs to \(\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}\), in accordance with the target space of the LT symbol. This is consistent with the use of "\(\otimes\)" in GLT 7, where at the matrix level "\(\otimes\)" denotes the Kronecker product, while at the symbol level it denotes the pointwise product of the symbols.<br>
Building on this concept, a GLT matrix sequence is defined as the limit, in the sense of the a.c.s., as described in Definition 2.3, of a finite sum of LT sequences. The construction of GLT sequences was formalized through a.c.s. limits in \[124\] and refined to a **d-level** ∗**-algebra of structured matrix sequences** satisfying specific axioms, listed in \[42, Chapter 6, pp. 118-120\]. The theory of GLT sequences provides a fundamental framework for analyzing structured matrix sequences arising from discretizations of differential equations.<br>
A particularly relevant development in the spectral analysis of structured matrix sequences concerns symmetrized Toeplitz structures. Hon, Mursaleen, and Serra- Capizzano \[50\] provided a note on the spectral distribution of symmetrized Toeplitz sequences, establishing precise distribution results for this important class of matrices. Building on this work, Ferrari, Furci, Hon, Mursaleen, and Serra-Capizzano \[38\] studied the eigenvalue distribution of special \(2\times 2\) block matrix sequences, with applications to symmetrized Toeplitz structures. These results are particularly relevant for understanding the spectral behavior of preconditioned matrix sequences and for analyzing the convergence of iterative methods applied to non-Hermitian Toeplitz systems. The analysis presented in later chapters of this thesis, particularly concerning nonsymmetric Toeplitz systems, draws upon this theoretical framework.<tbc>
