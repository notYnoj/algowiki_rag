## 3.4 \(\tau\) matrices

A uni-level _r_-block \(\tau\) matrix is defined as a square matrix \(S_{n,r}\in\mathbb{R}^{nr\times nr}\), partitioned into \(r\times r\) blocks _S<sub>i,j</sub>_, whose blocks satisfy the so-called _cross-sum condition_<tbc>

\(S_{i-1,j}+S_{i+1,j}=S_{i,j-1}+S_{i,j+1},i,j=1,\dots,n,\)

<tbc>where we assume the boundary conditions

\(S_{n+1,j}=S_{i,n+1}=S_{0,j}=S_{i,0}=O_r.\)

For _r_=1 the blocks reduce to the scalar entries _s<sub>i,j</sub>_ and the classical definition is recovered. This relation implies the block-centrosymmetry of _S<sub>n,r</sub>_, that is, symmetry with respect to the center of the matrix at the block level. As a consequence, _S<sub>n,r</sub>_ is both symmetric and persymmetric, namely symmetric with respect to the main anti- diagonal, whenever the generating blocks are symmetric. Furthermore, all the blocks of _S<sub>n,r</sub>_ are uniquely determined by the blocks in its first block row or, equivalently, in its first block column. Additional details on this definition and the resulting properties can be found in \[19\].<br>
Much like circulant matrices, there is a generator for the set of \(\tau\) matrices, defined by<tbc>

\([W_n]_{i,j}:=\delta_{|i-j|-1}=\left\{\begin{array}{ c c }
1 & \text{if}i-j=\pm 1, \\
0 & \text{otherwise,}
\end{array}\right.i,j=1,\dots,n,\)

<tbc>or, in explicit matrix form<tbc>

\(W_n=\left[\begin{array}{ c c c c c c c c c }
0 & 1 &  &  &  &  &  &  \\
1 & 0 & 1 &  &  &  &  &  \\
 & 1 & \ddots &  & \ddots &  &  &  \\
 &  & \ddots &  & \ddots &  & \ddots &  \\
 &  &  &  & \ddots &  & \ddots & 1 \\
 &  &  &  &  &  & 1 & 0
\end{array}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}.\)

<tbc>The _r_-block \(\tau\) algebra is defined as the set of matrices generated by _W<sub>n</sub>_ in the block sense, meaning that every _r_-block \(\tau\) matrix can be represented as

\(S_{n,r}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}W_n^k\otimes A_k,A_k\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r},\)

that is, as a linear combination, with matrix coefficients, of the powers \(W_n^0=I_n\), _W<sub>n</sub>_, \(W_n^2\), _..._, \(W_n^{n-1}\). This representation is equivalent to the cross-sum condition stated above, since the latter acts independently on each of the _r_<sup>2</sup> entry positions inside the blocks. As is characteristic of matrix algebras, for _r_=1 this set is closed under inversion, a property that follows from the Hamilton-Cayley theorem \[17\]. Furthermore, for any \(n,k\in\mathbb{N}\), the matrix \(W_n^k\) is symmetric and is invertible if and only if _n_ is even.
