## Conclusions and future work

Throughout the dissertation, we analyzed various structured matrix sequences arising from the discretization of multi-dimensional fractional partial differential equations. After introducing the analytical foundations of fractional operators, the spectral framework of matrix sequences, the main classes of structured matrices, and the GLT theory in Part I to provide a foundation for the numerical applications, in Part II we discretized several types of equations and addressed both direct and inverse problems. The associated linear systems are generally computationally challenging due to the dense and ill-conditioned nature of the coefficient matrices. However, we showed that the rich framework of GLT theory can effectively support the development of fast solvers, focusing in particular on preconditioning strategies. This final chapter summarizes our findings, highlights key similarities and differences across the studied scenarios, and suggests possible directions for future research.

### Summary of research contributions

In Chapter 5, we studied ISPs for a two-dimensional generalized diffusion equation involving a Caputo-type time-fractional derivative with a general memory kernel and nonlocal boundary conditions. Using the spectral decomposition of the associated elliptic operator, we derived explicit series representations of the solutions in terms of bi-orthogonal systems of eigenfunctions. We established rigorous existence and uniqueness results for regular solutions and demonstrated that both ISPs are ill- posed in the Hadamard sense. Several special cases, including tempered, multi-term, mono-term, and classical diffusion models, were obtained as particular choices of the memory kernel, illustrating the unifying nature of the generalized formulation. An interesting direction for future research would be to combine the present theoretical study with the computational features of structured matrices and the asymptotic spectral theory for matrix sequences, in the spirit of the works [33, 88, 102]. The design of fast regularization algorithms for the ISPs examined in this chapter presents a significant challenge.

In Chapter 6, we addressed an ISP for a space-time FDE involving a tempered fractional derivative in time and a Caputo fractional derivative in space. The ill-posed ISP was regularized using the quasi-boundary value method. The finite difference discretization led to a large-scale two-by-two block linear system with structured coefficients, for which we developed an efficient preconditioning strategy based on the block triangular structure. A detailed spectral analysis of the associated matrix sequences was conducted using the GLT framework, which provided a rigorous description of their asymptotic spectral behavior despite the presence of the variable coefficient that spoils the pure Toeplitz structure. Numerical experiments confirmed the stability, accuracy, and computational efficiency of the proposed approach in reconstructing the source term from noisy data. For future work, we plan to construct a more accurate preconditioner that accounts for the remaining outliers and possibly leads to a strong eigenvalue cluster at 1.
