To address the challenges arising from ill-posed IPs in FDEs, various regularization techniques have been developed to restore stability and ensure meaningful reconstructions. Motivated by their wide-ranging practical applications, IPs for FDEs have been extensively studied using diverse analytical and computational approaches \[58, 59, 129, 60, 61, 62, 63, 70, 111\]. In general, regularization methods incorporate additional information or constraints on the solution in order to control instability and suppress the amplification of noise. Among the most widely used approaches, Tikhonov regularization and its variants, including simplified and generalized formulations, stabilize the solution by introducing penalty terms that control smoothness or enforce _a priori_ assumptions on the unknown solution \[80, 96\]. Another effective approach is the quasi-boundary value method, also known as the nonlocal boundary value method, which has been successfully applied to a variety of ill-posed IPs, particularly for parabolic equations and fractional diffusion models \[29, 48\]. In FDEs, regularization methods must ensure stability and accuracy while accounting for the nonlocal nature of the operators.<br>
The numerical treatment of both direct and IPs for FDEs ultimately leads to the solution of large linear systems of the form<tbc>

\(A_nx_n=b_n,\)

<tbc>where \(A_n\in\mathbb{C}^{d_n\times d_n}\) is a square matrix and the index \(n\in\mathbb{N}\) is related to the fineness of the discretization mesh. As the grid is refined, the dimension _d<sub>n</sub>_ increases and typically satisfies \(d_n\rightarrow+\infty\) as \(n\rightarrow+\infty\), so that one naturally deals with a _sequence_ of matrices {_A<sub>n</sub>}<sub>n</sub>_ of growing size rather than a single matrix. The collective spectral behavior of {_A<sub>n</sub>}<sub>n</sub>_ is therefore a key object of interest, since it reveals important numerical features and potential difficulties, such as severe ill- conditioning and slow convergence of iterative methods \[25, 116, 127\]. In this matrix- sequence setting, the relevant spectral information is not contained in any individual matrix, but rather in the way the spectra of {_A<sub>n</sub>}<sub>n</sub>_ evolve as the discretization is refined. This naturally leads to the notion of asymptotic eigenvalue and singular value distributions in the Weyl sense, which capture the limiting spectral content of the entire sequence as \(n\rightarrow+\infty\). For a wide class of discretizations, this limiting behavior can be described by a measurable function, referred to as the _symbol_, which reflects the combined influence of the underlying (fractional) differential operator, its coefficients, the computational domain, and the discretization scheme. This symbol- based spectral description plays a central role in numerical analysis, as it explains the observed conditioning of the linear systems and provides a rigorous framework for the design and analysis of effective preconditioners and scalable iterative methods. Structured matrices play a central role in this analysis, because in many relevant settings the discretization of FDEs produces coefficient matrices with Toeplitz or Toeplitz-like patterns that can be exploited to build fast solvers and preconditioners. In particular, when FDEs have constant coefficients and are discretized on uniform grids, the resulting matrices often exhibit a Toeplitz or multilevel Toeplitz structure \[20, 26, 27, 95\]. Such matrices arise from the shift-invariant nature of the underlying operators and possess a rich spectral theory developed over the last century.
