Since \(\eta\in(0,1)\), we deduce that the series<tbc>

\(\sum_{k=0}^\infty|\delta_k|\)

<tbc>is convergent, and consequently \(g_\eta\) belongs to the Wiener class. Therefore, since \(g_\eta\) belongs to the Wiener class, it is a continuous and hence Lebesgue integrable function, so that \(B_n=T_n(g_\eta)\) and Axiom GLT 2 and Axiom GLT 1 imply<tbc>

\(\{B_n\}_n\sim_{\mathrm{GLT},\sigma} g_\eta(\theta).\)

<tbc>Finally, all the eigenvalues of _B<sub>n</sub>_ are trivially equal to \(\delta_0\) because of the triangular structure, so that

\(\{B_n\}_n\sim_\lambda\delta_0\)

follows immediately from the definition of distribution.

**Corollary 6.1** _If_ \(a:[0,1]^2\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\) _is continuous a.e, then_<tbc>

\(\{G_n\}_n\sim_{\mathrm{GLT},\sigma} a(x)g_\eta(\theta),x\in[0,1],\theta\in[-\pi,\pi].\)

<tbc>_with_ \(g_\eta\) _as in Theorem 6.1. Moreover,_<tbc>

\(\{G_n\}_n\sim_\lambda\delta_0a(x),x\in[0,1].\)

<tbc>**Proof:** Recalling (6.10), by GLT 2 we have<tbc>

\(\{D_n(a)\}_n\sim_\mathrm{GLT}a(x),x\in[0,1].\)

<tbc>Then, by Theorem 6.1 and the ∗-algebra properties detailed in GLT 3, we deduce<tbc>

\(\{G_n\}_n\sim_\mathrm{GLT}a(x)g_\eta(\theta),x\in[0,1],\theta\in[-\pi,\pi].\)

<tbc>By GLT 1, we immediately have<tbc>

\(\{G_n\}_n\sim_\sigma a(x)g_\eta(\theta),x\in[0,1],\theta\in[-\pi,\pi].\)

<tbc>Finally, we notice that _G<sub>n</sub>_ is lower triangular and the main diagonal is given by \(\left[\delta_0a(x_i)\right]_{i=1}^n\). In conclusion

\(\{G_n\}_n\sim_\lambda\delta_0a(x),x\in[0,1].\)

We proceed to the analysis of {_U<sub>m</sub>}<sub>m</sub>_, relying on the same technique employed in the proof of Theorem 6.1.

**Theorem 6.2** _There exists a continuous_ \(2\pi\)_-periodic function_ \(h_\xi\) _such that_<tbc>

\(\{U_m\}_m\sim_{\mathrm{GLT},\sigma} h_\xi(\theta),\theta\in[-\pi,\pi].\)

<tbc>_Moreover,_<tbc>

\(\{U_m\}_m\sim_\lambda\gamma_0.\)
