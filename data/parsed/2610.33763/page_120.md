i.e. the union of the scaled copies of range\((f_\alpha)\) by the real factors \(y\in[1,2]\), in agreement with the behaviour depicted in Figure 8.2.

Conversely, the theoretical proof is not available yet and it will be the subject of future investigations, starting from the key tools given in \[21\].

The question _a_3) concerning the eigenvalue distribution of the non-Hermitian matrix sequence \(\{\mathcal{D}_N(a)\overline{G_{\alpha,N}}\}_N\) deserves careful attention. Since the matrices are non-Hermitian, the GLT symbol obtained in _a_1) directly yields the singular value distribution but does not automatically imply the eigenvalue distribution. The numerical evidence presented in Figures 8.1-8.2 suggests that the same symbol may describe the eigenvalue distribution in this setting, and this is indeed rigorously established in the constant coefficient case by the results of Bogoya, Böttcher, and Grudsky \[21\]. However, for the variable coefficient case, a rigorous proof remains open. Thus, while the numerical observations are compelling, the eigenvalue distribution should be regarded as a conjecture supported by numerical evidence rather than a proven result.
