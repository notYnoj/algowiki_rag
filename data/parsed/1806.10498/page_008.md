We maintain the almost-optimal tree _T_ as a subset of _T_<sup>S</sup> using the approach of Section 4. After an update of _T_<sup>S</sup>, some nodes of _T_<sup>S</sup> (and, hence, some nodes of _T_) can be moved. Suppose that a node _u_ was moved by one position to the left and the node \(u'\) to the right of _u_ was also moved by one position to the left. If all leaf descendants of _u_ are associated with an element _e<sub>i</sub>_ and all leaf descendants of \(u'\) are associated with some \(e_j\neq e_i\), then we may have to update \(\varepsilon_i\). At most _O_(log_n_) nodes of _T_<sup>S</sup> are moved during every update \[4\]; hence, the total update cost is _O_(log<sup>2</sup>_n_).

When the total weight _W_ is increased by a factor 2 or when the total number of elements is increased by a factor 2, we update the value of \(\tau=\frac{W}{n}\) , compute the new values \(w'_i\) and as noted, re-build the tree from scratch. The amortized cost of rebuilding _T_<sup>S</sup> from scratch is _O_(1) per step since the balanced tree can be built in linear time. When we re-build the tree _T_<sup>S</sup>, we use the new value of _k_=log_n_.

## 6 Faster Updates

We can reduce the update time by grouping pseudo-leaves in the tree _T_<sup>S</sup>. All pseudo-leaves are divided into \(\Theta(n/log^2n)\) groups so that each group contains at least log<sup>2</sup>_n_ and at most 2log<sup>2</sup>_n_ pseudo-leaves.

The tree _T_<sup>S</sup> is divided into two components: a macro-tree _T<sup>M</sup>_with _O(n/_log<sup>2</sup>_n_) leaves and _O(n/_log<sup>2</sup>_n_) mini-trees \(T_j^\mathrm{S}\). See Fig. 2. Mini-trees correspond to groups of pseudo-leaves: all pseudo-leaves in the group _G<sub>j</sub>_ are stored in a mini-tree \(T_j^\mathrm{S}\). The _j_’th leaf of macro-tree _T<sup>M</sup>_ is the root of mini-tree \(T_j^\mathrm{S}\). As before, the almost-optimal tree _T_ is a subtree of _T_<sup>S</sup>. An element _e<sub>i</sub>_ is assigned to a node \(\varepsilon_i\) of _T_, such that the height of \(\varepsilon_i\) in _T_<sup>S</sup> is \(log(w'_i)\) (up to an additive constant error) and all leaves in the subtree of \(\varepsilon_i\) are associated to _e<sub>i</sub>_. _T_ is the subtree of _T_<sup>S</sup> induced by nodes \(\varepsilon_i\) and their ancestors. The division of a tree into macro-trees and mini-trees is a standard data structuring technique; see e.g., \[6\].
