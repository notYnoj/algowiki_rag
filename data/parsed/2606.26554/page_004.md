_Is there an_ MSSP _algorithm faster than_ \(\widehat{O}(min(|S_\mathrm{in}|m,n^\omega))\)?

We answer this question in the affirmative, giving the first algorithm that beats this running time. In fact, our algorithm is essentially optimal, by tying the running time of our algorithm to Boolean matrix multiplication.

**Theorem 1.2.** MSSP _on undirected unweighted graphs is equivalent to_ BMM(|_S_<sub>in</sub>|_,n,n), up to n_<sup>_o_(1)</sup> _factors, for randomized algorithms._<sup>3</sup> _In particular, there is a randomized algorithm computing_ MSSP _on undirected, unweighted graphs in n_<sup>_o_(1)</sup>T<sub>BMM</sub>(|_S_<sub>in</sub>|_,n,n_) _time._

Here, BMM(_a,b,c_) denotes Boolean matrix product instances between \(a\times b\) matrices and \(b\times c\) matrices, and T<sub>BMM</sub>(_a,b,c_) denotes the running time of such instances. In particular, T<sub>BMM</sub>(_a,b,c_) can be upper bounded by the running time for computing products of general \(a\times b\) and \(b\times c\) matrices, utilizing fast matrix multiplication algorithms (see Figure 1 for an illustration of the quantitative improvements we achieve). The reduction from Boolean matrix multiplication to MSSP in the above theorem can be achieved with standard approaches.<sup>4</sup> We also extend our algorithm to handle graphs with small integer weights.

**Theorem 1.3.** MSSP _on undirected graphs with integer edge weights in_ \[_B_\] _is equivalent to_ MPP(|_S_<sub>in</sub>|_,n,n|B), up to n_<sup>_o_(1)</sup>log_B factors, for randomized algorithms._<sup>5</sup> _In particular, if_ \(B\le\mathrm{poly}(n)\)_, there is a randomized algorithm computing_ MSSP _on undirected graphs with integer edge weights in_ \[_B_\] _in n_<sup>_o_(1)</sup>T<sub>MPP</sub>(|_S_<sub>in</sub>|_,n,n|B_) _time._

Here, MPP(|_S_<sub>in</sub>|_,n,n|B_) denotes Min-Plus product between an \(|S_\mathrm{in}|\times n\) matrix and an \(n\times n\) matrix, whose entries are from \([B]\cup\{\infty\}\), and T<sub>MPP</sub>(|_S_<sub>in</sub>|_,n,n|B_) denotes the running time of such instances. It is known that \(\mathsf{T}_\mathrm{MPP}(a,b,c|B)=\widetilde{O}\left(B\mathsf{T}_\mathrm{MUL}(a,b,c)\right)\) \[AGM97\].

Our algorithms give a smooth interpolation between the SSSP algorithm and the APSP algorithms \[Sei95\] and \[SZ99\]. When _s=n_, our algorithm serves as an alternative algorithm for APSP in undirected graphs with unit or small integer weights (See \[CVX21\] for another alternative algorithm).

The key technical ingredient of our algorithms is a novel low diameter decomposition, which may be of independent interest. We define the notion of a low diameter small neighborhood decomposition. We say \(V_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_k\) is a \((d,\phi)\)-low diameter small neighborhood decomposition if (1) \(\mathrm{diam}(G[V_i])\le d\) for all _i_, and (2) \(\sum_i|N(V_i)|\le\phi n\). While the first condition is reminiscent of standard low diameter decompositions, the second condition is different: instead of requiring that the number of edges between clusters is small (see e.g. \[BNW22\]), our decomposition requires that the neighborhoods of the clusters are small. In particular, while there may be many edges incident to any given cluster, we ensure that they do not expand and hit too many distinct vertices. Note that our bound on the neighborhood size is global rather than local, there may be clusters where \(|N(V_i)|>\phi|V_i|\), but the sum of the neighborhoods is at most \(\phi n\). We show how to obtain an (_n_<sup>_o_(1)</sup>_,n_<sup>_o_(1)</sup>)-low diameter small neighborhood decomposition in _n_<sup>2+_o_(1)</sup> time.

**Theorem 1.4.** _There is a randomized n_<sup>2+_o_(1)</sup> _time algorithm that computes a_ \(\left(2^{O\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{log}n}\right)},2^{O\left(\sqrt{\mathrm{log}n}\right)}\right)\)_-low diameter small neighborhood decomposition._

We also give some natural applications of our MSSP algorithm. We obtain improvements over the state- of-the-art algorithms with simple reductions, establishing the practical impact of the MSSP problem (see Section 5.3 for formal statements).
