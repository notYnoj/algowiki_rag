_Alternatively, since we are interested in solving linear systems, it is often convenient to remove the scaling factor and consider the normalized sequence_

\(\{\gamma^{-1}G_N\}_N\sim_\mathrm{GLT}a(x)f_\alpha(\theta),\) (11)

\(\{\gamma^{-1}G_N\}_N\sim_\sigma a(x)f_\alpha(\theta).\) (12)

_These normalized relations require only that_ \(a:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\) _be continuous almost everywhere, which is equivalent to the Riemann integrability of a._

_Finally, statements a_1)_, a_2), (9)-(10)_, and_ (11)-(12) _remain valid if a is complex-valued, although in applications a is typically assumed to be nonnegative, often strictly positive._

**Theorem 4.3.** _There exists a continuous_ \(2\pi\)_-periodic function_ \(g_\beta(\theta)\) _such that_

\(\{B_M\}_M\sim_{\mathrm{GLT},\sigma} g_\beta(\theta),\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]\)

_Moreover,_ \(\{B_M\}_M\sim_\lambda b_0\).

_Proof._ To demonstrate that {_B<sub>M</sub>}<sub>M</sub>_ is a GLT sequence, we claim that _B<sub>M</sub>_ is a Toeplitz matrix generated by a Lebesgue integrable function function, i.e., there exists \(g_\beta\) such that \(B_M=T_M(g_\beta)\) for all \(M\in\mathbb{N}\) according to the notation in (2)-(3). The idea is simple: we prove that the series<tbc>

\(g_\beta(\theta):=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\ell_ke^{ik\theta},\)

<tbc>where the coefficients \(\ell_k\) are given in Section (3.1), belongs to the Wiener class, so that \(g_\beta\) is well defined, continuous and \(2\pi\)-periodic, and the corresponding Toeplitz matrix sequence is precisely {_B<sub>M</sub>}<sub>M</sub>_ by definition. From the definition of the L1 coefficients in Section 3.1, we have \(\ell_k=(b_k-b_{k-1})\) for \(k\ge 1\). Substituting \(b_m=(j+1)^{1-\beta}-j^{1-\beta}\) and simplifying directly yields<tbc>

\(\ell_k=\left[(k+1)^{1-\beta}-2k^{1-\beta}+(k-1)^{1-\beta}\right].\)

<tbc>Let us consider the auxiliary function \(\psi_\beta(t):=t^{1-\beta}\), where \(\Delta t=T/M\). Using \(k=t_k/\Delta t\) with \(t_k=k\Delta t\), we obtain<tbc>

\(\ell_k=(\Delta t)^{\beta-1}\left[(t_k+\Delta t)^{1-\beta}-2t_k^{1-\beta}+(t_k-\Delta t)^{1-\beta}\right].\)

<tbc>Then replace \((t_k+\Delta t)^{1-\beta}\) with \(\psi_\beta(t_{k+1})\), \(t_k^{1-\beta}\) with \(\psi_\beta(t_k)\), and \((t_k-\Delta t)^{1-\beta}\) with \(\psi_\beta(t_{k-1})\), giving<tbc>

\(\ell_k=(\Delta t)^{\beta-1}\left[\psi_\beta(t_{k+1})-2\psi_\beta(t_k)+\psi_\beta(t_{k-1})\right].\)

<tbc>Dividing and multiplying by \((\Delta t)^2\),<tbc>

\(\ell_k=(\Delta t)^{\beta+1}\left[\frac{\psi_\beta(t_{k+1})-2\psi_\beta(t_k)+\psi_\beta(t_{k-1})}{(\Delta t)^2}\right].\)

<tbc>The fraction is a second-order central difference approximation of \(\psi''_\beta(t_k)\). Hence,<tbc>

\(\ell_k=(\Delta t)^{\beta+1}\left[\psi''_\beta(t_k)+O((\Delta t)^2)\right].\)
