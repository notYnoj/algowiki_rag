1. _The number of i such that i\<n/_2 _and_ \(w_i(\ell)=1\) _is at most_ \(n^{(1+\alpha)/2}/2\).<tbc>

_2._ _The number of i such that_ \(i\ge n/2\) _and_ \(w_i(\ell)=0\) _is at most_ \(n^{(1+\alpha)/2}/2\).

_Proof._ We start by proving this for \(\ell=2\).

For _i\<n/_4, _w<sub>i</sub>_(2)=_w<sub>i</sub>_(1)=min(_w<sub>i</sub>_(0)_,w_<sub>_i+n/_2</sub>(0)). We have already established using Proposition 2.14 that when \(i\le(n-n^{(1+\alpha)/2})/4\), each _w<sub>i</sub>_(0)=0 except with probability at most \(e^{-n^\alpha/4}\). By a union bound, the probability that there exists such an _i_ with _w<sub>i</sub>_(2)=1 is at most \(ne^{-n^\alpha/4}/4\). We give no bounds on the values of _w<sub>i</sub>_(2)=_w<sub>i</sub>_(1) for \(i\in[(n-n^{(1+\alpha)/2})/4,n/4-1]\), but we note that there are only \(n^{(1+\alpha)/2}/4\) such wires.

For \(i\in[n/4,n/2-1]\), \(w_i(2)=min(w_i(1),w_{i+n/4}(1))=min(w_i(1),max(w_{i+n/4}(0),w_{i-n/4}(0)))\). Again we have already established using Proposition 2.14 that, when \(i\in[n/4,n/2-n^{(1+\alpha)/2}/4]\), both _w_<sub>_i+n/_4</sub>(0) and _w_<sub>_i-n/_4</sub>(0) each equal 0 except with probability at most \(e^{-n^\alpha/4}\). Thus by a union bound, their max is 0 except with probability at most \(2e^{-n^\alpha/4}\). By a union bound, the probability that there exists such an _i_ with _w<sub>i</sub>_(2)=1 is at most \(ne^{-n^\alpha/4}/2\). We give no bounds on the values of _w<sub>i</sub>_(2) when \(i\in[n/2-n^{(1+\alpha)/2}/4,n/2-1]\), but we again note that there are only \(n^{(1+\alpha)/2}/4\) such wires.

For \(i\in[n/2,n-1]\) the proof is symmetric. By a union bound, the total failure probability across both halves of the argument is at most \(3ne^{-n^\alpha/4}/2\) and we have excluded only \(n^{(1+\alpha)/2}/2\) wires from each half of the analysis. Note that after applying each comparator gate, the number of 1’s in the first _n/_2 wires can only decrease while the number of 1’s in the last _n/_2 wires can only increase. Thus the bound will automatically extend to all \(\ell>2\).

Next, we partition the gates in layer \(\ell\ge 3\) into three groups each. The _upper gates_ are \((i,i+2^{k_\ell})\) such that \(i+2^{k_\ell}<n/2\) and they will use the heat function _Q_<sup>unif</sup>. By Claim 5.3, except with probability \(3ne^{-n^\alpha/4}/2\), at most \(n^{(1+\alpha)/2}/2\) upper gates receive an input other than (0,0). Thus, except with probability at most \(3ne^{-n^\alpha/4}/2\), the upper gates collectively release at most \(n^{(1+\alpha)/2}/2\) expected heat. With probability at most \(3ne^{-n^\alpha/4}/2\), every upper gate releases at most 1 heat. Since there are at most _n/_2 such gates in a layer, the expected heat released by all upper gates is at most \(n^{(1+\alpha)/2}/2+3n^2e^{-n^\alpha/4}/4\).

The _lower gates_ are \((i,i+2^{k_\ell})\) such that \(i\ge n/2\) and they will use the heat function _Q_<sup>unif</sup>. The reasoning is symmetric to the upper gates, so we get that the expected heat released by all lower gates is also at most \(n^{(1+\alpha)/2}/2+3n^2e^{-n^\alpha/4}/4\).

The _crossover gates_ are \((i,i+2^{k_\ell})\) where _i\<n/_2 but \(i+2^{k_\ell}\ge n/2\) and they will use the heat function \(Q_\varepsilon^{(0,1)}\) for some \(\varepsilon\le 1/2\) that we will specify later. By Claim 5.3, except with probability \(3ne^{-n^\alpha/4}/2\), there are at most \(n^{(1+\alpha)/2}/2\) crossover gates that receive the input (1,0). When this is the case, the expected heat released by all _\<n_ such gates is less than \(nlog(1/(1-\varepsilon))+n^{(1+\alpha)/2}log(1/\varepsilon)/2\). When this is not the case, there are _\<n_ gates that each release at most \(log(1/\varepsilon)\) expected heat. Thus the overall expected heat released by the crossover gates is less than \(nlog(1/(1-\varepsilon))+n^{(1+\alpha)/2}log(1/\varepsilon)/2+3n^2e^{-n^\alpha/4}log(1/\varepsilon)/2\le 2log(e)\varepsilon n+n^{(1+\alpha)/2}log(1/\varepsilon)/2+3n^2e^{-n^\alpha/4}log(1/\varepsilon)/2\). Picking \(\varepsilon=n^{(\alpha-1)/2}\) when \(\alpha=1/2\) makes \(\varepsilon\le 1/2\) and therefore this expression at most \(2log(e)n^{(1+\alpha)/2}+n^{(1+\alpha)/2}log(n^{(1-\alpha)/2})/2+3n^2e^{-n^\alpha/4}log(n^{(1-\alpha)/2})/2\).

Thus, in total, the expected heat released in each layer \(\ell\ge 3\) is less than \((log(n^{(1-\alpha)/2})/2+4log(e))n^{(1+\alpha)/2}+(log(n^{(1-\alpha)/2})+1)3n^2e^{-n^\alpha/4}/2\).
