## Overwriting circuits.

It is worth noting that in \[YCW26, YYW26\] the authors use a different notion of a thermodynamic circuit than we consider here. In their _overwriting_ model, a circuit is defined (as is common in most non-quantum circuit complexity classes) as a DAG where the nodes are labeled with gates and the in-edges define the inputs for each gate. The authors are interested in repeated runs of their circuit where the old logical configuration of each gate is overwritten one gate at a time during the next run, so the thermodynamic costs are incurred incrementally as each gate is updated and replaces its old output with the new one. The overwriting model is an accurate abstraction of the CMOS architecture; however, it places some restrictions on what is feasible with circuits from a theoretical perspective. For example a logically reversible gate in the our circuit model admits the valid heat function _Q(x_)=0 on all inputs. In the overwriting model, this gate must release heat when run on independently distributed inputs unless it is computing a constant function.<sup>24</sup> In fact, we can see that any circuit in the overwriting model can be simulated in our circuit model, simulating a _k_-input, 1-output gate by using _k_ ‘input’ wires and one ‘output’ wire in each of our gates, setting the other _k_-1 wires to 0. Each gate in the simulated overwriting circuit is now a gate in our circuit model that treats the output wires of its parents (or the input) as read-only inputs and only modifies its own output wire. At the end of the computation, the values stored in all output wires for intermediate gates are erased.<sup>25</sup>
