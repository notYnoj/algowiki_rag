We first require a technical lemma about converting a formula \(\phi\) to another \(\psi\) such that, depending on whether \(\phi\) is satisfiable, \(\psi\) has either 1 or a much larger number of satisfying assignments. This follows by adding _t_ dummy variables to a construction by Blass and Gurevich \[BG82\] that converts any Sat formula \(\phi\) on _n_ variables into another formula \(\psi\) on _n_+1 variables which contains exactly one additional satisfying assignment.

**Lemma 4.6.** _Let_ \(\phi(x_1,\dots,x_n)\) _be a_ Sat _instance on n variables with_ \(Z(\phi)\) _satisfying assignments. For every_ \(t\ge 0\)_, there exists another_ Sat _instance_ \(\psi(x_1,\dots,x_n,y_1,\dots,y_t,z)\) _on n+t_+1 _variables that has_ \(Z(\psi)=1+2^tZ(\phi)\) _satisfying assignments._

_Proof._ We fix \(\psi(x_1,\dots,x_n,y_1,\dots,y_t,z)=\big(\big(\wedge_{i\in[n]}x_i\big)\wedge\big(\wedge_{i\in[t]}y_i\big)\wedge z\big)\vee(\phi(x_1,\dots,x_n)\wedge\neg z)\). When _z_=1, \(\psi\) is satisfied exactly when all _x<sub>i</sub>_ and _y<sub>i</sub>_ are assigned 1. When _z_=0, each assignment of _x_<sub>1</sub>_,...,x<sub>n</sub>_ that satisfy \(\phi\) now results in 2_<sup>t</sup>_ assignments of _y_<sub>1</sub>_,...,y<sub>t</sub>_ that all satisfy \(\psi\). Thus the total number of satisfying assignments is now \(1+2^tZ(\phi)\).
