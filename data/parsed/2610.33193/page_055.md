### 8.1 Discussion of substantive LLM use

In Section 4.2, we used ChatGPT 5.6 to help us strengthen Lemma 4.7 to inputs where it is promised that E<sub>_x_∼D</sub>[_Q_<sup>∗</sup>(_x_)]_\>_0. In particular, the LLM pointed us towards Lemma 4.6 as a black-box way to convert any Sat instance \(\phi\) into a new one \(\psi\) where (1) there is always some satisfying assignment and (2) there is a programmable gap between the number of satisfying assignments for \(\phi\) depending on whether or not \(\phi\) had some satisfying assignment. We uncovered that the LLM likely arrived at this exact construction based on a similar construction by Blass and Gurevich [BG82] designed to handle (1) and then observing that (2) follows directly from adding additional dummy variables. The LLM also generated a full proof of Lemma 4.7 which we have heavily simplified into the version presented in this paper.

We suspected that the add-one smoothing estimator would be a natural candidate to use in the proof of Lemma 4.8, as it is a simple estimator which avoids the catastrophic pitfall of assigning zero probability on any event with positive probability, and asked the LLM to show that this estimator gives our polynomial time additive approximation. The LLM observed that the excess heat dissipation associated with using an estimator is upper-bounded by the expected KL-divergence between the input distribution and its estimated version. We identified that this is an immediate consequence of what is now known as _mismatch costs_ that were first identified by Kolchinsky and Wolpert [KW17]. Next, the LLM proceeded to give a rather convoluted proof that upper bounds the expected KL-divergence between an input distribution and it’s add-one smoothed estimator. We instead identified that Braess, Forster, Sauer, and Simon [BFSS02] directly proved that the add-one smoothing estimator is well suited for minimizing the expected KL-divergence.

We independently discovered proofs of Lemma 5.12 and Theorem 5.13 where the heat function was restricted to only depend on the value of the comparison (i.e. is \(x_1\le x_2\)?) rather than the actual input values. The LLM helped us find that shifting exactly \(2^{k_\ell}\) small elements between sets _A_ and _B_ causes a central gate to receive input _x_<sub>2</sub>_,x_<sub>1</sub> instead of input _x_<sub>1</sub>_,x_<sub>2</sub>, which was the key ingredient in our proof of Lemma 5.11, that let us extend our lower bound to general heat functions.<tbc>
