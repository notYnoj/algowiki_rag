Concretely, suppose we perform the most widely discussed logical operation in the thermodynamics of computing: erasing a single bit \(b\in\{0,1\}\). Here "erasing _b_" generally means "setting _b_ to 0", i.e., no matter whether _b_ was 0 or 1 before, afterward it is 0 and so the system has "erased" any knowledge of _b_’s previous value. In this case, D<sub>1</sub> is the degenerate point-mass distribution with Pr<sub>D<sub>1</sub></sub>\[_b_=0\]=1. The prior distribution D<sub>0</sub> may be a number of things, but the worst case in terms of logical irreversibility is the uniform distribution D with Pr<sub>D<sub>0</sub></sub>\[_b_=0\]=Pr<sub>D<sub>0</sub></sub>\[_b_=1\]=1/2, i.e., prior to erasing the bit, we have maximal uncertainty about its current value. The term S(D<sub>0</sub>)-S(D<sub>1</sub>), the difference in Shannon entropy between distributions D<sub>0</sub> and D<sub>1</sub>, is the so-called _Landauer cost_.<sup>9</sup> In this example, _S_ is the simple binary entropy function on _p_=Pr<sub>D<sub>_i_</sub></sub>\[_b_=0\], so S(D<sub>0</sub>)=1, S(D<sub>1</sub>)=0, and the Landauer cost is 1 bit of entropy. Importantly, (1) means that the Landauer cost does not necessarily imply positive entropy production. By focusing instead on entropy production, we open the door to modeling and analyzing sources of heat dissipation in computation, over and above the Landauer cost, that cause EP to be positive.

The other two terms EP and Q account for how this local drop in entropy relates to the outside universe. EP, the _entropy production_, represents the total increase in entropy of the universe. The Second Law dictates that \(\mathsf{EP}\ge 0\). This is related to the _local_ decrease in entropy S(D<sub>0</sub>)-S(D<sub>1</sub>) by the _heat released_ Q.<sup>10</sup> Q corresponds to the entropy change of the environment due to heat exchange (releasing heat increases the entropy of the environment) and encompasses the underlying physical implementation of our computation. Note that to maintain the Second Law (\(\mathsf{EP}\ge 0\)), we must have \(\mathsf{Q}\ge\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D}_0)-\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D}_1)\). If the local entropy strictly dropped through a logically irreversible step, i.e., if S(D<sub>0</sub>)_\>_S(D<sub>1</sub>), then in this case Q must be strictly positive to maintain \(\mathsf{EP}\ge 0\).

The ability of randomized computation to have a negative Landauer cost has implications for energy use as well, allowing a computation to use randomized operations to counteract the heat released in other steps by logically irreversible operations.
