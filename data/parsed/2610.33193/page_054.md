Then it becomes more important to reduce the size of the compaction/sorting circuit implementing the underlying routing logic in Theorems 6.1 and 6.3. For instance, Theorem 6.1 can very likely be improved to use the _O(n_polylog<sup>∗</sup>(_n_))-gate compaction circuit of Asharov, Lin, and Shi [ALS21], instead of a sorting network that requires \(\Omega(n\mathrm{log}n)\) gates by the 0/1 principle.<sup>44</sup>

Rather than insisting on EP=0, one can ask for the minimum EP achievable by compaction circuits of near-linear size or polylogarithmic depth. We know of no lower bound of the form "depth _d_ forces \(\mathsf{EP}\ge g(n,d)\)" for any _g\>_0; such a bound would be the first general quantitative trade-off between a standard circuit resource and entropy production.

Theorem 6.1 uses a single circuit, with finitely many heat functions, that has zero EP for every exchangeable input distribution simultaneously, because the conditional probability that each erased bit is 1 depends only on exchangeability. For which other classes of distributions is this possible? If, for instance, the keys are independent but position-dependent, the conditional probabilities depend on the bias of every position, and it is unclear whether finitely many heat functions suffice. Conversely, what is the EP of our circuit when the input is only approximately exchangeable, say as a function of a divergence between the actual distribution and the nearest exchangeable one?

Analogous questions about the gate and depth complexity for zero circuit entropy production sorting are natural. In general, a more detailed study of gate-depth-entropy tradeoffs for sorting is worthy of further investigation.

Our results concern expected EP and expected heat. What are the variance, and the worst case over inputs, of the heat released by our circuits, and is there a trade-off between the fluctuations of heat and circuit size or depth, in the spirit of the thermodynamic uncertainty relations of stochastic thermodynamics [HG20]? Can we obtain concentration guarantees on the distribution of expected heat and entropy production?

Section 5.2 shows that Batcher’s network has \(\mathsf{EP}=\Omega(n)\) on uniformly random bits and \(\Omega(n\mathrm{log}^2n)\) on uniformly random distinct inputs, and Section 5.3 gives a network releasing only _O(n_log_n_) expected heat, but, as noted in Section 5.2, it is open whether every sorting network has \(\mathsf{EP}=\Omega(n)\), or even EP_\>_0, on uniformly random bits. (Every sorting network must release \(\Omega(n)\) heat, since that is the Landauer cost of sorting bits; zero EP means releasing exactly that much.) A positive lower bound would show that comparators alone cannot achieve zero EP, so that the more general gates of Theorem 6.1 are necessary.<sup>45</sup> In the other direction, Theorem 6.1 uses _O(t(n_)+_nm_) ancilla wires, where _t(n_) is the size of the underlying sorting network. What is the minimum number of ancilla wires in a zero EP sorting or compaction circuit? In particular, can zero EP be achieved in place, with no ancilla wires, using _O_(1)-size gates more general than comparators?
