## 3.2 Sensitivity Oracle for Matroid Covering

We next present an algebraic framework for addressing _matroid covering_.

Let _M_=(_E,_I) be a linear matroid of rank _r_ represented by an \(r\times m\) matrix _A_ over a field F. The _k-covering problem_ asks whether the entire ground set _E_ can be covered by (partitioned into) _k_ independent sets of _M_. This is equivalent to asking whether the entire ground set _E_ is an independent set in the _k_-fold union matroid \(M^{\vee k}\).

For _E_ to be an independent set in \(M^{\vee k}\), its cardinality _m_ must be at most _kr_. Let \(U\in\mathbb{F}^{kr\times m}\) be the union representation matrix obtained via Theorem 3.2. The set _E_ is a _k_-covering if and only if the columns of _U_ are linearly independent. To test this, we augment _U_ into a square matrix by adding random columns.

**Lemma 3.9** (Augmenting Linear Representations)**.** _Let_ \(U\in\mathbb{F}^{kr\times m}\) _be the k-fold union representation matrix for_ \(M^{\vee k}\)_. Suppose_ \(m\le kr\)_. Let_ \(\Delta=kr-m\)_, and_ \(R\in\mathbb{F}^{kr\times\Delta}\) _be a random matrix whose entries are chosen independently and uniformly from a sufficiently large finite field_ F.

_Then, with high probability, the augmented square matrix_ \(U_{aug}:=[U|R]\in\mathbb{F}^{kr\times kr}\) _has full rank kr if and only if the row rank of U is m, i.e., E is k-coverable._

_Proof._ We treat the entries of the random matrix _R_ as indeterminates and work over the polynomial ring F\[X\], where<tbc>

\(\mathcal{X}:=\{R_{i,j}:1\le i\le kr,1\le j\le\Delta\}.\)

<tbc>Using the block decomposition, the determinant of the \(kr\times kr\) augmented matrix _U<sub>aug</sub>_=\[_U|R_\] can be expressed as:

\(det(U_{aug})=\sum_{S\subseteq[kr]|S|=m}(-1)^{\sigma(S)}det(U_S)det(R_{S^c}),\)

where _U<sub>S</sub>_ denotes the \(m\times m\) submatrix of _U_ induced by the rows in _S_, _R<sub>S<sup>c</sup></sub>_ denotes the \(\Delta\times\Delta\) submatrix of _R_ induced by the rows in _S<sup>c</sup>_, and \(\sigma(S)\) is the corresponding sign parity.

If the row rank of _U_ is less than _m_, then every \(m\times m\) minor det(_U<sub>S</sub>_) evaluates to 0, making det(_U<sub>aug</sub>_) identically the zero polynomial.

Let us suppose the row rank of _U_ is exactly _m_. Then there exists at least one subset of rows \(S\subseteq[kr]\) such that the minor det(_U<sub>S</sub>_) is a non-zero constant in F. For each such _S_, the corresponding determinant det(_R<sub>S<sup>c</sup></sub>_) contributes distinct monomials in the variables X. Moreover, the monomials generated by one row subset _S_ cannot be cancelled out by the monomials generated by any other subset \(S'\neq S\). Thus, det(_U<sub>aug</sub>_) is a non-zero polynomial.

As entries of _R_ are chosen independently and uniformly at random from a sufficiently large finite field, by the Schwartz-Zippel lemma det(_U<sub>aug</sub>_) evaluates to a non-zero value with high probability. This proves that with high probability, the matrix _U<sub>aug</sub>_ has rank _kr_ if and only if _U_ has rank _m_. □

### 3.2.1 Sensitivity Oracle for Sparse Linear Matroids

Building on Theorem 3.9, we now present the sensitivity oracle for the _k_-covering problem.

**Theorem 3.10.** _Let_ \(k\ge 1\) _be an integer. Let M_=(_E,_I) _be a linear matroid of rank r defined on a ground set E of size_ \(m(\le kr)\)_, represented by an_ \(r\times m\) _matrix A over a field_ F_. Then, there exists a sensitivity oracle that, for any changes to M consisting of at most_ \(\delta\) _updates to A, can decide whether the entire ground set E can be covered by (partitioned into) k independent sets of the updated matroid_ \(M'\)_. This oracle requires_ \(O((kr)^\omega)\) _preprocessing time, O_((_kr_)<sup>2</sup>log(_kr_)) _space, and answers queries in_ \(O((\delta k)^\omega)\) _time._
