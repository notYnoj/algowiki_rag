### \(\alpha\)-factors with arbitrary degree bounds.

For arbitrary \(\alpha\), the parity instance has dimension \(\sum_v\alpha(v)\), which can be \(\Theta(nm)\). To tackle this, we fix a min-cost \(\alpha\)-factor _M_ at preprocessing; for any updated optimum \(M'\), the symmetric difference \(M\triangle M'\) decomposes into closed alternating trails. We prove two facts (i) there exists optimal \(M'\) such that the difference decomposes into at most _f_ trails; and (ii) a trail with no closed alternating sub-trail meets each vertex in at most two _M_-edges and two \(M'\)-edges (else a sub-trail could be extracted, an argument inspired by the bicoloured-cycle technique of \[FS05\]). Thus, an optimal \(\alpha\)-factor of _G+U_ drops at most 2_f_ edges of _M_ at every vertex. This local bound lets us build an auxiliary two-layer graph (a drop layer for _M_-edges, an add layer for non-_M_-edges, coupled by parallel edges and unit budget gadgets), on which factors of _G+U_ correspond exactly, with matching costs, to factors of the auxiliary graph (Theorem 7.7). The bounded-degree oracle with _k=O(f_) then gives _O_((_fn_)<sup>2</sup>log_n_) space and \(O(f^{3\omega})\) query time, independent of \(max_v\alpha(v)\) (Theorem 7.8).

## 1.3 Related Works

### Matroid Algorithms.

Polynomial-time algorithms for matroid union and intersection date back to Edmonds \[Edm03\] and Lawler \[Law75\], followed by a long line of work improving exact and approximate algorithms for matroid intersection, union, packing, and covering \[Cun86, Har09, Qua24, Ter25\]. More recently, there has been growing interest in matroid algorithms over uncertain inputs. Blikstad, Mukhopadhyay, Nanongkai, and Tu \[BMNT23\] maintain a minimum-weight basis in the decremental setting using \(\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{rank(M)})\) rank queries per update. Vos and Grilnberger \[dVG26\] gave a deterministic dynamic \((1\pm\epsilon)\)-approximation for the packing number \(\Phi\) and covering number \(\beta\) of a matroid, using \(\Phi\cdot poly(logn,1/\epsilon)\) (resp. \(\beta\cdot poly(logn,1/\epsilon)\)) rank queries per update. Sparsification techniques of Huang and Sellier \[HS24\] led to progress in streaming matroid intersection; Chandrasekaran, Chekuri, and Zhu \[CCZ25\] initiated the study of base packing in online matroids, and Buchbinder, Gupta, Hathcock, Karlin, and Sarkar \[BGH<sup>+</sup>24\] gave an \(\widetilde{O}(\text{OPT})\)-competitive algorithm for intersecting a matroid with an online partition matroid. Fault-tolerant aspects of matroids have also been studied recently \[BFGM25, DK26\].

### Static Algebraic Algorithms.

Cheung, Lau, and Leung \[CLL11\] pioneered the use of fast matrix multiplication for graph connectivity, solving All-Pairs Connectivity in \(\widetilde{O}(m^\omega)\) time. Georgiadis, Italiano, Karanasiou, Papadopoulos, and Parotsidis \[GIK<sup>+</sup>17\] presented a deterministic algorithm for all-pairs 2-bounded edge (vertex) connectivity in \(\widetilde{O}(n^\omega)\) time. Authors in \[AGI<sup>+</sup>19\] extended this to general _k_ in the vertex-capacitated setting, with a randomized \(O((nk)^\omega)\)-time algorithm; they further showed that on DAGs, all-pairs _k_-bounded max-flow can be solved in \(O((klogn)^{4^k+o(k)}\cdot n^\omega)\) time, and in \(O(2^{O(k^2)}\cdot mn)\) time for sparse DAGs. The framework of \[CLL11\] was lifted to the _k_-bounded setting (_k_-APC) by Akmal and Jin \[AJ23\] in \(\widetilde{O}((kn)^\omega)\) time, and refined by Akmal \[Akm24\] via enumerating polynomials. Analogous algebraic algorithms exist for linear matroid intersection \[Har09\], linear matroid parity \[CLL14\], and weighted \(\alpha\)-factors \[GS21a, GS21b\].

### Min-Cut Sensitivity Oracles.

Results of Picard and Queyranne \[PQ82\] and Dinitz and Vainshtein \[DV00\] imply that a single edge deletion’s effect on the (_s,t_)-max-flow value can be detected from the structure of the flow itself.
