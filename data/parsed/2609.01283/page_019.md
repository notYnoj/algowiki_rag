<tbc>the number of non-zero entries in the resulting additive update to the mixed Laplacian. The matrix L_<sub>pack</sub>_ can be expressed as:

\(\mathcal{L}_{pack}=\sum_{e\in E}D_{e,e}(u_1)_e(u_2)_e^T\)

where (_u_<sub>1</sub>)_<sub>e</sub>_ and (_u_<sub>2</sub>)_<sub>e</sub>_ are the _e_-th columns of _U_<sub>1</sub> and _U_<sub>2</sub>, respectively. Computing the matrix L_<sub>pack</sub>_ takes _O(k_<sup>2</sup>_m_) time, assuming that representation matrices _A_<sub>1</sub>_,A_<sub>2</sub> are provided in sparse column format (see Theorem 2.11). Note that the updates to the _e_-th column of _A_<sub>1</sub>_,A_<sub>2</sub>, or to **_wt_**(_e_) modify only the _e_-th term in the sum, and since _A_<sub>1</sub> and _A_<sub>2</sub> are column-sparse, the product \((u_1)_e(u_2)_e^T\) has at most _O(k_<sup>2</sup>) non-zero entries. Thus, for any \(\delta\) base changes, the updated Laplacian can be represented as \(\mathcal{L}'_{pack}=\mathcal{L}_{pack}+C\), where matrix _C_ has at most \(O(k^2\delta)\) non-zero entries. By Theorem 3.4, identifying the existence or computing the minimum weight of a _k_-packing set _S_ in the updated matroids reduces to evaluating the determinant of \(\mathcal{L}'_{pack}\).

To efficiently support these queries, we construct two separate oracles based on the data structure from Theorem A.2:

### Decision Oracle

We ignore the polynomial weights and work with the Laplacian L_<sub>pack</sub>|<sub>Y=1</sub>_ (where the maximum degree is _d_=0). Theorem A.2 together with the fact that _M_<sub>1</sub>_,M_<sub>2</sub> are column sparse implies that preprocessing this matrix requires \(O(k^2m+(kr)^\omega)\) time and the corresponding data structure takes _O(km_+(_kr_)<sup>2</sup>log(_kr_)) space. The additive _O(km_) term arises from the fact that storing the union representations _U_<sub>1</sub> and _U_<sub>2</sub> requires _O(km_) space. After at most \(\delta\) updates to representation matrices _A_<sub>1</sub>_,A_<sub>2</sub>, and **_wt_**, querying the determinant of the Laplacian \(\mathcal{L}'_{pack}|_{Y=1}=\mathcal{L}_{pack}|_{Y=1}+C|_{Y=1}\) requires \(O((k^2\delta)^\omega)\) query time.

### Weighted Oracle

The matrix \(\mathcal{L}_{pack}\in\mathbb{F}_{2W}[Y]^{kr\times kr}\) is a polynomial matrix where the maximum degree of _Y_ in any entry is bounded by _d_=2_W_. Theorem A.2 together with the fact that _M_<sub>1</sub>_,M_<sub>2</sub> are column sparse implies that preprocessing this matrix requires \(\widetilde{O}(k^2m+W(kr)^3)\) time and the oracle takes _O(km+W(kr_)<sup>3</sup>log(_kr_)) space. To compute the weight after any \(\delta\) updates to _A_<sub>1</sub>_,A_<sub>2</sub>_,**wt**_, we evaluate the determinant of the polynomial matrix \(\mathcal{L}'_{pack}\) and extract the coefficient of the minimum degree term of _Y_ . Querying the determinant of this updated matrix takes \(\widetilde{O}(W\cdot kr\cdot(k^2\delta)^\omega)\) time, and isolating the smallest exponent of _Y_ in \(\mathrm{det}(\mathcal{L}'_{pack})\) and subtracting _krW_ yields the exact weight of the minimum-weight _k_-packing set. This completes the proof. □

**Remark 3.6** (Cost of Parallel Updates)**.** _The query complexity in Theorem 3.5 can be refined when some of the updates correspond to changes in the multiplicity of parallel elements. Suppose the updates affect distinct elements_ \(e_1,\dots,e_\rho\) _(with_ \(\rho\le\delta\)_), where the multiplicity of e<sub>i</sub> increases or decreases by_ \(\rho_i\le k\)_, so that_ \(\sum_{i=1}^\rho\rho_i=\delta\)_. Column-sparsity of A_<sub>1</sub>_,A_<sub>2</sub> _ensures that each_ \((e_i,\rho_i)\) _contributes only O(k_<sup>2</sup>) _non-zero entries within an_ \(O(k)\times O(k)\) _submatrix of the additive update C to the packing Laplacian_ \(\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{pack}=U_1DU_2^T\)_. Aggregating over all_ \(\rho\) _affected elements, this yields query time_ \(O((k^2\rho)^\omega)\) _rather than_ \(O((k^2\delta)^\omega)\).

## 3.1.2 Subset Sensitivity Oracle for General Linear Matroids

The oracle in Theorem 3.5 handles arbitrary updates to the ground set, incurring a query cost of \(\Omega((k^2\delta)^\omega)\). We now present a complementary oracle for the _subset sensitivity model_: a _susceptible set E<sub>Q</sub>_ of size \(\sigma\) is identified at preprocessing time, and all future queries modify only elements in _E<sub>Q</sub>_. The set _E<sub>Q</sub>_ need not be a subset of _E_; we require only that the matroids _M_<sub>1</sub> and _M_<sub>2</sub> admit well-defined extensions to the augmented ground set \(E_0:=E\cup E_Q\).
