Therefore, at each level of the recursion except for the first one, the sum of \(\delta(A_i^*)\) is bounded by the initial \(\delta(A^*)\), the sum of \(\delta(B_i^*)\) is bounded by the initial \(\delta(B^*)\), the sum of \(p_i^*\) is bounded by \(4\cdot(\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*))\), the sum of \(q_i^*\) is bounded by \(2\cdot(\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*))\), and the sum of \(r_i^*\) is bounded by \(4\cdot(\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*))\). Hence, at each level of the recursion except for the first one, the algorithm works in total time \(\mathcal{O}(\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*))\). At the first level of the recursion, the algorithm takes \(\mathcal{O}(p^*+q^*+r^*+\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*))\) time.

Therefore, it remains to show that there are at most \(1+log(1+\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*))\) levels of recursion in total. We show that \(\delta(A_i^L)+\delta(B_i^L)\le(\delta(A_i)+\delta(B_i))/2\) and \(\delta(A_i^R)+\delta(B_i^R)\le(\delta(A_i)+\delta(B_i))/2\). The inequality \(\delta(A_i^L)+\delta(B_i^L)\le(\delta(A_i)+\delta(B_i))/2\) follows from the definition of _m<sub>i</sub>_. On the other hand, as \(m\in(0..q]\) is the largest index with this property, we have that \(\delta(A_i[0..p)[0..m])+\delta(B_i[0..m][0..r))>(\delta(A_i)+\delta(B_i))/2\), and thus \(\delta(A_i^R)+\delta(B_i^R)=(\delta(A_i)-\delta(A_i[0..p)[0..m]))+(\delta(B_i)-\delta(B_i[0..m][0..r)))\le(\delta(A_i)+\delta(B_i))/2\). Thus, after at most \(log(1+\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*))\) iterations of the recursion, we have \(\delta(A_i^*)+\delta(B_i^*)=0\); such a recursive call is a leaf recursive call since \(p_i\le\delta(A_i^*)+1=1\) and \(r_i\le\delta(B_i^*)+1=1\). \(\blacktriangleleft\)

\(\blacktriangleright\) **Theorem 4.7.** _The algorithm of Theorem 1.2 can be extended so that, within the same time complexity, it also builds a data structure that takes_ \(\mathcal{O}(n_1+n_2+n_3+\delta(M_1)+\delta(M_2))\) _space and provides_ \(\mathcal{O}(log(2+\delta(M_1)+\delta(M_2)))\)_-time oracle access to_ W<sup>_M_<sub>1</sub>_,M_<sub>2</sub></sup>.

**Proof.** We slightly modify the algorithm of Theorem 1.2. In addition to the returned condensed representation of _C_<sup>∗</sup>, we also store the value _m_ and the computed list _lst_ at each non-leaf level of the recursion, and \(\mathcal{W}_{0,0}^{A,B}\) in the leaf case of _p=r_=1. Furthermore, in the compression-decomposition algorithm of Lemma 4.6, we store, for all rows and columns of _A_ and _B_ to which rows and columns of _A_<sup>∗</sup> and _B_<sup>∗</sup> they correspond. In particular, for every \(k\in[0..q)\), let _h(k_) be the column of _A_<sup>∗</sup> that corresponds to the _k_-th column of _A_. Note that if the _i_-th row of _A_<sup>∗</sup> is redundant, then W<sup>_A_<sup>∗</sup>_,B_<sup>∗</sup></sup>[_i..i_][0_..r_<sup>∗</sup>)=W<sup>_A_<sup>∗</sup>_,B_<sup>∗</sup></sup>[_i_-1_..i_-1][0_..r_<sup>∗</sup>), and thus, if row _i_ of _C_<sup>∗</sup> corresponds to row \(i'\) of _C_ and column _j_ of _C_<sup>∗</sup> corresponds to column \(j'\) of _C_, we have \(\mathcal{W}_{i,j}^{A^*,B^*}=h(\mathcal{W}_{i^\prime,j^\prime}^{A,B})\).

To compute \(\mathcal{W}_{i,j}^{A^*,B^*}\), we start at the root of the recursion. In constant time, we replace (_i,j_) with \((i',j')\) such that \(\mathcal{W}_{i,j}^{A^*,B^*}=h(\mathcal{W}_{i^\prime,j^\prime}^{A,B})\). After that, we know that \(\mathcal{W}_{i^\prime,j^\prime}^{A,B}<m\) if and only if \(j'\le lst_{i^\prime}\). This can be decided in constant time. If \(j'\le lst_{i^\prime}\), we have \(\mathcal{W}_{i^\prime,j^\prime}^{A,B}=\mathcal{W}_{i^\prime,j^\prime}^{A^L,B^L}\), which can be computed recursively. Otherwise, if \(j'>lst_{i^\prime}\), we have \(\mathcal{W}_{i^\prime,j^\prime}^{A,B}=\mathcal{W}_{i^\prime,j^\prime}^{A^R,B^R}+m\), and \(\mathcal{W}_{i^\prime,j^\prime}^{A^R,B^R}\) can be computed recursively. In the leaf level of the recursion, the smallest witness of the only entry of _C_ is stored explicitly.

As the depth of the recursion is at most \(1+log(1+\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*))\), and we spend constant time at each level of the recursion, the total time complexity for the data structure query is \(\mathcal{O}(log(2+\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*)))\).

It remains to show how to store such a data structure in linear space. For each recursive call, we store some constant number of integers and some lists. The lists we store are the _lst_ list and the correspondence between rows and columns of _A_ and _A_<sup>∗</sup> and _B_ and _B_<sup>∗</sup>. All these lists are monotone, of length at most _p_<sup>∗</sup>+_q_<sup>∗</sup>+_r_<sup>∗</sup>, and with values in \[-1_..p_<sup>∗</sup>+_q_<sup>∗</sup>+_r_<sup>∗</sup>\], so they can be stored as bit-masks occupying O(1+(_p_<sup>∗</sup>+_q_<sup>∗</sup>+_r_<sup>∗</sup>)_/w_) space in total, where _w_ is the machine word size. Using the rank-select data structure \[4,18,27,36,37\], we provide constant-time access to lists, so the query time complexity does not change. Including this data structure affects neither the asymptotic space complexity nor the construction time.
