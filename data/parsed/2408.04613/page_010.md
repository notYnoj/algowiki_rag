We now show a helper lemma that compresses "ultra-sparse" Monge matrices to limit their dimensions by the size of their core.

\(\blacktriangleright\) **Lemma 4.6** (Matrix compression)**.** _There are two algorithms_ `Compress` _and_ `Decompress` _with the following properties: The_ `Compress` _algorithm, given the condensed representations of a_ \(p^*\times q^*\) _Monge matrix A_<sup>∗</sup> _and a_ \(q^*\times r^*\) _Monge matrix B_<sup>∗</sup>_, in time_ \(\mathcal{O}(p^*+q^*+r^*+\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*))\)_, builds a_ \(p\times q\) _Monge matrix A and a_ \(q\times r\) _Monge matrix B such that_ \(p\le\delta(A^*)+1\), \(q\le\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*)+1\), \(r\le\delta(B^*)+1\), \(\delta(A)=\delta(A^*)\)_, and_ \(\delta(B)=\delta(B^*)\)_. The_ `Decompress` _algorithm, given the condensed representations of A_<sup>∗</sup>_, B_<sup>∗</sup>_, and_ \(A\otimes B\)_, where_ (_A,B_)=`Compress`(_A_<sup>∗</sup>_,B_<sup>∗</sup>)_, computes the condensed representation of_ \(A^*\otimes B^*\) _in time_ \(\mathcal{O}(p^*+r^*+\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*))\).

**Proof Sketch.** The `Compress` algorithm deletes all rows of _A_<sup>∗</sup> that do not contain any core elements and all columns of _B_<sup>∗</sup> that do not contain any core elements. This way, \(p\le\delta(A^*)+1\) and \(r\le\delta(B^*)+1\) are guaranteed. Furthermore, all core elements of these two matrices are preserved. If there are no core elements between rows _i_ and _i_+1 of _A_<sup>∗</sup>, the corresponding entries of these two rows differ by some constant _c_, and thus all the entries of the row _i_+1 of \(A^*\otimes B^*\) can be obtained from the corresponding entries of row _i_ of \(A^*\otimes B^*\) by adding _c_. Since we can recover _c_ from _A_<sup>∗</sup>, no information about the answer is "lost" when deleting such rows. An analogous property holds for the removed columns of _B_<sup>∗</sup>. The `Decompress` algorithm reverses the row and column removals performed by the `Compress` algorithm.

The `Compress` algorithm also reduces the number _q_<sup>∗</sup> of columns of _A_<sup>∗</sup> and rows of _B_<sup>∗</sup> to \(q\le\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*)+1\). Observe that if there are no core elements between columns _j_ and _j_+1 of _A_<sup>∗</sup> and between rows _j_ and _j_+1 of _B_<sup>∗</sup>, then the values \(A_{i,j}^*+B_{j,k}^*\) and \(A_{i,j+1}^*+B_{j+1,k}^*\) differ by a constant independent of _i_ and _k_. Depending on the sign of this constant difference, we can delete either the _j_-th column of _A_<sup>∗</sup> and the _j_-th row of _B_<sup>∗</sup> or the (_j_+1)-th column of _A_<sup>∗</sup> and the (_j_+1)-th row of _B_<sup>∗</sup> so that we do not change the min-plus product of these matrices. By repeating this process for all indices _j_ that do not "contribute" to the cores of _A_<sup>∗</sup> and _B_<sup>∗</sup>, we get \(q\le\delta(A^*)+\delta(B^*)+1\). The formal proof is deferred to Appendix A. \(\blacktriangleleft\)

Finally, we show our main Monge matrix multiplication algorithm.

\(\blacktriangleright\) **Theorem 1.2.** _There is a (deterministic) algorithm that, given the condensed representations of an_ \(n_1\times n_2\) _Monge matrix M_<sub>1</sub> _and an_ \(n_2\times n_3\) _Monge matrix M_<sub>2</sub>_, in time_ \(\mathcal{O}(n_1+n_2+n_3+(\delta(M_1)+\delta(M_2))log(1+\delta(M_1)+\delta(M_2)))\) _computes the condensed representation of_ \(M_1\otimes M_2\).

**Proof Sketch.** We design a recursive divide-and-conquer procedure `Multiply`(_A_<sup>∗</sup>_,B_<sup>∗</sup>) (see Algorithm 1 for the details) and solve the problem by initially running `Multiply`(_M_<sub>1</sub>_,M_<sub>2</sub>). Given the matrices _A_<sup>∗</sup> and _B_<sup>∗</sup>, we first apply the `Compress` algorithm of Lemma 4.6 to compress _A_<sup>∗</sup> and _B_<sup>∗</sup> into a \(p\times q\) matrix _A_ and a \(q\times r\) matrix _B_ respectively. We then compute the condensed representation of \(C:=A\otimes B\) and finally use the `Decompress` algorithm of Lemma 4.6 to decompress _C_ into \(A^*\otimes B^*\).

If _p=r_=1, we compute the matrix _C_ trivially. Otherwise, we pick a splitting point \(m\in(0..q)\), split the matrix _A_ vertically into the matrix _A<sup>L</sup>_ of size \(p\times m\) and the matrix _A<sup>R</sup>_ of size \(p\times(q-m)\), and split the matrix _B_ horizontally into the matrix _B<sup>L</sup>_ of size \(m\times r\) and the matrix _B<sup>R</sup>_ of size \((q-m)\times r\). We pick _m_ in such a way that it splits the cores of _A_ and _B_ almost equally across _A<sup>L</sup>_ and _B<sup>L</sup>_ and _A<sup>R</sup>_ and _B<sup>R</sup>_, that is, \(\delta(A^L)+\delta(B^L)\) and \(\delta(A^R)+\delta(B^R)\) are at most \((\delta(A)+\delta(B))/2\). We recursively compute the condensed representations of the matrices \(C^L:=A^L\otimes B^L\) and \(C^R:=A^R\otimes B^R\).
