Symmetrically, \(0<C_{i,k}^\square=\delta^\Sigma(C[i..i+1][k..k+1])\le\delta^\Sigma(B[j..j'][k..k+1])\), and there exists a core element in \(B^\square[j..j')[k..k+1)\). \(\blacktriangleleft\)

We now use Corollary 3.6 to derive a bound on \(\delta(A\otimes B)\) in terms of \(\delta(A)\) and \(\delta(B)\). The underlying idea is to show that every core element of _C_ is either the first or the last one (in the lexicographic ordering) that the mapping of Corollary 3.6 attributes to the corresponding core element of _A or B_ (see Figure 1 for why the opposite would lead to a contradiction).

\(\blacktriangleright\) **Theorem 1.1.** _Let A be a_ \(p\times q\) _Monge matrix, and let B be a_ \(q\times r\) _Monge matrix. We have_ \(\delta(A\otimes B)\le 2\cdot(\delta(A)+\delta(B))\).

**Proof.** Let \(C:=A\otimes B\). Define a function \(f_A:\mathrm{core}(C)\rightarrow\mathrm{core}(A)\) that maps every core element \((i,k,C_{i,k}^\square)\in\mathrm{core}(C)\) to \((i,j,A_{i,j}^\square)\) for the smallest \(j\in[\mathcal{W}_{i,k}^{A,B}..\mathcal{W}_{i+1,k+1}^{A,B})\) with \(A_{i,j}^\square\neq 0\); such a _j_ exists due to Corollary 3.6. Analogously, we define a function \(f_B:\mathrm{core}(C)\rightarrow\mathrm{core}(B)\) that maps every core element \((i,k,C_{i,k}^\square)\in\mathrm{core}(C)\) to \((j,k,B_{j,k}^\square)\) for the smallest \(j\in[\mathcal{W}_{i,k}^{A,B}..\mathcal{W}_{i+1,k+1}^{A,B})\) with \(B_{j,k}^\square\neq 0\); again, such a _j_ exists due to Corollary 3.6. \(\triangleright\) Claim 3.7. Every core element \(c\in\mathrm{core}(C)\) is the lexicographically minimal or maximal one in its pre-image \(f_A^{-1}(f_A(c))\) under _f<sub>A</sub>_ or its pre-image \(f_B^{-1}(f_B(c))\) under _f<sub>B</sub>_.
