The computational results for the reflective Ramsey numbers of alternating paths versus monotone cycles suggest that, provided \(a,b\ge 3\), we have \(R_\mathrm{ref}(P_a^\mathrm{alt},C_b^\mathrm{mon})=R_\mathrm{ord}(P_a^\mathrm{alt},C_b^\mathrm{mon})\) if _a_ is odd, and \(R_\mathrm{ref}(P_a^\mathrm{alt},C_b^\mathrm{mon})=R_\mathrm{ord}(P_a^\mathrm{alt},C_b^\mathrm{mon})-1\) if _a_ is even; see  8. This points to a close relationship between the two types of Ramsey numbers. As for the dihedral Ramsey numbers, they once again appear to coincide with the cyclic Ramsey numbers, as shown in  9. We therefore pose the following two conjectures.

**Conjecture 4.7.** _For any_ \(a,b\ge 3\)_, we have_ \(R_\mathrm{ref}(P_a^\mathrm{alt},C_b^\mathrm{mon})=\lfloor(a-1)(b-\frac{1}{2})\rfloor\).

**Conjecture 4.8.** _For any_ \(a\in\mathbb{N}\) _and_ \(b\ge 2\)_, we have_ \(R_\mathrm{dih}(P_a^\mathrm{alt},C_b^\mathrm{mon})=1+(a-1)(b-1)\).

The computational results for the reflective and dihedral Ramsey numbers of alternating paths versus complete graphs are given in s 10 and 13. Although the reflective Ramsey numbers seem to have values similar to the corresponding ordered Ramsey numbers, the underlying pattern is difficult to discern. The dihedral Ramsey numbers, on the other hand, appear to coincide with the cyclic Ramsey numbers, leading to the following conjecture.

**Conjecture 4.9.** _For any_ \(a,b\in\mathbb{N}\)_, we have_ \(R_\mathrm{dih}(P_a^\mathrm{alt},K_b)=1+(a-1)(b-1)\).

We conclude the investigation of Ramsey numbers involving alternating paths with s 11 and 12, which contain the computational results for the reflective and dihedral Ramsey numbers of alternating paths versus nested matchings. As in the case of ordered and cyclic Ramsey numbers, the reflective and dihedral Ramsey numbers for this combination of graph classes also exhibit a structure that seems difficult to discern.
