Recently, standard and ordered Ramsey numbers were unified within a group-theoretic framework through the introduction of permutational Ramsey numbers \[4\]. For convenience, we first introduce the following terminology. Let _G_ and _H_ be graphs, and let \(\Gamma\) be a permutation group on _V(H_). We say that _H_ is \(\Gamma\)_-embeddable_ in _G_ if there exists a graph homomorphism from _H_ to _G_ of the form \(\psi\circ\varphi\), where \(\varphi\in\Gamma\) and \(\psi:V(H)\rightarrow V(G)\) is an increasing injection. Using this terminology, the definition of permutational Ramsey numbers from \[4\] can be stated as follows. For graphs _H_<sub>1</sub>_,H_<sub>2</sub>_,...,H<sub>k</sub>_ and permutation groups \(\Gamma_1,\Gamma_2,\dots,\Gamma_k\) on their respective vertex sets, the _permutational Ramsey number_ \(R(H_1^{\Gamma_1},H_2^{\Gamma_2},\dots,H_k^{\Gamma_k})\) is the minimum \(n\in\mathbb{N}\) such that for every _k_-edge-coloring of _K<sub>n</sub>_, there exists some \(j\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}\) for which _H<sub>j</sub>_ is \(\Gamma_j\)-embeddable in the spanning subgraph of _K<sub>n</sub>_ comprising the edges of color _j_. The well-definedness of permutational Ramsey numbers follows directly from Ramsey’s theorem \[25\].

Different choices of permutation groups give rise to several previously studied classes of Ramsey numbers. In particular, standard Ramsey numbers are obtained by taking each \(\Gamma_j\) to be the symmetric group on _V(H<sub>j</sub>_), whereas ordered Ramsey numbers are obtained by taking each \(\Gamma_j\) to be the trivial permutation group on _V(H<sub>j</sub>_). The same framework also encompasses cyclic Ramsey numbers _R_<sub>cyc</sub>(_H_<sub>1</sub>_,H_<sub>2</sub>_,...,H<sub>k</sub>_), introduced and investigated in \[4\], which arise by taking each \(\Gamma_j\) to be the cyclic group generated by the cyclic shift permutation with respect to the natural vertex order, i.e., \(\Gamma_j=\left\langle\left(\begin{array}{ c c c }
0 & 1 & 2 & \cdots \\
1 & 2 & 3 & \cdots \\
H_j|-1 & 0 & 
\end{array}\right)\right\rangle\) for each \(j\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}\).

Motivated by the results of \[4\] on the computation of small ordered and cyclic Ramsey numbers, we investigate two other natural classes of permutational Ramsey numbers proposed in the same paper:

**(1)** the reflective Ramsey numbers _R_<sub>ref</sub>(_H_<sub>1</sub>_,H_<sub>2</sub>_,...,H<sub>k</sub>_), which arise by taking each \(\Gamma_j\) to be the group gener ated by the reflection with respect to the natural vertex order, i.e., \(\Gamma_j=\left\langle\left(\begin{array}{ c c c c }
0 & 1 & 2 & \cdots \\
|H_j|-1 & |H_j|-2 & |H_j|-3 & \cdots \\
|H_j|-1 & |H_j|-2 & |H_j|-3 & 0
\end{array}\right)\right\rangle\) for each \(j\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}\); and

**(2)** the dihedral Ramsey numbers _R_<sub>dih</sub>(_H_<sub>1</sub>_,H_<sub>2</sub>_,...,H<sub>k</sub>_), which arise by taking each \(\Gamma_j\) to be the group generated by the cyclic shift permutation and the reflection with respect to the natural vertex order, i.e.,

\(\Gamma_j=\left\langle\left(\begin{array}{ c c c c }
0 & 1 & 2 & \cdots \\
1 & 2 & 3 & \cdots \\
|H_j|-1 & 0 & 
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}{ c c c c }
0 & 1 & 2 & \cdots \\
|H_j|-1 & |H_j|-1 & |H_j|-3 & \cdots \\
|H_j|-1 & |H_j|-2 & |H_j|-3 & 0
\end{array}\right)\right\rangle\)

for each \(j\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}\).

It is natural to study reflective (resp. dihedral) Ramsey numbers because they correspond to graphs whose vertices are linearly (resp. cyclically) ordered, but the direction of the ordering is ignored, unlike in the ordered (resp. cyclic) Ramsey numbers.

We focus on the two-color case, with the goal of computing small reflective and dihedral Ramsey numbers whose arguments belong to the following graph classes: monotone and alternating paths, monotone cycles, start-central stars, complete graphs and nested matchings. Formal definitions of these graph classes are given in Section 2. The Ramsey numbers are computed by formulating Boolean satisfiability (SAT) problems that encode the search for a 2-edge-coloring of _K<sub>n</sub>_ containing no forbidden embeddings, following the approach originally proposed by Poljak \[23\] for ordered Ramsey numbers and later extended to cyclic Ramsey numbers \[4\]. Due to its strong performance in the main track of the SAT Competition 2024, we use the Kissat SAT solver \[6, 7\] to handle all the resulting SAT instances. We automate the computation of small reflective and dihedral Ramsey numbers by using and extending the source code available at \[5\], originally developed for computing ordered and cyclic Ramsey numbers. We also establish several general results and formulate conjectures motivated by the computational findings.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the necessary terminology, establishes some basic properties of permutational Ramsey numbers and formally defines the graph classes considered throughout the paper. Section 3 describes the SAT-based approach and the software used to compute small reflective and dihedral Ramsey numbers. Section 4 presents the computational results, establishes several general results, formulates conjectures motivated by the computational findings and compares the computed Ramsey numbers with the previously known ordered and cyclic Ramsey numbers. Finally, Section 5 concludes the paper with a brief summary and directions for future research. The source code used to obtain the computational results is available at \[12\], together with supplementary data, including the computed Ramsey numbers, the Kissat output files and all the 2-edge-colorings yielding the lower bounds.

## 2 Preliminaries

We begin with the following notion, originally introduced in \[4\]. Let _G_<sub>1</sub> and _G_<sub>2</sub> be graphs of the same order, and let \(\Gamma\) be a permutation group on _V(G_<sub>1</sub>)=_V(G_<sub>2</sub>). We say that _G_<sub>1</sub> is \(\Gamma\)_-isomorphic_ to _G_<sub>2</sub>, and write \(G_1\cong_\Gamma G_2\), if there exists an isomorphism \(\varphi\) from _G_<sub>1</sub> to _G_<sub>2</sub> such that \(\varphi\in\Gamma\). The relation \(\cong_\Gamma\) is clearly an equivalence relation. Moreover, graphs belonging to the same equivalence class are interchangeable as arguments of a permutational Ramsey number whenever the associated permutation group is \(\Gamma\).
