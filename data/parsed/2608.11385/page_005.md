Krovi later proved average-case hardness results [35] for approximating output probabilities of random quantum circuits, including random _p_=1 QAOA ensembles. In [36] the authors refine asymptotic supremacy arguments into a fine-grained framework, allowing quantitative statements about the scale required for classical intractability based on standard conjectures in complexity theory. Hence in terms of sampling tasks, QAOA circuits provide a potential path to quantum computational supremacy [13, 36, 37]. Our complementary results show a stronger conditional collapse of the polynomial hierarchy to PH=P if one could efficiently evaluate QAOA expectation values (to exponential precision), but with distinct threshold _p_=2.

Our result should also be distinguished from work on more general quantum mean value problems for constant-depth circuits [38]. In that setting, depth refers to physical circuit depth in a local gate model, and substantially broader circuit and observable families are considered. By contrast the fixed parameter _p_ in our theorems counts QAOA rounds. As the graphs produced by our reduction contain universal vertices, a standard two-qubit-gate implementation of a cost layer has depth \(\Omega(N)\); consequently, our theorem is not a hardness result for constant physical-depth circuits. Its novelty is instead that exact #P-hardness already occurs within the rigid family of standard fixed-round MaxCut-QAOA circuits, with either the MaxCut Hamiltonian or one designated two-qubit correlator as the observable. Moreover, our proof is qualitatively different, deriving hardness directly from an extreme Laurent-polynomial coefficient rather than from postselection-induced universality [33, 38]. On the other hand, our theorem concerns exact, or exponentially precise, evaluation and therefore does not establish hardness in the constant-additive-error regime considered in Ref. [38].

Further afield, Szegedy [39] previously proposed viewing QAOA expectation values as graph invariants, and investigated whether structural information such as graph non-isomorphism can be recovered from comparing these quantities. Distinct from the expectation value setting, in [40] it was shown that #P-hardness can arise in the construction of Hamiltonian representations for compactly specified Boolean functions. Complementary work establishes complexity barriers to efficiently preparing states whose measured solutions recover a fixed positive fraction of the optimal MaxCut gain over a random cut on every input [41] and to optimizing Hamiltonians compressed by quantum random access optimization [42]. Here we study the complexity of evaluating expectation values for specified QAOA circuits. QAOA has also been proposed as a solution sampler for approximate counting [43]. Finally, we note that beyond [5], Laurent polynomials have also proven useful in two recent works solving QAOA for MaxCut on the special case of "ring of disagrees" (two-regular graphs) [44, 45], confirming optimal expected cost scaling with _p_ originally conjectured in [2].

## 2 A balanced maximum-cut counting gadget

To encode the maximum cuts of an input graph _H_, we replace each vertex by a balanced complete bipartite gadget. In every maximum cut of the resulting graph _G_, each gadget’s two parts lie on opposite sides, and their orientations specify a maximum cut of _H_ (Lemma 2.1). Two anchor vertices, joined to every other vertex, make their shared edge the sole contributor to the largest Laurent exponent at depth two (Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3). The balance ensures that every maximum cut contributes the same weight to the corresponding coefficient, making it a known nonzero multiple of |#MC(_H_)| at our chosen mixer angles (Theorem 2.4). Polynomial interpolation using Fourier recovery then yields the count (Section 2.2).
