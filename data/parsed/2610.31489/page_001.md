An Improved Bound for Testing _k_-Colorability

Bangjie Xu<br>
Tsinghua University<br>
`xbj24@mails.tsinghua.edu.cn`

**Abstract**

We study the sample complexity of testing _k_-colorability in the dense graph model. A graph is \(\varepsilon\)-far from being _k_-colorable if at least \(\varepsilon n^2\) edge deletions are required to make it _k_-colorable. We prove that a uniformly random induced subgraph on \(O(klogk/\varepsilon)\) vertices is non-_k_-colorable with probability at least 2/3 whenever the original graph is \(\varepsilon\)-far from _k_-colorable. The proof combines a palette-based coloring process with a container-style encoding-and-reconstruction argument. This yields \(O(klogk/\varepsilon)\), which is no worse than the previous state of the art \(O(klog(1/\varepsilon)/\varepsilon)\) throughout the nonvacuous parameter range, and is an improvement when \(logk=o(log(1/\varepsilon))\). For each fixed _k_, combining this upper bound with the known \(\Omega(logk/\varepsilon)\) lower bound gives \(\Theta(1/\varepsilon)\) dependence on \(\varepsilon\).

## 1 Introduction

Deciding whether a graph _G_ satisfies \(\chi(G)\le k\) is NP-complete for every fixed \(k\ge 3\) [16, 15]; under the Exponential Time Hypothesis it cannot be decided in time 2<sup>_o(n_)</sup> [17], where _n_ is the number of vertices; nevertheless, extensive researches have shown that for every fixed _k_ it can be decided in exponential but sub-2_<sup>n</sup>_ time: for _k_=3 there is an algorithm running in time _O_(1.3289_<sup>n</sup>_) [10], for _k_=4 one running in time _O_(1.7159_<sup>n</sup>_) [24], and for every fixed _k_ there is \(\varepsilon_k>0\) such that _k_-colorability can be decided by a randomized algorithm in time \(O((2-\varepsilon_k)n)\) [25]. In fact, for arbitrary _k_ and _n_ there are _n_-vertex graphs of chromatic number _k_ whose induced subgraphs on linear-size vertex sets are all 3-colorable [11]. This lack of local witnesses is what makes the global condition \(\chi(G)>k\) hard to detect, and it is what makes testing _k_-colorability nontrivial. However, a body of work has shown that if there is a substantial gap between _G_ and a _k_-colorable graph (that is, a constant fraction of _G_’s edges has to be deleted before it becomes _k_-colorable), then even a random small induced subgraph of _G_ is non-_k_-colorable with constant probability, where the size of the subgraph depends only on _k_ and \(\varepsilon\) and not on |_V(G_)|.

A graph _G_ on _n_ vertices is \(\varepsilon\)-far from being _k_-colorable if at least \(\varepsilon n^2\) of its edges have to be removed to make it _k_-colorable, and the sample complexity \(S_k(n,\varepsilon)\) of _k_-colorability is the smallest _q_ such that, for every \(\varepsilon\)-far _G_ with _n_ vertices, a uniformly random _q_-vertex induced subgraph of _G_ is non-_k_-colorable with probability at least 2/3. The quantity \(S_k(n,\varepsilon)\) is an important parameter in property testing in the dense graph model [13, 14, 12]; it is the minimum sample complexity of a canonical \(\varepsilon\)-tester for _k_-colorability on graphs over _n_ vertices. As we will soon see, \(S_k(n,\varepsilon)\) is in fact independent of _n_, so throughout the rest of the paper we write \(S_k(\varepsilon)\).

Bollobás, Erdős, Simonovits and Szemerédi [9] showed for _k_=2, and Rödl and Duke [20] showed for every fixed _k_ the existence of a constant \(f_k(\varepsilon)\) such that any graph having sufficiently many vertices that is \(\varepsilon\)-far from being _k_-colorable has an induced subgraph with no more than \(f_k(\varepsilon)\) vertices that is non-_k_-colorable; both proofs used Szemerédi’s regularity lemma [23].
