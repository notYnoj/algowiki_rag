_Proof._ We define a distribution of multihypergraphs H on _n=N_+2_t_ vertices, with \(t=\sqrt{N}\) pairs of vertices \(\{(u_i,v_i)\}_{i\in[t]}\) and a set _W_={_w_<sub>1</sub>_,...,w<sub>N</sub>_} of _N_ vertices. Let H be the uniform distribution over hypergraphs _H<sub>s</sub>_ for \(s\in[t]\), where we construct _H<sub>s</sub>_ by adding a copy of _F_<sup>2</sup> along the edge (_u<sub>s</sub>,v<sub>s</sub>_) and leaves _W_, and then for each other \(i\in[t]\backslash\{s\}\), adding a copy of _F_<sup>3</sup> along (_u<sub>i</sub>,v<sub>i</sub>_) and _W_. Note that each hypergraph _H<sub>s</sub>_ has the same cut profile and maximum density \(\rho^*(H_s)=\rho(\{u_s,v_s\})=N/2\).

Let _H=H<sub>s</sub>_∼H, i.e. _s_∼\[_t_\]. To argue that it is hard for a cut algorithm to approximate DSH(_H_) well, we bound the approximation ratio of any deterministic algorithm that outputs a fixed set of vertices. By Yao’s Principle, this yields a matching bound on the approximation ratio of randomized algorithms.

First, observe that a deterministic cut algorithm returns a fixed set of vertices _S_, as the output to any series of cut queries is identical regardless of which hypergraph _H_ is. A solution _S_ is clearly suboptimal if it contains one but not both of any pair (_u<sub>i</sub>,v<sub>i</sub>_), so we assume this is not the case. Now, let \(A\subseteq[t]\) be the set of indices _i_ such that \(\{u_i,v_i\}\subseteq S\), and let _a_=|_A_| and \(b=|S\cap W|\). Since _s_ is uniformly distributed, then we have \(s\in A\) with probability _a/t_. If \(s\notin A\), then \(\rho(S)\le 2\sqrt{N}\) by the fact that each of the \(\sqrt{N}-1\) copies of _F_<sup>3</sup> has maximum density less than 2. Therefore, we must have \(a\ge pt\) in order for _S_ to achieve an \(o(\sqrt{N})\)-approximation with probability at least _p_. We now upper bound the density of _S_ even when \(k\in A\):

\(\rho(S)=\frac{(N+2b)+2ab}{2a+b}\le\frac{N}{2a}+2a+2\le O(N/pt+t)=O(\sqrt{N}(1+p^{-1}))\)

The second inequality follows from \(a\ge pt\) as well as \(a\le t\). Since the maximum density of _H_ is _N/_2, then _S_ is at best an \(\Omega(\sqrt{N}/(1+p^{-1}))\)-approximation to D_k_S, stipulated on _S_ achieving this bound with probability at least _p_. We complete the proof by noting that \(n=\Theta(N)\) and appealing to Yao’s Principle to generalize this bound on deterministic algorithms to a bound on randomized ones.

In the above construction, we cannot increase _t_ beyond \(\sqrt{N}\) as _H<sub>s</sub>_ has \(\Theta(tN)\) hyperedges and so outputting the entire hypergraph yields an _O(N/t_)-approximation to DSH. However, this is not a barrier in the case of D_k_SH, where algorithms must output a subgraph on exactly _k_ vertices; thus, we are able to rule out cut approximations for this problem by constructing a distribution of hypergraphs with a single copy of _F_<sup>2</sup> hidden among _N_ copies of _F_<sup>3</sup>.

**Theorem 4.2.** With only cut access to a hypergraph, it is impossible to obtain any approximation for D_k_SH with probability greater than 3_/n_.

_Proof._ We set _k_=2 and define the distribution of multihypergraphs H as in the proof of Theorem 4.1, but with _t=N_. Let _H=H<sub>s</sub>_∼H, i.e, _s_∼\[_t_\]. Again, we bound the approximation ratio of any deterministic algorithm, which necessarily outputs a fixed set _S_ of 2 vertices. Observe that _S_ has non-zero density only if \(S\cap\{u_s,v_s\}\neq\emptyset\). More specifically, we have \(\rho(\{u_s,v_s\})=N/2\) and \(\rho(\{u_s,w_j\})=\rho(\{v_s,w_j\})=1/2\) for each \(j\in[N]\), with \(\rho(S)=0\) for any other _S_ of cardinality 2. We can therefore assume that that _S_={_u<sub>i</sub>,v<sub>i</sub>_} for some \(i\in[t]\), without loss of generality, as its density dominates that of {_u<sub>i</sub>,w<sub>j</sub>_}. Since _s_ is uniformly distributed, then we have _i=s_ and so \(\rho(S)=N/2\) with probability 1_/N_, or otherwise \(\rho(S)=0\). By the fact that the maximum 2-density is always positive, then no deterministic algorithm can obtain any approximation for D_k_SH with probability greater than 1_/N_. We complete the proof by noting that _n_=3_N_ and appealing to Yao’s Principle for a matching bound on randomized algorithms.
