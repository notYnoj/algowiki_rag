<tbc>use a similar approach to Chen et al. \[14, Lemma 6.2\], who rule out finding \((1\pm\varepsilon)\)-approximate cut sparsifiers with polynomially-many edge queries. They define a distribution of hypergraphs H such that for any hypergraph _H_∼H, the distribution of outputs to any _t_ edge queries differs only negligibly based on the underlying hypergraph _H_. In order to give an approximate cut sparsifier, by construction algorithms must be able to determine which hypergraph _H_ is. The authors then prove an upper bound on the probability of an algorithm correctly guessing the hypergraph _H_ by upper bounding the _total variation distance_ between the distributions of answers to any set of queries, where total variation distance is defined on discrete domains to be half the _L_<sup>1</sup> distance:

\(\Delta_\text{TV}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}')=\frac{1}{2}\sum_x|P_\mathcal{D}(x)-P_{\mathcal{D}^\prime}(x)|.\)

Implicitly, the argument of \[14\] uses the elegant fact that the (statistical) distinguishing advantage for any two distributions can be upper bounded by their total variation distance. However, this approach is inherently limited to differentiating between a pair of distributions and yields upper bounds of \(1/2+\delta\) on the probability of achieving approximations. In order to prove that D_k_SH cannot be approximated with non-negligible probability, we come up with a more general argument that lifts to a setting with many distributions.

Our hard instance for D_k_SH is an exponentially-large family of hypergraphs hiding a single hyperedge _X_ of cardinality _n/_3 among exponentially-many (2_n/_3)-hyperedges. With only one (_n/_3)-subhypergraph of non-zero density, approximating D_k_SH for _k=n/_3 can be reduced to finding _X_. Intuitively, this is difficult because an edge query has exponentially-small probability to output _X_ among the many (2_n/_3)-hyperedges. To bound the probability that an algorithm learns any meaningful information, we construct an explicit coupling of the answers to our edge queries for each possible _X_ using the following result.

**Proposition 6** (\[2, Proposition 6\])**.** For any collection S of discrete random variables taking values in a common finite set, there exists a coupling such that, for any \(X,Y\in\mathcal{S}\), \(Pr\left[X=Y\right]\ge 1-2\Delta_\text{TV}(X,Y)\).

We note that Proposition 6 was originally proven in \[27, Lemma 3.2\], but in a substantially different setting.
