Let _e_<sub>2_t_+2</sub>:=_e_<sub>1</sub>. Then some pair _e<sub>j</sub>,e_<sub>_j_+1</sub> with \(j\in[i+1,2t+1]\) also lies in different weakly connected components of _T-e<sub>i</sub>_. Observe that \(e_je_{j+1}\in E(H)\). Hence by Claim 11, {_e<sub>j</sub>,e_<sub>_j_+1</sub>_,e<sub>i</sub>_} is a triangle in _H_.

**Case** _e_<sub>_i_+1</sub> **is an ancestor of** _e<sub>i</sub>_**.** Similarly to the first case, we obtain that _e<sub>i</sub>_ and _e_<sub>1</sub> lie in different weakly connected components of _T-e_<sub>_i_+1</sub>. Then there exists a pair of edges _e<sub>j</sub>,e_<sub>_j_+1</sub> with the same property, where \(j\in[1,i-1]\). Again, together with Claim 11, this yields a triangle {_e<sub>j</sub>,e_<sub>_j_+1</sub>_,e_<sub>_i_+1</sub>} in _H_.

In both cases, we found a triangle formed by three vertices of the hole, which leads us to a contradiction. Therefore, _H_ does not contain an odd hole.

Next, suppose that _H_ contains an odd antihole _e_<sub>1</sub>_,...,e_<sub>2_t_+1</sub>. Since an antihole of size 5 is isomorphic to a hole of size 5, we may assume that \(t\ge 3\). Define a partial order on tree edges: _e_ precedes \(e'\) if _T_ contains a directed path from _e_ to \(e'\). Because _T_ is acyclic, we can topologically sort the edges with respect to this order. For a tree edge \(e\in E(T)\), by _f(e_) we denote its position in the sorted order. Without loss of generality, let _f(e_<sub>1</sub>) be the minimum among _f(e_<sub>1</sub>)_,f(e_<sub>2</sub>)_,...,f(e_<sub>2_t_+1</sub>). Also, let _e<sub>i</sub>_ be the edge with the minimum _f_ among _e_<sub>3</sub>_,...,e_<sub>6</sub>. Observe that for each \(i\in\{3,4,5,6\}\) there exist \(j_1,j_2\in\{3,4,5,6\}\) such that |_i-j_<sub>1</sub>|=1, |_i-j_<sub>2</sub>|_\>_1 and |_j_<sub>1</sub>-_j_<sub>2</sub>|_\>_1:

• for _i_=3, take _j_<sub>1</sub>=4 and _j_<sub>2</sub>=6;<tbc>

• for _i_=4, take _j_<sub>1</sub>=3 and _j_<sub>2</sub>=6;<tbc>

• two remaining cases are symmetrical.

Notice that the given properties imply that _i_, _j_<sub>1</sub> and _j_<sub>2</sub> are distinct. Since \(i,j_1,j_2\in\{3,4,5,6\}\) and \(t\ge 3\), we also have |_i_-1|,|_j_<sub>1</sub>-1|,|_j_<sub>2</sub>-1|_\>_1, and _i,j_<sub>1</sub>_,j_<sub>2</sub>_\<_2_t_+1. Recall that the antihole contains an edge between any two non-consecutive vertices. Consequently, \(e_ie_i,e_1e_{j_1},e_1e_{j_2},e_ie_{j_2},e_{j_1}e_{j_2}\in E(H)\), but \(e_ie_{j_1}\notin E(H)\). We next make use of the following claim.

**Claim 12.** _Let_ \(h_1,h_2,h_3\in E(T)\) _be a triple of tree edges such that_ \(h_1h_2,h_2h_3\in E(H)\) _and f(h_<sub>1</sub>)_\<f(h_<sub>2</sub>)_\<f(h_<sub>3</sub>)_. Then there is a directed path in T that starts at h_<sub>1</sub>_, ends at h_<sub>3</sub> _and goes through h_<sub>2</sub>.

_Proof of Claim 12._ By Claim 11, _T_ contains a directed path _P_<sub>1</sub> between _h_<sub>1</sub> and _h_<sub>2</sub>. Since _f(h_<sub>1</sub>)_\<f(h_<sub>2</sub>), _P_<sub>1</sub> must go from _h_<sub>1</sub> to _h_<sub>2</sub>. Similarly, _T_ contains a directed path _P_<sub>2</sub> that goes from _h_<sub>2</sub> to _h_<sub>3</sub>. Then the concatenation of _P_<sub>1</sub> and _P_<sub>2</sub> yields a needed path. \(\lrcorner\)

By the choice of _e_<sub>1</sub> and _e<sub>i</sub>_, we have _f(e_<sub>1</sub>)_\<f(e<sub>i</sub>)\<f(e_<sub>_j_<sub>2</sub></sub>). Since \(e_ie_i\in E(H)\) and \(e_ie_{j_2}\in E(H)\), Claim 12 guarantees that there is a directed path _P_ that starts at _e_<sub>1</sub>, ends at _e_<sub>_j_<sub>2</sub></sub> and goes through _e<sub>i</sub>_. Now consider a triple _e_<sub>1</sub>, _e_<sub>_j_<sub>1</sub></sub> and _e_<sub>_j_<sub>2</sub></sub>. By the choice of _e_<sub>1</sub>, we know that _f(e_<sub>1</sub>)_\<f(e_<sub>_j_<sub>1</sub></sub>) and _f(e_<sub>1</sub>)_\<f(e_<sub>_j_<sub>2</sub></sub>). Also, {_e_<sub>1</sub>_,e_<sub>_j_<sub>1</sub></sub>_,e_<sub>_j_<sub>2</sub></sub>} is a triangle in _H_. Therefore, we can apply Claim 12 to this triple either in the order _e_<sub>1</sub>_,e_<sub>_j_<sub>1</sub></sub>_,e_<sub>_j_<sub>2</sub></sub> or in the order _e_<sub>1</sub>_,e_<sub>_j_<sub>2</sub></sub>_,e_<sub>_j_<sub>1</sub></sub>, depending on the result of comparison of _f(e_<sub>_j_<sub>1</sub></sub>) and _f(e_<sub>_j_<sub>2</sub></sub>). As a result, we obtain that _T_ contains a directed path \(P'\) that goes through these three edges in one of the orders. We consider two cases.

**Case** _f(e_<sub>_j_<sub>1</sub></sub>)_\<f(e_<sub>_j_<sub>2</sub></sub>). In this case _e_<sub>_j_<sub>1</sub></sub> lies between _e_<sub>1</sub> and _e_<sub>_j_<sub>2</sub></sub> on \(P'\). Since the tree paths _P_ and \(P'\) both go from _e_<sub>1</sub> to _e_<sub>_j_<sub>2</sub></sub>, we have \(P=P'\). Recall that _e<sub>i</sub>_ also lies on _P_. From \(e_1e_{j_2}\in E(H)\) it follows that there exists an edge _e_ in the intersection \(\mathrm{Inv}(e_1)\cap\mathrm{Inv}(e_{j_2})\). Suppose that it goes from _u_ to _v_. By Observation 1, there is a directed path _L_ from _v_ to _u_ in _T_, and both _e_<sub>1</sub> and _e_<sub>_j_<sub>2</sub></sub> lie on _L_. Hence \(P\subseteq L\). In particular, \(e_i,e_{j_1}\in L\). Then by the same observation, we have \(e\in\mathrm{Inv}(e_i)\) and \(e\in\mathrm{Inv}(e_{j_1})\). Therefore, \(\mathrm{Inv}(e_i)\cap\mathrm{Inv}(e_{j_1})\neq\emptyset\) and thus \(e_ie_{j_1}\in E(H)\).
