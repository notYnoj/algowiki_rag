**Claim 5.** _Let_ \(u,v\in S'\) _be a pair of vertices such that there is a directed path from u to v in T, with all internal vertices outside both S and_ \(S'\)_. Let E<sub>uv</sub> be the set of edges on this path. Then_ \(e\in E_{uv}\) _cannot share an inverse edge with_ \(e'\in(E(T)\backslash E_{uv})\).

_Proof of Claim 5._ Since \(u,v\in S'\), we know that each of them is either a sink or a source in _T_. Note that, given the existence of a directed path from _u_ to _v_, the vertex _u_ must be a source and _v_ must be a sink. Moreover, as all internal vertices of the path lie outside both _S_ and \(S'\), their in-degree and out-degree in _T_ are equal to one.

Consider an edge \(e\in E_{uv}\) that leads from \(u'\) to \(v'\) and suppose that it has an inverse edge \(\tilde{e}\in\mathrm{Inv}(e)\) that goes from _a_ to _b_. By Observation 1, _T_ contains a directed path from \(v'\) to \(u'\), and this path contains \(\tilde{e}\). Then there also must be paths in _T_ from \(v'\) to _a_ and from _b_ to \(u'\). If \(v'\) is an internal vertex of the path from _u_ to _v_, then its only outgoing edge in _T_ leads to the next vertex on the path. Otherwise, \(v'=v\) and its out-degree is zero. This means that all vertices that are reachable from \(v'\) in _T_ lie on the path from \(v'\) to _v_, and hence _a_ also must be on this path. Similarly, we can show that _b_ lies on the path from _u_ to \(u'\). Thus, a path from _b_ to _a_ in _T_ consists only of edges from _E<sub>uv</sub>_. Applying the Observation 1, we obtain that if \(\tilde{e}\in\mathrm{Inv}(e')\), then \(e'\in E_{uv}\). \(\lrcorner\)

We move on to the second reduction rule.

**Reduction Rule 2.** _Consider four distinct vertices_ \(v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4\in S'\) _for which T contains edge- disjoint directed paths from v_<sub>1</sub> _to v_<sub>2</sub>_, from v_<sub>3</sub> _to v_<sub>2</sub>_, and from v_<sub>3</sub> _to v_<sub>4</sub>_, all of whose internal vertices lie outside both S and_ \(S'\)_. If all edges on these paths are unprofitable, contract the first edge on the path from v_<sub>1</sub> _to v_<sub>2</sub>.

**Claim 6.** _Reduction Rule 2 is safe._

_Proof of Claim 6._ Recall that by Claim 1, we only need to show that the existence of a solution for I implies that there is also a solution for \(\mathcal{I}':=(G/e_c,\mathbf{w}',k)\), where _e<sub>c</sub>_ is the first edge on the path from _v_<sub>1</sub> to _v_<sub>2</sub>, and \(\mathbf{w}'\) is the weight function naturally inherited from **w**.

Let _E<sub>p</sub>_ be the set of edges on the paths from _v_<sub>1</sub> to _v_<sub>2</sub>, from _v_<sub>3</sub> to _v_<sub>2</sub>, and from _v_<sub>3</sub> to _v_<sub>4</sub> in _T_, and let _V<sub>p</sub>_ be the set of all vertices on these paths excluding _v_<sub>1</sub> and _v_<sub>4</sub>. Consider a vertex \(v\in V_p\). Note that \(v\notin S\). For internal vertices of the paths, this is ensured by the lemma statement; for _v_<sub>2</sub> and _v_<sub>3</sub>, it follows from the fact that \(S\cap S'=\emptyset\). Consequently, _v_ is incident to no allowed edges, and its degree in _T_ is two. Thus, _E<sub>p</sub>_ contains all edges that are incident to _v_ in _T_: indeed, if _v_ is an internal vertex of a path, then it has two incident edges on this path. Otherwise, _v_ is either _v_<sub>2</sub> or _v_<sub>3</sub>. In both cases, _v_ belongs to two paths, and since these paths are edge-disjoint, it also has two incident edges in _E<sub>p</sub>_.

Suppose that _X_ is a solution for I. Let _E<sub>r</sub>_:=_E<sub>p</sub>_\\_E(X_). We claim that \(\tilde{X}:=X+E_r-\mathrm{Inv}(E_r)\) is also a solution for I, and every vertex from _V<sub>p</sub>_ has exactly two incident edges in \(\tilde{X}\), both from _E<sub>p</sub>_.

We start with the latter property. Consider a vertex \(v\in V_p\). As argued above, _v_ is incident to no allowed edges. Moreover, _v_ has two incident edges in _T_, both of which belong to _E<sub>p</sub>_. Since \(E_p\subseteq E(\tilde{X})\), these edges are also present in \(\tilde{X}\). Suppose, for contradiction, that some blocked edge \(e'\in E(\tilde{X})\) is incident to _v_, and let _u_ be its other endpoint. By Observation 1, there must be a directed path _P_ between _u_ and _v_ in _T_, and for every edge _e_ on this path, we have \(e'\in\mathrm{Inv}(e)\). We distinguish two cases.
