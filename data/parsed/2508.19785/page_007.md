To address the first question, we design a search algorithm called `NoisySearch` that finds an approximate rank of a new element with respect to an approximately sorted sequence of _m_ elements in _O_(log_m_) time, w.h.p. The computed approximate rank differs from the true rank of the new element by at most a constant times the maximum dislocation of the sequence. The details of this algorithm are given in Section 4 and its guarantees can be summarized as follows:

Theorem 4.10. _Let S be a sequence of m elements, let d be an integer with_ \(d\ge max\{\mathrm{disl}(S),\mathrm{ln}m\}\)_, and let_ \(x\notin S\) _be an additional element. Under our error model for approximate noisy binary searching, there exists an algorithm_ `NoisySearch`(_S,x,d_) _that runs in O_(log_m_) _worst-case time and returns an index_ \(\tau_x\in[\mathrm{rank}(x,S)-\alpha d,\mathrm{rank}(x,S)+\alpha d]\) _with probability at least_ 1-_O(m_<sup>-6</sup>)_, where_ \(\alpha\ge 2\) _is a constant that depends only on p._

For the second question, we connect the batch insertion to _an urn experiment_ that is seemingly unrelated to sorting and can be stated as a basic probability problem (see Lemma 3.2 in the analysis of the algorithm).

Finally, to restore the dislocation of a sequence, we develop a new sorting algorithm for persistent random comparison faults called `BasketSort`. This algorithm takes a sequence of _m_ elements and an upper bound _d_ on the maximum dislocation of such sequence as input and computes, in \(\Theta(md)\) time, a new sequence with a maximum dislocation of _O_(log_m_) and a total dislocation of _O(m_) w.h.p.<sup>1</sup> This algorithm is described in Section 5, which formally proves the following:

Theorem 5.20. _Consider a set of m elements subject to persistent random comparison faults with probability at least q and at most p, for some constants p,q with_ \(0\le q\le p<\frac{9q+1}{8q+11}\)_. Let S be a sequence of these m elements (possibly chosen as a function of the errors), and let_ \(w_S\ge\mathrm{disl}(S)\). `BasketSort`(_S,w<sub>S</sub>_) _is a deterministic algorithm that approximately sorts S in O(mw<sub>S</sub>_) _worst-case time. The maximum dislocation and the total dislocation of the returned sequence are at most_ \(\beta_{max}\cdot\mathrm{ln}m\) _and_ \(\beta_{tot}\cdot m\)_, respectively, with probability_ 1-_O(m_<sup>-3</sup>)_, where_ \(\beta_{max}\ge 2\) _and_ \(\beta_{tot}\) _are constants that come from Lemma 5.12 and Lemma 5.19, respectively, and depend only on p and q._
