\(\mathrm{rank}(y,A)\le\mathrm{rank}(x,A)\le r_x+\alpha d\le r_y+\alpha d\le\mathrm{rank}(y,A)+2\alpha d.\)

Hence, we have \(\mathrm{disl}(y,\widetilde{S})\le\alpha d+|X_y|\). Then, regardless of whether \(y\in A\) or \(y\in B\), \(\mathrm{disl}(y,\widetilde{S})\le\alpha d+|X_y|\) and we can focus on upper bounding |_X<sub>y</sub>_|.

Let \(z_y^-\) be the element of _A_ with \(\mathrm{rank}(z_y^-,A)=\mathrm{rank}(y,A)-\lfloor 2\alpha d\rfloor-1\); if no such element exists, then let \(z_y^-\) be the first element of _S_<sup>∗</sup>. The above choice of \(z_y^-\) ensures that \(z_y^-\le x\) for all \(x\in X_y\). This is trivial if \(z_y^-\) is the first element of _S_<sup>∗</sup> while, in the complementary case, it follows from \(\mathrm{rank}(z_y^-,A)<\mathrm{rank}(y,A)-2\alpha d\le\mathrm{rank}(x,A)\), where we the last inequality is due to the definition of _X<sub>y</sub>_. Symmetrically, let \(z_y^+\) be the element of _A_ with \(\mathrm{rank}(z_y^+,A)=\mathrm{rank}(y,A)+\lfloor 2\alpha d\rfloor\) or, if no such element exists, let \(z_y^+\) be the last element of _S_<sup>∗</sup>. We have \(x\le z_y^+\) for all \(x\in X_y\). Indeed, this is trivial when \(z_y^+\) is last element of _S_<sup>∗</sup>, and it follows from \(z_y^+\in A\) and \(\mathrm{rank}(x,A)\le\mathrm{rank}(y,A)+\lfloor 2\alpha d\rfloor=\mathrm{rank}(z_y^+,A)\) in the complementary case.

Then, since all \(x\in X_y\) satisfy \(z_y^-\le x\le z_y^+\), we have that all elements in _X<sub>y</sub>_ must appear in the contiguous subsequence <o>_S_</o> of _S_<sup>∗</sup> having \(z_y^-\) and \(z_y^+\) as its endpoints. Such a sequence <o>_S_</o> contains at most \(2\lfloor 2\alpha d\rfloor+2\) elements of _A_, hence our assumption guarantees that <o>_S_</o> contains at most \(216\alpha d+108\) elements. We conclude that the dislocation of _y_ in \(\widetilde{S}\) is at most \(\alpha d+|X_y|\le\alpha d+|\widetilde{S}|\le 217\alpha d+108\le 226\alpha d=\gamma d\), where we used \(\alpha d\ge 12\) since \(\alpha\ge 2\) and \(d\ge\mathrm{log}n\ge\mathrm{log}m\ge 6\). □

We can now apply Lemma 3.3, Theorem 4.10, and Theorem 5.20 together to analyze the maximum dislocation and the total dislocation of the sequence returned by RiffleSort.

Lemma 3.4. _Let n be larger than some sufficiently large constant (depending on p and q). With probability at least_ \(1-O\left(\frac{\mathrm{log}n}{n\sqrt{n}}\right)\)_, the maximum dislocation and the total dislocation of the sequence returned by_ RiffleSort _are at most_ \(\beta_{max}\cdot\mathrm{log}n\) _and at most_ \(\beta_{tot}\cdot n\)_, respectively._

Proof. We assume that \(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\mathrm{ln}n}\ge 2160\alpha\beta_{\mathrm{max}}\), which is satisified by all values of _n_ that are larger than some constant depending on \(\alpha\) and \(\beta_{\mathrm{max}}\), both of which, in turn, depend only on _p_ and _q_.

For _i_=1_,...,k_, we say that the _i_-th iteration of Algorithm 1 is _good_ if the sequence _S<sub>i</sub>_ computed at the end of the _i_-th iteration has a maximum dislocation of at most \(\beta_{\mathrm{max}}\cdot\mathrm{ln}n\) and a total dislocation of at most \(\beta_{\mathrm{tot}}\cdot n\). As a corner case, we say that iteration 0 is good if the above conditions are satisfied by the sequence _S_<sub>0</sub> computed in line 4 of Algorithm 1.

Since _S_<sub>0</sub> is the sequence returned by invoking BasketSort on \(\widetilde{S}_0\) using the trivial upper bound of \(|\widetilde{S}_0|\) on \(\mathrm{disl}(\widetilde{S}_0)\), Theorem 5.20 ensures that _S_<sub>0</sub> is good with probability at least 1-_O(n_<sup>-1.5</sup>).

We now focus on a generic iteration \(i\ge 1\) and show that, assuming that iteration _i_-1 is good, iteration _i_ is also good with probability at least 1-_O(n_<sup>-1.5</sup>). Since iteration _i_-1 is good, we have \(\mathrm{disl}(S_{i-1})\le\beta_{\mathrm{max}}\cdot\mathrm{ln}n\). Moreover, for each element \(x\in T_i\), we have that the comparison errors between _x_ and the elements in _S_<sub>_i_-1</sub> do not depend on the specific elements in _S_<sub>_i_-1</sub> nor on their order. Then, Theorem 4.10 together with the union bound ensure that all the approximate ranks _r<sub>x</sub>_, for \(x\in T_i\), computed by the calls to NoisySearch\((S_{i-1},x,\beta_{\mathrm{max}}\cdot\mathrm{ln}n)\) (see line 7) satisfy \(|r_x-\mathrm{rank}(x,S_{i-1})|\le\alpha\beta_{\mathrm{max}}\cdot\mathrm{ln}n\) with probability at least \(1-O(|T_i|\cdot|S_{i-1}|^{-6})=1-O(n^{-2})\). Whenever this happens, Lemma 3.3 with \(d=\beta_{\mathrm{max}}\cdot\mathrm{ln}n\) shows that the sequence \(\widetilde{S}_i\) computed in line 8 has a maximum dislocation of at most \(\gamma\cdot\beta_{\mathrm{max}}\cdot\mathrm{ln}n\) with probability at least 1-_O(n_<sup>-3</sup>). If such an upper bound on the maximum dislocation of \(\widetilde{S}_i\) does indeed hold, Theorem 5.20 further implies that the call to BasketSort\((\widetilde{S}_i,\gamma\cdot\beta_{\mathrm{max}}\cdot\mathrm{ln}n)\) in the _i_-th iteration (see line 9) returns a sequence _S<sub>i</sub>_ having a maximum (resp. total) dislocation of at most \(\beta_{\mathrm{max}}\cdot\mathrm{ln}|\widetilde{S}_i|\le\beta_{\mathrm{max}}\cdot\mathrm{ln}n\) (resp. \(\beta_{\mathrm{tot}}|\widetilde{S}_i|\le\beta_{\mathrm{tot}}n\)) with probability at least \(1-O\left(\frac{1}{|\widetilde{S}_i|^{-3}}\right)=1-O\left(n^{-1.5}\right)\), i.e., that iteration _i_ is good.<tbc>
