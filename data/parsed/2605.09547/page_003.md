## 1 Introduction

Space complexity is a important factor in streaming, parallel/distributed computing, communication complexity, and data structures, requiring computation and representation of objects using limited space. In this work, we study the space complexity of the minimum-cost flow problem, a generalization of the _st_-max flow problem. We focus on computing such flows in subquadratic space in the multi-pass streaming model, contrasting quadratic space lower bounds from \[CJWY25, ACK19, AGL<sup>+</sup>24\]. In particular, our goal is to compute the actual flow on each edge, rather than just the cost of the min-cost flow or the size of the maximum flow.

In the minimum-cost flow problem, we are given a directed _n_-node graph _G_=(_V,E_), a vertex-demand vector \(d\in\mathbb{Z}^V\), edge capacities \(u\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^E\) and edge costs \(c\in\mathbb{Z}^E\). The minimum-cost flow is a flow \(f\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^E\) that satisfies the vertex-demands and edge capacities, while minimizing the cost \(\sum_{e\in E}c_e\cdot f_e\). While the min-cut \(W\subset V\) (and thus size of a max flow) can be stored in _O(n_) space, observe that flows in general can use \(\Omega(n^2)\) edges. Thus it is not clear if one can compute the flow \(f\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^E\) (i.e., the amount of flow on each edge) in subquadratic space.

#### Streaming Algorithms

In the streaming model, the input (i.e., edge set) is given to the algorithm one-by-one as a stream of data. The algorithm is only allowed a small amount of space and thus cannot store the entire input. In a multi-pass algorithm, one may take multiple passes over the input stream. This computational model is motivated by large amounts of data being faster to read sequentially than via random access, e.g., when the data is too large for working memory and is read directly from hard-drive. Bipartite matching (a simpler special case of max flow) is one of the most successfully studied problems in streaming literature, both in the approximate (see, e.g.,\[EKS09, AG11, EKMS12, GKK12, KMM12, Kap13, AG18, BFKL21, AJJ<sup>+</sup>22, AS23, Ass24\] and references therein) and exact setting \[LSZ<sup>+</sup>23, AJJ<sup>+</sup>22\]. However, for flows much less is known. For undirected maximum flows, one can \((1+\epsilon)\)-approximate the size of the max flow in \(\widetilde{O}(n)\) space via duality by constructing a cut sparsifier (see, e.g., \[AG09, AGM12, KPPS17, CKST19, CJWY25\]). \[RSW18\] show one can compute the exact _st_-min cut (i.e., size of max flow) on unweighted undirected graphs in \(\widetilde{O}(n^{5/3})\) space. Computing the exact flow size/cost on weighted graphs in subquadratic space remains open.

When it comes to computing not just the size/cost of the flow but the flow \(f\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^E\) itself, existing upper bounds are substantially weaker. While sparsification approaches allow a \((1+\epsilon)\)-approximation of the size, only crude poly(_n_) approximations are known for computing flow _f_ in subquadratic space \[CJWY25\]. Indeed, this gap between size and flow _f_ can be explained and \[CJWY25\] provide respective lower bounds.

For intuition, why representing (i.e., not just computing) flows in subquadratic space is impossible, consider a complete bipartite graph with edges oriented left to right, an extra vertex _s_ connected to the left vertices, and an extra vertex _t_ reachable from all right vertices. The edges from _s_ and edges to _t_ have very high capacity, so the max flow has _f<sub>e</sub>=u<sub>e</sub>_ for each edge _e_ of the bipartite graph. Thus storing/representing all (or even most, in case of approximation) _f<sub>e</sub>_ in subquadratic space means we can store all (or most) capacities _u<sub>e</sub>_ in subquadratic space, but that is \(\Theta(n^2)\) information (\(u\in\mathbb{R}^E\) could be any _n_<sup>2</sup>-sized binary bit string). This lower bound argument was formalized by \[CJWY25\]<sup>1</sup>, who proved that for every encoding algorithm \(\mathcal{E}(G)\in\{0,1\}^d\) (computing a max-flow on _G_ and then encoding it in _d_ bit) and decoding algorithm A (where A(E(_G_)) returns a (1-1/36)-approximation of the max-flow) needs \(d=\Omega(n^2)\). Thus it’s impossible to encode max/min-cost flows _f_ in subquadratic space. Importantly, the lower bound by \[CJWY25\] applies not just to streaming but to any encoding scheme. Thus the argument can also be used to argue about the communication complexity for communicating _f_.

#### Communication Complexity

In the 2-party (or multi-party) communication model, the edges of the input graph are distributed among multiple parties. The task is to solve some graph problem while using as little communication as possible. This area, too, had a lot of success for various graph problems \[IKL<sup>+</sup>12, BJMY25, FM24, DNO14, PS82, BFS86, HMT88, DP89, MPSS24\], but flows remain elusive. For related problems like bipartite matching or transshipment, an \(\widetilde{O}(n)\) upper bound is known \[BBE<sup>+</sup>22, IKL<sup>+</sup>12\], crucially relying on the fact that the optimal solution consists of at most _O(n_) edges.
