**Computations on** \(G_i^*\)**s.** Computations on the dual graphs \(G_i^*\) (or their spanning trees \(T_i^*\)) is an issue because it is not clear how to simulate a dual graph distributively. Indeed, this topic was tackled for the first time in [9] who provided a method for simulating aggregates on faces of _G<sub>i</sub>_ and on dual spanning trees.

We face this challenge exactly as [9]. Namely, all computations we perform on the dual graphs \(G_i^*\), especially the algorithms we use in Lemmas 9, 10, and 11, boil down to aggregations on low-congestion shortcuts for the faces of _G<sub>i</sub>_s. The technical challenge that [9] overcome in this natural approach, is that the set of faces of a graph is not vertex-disjoint. Thus, it does not meet the standard definition of low-congestion shortcuts (Definition 10). However, they show how to construct an auxiliary graph \(\hat{G}_i\) deterministically in a constant number of rounds, such that, constructing low-congestion shortcuts for a specific partition of \(\hat{G}_i\) is done in \(\tilde{O}(D)\) rounds (the same complexity for constructing shortcuts inside _G_). This implies low-congestion shortcuts for the faces of _G<sub>i</sub>_ with the same near-optimal parameters that planar graphs admit (Lemma 5 of [9]). Our tasks then reduce to aggregate information on shortcuts in the primal graph, and this is done as discussed above for the primal.

**Conclusion.** In each given time, we have a partition of _G_ into _G<sub>i</sub>_s, this partition implies two shortcut sets that are built on _G_ (depending on the steps of the algorithm), such that the first is used to compute part-wise aggregation tasks on _G<sub>i</sub>_s simultaneously and the second one is for computations on the dual graphs \(G_i^*\)s (specifically, their spanning trees \(T_i^*\)s). Those shortcuts are constructed deterministically with near-optimal parameters _c_, \(d=\tilde{O}(D)\) as in Lemma 6. In the algorithm, we move between them, such that, aggregations at any given time are computed in at most one of them. After which, the partitions and their shortcuts may be recomputed (according to the steps of the algorithm). Thus, our algorithm is applied in all subgraphs \(\{G_i\}_{i=1}^k\) simultaneously deterministically within the same round complexity \(\tilde{O}(D)\) of applying it on a single graph _G<sub>i</sub>_.
