_2._ _Otherwise, there exists a_ \((\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})\)_-critical node f in T_<sup>∗</sup> _w.r.t._ \(w_F(\cdot)\)_. Then, there are two vertices u, v on f s.t. the u-to-v path P in T is a_ \(\frac{3}{4}\)_-balanced separator of G w.r.t._ \(w_\vee(\cdot)\)_. Moreover, if we add the edge e_=(_u,v_) _to G, it does not violate planarity, and_ \(P\cup\{e\}=C(T,e)\).

_Proof._ By Lemma 4, either a \((\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})\)-balanced node or a \((\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})\)-critical node exists w.r.t. \(w_F(\cdot)\).

**Case 1**: If a \((\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})\)-balanced node _f_ in _T_<sup>∗</sup> exists w.r.t. \(w_F(\cdot)\), then consider the fundamental cut \(\delta(T^*,e^*)\), where _e_<sup>∗</sup>=(_f,_parent(_f_)). The cut is dual to a fundamental cycle _S=C(T,e_) in _G_, such that its interior corresponds to \(T_f^*\) (Lemma 3). See Figure 1 for an illustration. We next prove that the total vertex weight of the strict interior and strict exterior of _S_ are both bounded by \(\frac{3}{4}\cdot w_\vee(G)\). Then, setting _P_ to be _S_\\{_e_} gives the balanced path separator. By Lemma 3, \(w(T_f^*)=w_F(S_{in})\). Also, as _f_ is \((\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})\)-balanced we have: \(\frac{1}{4}\cdot w_F(G)\le w(T_f^*)=w_F(S_{in})\le\frac{3}{4}\cdot w_F(G)\), and since \(w_\vee(S_{in}^-)\le w_F(S_{in})\le w_\vee(S_{in})\) (Observation 1), we get that \(w_\vee(S_{in}^-)\le w_F(S_{in})\le\frac{3}{4}\cdot w_F(G)\) and \(\frac{1}{4}\cdot w_F(G)\le w_\vee(S_{in})\). Finally, because \(w_F(G)=w_\vee(G)\) (Observation 1) we get that both the strict interior is bounded by \(w_\vee(S_{in}^-)\le\frac{3}{4}\cdot w_\vee(G)\) and the strict exterior is bounded as we have \(\frac{1}{4}\cdot w_\vee(G)\le w_\vee(S_{in})\), which implies that \(w_\vee(S_{out}^-)=w_\vee(G)-w_\vee(S_{in})\le w_\vee(G)-\frac{1}{4}\cdot w_\vee(G)=\frac{3}{4}\cdot w_\vee(G)\). Hence, we get a \(\frac{3}{4}\)-balanced separator with respect to vertex-weights.

**Case 2**: If a \((\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})\)-critical node _f_ exists in _T_<sup>∗</sup> w.r.t. \(w_F(\cdot)\), then by the bi-connectivity assumption, _f_ is a simple cycle in _G_ <sup>4</sup>. If _f_ has no children in _T_<sup>∗</sup>, then this cycle has only one of its edges _e_=(_f,_parent(_f_)) in _T_<sup>∗</sup>. The rest of its edges form a path _P_ in _T_ (by Lemma 1). In this case, _P_ is the balanced separator, because the strict interior of the face (cycle) _f_ is empty (so weighs zero), and the strict exterior is of weight at most \(\frac{3}{4}\cdot w_\vee(G)\). This is because as _f_ is a critical node with no children in _T_<sup>∗</sup>, we have that \(w(T_f^*)=w_F(f)>\frac{3}{4}\cdot w_F(G)=\frac{3}{4}\cdot w_\vee(G)\), where the last equality follows from Observation 1. Now, if the weight of _f_ is larger than \(\frac{3}{4}\cdot w_\vee(G)\) it means that the total weight of vertices in _f_ is at least \(\frac{3}{4}\cdot w_\vee(G)\), because the weight of _f_ is the total weight of vertices in _f_ that transferred their weight to _f_. This means that the strict exterior of _f_ weighs \(w_\vee(G\backslash f)=w_\vee(G)-w_\vee(f)\le w_\vee(G)-\frac{3}{4}\cdot w_\vee(G)<\frac{3}{4}\cdot w_\vee(G)\).

Otherwise, _f_ has at least one child, and each child’s subtree weighs less than \(\frac{1}{4}\cdot w_F(G)\). We show that there are two vertices _u_, _v_ on _f_ s.t. the _u_-to-_v_ path _P_ in _T_ is a balanced separator for _G_ w.r.t. \(w_\vee(\cdot)\). We do that by reducing this case to Case 1. In other words, we show that if one would triangulate _f_ with artificial edges (see Figure 2b), then one of the resulting faces is \((\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{4})\)-balanced. By Case 1, this implies that such two nodes _u_, _v_ exist. In this case, only the edge (_u,v_) will be artificial.

Formally, consider the primal edge _e_ whose dual is the edge (_f,_parent(_f_)) in _T_<sup>∗</sup>. Let _v_<sub>1</sub>_,...,v<sub>k</sub>_ be the vertices of _f_ such that _e_=(_v_<sub>1</sub>_,v<sub>k</sub>_), and _f_’s vertices are enumerated counter-clockwise from _v_<sub>1</sub> to _v<sub>k</sub>_. See Figure 2a. For the sake of the proof, we triangulate _f_ by adding artificial edges between _v<sub>k</sub>_ and vertices _v_<sub>2</sub>_,...,v_<sub>_k_-2</sub> of _f_. This creates _k_-2 new faces _f_<sub>1</sub>_,f_<sub>2</sub>_,...,f_<sub>_k_-2</sub>, where _f<sub>i</sub>_ is the face that contains the edge (_v<sub>i</sub>,v_<sub>_i_+1</sub>) of _f_ (see Figure 2b). Adding the triangulation edges changes _T_<sup>∗</sup> as follows: (1) The node _f_ is replaced by a _f_<sub>1</sub>-to-_f_<sub>_k_-2</sub> path _P(f_) of artificial edges, (2) The parent of _f_ is now the parent of _f_<sub>1</sub>, (3) Every _f<sub>i</sub>_ (where _i\<k_-2) has one (artificial) child _f_<sub>_i_+1</sub> and perhaps one more (real) child _g_, if _g_ was a child of _f_ in _T_<sup>∗</sup> (i.e., when (_f,g_) is the dual edge of (_v<sub>i</sub>,v_<sub>_i_+1</sub>)), (4) _f_<sub>_k_-2</sub> is the last vertex of _P(f_) so it does not have an artificial child, but it may have (at most) two real children (connected to _f_<sub>_k_-2</sub> via the edges dual to (_v_<sub>_k_-2</sub>_,v_<sub>_k_-1</sub>) and (_v_<sub>_k_-1</sub>_,v<sub>k</sub>_)).

Consider _T_<sup>∗</sup>, where each node is assigned a weight as in Observation 1. Recall that each vertex _v_ transfers its weight to one face. If this face is not _f_, then _v_ transfers its weight to the same face as before. If _v_ originally transferred its weight to _f_, then _v_ is contained in at least one face _f<sub>i</sub>_. We then have _v_ transfer its weight to one such (arbitrary) face _f<sub>i</sub>_. By this weight assignment the total weight of the new faces _f<sub>i</sub>_ is exactly equal to the original weight of the face _f_. Since the original vertex weights were \(\frac{1}{12}\)-proper and each _f<sub>i</sub>_ has size 3 (i.e., gets the weight of at most 3 vertices), we have the following.

**Claim 1.** _The weight of every f<sub>i</sub> is_ \(\frac{1}{4}\)_-proper._

<sup>4</sup>I.e, if not, then _f_ consists of a simple cycle and trees hanging from it. Then, removing a single vertex from the tree disconnects the graph, contradicting the assumption that it is bi-connected.
