**Lemma 3.4** _Algorithm 1 always outputs a 1.5-approximate solution._

_Proof._ Let _I_ be an input, _M<sub>opt</sub>_ be a maximum stable matching for _I_, and _M_ be the output of Algorithm 1. We show that \(\frac{|M_{opt}|}{|M|}\le 1.5\). Let \(G=(X\cup Y,E)\) be a bipartite (multi-)graph with vertex bipartition _X_ and _Y_ , where _X_ corresponds to men and _Y_ corresponds to women of _I_. The edge set _E_ is a union of _M_ and _M<sub>opt</sub>_, that is, \((m_i,w_j)\in E\) if and only if (_m<sub>i</sub>,w<sub>j</sub>_) is a pair in _M_ or _M<sub>opt</sub>_. If (_m<sub>i</sub>,w<sub>j</sub>_) is a pair in both _M_ and _M<sub>opt</sub>_, then _E_ contains two edges (_m<sub>i</sub>,w<sub>j</sub>_), which constitute a "cycle" of length two. An edge in _E_ corresponding to _M_ (resp. _M<sub>opt</sub>_) is called an _M_-edge (resp. _M<sub>opt</sub>_-edge). Since the degree of each vertex of _G_ is at most 2, each connected component of _G_ is an isolated vertex, a cycle, or a path.

It is easy to see that _G_ does not contain a single _M<sub>opt</sub>_-edge as a connected component, since if such an edge (_m<sub>i</sub>,w<sub>j</sub>_) exists, then (_m<sub>i</sub>,w<sub>j</sub>_) is a blocking pair for _M_, contradicting the stability of _M_. In the following, we show that _G_ does not contain, as a connected component, a path of length three \(m_i-w_j-m_k-w_\ell\) such that (_m<sub>i</sub>,w<sub>j</sub>_) and \((m_k,w_\ell)\) are _M<sub>opt</sub>_-edges and (_m<sub>k</sub>,w<sub>j</sub>_) is an _M_-edge. If this is true, then for any connected component _C_ of _G_, the number of _M_-edges in _C_ is at least two-thirds of the number of _M<sub>opt</sub>_-edges in _C_, implying \(\frac{|M_{opt}|}{|M|}\le 1.5\).

Suppose that such a path exists. Note that _m<sub>i</sub>_ and \(w_\ell\) are single in _M_. If \(m_i\succ_{w_j}m_k\), then (_m<sub>i</sub>,w<sub>j</sub>_) blocks _M_. Since women’s preference lists do not contain ties, we have that \(m_k\succ_{w_j}m_i\). If \(w_\ell\succ_{m_k}w_j\), then \((m_k,w_\ell)\) blocks _M_. If \(w_j\succ_{m_k}w_\ell\), then (_m<sub>k</sub>,w<sub>j</sub>_) blocks _M<sub>opt</sub>_. Hence _w<sub>j</sub>_ and \(w_\ell\) are tied in _m<sub>k</sub>_’s list. Then by construction of \(I'\), (i) \(t_\ell\succ_{a_k}s_j\). (Hereafter, referring to Fig. 4 would be helpful. Here, the order of _t<sub>j</sub>_ and \(t_\ell\) in _a<sub>k</sub>_’s list is uncertain, i.e., it may be the opposite, but this order is not important in the rest of the proof.) Since \(w_\ell\) is single in _M_, either \(s_\ell\) or \(t_\ell\) is single in \(M'\). If \(s_\ell\) is single in \(M'\), then \((b_\ell,s_\ell)\) blocks \(M'\), a contradiction. Hence (ii) \(t_\ell\) is single in \(M'\). Since _M(m<sub>k</sub>_)=_w<sub>j</sub>_, either \(M'(a_k)=s_j\) or \(M'(a_k)=t_j\) holds. In the former case, (i) and (ii) above imply that \((a_k,t_\ell)\) blocks \(M'\), so assume the latter, i.e., \(M'(a_k)=t_j\). Recall that either _s<sub>j</sub>_ or _t<sub>j</sub>_ is matched with _b<sub>j</sub>_ in \(M'\), so \(M'(s_j)=b_j\). Since (_m<sub>i</sub>,w<sub>j</sub>_) is an acceptable pair in _I_, we have that \(a_i\succ_{s_j}b_j\). Since _m<sub>i</sub>_ is single in _M_, _a<sub>i</sub>_ is single in \(M'\). Hence (_a<sub>i</sub>,s<sub>j</sub>_) blocks \(M'\), a contradiction.
