QAOA replaces this continuous analog evolution with a discretised, finite sequence of alternating unitaries generated by a cost Hamiltonian _H<sub>C</sub>_ and a mixer Hamiltonian _H<sub>M</sub>_, leading to the layered circuit structure illustrated in Fig. 1. Typically, the cost Hamiltonian _H<sub>C</sub>_ is constructed to be diagonal in the computational basis, mapping classical bitstrings directly to their objective function values, while the mixer Hamiltonian _H<sub>M</sub>_ generally consists of non-commuting transverse field operators that drive transitions between these basis states. To initialise the standard unconstrained QAOA circuit into the maximal eigenstate of this mixer, a Hadamard gate is applied to each qubit of the state \(|\psi_\mathrm{init}\rangle=|0\rangle^{\otimes n}\). This creates the uniform superposition state \(|+\rangle^{\otimes n}\) across all possible computational basis states, with _n_ representing the total qubit count.

Subsequently, a depth-_p_ parameterised ansatz is constructed by applying _p_ layers of these alternating operators to yield the final quantum state before measurement:

\(|\psi_p(\gamma,\beta)\rangle=\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^pe^{-i\beta_\ell H_Me^{-i\gamma_\ell H_C}}\right)|+\rangle^{\otimes n},\) (1)

with continuous variational parameters \(\gamma=(\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_p)\) controlling the phase separation generated by the cost Hamiltonian, and \(\beta=(\beta_1,\dots,\beta_p)\) controlling the interference generated by the mixer Hamiltonian.

After the circuit is executed and measured, the expectation value of the cost Hamiltonian,

\(F_p(\gamma,\beta)=\langle\psi_p(\gamma,\beta)|H_C|\psi_p(\gamma,\beta)\rangle\) (2)

is estimated through repeated state preparations and projective measurements. This expectation value acts as a parameterised loss or reward landscape and is used by a classical optimiser in an outer-loop procedure to iteratively update \((\gamma,\beta)\). The objective is maximised or minimised depending on the specific formulation of the optimisation problem.

For a maximisation problem, given optimised parameters \((\gamma^\star,\beta^\star)\) returned by the classical routine, the algorithm’s performance is commonly evaluated using the approximation ratio,

\(\alpha_p=\frac{F_p(\gamma^\star,\beta^\star)}{C_\mathrm{max}},\) (3)

where _C_<sub>max</sub> is the maximum objective value attainable. While standard adiabatic computing requires infinitely slow evolution to guarantee optimality, it is theoretically established that as \(p\rightarrow\infty\), QAOA recovers the adiabatic guarantee and yields the optimal solution \[Farhi et al., 2014\]. However, for near-term implementation at any finite depth _p_, QAOA effectively reduces the computationally intractable search over an exponentially large space of 2_<sup>n</sup>_ discrete bit strings to the continuous, heuristic optimisation of only 2_p_ parameters. Because this parameter dimensionality depends strictly on the chosen circuit depth and remains entirely decoupled from the size of the underlying problem instance, QAOA serves as a highly scalable variational framework.
