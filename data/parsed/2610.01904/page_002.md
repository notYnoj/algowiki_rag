QAOA is specifically designed for discrete optimisation, a paradigm that encompasses many practical decision making tasks through quadratic unconstrained binary optimisation (QUBO) or Ising formulations [Lucas, 2014, Glover et al., 2022]. Such formulations are deeply intertwined with Boolean satisfiability and constraint logic, as they mathematically map rigid logical requirements into trainable energetic penalties. These formulations encompass canonical problem classes such as covering [Lucas, 2014], packing [Martello and Toth, 1987], assignment [Kuhn, 1955], scheduling [Lawler et al., 1993], and routing [Dantzig and Ramser, 1959]. Important practical applications explored in current QAOA literature include logistics [Xie et al., 2024, Ruan et al., 2020], finance [Venturelli and Kondratyev, 2019], and communications [Kim et al., 2021].

To achieve high quality solutions in these complex application domains, the underlying quantum circuit must possess sufficient representational capacity. In standard QAOA, this capacity is traditionally scaled by deepening the circuit architecture; specifically, increasing the number of alternating cost and mixer layers generally improves the expressive power of the circuit and can lead to better approximation quality. On NISQ hardware, however, this route is severely limited by noise accumulation due to the aforementioned hardware constraints, making high depth QAOA difficult to execute without the desired information being destroyed by noise [Blekos et al., 2024]. One prominent solution to this challenge is multi-angle QAOA (ma-QAOA) [Herrman et al., 2022]. By assigning an independent variational parameter to each individual quantum gate, the required circuit depth can be substantially reduced for a given level of approximation quality. This architectural adjustment effectively shifts the computational burden from the fragile quantum hardware to the classical parameter optimisation routine. However, the resulting increase in parameter dimensionality drastically enlarges the optimisation landscape, making the classical search for an optimal set of parameters more difficult and increasingly susceptible to classical inefficiencies.

**Algorithm Concept.** To efficiently optimise the enlarged ma-QAOA parameter space, this paper proposes a cyclic coordinate descent strategy akin to sequential minimal optimisation and Roto- solve [Nakanishi et al., 2020, Ostaszewski et al., 2021]. This approach explicitly leverages the analytical properties of the quantum loss landscape to bypass noisy gradient estimations in high dimensions. Since each ma-QAOA angle is associated with a single rotation gate, the one dimensional objective is purely sinusoidal [Wierichs et al., 2022], enabling analytical coordinate updates from just a few expectation value evaluations.

**Contributions.** This paper investigates cyclic coordinate descent as a training method for ma-QAOA, offering a practical pathway to scale techniques utilising PQCs for complex combinatorial tasks. Although we apply this cyclic coordinate descent specifically to ma-QAOA, the theoretical foundation of the method, namely that the expectation value with respect to a chosen parameter can be analytically expressed as a finite Fourier series, is broadly applicable for optimising parameters across general tasks and problems solved via PQCs. We first explain why the cyclic coordinate descent is particularly compatible with ma-QAOA, highlighting how it mitigates the curse of dimensionality introduced by independent variational angles. We then compare its empirical performance against standard optimisation methods commonly used for ma-QAOA and broader PQC training. Finally, using the maximum independent set problem, a canonical NP hard task fundamentally linked to constrained satisfiability formulations, as a constrained combinatorial optimisation benchmark, we demonstrate that the proposed method achieves significantly more efficient parameter optimisation relative to the baselines considered.

### 2.1 Overview of QAOA
