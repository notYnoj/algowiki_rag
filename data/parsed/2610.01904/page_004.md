A common approach is to absorb the constraints into the objective using penalty terms, thereby converting the problem into an unconstrained QUBO or Ising formulation \[Blekos et al., 2024, Lucas, 2014, Glover et al., 2022, Hadfield et al., 2017\]. For a maximisation problem, this can be written as

\(C_\mathrm{pen}(x)=C(x)-\lambda V(x),\) (4)

where _V(x_)=0 for feasible strings and _V(x)\>_0 otherwise. This method is simple and compatible with standard QAOA, but its performance depends strongly on the penalty weight \(\lambda\): weak penalties may favour infeasible high-value states, while overly large penalties can dominate the original objective and complicate optimisation. Alternatively, penalty-free approaches enforce feasibility by construction, such as in constrained QAOA or the quantum alternating operator ansatz \[Hadfield et al., 2017, 2019\]. In this setting, the initial state is chosen from F, and the mixer is designed to map feasible states only to feasible states. Consequently, unlike the standard QAOA initialisation, hard-constrained variants do not generally apply Hadamard gates to all qubits at the start of the circuit because applying \(H^{\otimes n}\) would populate infeasible bitstrings. Examples include _XY_ -type mixers for cardinality constraints and problem-specific controlled mixers for independent-set problems \[Wang et al., 2020, Saleem, 2020, Fuchs et al., 2022, Bärtschi and Eidenbenz, 2020, Xie et al., 2024\]. Such mixers avoid penalty tuning and ensure feasible samples, but they require problem-dependent circuit design and may increase depth \[Bucher et al., 2025\].

## 3 Coordinate Descent for Multi-Angle QAOA

### 3.1 Overview of Multi-Angle QAOA

In standard QAOA, each layer is controlled by a single cost angle and a single mixer angle, shared across all corresponding gates in that layer. By contrast, ma-QAOA assigns independent variational angles to the individual gates in cost and mixer blocks \[Herrman et al., 2022\]. For instance, in the MaxCut problem on a graph with _n_=|_V_| vertices and _m_=|_E_| edges, the cost and mixer blocks in each layer require _m_ and _n_ gates, respectively. Correspondingly, the total number of parameters increases from 2_p_ in standard QAOA to (_m+n)p_ in ma-QAOA. Standard QAOA thus emerges as a specific instance of ma-QAOA where the parameters remain uniform across each layer. Because it strictly generalises the standard approach, ma-QAOA guarantees that its optimal objective value at any given depth _p_ is at least as good as that of standard QAOA \[Herrman et al., 2022\]. Empirical studies have shown that ma-QAOA can match or exceed standard-QAOA approximation ratios while using substantially fewer layers on a range of benchmark instances \[Herrman et al., 2022, Shi et al., 2022, Gaidai and Herrman, 2024\]. While ma-QAOA’s flexibility is ideal for depth-limited NISQ devices, it severely burdens classical optimisation due to the increased number of parameters. To achieve real-world performance gains on near-term hardware, it is essential to develop efficient strategies to handle this expanded parameter space.

### 3.2 Overview of Coordinate Descent Method

For a broad class of PQCs, suppose all parameters except one free parameter \(\theta_\mathrm{free}\) are held fixed, and that \(\theta_\mathrm{free}\) enters the circuit through Pauli rotations of the form \(U(\theta_\mathrm{free})=e^{-i\theta_\mathrm{free}P/2}\), where _P_<sup>2</sup>=_I_. Then, the expectation value of any observable, viewed as a function of \(\theta_\mathrm{free}\), admits a finite Fourier series representation whose degree scales linearly with the number of gates controlled by \(\theta_\mathrm{free}\) \[Nakanishi et al., 2020, Ostaszewski et al., 2021, Wierichs et al., 2022\]. This structure makes coordinate descent training particularly natural for ma-QAOA, since each variational parameter is associated with a single rotation gate. Consequently, the corresponding one-dimensional objective reduces to a single-frequency sinusoid, \(f(\theta_\mathrm{free})=A\mathrm{sin}(\theta_\mathrm{free}+\phi)+C\), for constants _A_, \(\phi\), and _C_. This sinusoidal form can be reconstructed from only three expectation-value evaluations, allowing the exact coordinate-wise extremum to be determined analytically. The full training procedure cyclically sweeps through all _D_ ma-QAOA parameters, updating one coordinate at a time until either a maximum number of sweeps is reached or the improvement falls below a prescribed tolerance. A direct implementation requires 3_D_ expectation-value evaluations per sweep. By reusing the current objective value after each coordinate update, this cost can be reduced to 2_D_+1 evaluations for the first sweep and 2_D_ evaluations for each subsequent sweep. As a gradient-free approach, the method requires no hyperparameters to govern search dynamics; the only required parameters define the stopping criteria.
