## 3.2. The lattice of T

In this section we take a detour to understanding the structure of T in terms of the integer span of the edges of T . (We shall make this precise via linear maps.) Whilst, from what follows in this section, the only result we shall require for the proof of Theorem 1.2 is the statement of Proposition 3.16 (which shall be used in Chapter 4), we take our time to motivate and formally derive the statement. Furthermore, we shall take the time to prove Proposition 3.17, the converse of Proposition 3.16 which, but defer this to Section 4.3. Though not required to prove Theorem 1.2, Proposition 3.17 is important for our proof of Theorem 1.3.

For a labelled (hyper)graph, _H_, and for \(i\ge j\ge 0\), the Z_-linear boundary_ / _shadow maps_ \(\partial_j:\mathbb{Z}^{K_i(H)}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{K_j(H)}\) are defined by \(\partial_j(J)_t=\sum_{t\subset u\in K_i(H)}J_u\). Informally, for \(J\in\mathbb{Z}^{K_i(H)}\), a vector indexed by all copies of _K<sub>i</sub>_ in _H_ assigning a weight to each copy, \(\partial_j(J)\) is the vector indexed by all copies of _K<sub>j</sub>_ in _H_, where the weight corresponding to a copy _K_ of _K<sub>j</sub>_ is given by the sum of the weights indexed by the copies of _K<sub>i</sub>_ in _J_ which contain _K_. For our purposes we shall be considering a vector indexed by edges \(\Phi\in\mathbb{Z}^{E(\mathcal{T})}\), and its vertex shadow \(\partial\Phi:=\partial_1\Phi\), the vector indexed by the vertices of T , such that \(v_i^J\) is the weight given to vertex _i<sup>J</sup>_ by the weights on the edges in \(\Phi\) that contain _i<sup>J</sup>_. Here, we note that we associate _i<sup>J</sup>_ both to the vertex in part _J_ which has numerical label _i_, and to the 4_n_-dimensional unit vector _e<sub>i<sup>J</sup></sub>_ which has a 1 at the place indexed by vertex _i<sup>J</sup>_, and 0s elsewhere.

We also extend this definition to identify multisets containing elements from a set _S_, where each element has a sign attached (_intsets_), with vectors \(\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^S\). Here, the value _v<sub>s</sub>_ in the coordinate indexed by \(s\in S\) corresponds to _s_ appearing in _S_, |_v<sub>s</sub>_| times, and the sign attached to _s_ is the same as the sign of _v<sub>s</sub>_.

Identifying the edges _e_ with their corresponding vector indexed by edges, we can describe _E_(T) as a set of indicator vectors \(\{\unknown_e\}_{e\in E(\mathcal{T})}\), where (\(\unknown_e\))_<sub>f</sub>_=1 if _e=f_ and 0 otherwise. Then \(\partial\unknown_e\) is a vector indexed by vertices with four 1 entries, and all other entries 0. Letting \(\mathbf{v}_\mathbf{e}:=\partial\unknown_e\), we write **v<sub>e</sub>**=(_v<sup>X</sup>,v<sup>Y</sup>,v_<sup>_X+Y_</sup>_,v_<sup>_X-Y_</sup>), so that every part has exactly one 1, and wherever \(v_i^X=1\) and \(v_j^Y=1\), we know that \(v_{i+j\mathrm{mod}n}^{X+Y}=1\) and \(v_{i-j\mathrm{mod}n}^{X-Y}=1\). Then we define the _lattice_, L(T), to be the integer span of \(\{\mathbf{v}_\mathbf{e}:e\in E(\mathcal{T})\}\).

**3.2.1. Modular notation.** Since we are working in the toroidal problem, wherever we refer to sums and differences, these will generally be considered mod _n_, unless stated otherwise. We say that \(a\pm b\) or _ab wrap around_ if \(a\pm b\neq a\pm b\mathrm{mod}n\), or \(ab\neq ab\mathrm{mod}n\), respectively. This is both in the context of an edge, and for a vector of weights \(\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T})\). If an integer should be read without modular arithmetic, it should be clear from the context, or will be marked with a superscript \(\circ\). (For example, when considering coordinates _a_, _b_ and _c_, _a+b=c_ will mean that _a+b=c_ mod _n_, and \(x=c^\circ\) will mean that _x_ is the representative of the congruence class containing _c_.) This will be important in Section 4.3, where we are interested in representatives which are powers of two.
