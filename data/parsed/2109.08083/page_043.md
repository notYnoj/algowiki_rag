<tbc>**The absorber**

We say that \(L^*\subseteq V(\mathcal{T})\) is a _qualifying leave_ if _L_<sup>∗</sup> satisfies the following properties:

(1) the support vector **v<sub>L<sup>∗</sup></sub>** of _L_<sup>∗</sup> satisfies \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{L}^*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T})\),<tbc>

(2) \(L^*\subseteq I_{n^{10-5}}\),<tbc>

(3) \(|L^*|\le p_Ln^{10-5}\), recalling _p<sub>L</sub>_ defined in Definition 3.5,<tbc>

(4) \(|V_O^{X+Y}(L^*)|=|V_O^{X-Y}(L^*)|\),

The aim of this chapter is to build an absorber _A_<sup>∗</sup> that can absorb any qualifying leave _L_<sup>∗</sup>. Formally, in our context, we say that \(A^*\subseteq V(\mathcal{T})\) is an _absorber_ for T if T\[_A_<sup>∗</sup>\] contains a perfect matching, and for every qualifying leave _L_<sup>∗</sup> we have that \(\mathcal{T}[A^*\cup L^*]\) contains a perfect matching. By slight abuse of notation we may think of an absorber _A_<sup>∗</sup> both as a vertex subset of T (as described in the definition) and also as the sub(hyper)graph of T induced by this set _A_<sup>∗</sup> of vertices. Wherever this distinction is important, it will be clear from the context. Now, supposing that we could find a set of disjoint edges \(\Phi^+\) such that \(L^*\subseteq\partial\Phi^+\), and a set of disjoint edges \(\Phi^-\) (not disjoint from \(\Phi^+\)) such that for the signed multi-set \(\Phi=\Phi^+\cup\Phi^-\), we have that \(\partial\Phi=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{L}^*}\). If \(\partial\Phi^-\subseteq A^*\) and \(A^*\backslash\partial\Phi^-\) has a perfect matching, then taking the perfect matching in \(A^*\backslash\partial\Phi^-\) along with \(\Phi^+\) yields a perfect matching for \(\mathcal{T}[A^*\cup L^*]\). Before finding an absorber _A_<sup>∗</sup> and setting \(\Phi^-\) with these properties, we first relax to finding an integral solution on T . That is, sets \(\Phi^-\) and \(\Phi^+\) may each consist of a multi-set of edges from T such that \(\partial\Phi=\mathbf{v}_\mathbf{L}\). In this way we have an _integral decomposition_ of _L_<sup>∗</sup> (we are able to describe _L_<sup>∗</sup> as the difference of two collections of edges in T). Key to our approach is that the number of edges used in \(\Phi\) is not too large (where what constitutes ‘not too large’ will become clear as we describe our process to obtain \(\Phi\)). On finding \(\Phi\) which is sufficiently small, we can show that we may modify \(\Phi\) so that \(\Phi^+\) and \(\Phi^-\) are both matchings in T , and still sufficiently small, such that \(\Phi\in\{-1,0,1\}^{E(\mathcal{T})}\). Details of this part of the strategy are covered in Section 4.1. From this step we wish to complete the process by modifying \(\Phi^+\) and \(\Phi^-\) once again so that \(\partial\Phi^-\subseteq A^*\) and \(A^*\backslash\partial\Phi^-\) has a perfect matching, all the time maintaining that \(\partial\Phi=\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{L}^*}\). In this way we have that _A_<sup>∗</sup> is an absorber for _L_<sup>∗</sup>, as required. This is much like the strategy of _hole_ in \[**31**\]. Our strategy for building _A_<sup>∗</sup>, detailed in Section 4.2, will start by considering a random subset of vertices taken from within a fixed subset of _V_(T) such that each vertex is included independently with some probability _p<sub>A</sub>_. Then we’ll show that with high probability such a set satisfies many properties we require, and we will end the process by fixing such a set and modifying it slightly to ensure that it maintains all the key properties we will have shown, but additionally has a perfect matching.
