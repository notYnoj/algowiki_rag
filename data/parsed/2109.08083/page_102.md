As such, our algorithm to progress through the vortex of nested subgraphs will ensure that such edges are not picked up in the matching that moves the process from _I<sub>t<sub>i</sub></sub>_ to _I_<sub>_t<sub>i</sub>_+1</sub>. To deal with this more concisely, we introduce the notion of _reachability_. This notion is more complex than just covering whether a vertex is in an edge that might be useful at a particular step of the vortex, and extends to vertices that might have degree-type properties affected by edges that can be useful at a particular step.

Definition 6.18 (Reachable vertices and edges). We say that a vertex _v_ is _i_-reachable if \(v\in H_{1*}[I_{t_i}]\) and there exist \(e,f\in H_{1*}[I_{t_i}]\) such that \(e\cap I_{t_i}\backslash I_{t_{i+1}}\neq\emptyset\), \(f\cap e\neq\emptyset\) and \(f\unknown v\). Similarly we say that an edge _f_ is _i_-reachable, if \(f\in H_{1*}[I_{t_i}]\) and there exists \(e\in H_{1*}[I_{t_i}]\) such that \(e\cap I_{t_i}\backslash I_{t_{i+1}}\neq\emptyset\) and \(f\cap e\neq\emptyset\).

That is, a vertex _v_ is _i_-reachable if and only if it is feasible that the process to go from _I<sub>t<sub>i</sub></sub>_ to _I_<sub>_t<sub>i</sub>_+1</sub> might affect the degree of _v_ and an edge _f_ is _i_-reachable if and only if matching edges assigned in the process to go from _I<sub>t<sub>i</sub></sub>_ to _I_<sub>_t<sub>i</sub>_+1</sub> might then mean that _f_ is not present in the subgraph induced on vertices remaining once the vertices of the matching are removed. In particular, a vertex _v_ is _i_-reachable if and only if _v_ is in an _i_-reachable edge. Note that if _H_<sub>1∗</sub>\[_I<sub>t<sub>i</sub></sub>_\] has depth _j_, then every vertex in _K_<sub>_j_+2</sub> is _not i_-reachable. That is, all edges sharing a vertex with a vertex in _K_<sub>_j_+2</sub> are contained in _K_<sub>_j_+1</sub>, and since _H_<sub>1∗</sub>\[_I<sub>t<sub>i</sub></sub>_\] has depth _j_, every edge containing a vertex in _I<sub>t<sub>i</sub></sub>_\\_I_<sub>_t<sub>i</sub>_+1</sub> must have a vertex in _K_<sub>_j_-1</sub>\\_K<sub>j</sub>_, and so only reaches into _K<sub>j</sub>_\\_K_<sub>_j_+1</sub>. It follows that edges that are _i_-reachable have all vertices outside _K_<sub>_j_+2</sub>.

Extending the notion of reachability further, we define _reachable sets_. We want to consider sets which might lose vertices in step _i_ of the iterative matching process. Additionally we define _open and closed i-reachable pairs_ where the motivation here is to distinguish pairs (_v,S_) where step _i_ of the iterative matching process will affect the degree-type properties of _v_ relating to _H<sub>i</sub>_ and _S_.

Definition 6.19 (Reachable sets). Given that _H_<sub>1∗</sub>\[_I<sub>t<sub>i</sub></sub>_\] has depth _j_, we say that a subset \(S\subseteq V(\mathcal{T})\) is _i-reachable_ if \(S\subseteq I_{t_i}\) is _i_-valid and \(S\subseteq K_{j-1}\backslash K_{j+1}\). We also say that (_v,S_) is a _closed i-reachable pair_ if (_v,S_) is a closed _i_-valid pair, such that _v_ is _i_-reachable, \(S\subseteq I_{t_i}\) and, if _v_ has depth _j_ or _j_+1, then \(S\cap(I_{t_i}\backslash K_{j+1})\neq\emptyset\), and if _v_ has depth _j_+2 then \(S\cap(K_j\backslash K_{j+1})\neq\emptyset\). Similarly, we say that (_v,S_) is an _open i-reachable pair_ if (_v,S_) is an open _i_-valid pair, such that _v_ is _i_-reachable, \(\bigcup E_{H_{1*}}(v,S,*,*)\subseteq I_{t_i}\) and, if _v_ has depth _j_ or _j_+1, then \((\bigcup E_{H_{1*}}(v,S,*,*))\cap(I_{t_i}\backslash K_{j+1})\neq\emptyset\), and if _v_ has depth _j_+2, then \((\bigcup E_{H_{1*}}(v,S,*,*))\cap(K_j\backslash K_{j+1})\neq\emptyset\).

Note that for most values of \(i\in[c_h]\), what is _i_-reachable is a subset of what is (_i_-1)-reachable; this only fails when T\[_I_<sub>_t<sub>i</sub>_-1</sub>\] has depth _j_, and T\[_I<sub>t<sub>i</sub></sub>_\] has depth _j_+1, in which case _i_-reachability extends to vertices at a depth one below that to which (_i_-1)-reachability extends.
