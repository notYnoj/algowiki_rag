As part of the strategy, we obtain the almost-perfect fractional matching _w_<sub>_i_+1</sub> from _w<sub>i</sub>_ for every \(i\neq 1\). The weight shuffle intervenes in this process once we have (_H_<sub>1</sub>_,w_<sub>1</sub>) and turns the almost-perfect fractional matching _w_<sub>1</sub> for _H_<sub>1</sub> into another almost- perfect fractional matching _w_<sub>1∗</sub> by transferring weight between edges via zero-sum configurations of specific forms as described in detail in Section 3.3.2. The intention of the weight shuffle is to shift weight around in such a way that the vertices closer to the centre of T do not get used too early on in the process. In particular, as we obtain disjoint edges to cover vertices remaining in _I<sub>t<sub>i</sub></sub>_\\_I_<sub>_t<sub>i</sub>_+1</sub> for each subgraph _H<sub>i</sub>_, if we use too many vertices from _I<sub>t<sub>j</sub></sub>_ where \(j\gg i\), then by the time we reach _I<sub>t<sub>j</sub></sub>_ we may have that _H<sub>j</sub>_ is too sparse to cover vertices in _I<sub>t<sub>j</sub></sub>_\\_I_<sub>_t<sub>j</sub>_+1</sub> by disjoint edges, and in this case the process would fail. The weight shuffle helps to avoid this as follows. It ensures that in our new weighting, _w_<sub>1∗</sub>, every bad edge _e_ satisfies _w_<sub>1∗</sub>(_e_)=0. This means that when covering vertices in an outer interval, we can never remove vertices too close to the centre of T at the same time. We start by ensuring that all of the weight on _c<sub>g</sub>_-bad edges is shifted to edges of type \((\alpha,\beta,0)_{c_g}\) and \((0,\beta,\gamma)_{c_g}\). We define _w_<sub>1</sub>=_w_<sub>(1_,c<sub>g</sub>_)</sub> and let _w_<sub>(1_,i_)</sub> denote the current weighting on _H_<sub>1</sub> once the reweighting of _j_-bad edges for all _j\>i_ has taken place. We shall use _i_-legal zero-sum configurations to reduce the weight on the _i_-bad edges to 0 by transferring the weight to the edges with the opposite sign in such configurations. By definition of an _i_-legal zero-sum configuration, this will subsequently shift weight from, in addition to _i_-bad edges, edges of type (0,1,3)_<sub>i</sub>_, and shift the weight onto edges of type \((\alpha,\beta,0)_i\) where \(\alpha\neq 0\), (0,0,4)_<sub>i</sub>_ and (0,1,3)_<sub>i</sub>_. Then with the updated weighting _w_<sub>(1_,i_-1)</sub>, we repeat the process to reduce the weight on all (_i_-1)-bad edges to 0, and continue until every edge _e_ in _H_<sub>1</sub> for which there exists _l_ such that _e_ contains one vertex in _K<sub>l</sub>_ and another in _I_<sub>_t_<sub>1</sub></sub>\\_K_<sub>_l_-1</sub> has _w_<sub>1∗</sub>(_e_)=0, and additionally, any edge _e_ with a vertex inside _K_<sub>1</sub> and a vertex outside _J_<sub>1</sub> also has _w_<sub>1∗</sub>(_e_)=0. The details of the running of this process follow in Section 6.2.

**6.1.2. Organisation.** In Section 6.1.3, we present the key tool that we use to do the ‘almost-cover’ at each step of the iterative matching process, a tool developed from a result of Ehard, Glock, Joos \[**18**\] which enables us at each step _i_, when we have reached _H<sub>i</sub>_, to remove a matching covering most of the vertices remaining in _I<sub>t<sub>i</sub></sub>_\\_I_<sub>_t<sub>i</sub>_+1</sub> in such a way as to ensure that the remaining graph has ‘nice’ random-like properties. In Section 6.2 we describe the process to reach _w_<sub>1∗</sub> and properties of _w_<sub>1∗</sub> given (_H_<sub>1</sub>_,w_<sub>1</sub>) as in Theorem 6.1. In Section 6.3 we describe the details of how we shall use the random matching tool in our setting and introduce new notions of reachability and graph permissibility. In Section 6.4 we give details that show a general step of the process to get from (_H<sub>i</sub>,w<sub>i</sub>_) to (_H_<sub>_i_+1</sub>_,w_<sub>_i_+1</sub>) for some \(i\in[c_h]\) and show that the process can continue to reach _L_<sup>∗</sup>. Finally, in Section 6.5, we show that a process very similar to that described in Section 6.4, but adjusted for additional parity constraints, allows us to go from _H_ to (_H_<sub>1</sub>_,w_<sub>1</sub>) as in Theorem 6.1.
