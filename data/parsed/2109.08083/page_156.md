<tbc>Thus, to prove the theorem, it remains to show that T<sup>∗</sup> has at least \(\big((1+o(1))\frac{n}{e^3}\big)^n\) perfect matchings.

The proof of this is exactly the proof of the lower bound for Theorem 1.2, that when \(n\equiv 1,5\mathrm{mod}6\), T(_n_) has at least \(\big((1+o(1))\frac{n}{e^3}\big)^n\) perfect matchings, but starting from T<sup>∗</sup>(_n_) in place of T(_n_). Note that the constant number of vertices removed to obtain T<sup>∗</sup> from T neither affect parity issues, nor can they have a significant effect on the other properties we track throughout the process. The only aspect of the proof that needs reverifying for T<sup>∗</sup>(_n_) in place of T(_n_) is that \(\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^*)\). In particular, this is the key element that ensures that _L_<sup>∗</sup>, what is left to be absorbed at the end of the process is a qualifying leave for the absorber _A_<sup>∗</sup> taken out at the beginning. Let **v**<sub>**L**<sup>∗</sup></sub> be the support vector of _L_<sup>∗</sup>. Then we require that \(\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{L}^*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^*)\) to ensure that _L_<sup>∗</sup> is a qualifying leave. Since _L_<sup>∗</sup> is obtained by removing a matching from T<sup>∗</sup>(_n_), it follows that showing that \(\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^*)\) will complete the proof.

By Lemma 3.18 we have that the vector **v** corresponding to weight 1 on all vertices in _V_(T)\\_W_ and weight 0 on vertices in _W_ satisfies \(\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T})\). (This is seen simply by verifying that **v** satisfies (i)-(iv) when _n_ is odd and 3|_n_, and (a)-(d) when _n_ is even. We give more details of these calculations below the proof.) This in fact also implies that \(\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^*)\) as follows. Consider an integer collection of edges \(E_1\subseteq\mathcal{T}\) whose vertex shadow yields the vector corresponding to **v**. If \(e\cap W=\emptyset\) for every \(e\in E_1\) then \(E_1\subseteq\mathcal{T}^*\) so \(\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^*)\). Supposing this is not the case, let \(V^*(E_1):=\{v_1,\dots,v_\chi\}\) be an enumeration of the vertices with multiplicity and sign \(v\in W\) such that there exists \(e\in E_1\) with \(e\unknown v\). Without loss of generality, suppose that _v_<sub>1</sub> appears in an edge \(e\in E_1\) with negative sign. Since we know that _v_<sub>1</sub> has total weight 0 we may also choose another edge \(e'\in E_1\) with positive sign such that \(v_1\in e'\). Then we form a zero-sum configuration _z_ containing _e_ with positive sign and \(e'\) with negative sign and only other vertices in _V_(T<sup>∗</sup>). (Since we have one degree of freedom left to dictate _z_ and only a constant number of vertices - those in _W_ - to avoid, this is possible.) Updating _E_<sub>1</sub> to _E_<sub>2</sub> by adding _z_ removes the edges \(e,e'\) from _E_<sub>1</sub> and adds edges only using new vertices outside _W_. Thus we have |_V_<sup>∗</sup>(_E_<sub>2</sub>)|_\<|V_<sup>∗</sup>(_E_<sub>1</sub>)|. Repeating the process we eventually obtain _E_<sup>∗</sup> such that \(V^*(E^*)=\emptyset\). Then _E_<sup>∗</sup> yields \(\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T}^*)\), as required. □

To see that our vector **v** with a 1 on every element in _V_(T<sup>∗</sup>)\\_W_ and 0 on every element in _W_ is in L(T) simply requires checking (i)-(iv) or (a)-(d) of Lemma 3.18. It is easy to verify (i)-(iii) and (a)-(c). We give some intermediate details of the calculations for (_iv_) and (_d_) here for transparency.

When _n_ is odd (and 3|_n_), to satisfy (iv) in Lemma 3.18 reduces, after cancelling and regrouping terms, to showing that<tbc>

\(\frac{2n^2}{3}+n+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{2n}{3}+\frac{2}{3}\sum_{i\in[3]}(a_i+b_i+c_i-d_i)\in\mathbb{Z}.\)

<tbc>Then since 3|_n_, this reduces to having

\(1+2\sum_{i\in[3]}(a_i+b_i+c_i-d_i)\in 3\mathbb{Z},\)

which is the requirement given in the proof of Theorem 1.3.
