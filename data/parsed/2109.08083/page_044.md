## 4.1. Finding an integral decomposition for _L_<sup>∗</sup>

We aim to find a vector of edges \(\Phi\in\mathbb{Z}^{E(\mathcal{T})}\) such that \(\partial\Phi-L^*=\mathbf{0}\).

Lemma 4.1. _For any qualifying leave L_<sup>∗</sup> _there exists_ \(\Phi\in\mathbb{Z}^{E(\mathcal{T})}\) _such that every edge in_ \(\Phi\) _is contained in_ \(I'_{n^{10-5}}\), \(|\Phi|=O\left(p_Ln^{2.6\times 10^{-5}}\right)\)_, and_ \(\mathbf{v}=\partial\Phi-L^*=\mathbf{0}\).

Whilst we shall eventually want \(\Phi\in\{-1,0,1\}^{E(\mathcal{T})}\), the first key step to doing this is to find a bounded integral decomposition. That is, we shall do this by first finding \(\Phi\in\mathbb{Z}^{E(\mathcal{T})}\), such that \(\partial\Phi-L^*=\mathbf{0}\) and \(|\Phi|:=\sum_{e\in E(\mathcal{T})}|\Phi_e|=o(n)\). We call \(|\Phi|\) the _size of_ \(\Phi\). For our setting of building the absorber we’ll need to be more precise about \(\Phi\), both in the types of edges we are allowed to add to \(\Phi\) and the size of \(\Phi\) - we’ll show that \(|\Phi|=O(n^{2.6\times 10^{-5}})\). Let \(\mathbf{v}:=\partial\Phi-L^*\). We view \(\Phi\) dynamically - we are continually adding signed edges to an initially empty set \(\Phi\) with the goal to stop when we find \(\Phi\) such that \(\mathbf{v}:=\partial\Phi-L^*=\mathbf{0}\), thus finding the required integral decomposition. Our strategy relies on first covering _L_<sup>∗</sup> by edges (and adding these to \(\Phi\)) such that the support of **v** is pushed closer and closer to the centre of T . Indeed, we show that we may push the support so that it is contained only on coordinates in {-1,0,1}. We’ll then show that we can reduce such a support to **0**. Throughout this section we do not repeat the running assumptions when stating the following propositions.

From now on for a coordinate _i_ in _V<sup>J</sup>_ with weight \(v_i^J\neq 0\) in **v**, we call _i<sup>J</sup>_ with weight \(\pm 1\) (according to whether \(v_i^J\) is positive or negative) a _unit_ of **v**. In this way, for each _i_ and _J_, \(v_i^J\) contributes \(|v_i^J|\) units to **v**. For any multi-subset of units _U_, write |_U_| to be the size of the multi-set, and \(\sum_{i\in U}v_i\) when \(i\in U\) refers to the set of vertices \(U\subseteq V\) , so that we may recognise _U_ both as a multi-set of individual units, and as a set of the vertices with non-zero weight in **v**. For our strategy to work, we shall wish to add edges to \(\Phi\) to ensure \(\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{L}_2^{X,Y}(\mathcal{T})\), and we refer to this as _zero- summing_ the support of **v**. In addition, we shall wish to avoid any wrap-around edges in building \(\Phi\), as we want to retain some parity properties that we inherit from _L_<sup>∗</sup>. In particular, if the support is contained in a bounded interval \[-_n_<sub>0</sub>_,n_<sub>0</sub>\], for some _n_<sub>0</sub>_\<n/_4, then in order to ensure that we stay within this interval when we zero-sum the support, we must add edges dictated by pairing up odd vertices in _V_ <sup>_X+Y_</sup> with odd vertices in _V_ <sup>_X-Y_</sup> and similarly even with even. (When _n_ is even the parity issue is not a concern, and we choose the non-wrap around edge dictated by such a pairing in order to stay within this interval when zero-summing the support.) Let \(U_{O,\pm}^J(\mathbf{v})\) be the multi-set of units of odd parity with positive or negative weight in _V<sup>J</sup>_ from **v** respectively, where \(J\in\{X,Y,X+Y,X-Y\}\). Similarly, define \(U_{E,\pm}^J(\mathbf{v})\) to be the units of even parity with positive or negative weight in _V<sup>J</sup>_. When **v** is clear from the context, we just write \(U_{O/E,\pm}^J\). We claim the following:

Proposition 4.2. _Let_ \(\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{T})\) _be such that_<tbc>

\(|U_{O,+}^{X+Y}(\mathbf{u})|-|U_{O,+}^{X-Y}(\mathbf{u})|=|U_{O,-}^{X+Y}(\mathbf{u})|-|U_{O,-}^{X-Y}(\mathbf{u})|,\)

<tbc>_and_

\(|U_{E,+}^{X+Y}(\mathbf{u})|-|U_{E,+}^{X-Y}(\mathbf{u})|=|U_{E,-}^{X+Y}(\mathbf{u})|-|U_{E,-}^{X-Y}(\mathbf{u})|.\)

_Then there exists a signed multi-set of edges_ \(\Phi'\)_, all of which are not wrap around edges, such that_ \(\mathbf{u}':=\partial\Phi'+\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{L}_2^{X,Y}(\mathcal{T})\).
