In this section, we will briefly highlight that minimising the number of blocking deviators remains NP-hard. However, we will also show how to extend the FPT algorithms to this setting. Let Deviator-BA-sri and Deviator-BA-Max-sri denote versions of Deviator-sri and Deviator-Max-sri that minimise \(|\{a_i\in D|bp_{a_i}(M)\neq\varnothing\}|\) among all matchings and all maximum-cardinality matchings, respectively. Furthermore, let _k_-Deviator-BA-sri and _k_-Deviator-BA-Max-sri denote the problems of computing a matching and a maximum- cardinality matching admitting at most _k_ blocking agents in _D_, if they exist, respectively. Also, let 0-Deviator-BA-sri-Dec and 0-Deviator-BA-Perfect-smi-Dec denote the restricted problems of deciding whether such a solution exists when _k_=0 and, in the smi case, where maximum-cardinality matchings are perfect, and the instance is bipartite. We can immediately conclude the following.

**Theorem 5.1.** 0-Deviator-BA-sri-Dec _is_ NP-complete_, regardless of whether preference lists are of length at most 5 or complete._ 0-Deviator-BA-Perfect-smi-Dec _is_ NP-complete_, even if all preference lists are of length at most 3 and all deviators are on the same side._

_Proof._ Clearly, both problems are in NP: we can easily check a possible solution _M_ by iterating through the set of deviator agents and checking that none of them block with any agent that they prefer to their partner in _M_ (which might be none). For the Perfect-smi case, we furthermore need to check that every agent in the instance has a partner in _M_, which can be done efficiently by iterating through the set of agents once.

Now, let \(I=(A,\succ)\) be an sri instance and let \(D\subseteq A\). Then there exists a matching \(M\in\mathcal{M}\) such that \(|\{a_i\in D|bp_{a_i}(M)\neq\varnothing\}|=0\) if and only if \(\bigcup_{a_i\in D}bp_{a_i}(M)=\varnothing\). Hence, (_I,D_) is a yes-instance to 0-Deviator-BA-sri-Dec if and only if it is a yes-instance to 0- Deviator-sri-Dec, which we showed to be NP-complete regardless of whether preference lists are of length at most 5 or complete in Theorem 3.5.

Similarly, let \(I=(A,\succ)\) be an smi instance and let \(D\subseteq A\). Then there exists a matching \(M\in\mathcal{M}^+\) such that \(|\{a_i\in D|bp_{a_i}(M)\neq\varnothing\}|=0\) if and only if \(\bigcup_{a_i\in D}bp_{a_i}(M)=\varnothing\). Hence, (_I,D_) is a yes-instance to 0-Deviator-BA-Perfect-smi-Dec if and only if it is a yes-instance to 0-Deviator-Perfect-smi-Dec, which we showed to be NP-complete even in the case where preference lists are of length at most 3 and all deviators are on the same side in Theorem 3.1.

Thus, we can immediately conclude the following intractability results for our desired optimisation problems.

**Corollary 5.2.** Deviator-BA-sri _and_ Deviator-BA-Max-smi _are_ NP-hard _and are not in_ XP _(unless_ P=NP_) with respect to optimum values_ \(\kappa=min_{M\in\mathcal{M}}|\{a_i\in D|bp_{a_i}(M)\neq\varnothing\}|\) _and_ \(\kappa=min_{M\in\mathcal{M}^+}|\{a_i\in D|bp_{a_i}(M)\neq\varnothing\}|\)_, respectively._
