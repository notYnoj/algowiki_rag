<tbc>to the end of the preference list according to the global complete ranking \(\succ_G\) on the agents in \(A'\) such that \(a_1\succ_Ga_1^1\succ_G\cdots\succ_Ga_1^k\succ_Ga_2\succ_Ga_2^1\succ_G\cdots\succ_Ga_2^k\succ_Ga_3\succ_Ga_3^1\succ_G\cdots\succ_Ga_n^k\).

To illustrate this, consider the following example with three agents _A_={_a_<sub>1</sub>_,a_<sub>2</sub>_,a_<sub>3</sub>}, and let _k_=1. Then, because _k_=1, we first create three dummy agents, one dummy agent per original agent in _A_), and let \(A'=A\cup\{a_1^1,a_2^1,a_3^1\}\). The global ranking \(\succ_G\) is \(a_1\succ_Ga_1^1\succ_Ga_2\succ_Ga_2^1\succ_Ga_3\succ_Ga_3^1\). Now suppose _a_<sub>1</sub>’s preferences \(\succ_{a_1}\) (in \(\succ\)) consist of just _a_<sub>3</sub>. Then our final construction of \(I'\) results in the preference list \(\succ'_{a_1}\) (in \(\succ'\)) such that \(a_3\succ'_{a_1}a_1^1\succ'_{a_1}a_2\succ'_{a_1}a_2^1\succ'_{a_1}a_3^1\). If _a_<sub>1</sub>’s preferences \(\succ_{a_1}\) (in \(\succ\)) were \(a_3\succ_{a_1}a_2\) instead, then our final construction of \(I'\) would result in the preference list \(\succ'_{a_1}\) (in \(\succ'\)) such that \(a_3\succ'_{a_1}a_2\succ'_{a_1}a_1^1\succ'_{a_1}a_2^1\succ'_{a_1}a_3^1\).

Returning to the abstract setting, clearly \(|A'|=(k+1)|A|\) and the preference lists have a total of \(\Theta(|A'|^2)\) entries, so the construction is polynomial in our original instance \((I=(A,\succ),k)\). We will now establish a parameter-preserving correspondence between the two instances _I_ and \(I'\) (with respect to parameter _k_) in the two claims below.

**Claim 4.** _If I admits a matching M such that_ \(|ba^I(M)|\le k\),<sup>2</sup> _then_ \(I'\) _admits a matching_ \(M'\) _such that_ \(|ba^{I^\prime}(M')|\le k\).

_Proof of Claim 4._ We prove this by constructing \(M'\) explicitly from _M_, starting with \(M'=M\). Now, for every unmatched agent \(a_i\in A\), we can add \(\{a_i,a_i^1\}\) to \(M'\). Now, for the remaining unmatched dummy agents \(a_j^s\in A'\), we match according to \(\succ_G\): add the two best-ranked unmatched agents as a match to \(M'\), then the third and fourth best-ranked unmatched agents, and so on. If the number of unmatched agents is odd, leave the last such agent unmatched.

We will now prove that \(bp^{I^\prime}(M')\subseteq bp^I(M)\), in which case \(ba^{I^\prime}(M')\subseteq ba^I(M)\) and thus \(|ba^{I^\prime}(M')|\le|ba^I(M)|\le k\). Let \(\{a_r,a_s\}\in bp^{I^\prime}(M')\) (where either or both agents may be either dummy or non-dummy agents in \(A'\)). Then, by definition, both \(a_s\succ'_{a_r}M'(a_r)\) and \(a_r\succ'_{a_s}M'(a_s)\). Notice that no agent in _A_ can be in more blocking pairs in \(M'\) than in _M_ because they either remain with the same partner or get their first choice in the extended preference list. Thus, if either \(a_r\in A\) or \(a_s\in A\), then both \(a_r,a_s\in A\) and \(\{a_r,a_s\}\in bp^I(M)\). Now suppose that both \(a_r,a_s\in A'\backslash A\), i.e., both agents are dummy agents. Clearly, no dummy agent that is matched to their first choice is blocking, so both agents are matched according to \(\succ_G\). Without loss of generality, suppose that \(a_r\succ_Ga_s\). Then, by construction of \(M'\), either _a<sub>r</sub>_ has a partner in \(M'\) that is strictly better than _a<sub>s</sub>_ (according to \(\succ_G\) and thus also according to _a<sub>s</sub>_’ preferences) or _a<sub>r</sub>_ is matched to _a<sub>s</sub>_ in \(M'\) (as we iteratively match the best two remaining unmatched agents according to \(\succ_G\)). Thus, \(M'(a_r)\succ_Ga_s\) or \(M'(a_r)=a_s\), but, by construction of \(\succ'\), then either \(M'(a_r)\succ'_{a_r}a_s\) or \(M'(a_r)=a_s\), contradicting that \(a_s\succ'_{a_r}M'(a_r)\). Thus, no two dummy agents can form a blocking pair with respect to \(I'\) in \(M'\). Therefore, \(bp^{I^\prime}(M')=bp^I(M)\) and so \(|ba^{I^\prime}(M')|\le k\) as required.

**Claim 5.** _If_ \(I'\) _admits a matching_ \(M'\) _such that_ \(|ba^I(M')|\le k\)_, then I admits a matching M such that_ \(|ba^{I^\prime}(M)|\le k\).

_Proof of Claim 5._ Clearly, any agent \(a_i\in A\) has a partner at least as good as \(a_i^k\) in \(M'\) according to \(\succ'_{a_i}\), as otherwise every \(a_i^s\) (for \(1\le s\le k\)) would be blocking with _a<sub>i</sub>_, in which case \(|ba^{I^\prime}(M')|\ge k+1\) (all _k_ dummy agents block and _a<sub>i</sub>_ blocks with them), contradicting our assumption. Thus, we can simply construct a matching _M_ of _I_ in which, for every agent \(a_i\in A\), if \(M'(a_i)\in A\), then \(M(a_i)=M'(a_i)\), and _M(a<sub>i</sub>_)=_a<sub>i</sub>_ (i.e., we leave _a<sub>i</sub>_ unmatched) otherwise. It is easy to see that \(|ba^I(M)|=|ba^{I^\prime}(M')\cap A|\le|ba^{I^\prime}(M')|\le k\).

The stated result follows immediately.

<sup>2</sup>We use the notation _ba<sup>I</sup>(M_) for clarity to indicate that we mean the set of blocking agents that _M_ admits with respect to instance _I_.
