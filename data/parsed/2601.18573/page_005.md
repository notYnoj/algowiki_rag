This rather restrictive definition of classical sat has a nice structure. For example, the following formula is a yes-instance: \(B=(V_1\vee V_2\vee V_3)\wedge(\bar{V}_1\vee\bar{V}_2\vee\bar{V}_3)\wedge(V_1\vee\bar{V}_2\vee V_3)\wedge(\bar{V}_1\vee V_2\vee\bar{V}_3)\), as the truth assignment setting _V_<sub>1</sub> to true and _V_<sub>2</sub> and _V_<sub>3</sub> to false satisfies _B_.

## 2.2 Related Work

Abraham et al. \[1\] established that the problem MinBP-AlmostStable-sri is NP-hard even for complete preference lists, and Biró et al. \[7\] extended this intractability result to instances where every agent has a preference list of length at most 3. The first set of authors also proved that it is NP-hard to approximate MinBP-AlmostStable-sri within any \(n^{1/2-\varepsilon}\) (for any \(\varepsilon>0\), where _n_ is the number of agents), and Chen et al. \[11\] proved that the problem is W\[1\]-hard with respect to the parameter \(min_{M\in\mathcal{M}}|bp(M)|\) even when preference lists are of length at most 5.

For smi, Biró, Manlove and Mittal \[8\] studied the trade-off between matching size and stability from a computational complexity perspective. They showed that MinBP-AlmostStable- Max-smi (and a similar problem minimising the number of blocking agents) is NP-hard even to approximate within \(n^{1-\varepsilon}\) (for any \(\varepsilon>0\)) \[8\]. Later, Hamada et al. \[31\] strengthened this result to hold even in the presence of short preference lists of length at most 3. In contrast, for preference lists of length at most 2, Biró, Manlove and Mittal \[8\] showed that MinBP- AlmostStable-Max-smi (and the minimisation of blocking agents) is polynomial-time solvable. However, MinBP-AlmostStable-Max-smi is W\[1\]-hard even for a variety of combined parameters, which was studied extensively by Gupta et al. \[28\]. Recently, Chen, Roy and Simola \[12\] proved in a preprint that it is W\[1\]-hard (with respect to the optimal value) even to approximate the problems MinBP-AlmostStable-sri and MinBP-AlmostStable-Max-smi within any computable function depending only on the optimal solution value.

Complementary to the optimisation problems, we denote the decision problems asking whether there exists a matching (respectively a perfect matching in the smi setting) with at most _k_ blocking pairs by _k_-BP-AlmostStable-sri and _k_-BP-AlmostStable-Perfect-smi. It is known that both of these problems are NP-complete in general, but solvable in polynomial time for a fixed parameter _k_ (i.e., in XP with respect to the fixed parameter \[20\]) \[1, 7, 8, 10\].

Glitzner and Manlove \[25\] recently studied an alternative minimax notion of almost-stability, where the maximum number of blocking pairs that any agent is contained in is minimised. They proved, for example, that even deciding whether there exists a matching in which no agent is in more than one blocking pair is NP-complete. On the positive side, they provided efficient algorithms for instances with preference lists of length at most 3, and an approximation algorithm for the sri setting.

We note that Floréen et al. \[19\] and Ostrovsky and Rosenbaum \[42\] considered other definitions of almost-stability, and almost-stable matchings have also been studied in other contexts, such as the Hospitals/Residents problem with couples \[39, 14\]. Furthermore, there is a wide literature on other solution concepts for sri instances \[29, 18, 45, 32, 46, 26, 27\].

The assumption that underlies our problem models (allowing some agents to block with each other, while disallowing other agents to block) is also related to so-called _socially-stable matchings_ and the related concept of _free edges_.

In the socially-stable matching problem (in the interpretation of \[3\]), there is an smi instance (or a many-to-one Hospitals/Residents instance), as well as a social network graph where the agents are the vertices and edges between agents indicate the social connections, and the aim is to find the largest _socially-stable_ matching, that is, the largest matching that admits no _socially-blocking pair_ (a blocking pair of agents who are connected by an edge in the social network graph). This problem is denoted by max-smiss. In our context, social stability and deviator stability coincide when the social network graph consists of every pair of (acceptable) agents that contains at least one deviator. max-smiss is known to be NP-hard even for preference lists of length at most 3 and NP-hard to approximate within some constant \[3\].
