<tbc>_x_ uses _x_ together with _k_-1 variables from the blocks. The removal of duplicate scopes makes each family simple, and attached constraints for two different variables of _X_ cannot have the same scope. The number of new variables is |_D|m=O<sub>R</sub>(N_). This construction uses the variable set of _P_ but not its constraints, as required in the final part of the lemma.

We next show the counting bound that we need. Let _q_=|_D_|. Under any assignment \(\alpha\), choose a most frequent value in each block _Z<sub>a</sub>_ and denote it by \(\sigma(a)\). This value appears at least _m/q_ times in _Z<sub>a</sub>_. Consider any sequence of \(\ell\le k\) blocks and choose one variable from each block in order. At every step, at most _k_-1 variables are forbidden by the earlier choices. Hence, when \(m\ge 2qk\), there are at least _m/_(2_q_) choices at every step and at least \((m/(2q))^\ell\) pairwise distinct choices in total. At most _k_! ordered choices give the same unordered scope. We may therefore fix a constant \(\eta_R>0\) such that there are at least \(\eta_Rm^\ell\) distinct ordered scopes, and also at least \(\eta_Rm^\ell\) distinct unordered scopes when _R_ is symmetric. For a fixed tuple _r_, different ordered choices give different ordered scopes of block constraints. The same is true for the attached constraints when _x_, _r_, and _t_ are fixed. Thus the bound continues to hold after we remove repeated scopes.

Choose _C<sub>R</sub>_ large enough that \(C_R\ge 2qk\), \(\eta_RC_R^k>1\), and \(\eta_RC_R^{k-1}>1\). Since _m=C<sub>R</sub>N_, \(B\le N^d\), \(\Delta\le N^{d-1}\), and \(d\le k\), we then have \(\eta_Rm^k>B\) and \(\eta_Rm^{k-1}>\Delta\) for every \(N\ge 1\).

We can now compare every assignment with the intended one. Assigning \(x\mapsto s(x)\) for \(x\in X\) and assigning every variable in _Z<sub>a</sub>_ the value _a_ satisfies all constraints of _H<sub>s</sub>_. Therefore \(\mathrm{OPT}_\mathrm{max}(P\cup H_s)\ge|H_s|+\mathrm{val}_P(s)\).

For the reverse inequality, fix an arbitrary assignment \(\alpha\) and define \(\sigma:D\rightarrow D\) from the most frequent values of the blocks as above. Suppose first that \(\sigma\) is not an endomorphism of _R_. Choose \(r\in R\) with \(\sigma(r)\notin R\). At least \(\eta_Rm^k>B\) block constraints are assigned the tuple \(\sigma(r)\), so all of them are violated. Even if \(\alpha\) satisfies every constraint of _P_, it has value less than |_H<sub>s</sub>_|, while the intended assignment has value at least |_H<sub>s</sub>_|. Thus any assignment that attains the optimum must have \(\sigma\) as an endomorphism and, since _R_ is a core, as an automorphism.

Thus, we only need to consider the case in which \(\sigma\) is an automorphism. Let \(W=\{x\in X:\alpha(x)\neq\sigma(s(x))\}\). Fix \(x\in W\), put _a=s(x_), and put \(b=\sigma^{-1}(\alpha(x))\), so \(b\neq a\). By Lemma 4.5, there are \(r\in R\) and \(t\in[k]\) such that _r<sub>t</sub>=a_ and \(r^{t\leftarrow b}\notin R\). In every other coordinate _j_, choose a variable from _Z<sub>r<sub>j</sub></sub>_ assigned its most frequent value \(\sigma(r_j)\). There are at least \(\eta_Rm^{k-1}\) such attached constraints for _x_, and each is assigned the tuple \(\sigma(r^{t\leftarrow b})\). All of them are violated because \(\sigma\) is an automorphism. The constraints counted for different variables _x_ are disjoint, so \(\alpha\) violates at least \(\eta_Rm^{k-1}|W|\) attached constraints.

The assignment \(\sigma\circ s\) has value val_<sub>P</sub>(s_) on _P_ by Observation 4.1. Changing the variables in _W_ can affect at most \(\Delta|W|\) constraints of _P_, and hence \(\mathrm{val}_P(\alpha|_X)\le\mathrm{val}_P(s)+\Delta|W|\). Since \(\alpha\) violates at least \(\eta_Rm^{k-1}|W|\) attached constraints, we have \(\mathrm{val}_{P\cup H_s}(\alpha)\le|H_s|+\mathrm{val}_P(s)+(\Delta-\eta_Rm^{k-1})|W|\le|H_s|+\mathrm{val}_P(s)\). Together with the intended assignment, this proves the required equality.

The exact-pinning gadget still leaves one common automorphism, but that is fine since the objective of _P_ is invariant under it, while every other disagreement with _s_ loses more gadget constraints than it can gain in _P_.

## 5 Satisfability

We now characterize the one-pass streaming space needed to decide satisfiability. We will prove that after reducing to the core, the right parameter is non-redundancy. The upper bound stores a small instance with the same satisfying assignments as the constraints seen so far. For the lower bound, a maximum nonredundant instance gives the bits of an INDEX instance, and hard pinning lets Bob test the bit corresponding to his constraint.
