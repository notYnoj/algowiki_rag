## 1 Introduction

Constraint satisfaction problems (CSPs) are one of the basic objects in theoretical computer science. Fix a finite domain _D_, an arity _k_, and a relation \(R\subseteq D^k\). An _R_-constraint applies _R_ to an ordered _k_-tuple of variables, with repetitions allowed, and an _R_-instance is a collection of such constraints. The basic satisfiability problem asks whether there is an assignment that satisfies every constraint. By varying the relation _R_, CSPs can capture a huge variety of natural problems, from bipartiteness, to 3-SAT, to graph coloring, and to systems of linear equations.

More recently, a line of work has emerged which studies the complexity of solving such CSPs in the _streaming model_. In this setting, each of the _m_ constraints is revealed one at a time. The goal, as before, is to decide satisfiability or a related objective for the resulting instance using _as little space_ as possible. We assume throughout that the stream length is polynomially bounded in _n_. Naturally, storing every constraint explicitly as it arrives allows one to store the entire CSP instance, meaning there is a trivial upper bound of _O(n<sup>k</sup>_log_n_) bits of space for any such task.

The streaming study of CSP optimization was initiated by Kapralov, Khanna, and Sudan [KKS15], who proved strong lower bounds for approximating Max-Cut. Kapralov and Krachun [KK19] subsequently proved that there is no non-trivial approximation for Max-Cut in sublinear-space in the streaming model. Guruswami, Velingker, and Velusamy [GVV17] and Chou, Golovnev, and Velusamy [CGV20] studied Boolean Max-CSPs of arity two, while Chou, Golovnev, Sudan, and Velusamy [CGSV24] extended this line of work to arbitrary finite Max-CSPs.

Very recently, this line of work has led to substantially broader characterizations of the streaming approximability of Max-CSPs. A sequence of works developed increasingly strong sublinear-space algorithms and lower bounds for Max-DiCut and Max-Cut [SSSV25; FMW25; Vel25; ABFS26], while Singer, Tulsiani, and Velusamy [STV25] and Fei, Minzer, and Wang [FMW26a] uncovered a close connection between streaming approximability and the standard BasicLP relaxation for CSPs. Subsequent works essentially completed this picture in the one-pass setting, giving sublinear-space algorithms up to the approximation threshold predicted by BasicLP and linear-space lower bounds beyond it [ABF26; STV26; FMW26b]. Thus, the streaming approximability of Max-CSPs is now understood to a remarkable extent, in sharp contrast to the objectives that we study in this work.

Kol, Paramonov, Saxena, and Yu [KPSY23] studied exact optimization for Boolean CSPs with literals. Their formulation asks, for a given threshold _v_, whether some assignment satisfies at least _v_ constraints, which is equivalent to computing the maximum number of satisfied constraints exactly. They showed that if the indicator of the predicate has polynomial degree _d_, then the streaming space complexity is \(\widetilde{\Theta}(n^d)\). Other work has focused on satisfiability. Vu [Vu24] proved an \(\Omega(n^k)\) lower bound for _k_-SAT, while Chou, Golovnev, Sudan, and Velusamy [CGSV24] proved an \(\Omega(n)\) lower bound for arbitrary CSPs.

The streaming complexity of satisfiability was characterized more recently by Sharma and Velusamy [SV26a], who showed that for a relation _R_, its space complexity is characterized, up to a logarithmic factor, by the non-redundancy of _R_. Non-redundancy in turn is a fundamental parameter in the study of CSPs, known to govern the query complexity of learning CSPs [BCK20; Car22], the kernelizability of CSPs [JP19; CJP20; LW20], and approximate sparsification of CSPs [KK15; FK17; BŽ20; KPS24; KPS25; BG25]. Given this rapidly-growing list of connections, there has been a recent surge of interest in obtaining sharp asymptotic estimates for the non-redundancy of many CSP predicates [BCK20; CJP20; LW20; Car22; KPS25; BG25; BGJ<sup>+</sup>25; LGE26; BGP26a; SV26b; BGJ<sup>+</sup>26], often combining ideas from logic, algebra, and extremal combinatorics. Recent work has obtained further bounds and structural results for non-redundancy and sparsification [BGP26b; BGP26c; BGP26d].
