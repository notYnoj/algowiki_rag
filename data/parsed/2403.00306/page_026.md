## 2.3.3 RNA \(\rightarrow\) Protein: Translation

There are twenty types of tRNAs, and twenty types of amino acids. Each type of amino acid binds to a different tRNA, and the tRNA molecules have a three-base segment called an anticodon that is complementary to the "codon" in the mRNA. As in DNA base-pairing, the anticodon on the tRNA sticks to the codon on the RNA, which makes the amino acid available to the ribosome to add to the polypeptide chain. When one amino acid has been added, the ribosome shifts one codon to the right, and the process repeats. The process of turning an mRNA into a protein is called translation, since it translates information from the RNA into the protein.
