**The Reduced Instance** _H_**.** Let _H_ be the marriage instance obtained by deleting the vertices of _U_<sup>∗</sup> and their incident edges from \(G^*=(A\cup B,E^*)\). Recall that _U_<sup>∗</sup> is the set of unstable vertices of _G_<sup>∗</sup>. So the vertex set of _H_ is \((A\cup B)\backslash U^*\) and its edge set \(E_H=E^*\backslash\cup_{u\in U^*}\delta^*(u)\) (note that, for \(u\in U^*\), \(\delta^*(u)=\delta(u)\)). Preferences in _H_ are induced by preferences in _G_<sup>∗</sup>, i.e., vertices in _U_<sup>∗</sup> are deleted from the preference lists of their neighbors. Since _U_<sup>∗</sup> is the set of unstable vertices in _G_<sup>∗</sup>, every stable matching in _H_ is perfect. So every popular matching in _H_ is perfect and hence, dominant (by Theorem 6).

The polytope E_<sub>H</sub>_ is defined by constraints (2)-(6) given in Section 2.1, where the vertex set in these constraints is now \((A\cup B)\backslash U^*\) and the edge set is _E<sub>H</sub>_. We moreover claim that _x_<sub>(_u,u_)</sub>=0 for all \(u\in(A\cup B)\backslash U^*\). This holds because all vertices in \((A\cup B)\backslash U^*\) are stable, and a popular fractional matching **_x_** has to fully match each stable vertex to neighbors other than itself, otherwise wt_<sub>x</sub>(S)\>_0 for any stable matching _S_ \[27, footnote 2\].

The following theorem will be useful. For any matching _M_, let _I<sub>M</sub>_ be its incidence vector.

**Theorem 10 (\[27\]).** _In any marriage instance H that admits a perfect stable matching, each extreme point_ \((\mathbf{x},\unknown)\) _of_ E_<sub>H</sub> is such that **x**=I<sub>M</sub>, where M is a popular matching in H,_ \(\unknown\in\{\pm 1\}^{n_0}\) _is a (dominant) witness of M, and n_<sub>0</sub> _is the number of vertices in H._

It is easy to see (from Theorem 10) that for any vertex _u_ in _H_, the constraint \(\alpha_u\le 1\) is a valid inequality for E_<sub>H</sub>_. Therefore \(\mathcal{E}_H\cap\{\alpha_v=1:\forall v\in\mathsf{Nbr}(U^*)\}\) is a face of E_<sub>H</sub>_, where \(\mathsf{Nbr}(U^*)\subseteq B\) is the set of neighbors in _G_<sup>∗</sup> (equivalently, in _G_) of vertices in _U_<sup>∗</sup>. Call this face Z_<sub>H</sub>_. Theorem 10 and the definition of Z_<sub>H</sub>_ imply the following: (let \(n_0=|(A\cup B)\backslash U^*|\))

- every extreme point of Z_<sub>H</sub>_ is of the form \((I_N,\unknown)\) where _I<sub>N</sub>_ is the incidence vector of a popular matching _N_ in _H_ and \(\unknown\in\{\pm 1\}^{n_0}\) is a (dominant) witness of _N_ such that \(\gamma_v=1\) for all \(v\in\mathsf{Nbr}(U^*)\).

_Proof._ (of Theorem 9). Let \((\mathbf{x},\unknown)\in\mathcal{C}_{G^*}\). It follows from constraint (12) that _x<sub>e</sub>_=0 for all edges _e_ incident to vertices in _U_<sup>∗</sup> and from constraint (10) that \(\alpha_a=0\) for all \(a\in U^*\) (recall that \(U^*\subseteq A\)). Let \((\mathbf{x}',\unknown')\) be obtained from \((\mathbf{x},\unknown)\) by projecting out coordinates _x<sub>e</sub>_ for \(e\in\delta^*(a),a\in U^*\) and \(\alpha_a\) for \(a\in U^*\).

It is straightforward to see that \((\mathbf{x}',\unknown')\) satisfies constraints (2)-(6) that define E_<sub>H</sub>_. Moreover, we have \(\alpha_b\ge 1\) for all \(b\in\mathsf{Nbr}(U^*)\) due to constraint (8) for the edge (_a,b_) where \(a\in U^*\). This is because _c<sub>x</sub>(a,b_)=1 for such an edge (_a,b_) and \(\alpha_a=0\). Thus \((\mathbf{x}',\unknown')\in\mathcal{Z}_H\).

Since Z_<sub>H</sub>_ is integral, \((\mathbf{x}',\unknown')\) is a convex combination of \((I_{N_1},\unknown^1),\dots,(I_{N_k},\unknown^k)\) where _N_<sub>1</sub>_,...,N<sub>k</sub>_ are popular matchings in _H_ with respective dominant witnesses \(\unknown^1,\dots,\unknown^k\) and we have \(\gamma_v^i=1\) for all \(v\in\mathsf{Nbr}(U^*)\) and \(1\le i\le k\). For each _i_, matching _N<sub>i</sub>_ can be viewed as a matching \(\widetilde{N}_i\) in _G_<sup>∗</sup>, so \(I_{\widetilde{N}_i}\) has value 0 in coordinates corresponding to edges in \(\delta^*(u)\) for \(u\in U^*\). Similarly, we extend the witness vector \(\unknown^i\in\{\pm 1\}^{n_0}\) to a vector \(\widetilde{\unknown}^i\in\{0,\pm 1\}^n\) by assigning \(\widetilde{\gamma}_u^i=0\) for all \(u\in U^*\). Thus \((\mathbf{x},\unknown)\) is a convex combination of \((I_{\widetilde{N}_1},\widetilde{\unknown}^1),\dots,(I_{\widetilde{N}_k},\widetilde{\unknown}^k)\). Hence C<sub>_G_<sup>∗</sup></sub> is integral.

Note that \(\widetilde{\unknown}^i\) is a dominant witness of \(\widetilde{N}_i\), thus \(\widetilde{N}_i\) is a dominant matching in _G_<sup>∗</sup> (by Theorem 7). Hence, all extreme points of C<sub>_G_<sup>∗</sup></sub> are of the form \((I_N,\unknown)\), where _N_ is a dominant matching in _G_<sup>∗</sup> and \(\unknown\) is a dominant witness. The following claim completes the proof of Theorem 9.

**Claim 1** _For any dominant matching M in G_<sup>∗</sup> _and any dominant witness_ \(\unknown\) _of M, we have_ \((I_M,\unknown)\in\mathcal{C}_{G^*}\).

_Proof._ We need to show that \((I_M,\unknown)\) satisfies constraints (7)-(13). It follows from the definition of witness that \(\sum_{u\in A\cup B}\beta_u=0\). We also know that \(\beta_u\in\{\pm 1\}\) for \(u\in(A\cup B)\backslash U^*\) (by definition of dominant witness) and \(\beta_u=0\) for \(u\in U^*\) (see Lemma 1). Since _M_ is a dominant matching, all popular vertices, i.e., those in \((A\cup B)\backslash U^*\), are matched in _M_; also \(M\subseteq E_D^*\) (the set of dominant edges in _G_<sup>∗</sup>). Hence all constraints in (9)-(13) along with (7) are satisfied by \((I_M,\unknown)\).

Regarding the constraints in (8), it follows from the definition of witness that \(\beta_a+\beta_b\ge\mathsf{wt}_M(a,b)\) for all \((a,b)\in E^*\). We have _c<sub>M</sub>(a,b_)=wt_<sub>M</sub>(a,b_) for all edges (_a,b_) except when \(a\in U^*\). For such an edge \((a,b)\in U^*\times\mathsf{Nbr}(U^*)\), we have wt_<sub>M</sub>(a,b_)=0 but _c<sub>M</sub>(a,b_)=1. Since \(\beta_a=0\) and \(\beta_b\in\{\pm 1\}\), we deduce \(\beta_b=1\) and \(\beta_a+\beta_b=1=c_M(a,b)\). Thus \((I_M,\unknown)\) satisfies all the constraints defining C<sub>_G_<sup>∗</sup></sub>. \(\square\)
