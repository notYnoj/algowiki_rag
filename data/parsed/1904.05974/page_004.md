Every dominant matching is a max-size popular matching (by Definition 2). Dominant matchings always exist in a marriage instance _G_ \[25\]. Dominant matchings coincide with the linear image of stable matchings in a related bipartite graph \(G'\) on _n_ vertices and 2_m_ edges \[11,13\]. Hence an extension of the dominant matching polytope D_<sub>G</sub>_ in R<sup>2_m_</sup> (as the linear image of the stable matching polytope of \(G'\)) was known. We obtain the above polytope F<sub>_G_<sup>∗</sup></sub> as a face of a new extension of D<sub>_G_<sup>∗</sup></sub> of size _O(m+n_) in R<sup>_m+n_</sup> (see Theorem 8 and Theorem 9). The main tool for proving the integrality of this new extension is a compact extended formulation of the popular matching polytope in a marriage instance where every stable matching is perfect \[27\].

The second algorithm shows actually a result stronger than Theorem 2: we show that we can obtain in polynomial time a quasi-popular matching of cost at most that of a min-cost _popular fractional matching_. The set of all popular fractional matchings includes all popular matchings. We defer a discussion on popular fractional matchings and a description of their polytope to Section 2.

**Polyhedral Results.** It is a natural question whether the positive/negative results on popular and quasi- popular matchings are mirrored by the sizes of the descriptions of the associated polytopes. Let P=conv(M<sub>1</sub>) (respectively, Q=conv(M<sub>2</sub>)) be the popular (resp., quasi-popular) matching polytope of _G_, for M<sub>1</sub> and M<sub>2</sub> as defined above (just below Theorem 2). We prove that both P and Q have near-exponential _extension complexity_ (see Section 6 for definitions). These results follow from our hardness result and a new reduction that we show here, along with known lower bounds in polyhedral combinatorics. In particular, we show that a face of P (resp., Q) is an extension of certain independent set polytopes for which a lower bound on the extension complexity is known \[20\].

**Theorem 3.** _The extension complexity of the polytope_ P _(also, of the polytope_ Q_) is_ \(2^{\Omega(\frac{m}{logm})}\).

The proof of Theorem 2 shows that conv(M<sup>∗</sup>) is an "easy-to-describe" integral polytope sandwiched between two hard ones, see Fig. 1 for a representation. As conv(M<sup>∗</sup>) has a formulation of size _O(n+m_), it is, in a way, a polytope with "smallest extension complexity" sandwiched between P and Q. Theorem 3 actually shows that the face \(F'\) of P containing all and only the popular matchings of maximum size has near-exponential extension complexity. This is in sharp contrast with the dominant matching polytope D_<sub>G</sub>_ (contained in \(F'\)), that admits a compact extended formulation. However, unlike stable matchings whose polytope in R_<sup>m</sup>_ has a linear number of facets \[45,39\], we show that D_<sub>G</sub>_ does not admit a polynomial-size description in R_<sup>m</sup>_. In fact, a complete linear description of D_<sub>G</sub>_ in the original space was not known so far. We give one here, and show that D_<sub>G</sub>_ has an exponential number of facets (in the size of the graph). Recall that M_<sub>G</sub>_ is the set of matchings in \(G=(A\cup B,E)\) and |_E_|=_m_.

**Theorem 4.** _The dominant matching polytope_ D_<sub>G</sub> admits a formulation of size O_(|M_<sub>G</sub>_|) _in_ R_<sup>m</sup>. Moreover, there exists a constant c\>_1 _such that_ D_<sub>G</sub> has_ \(\Omega(c^m)\) _facets._

**Optimal Quasi-popular Matchings.** In order to prove Theorem 1, we first prove structural results on quasi-popular matchings. Stable matchings (by definition) and popular matchings (see \[25\]) have simple _forbidden structures_ in terms of blocking edges--to come up with such forbidden structures for quasi-popular matchings seems much more complex. Hence, we do not pursue this combinatorial approach. Instead, we extend the LP-method used for popular fractional matchings \[27,30,31\] to design an appropriate dual certificate or _witness_ (a vector in R_<sup>n</sup>_: see Section 2 for details) for quasi-popularity, and deduce Theorem 1 (proved in Section 5) from this.
