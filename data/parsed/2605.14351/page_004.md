\(\sum_{t=0}^\infty|h(t)|\le\frac{\sqrt{\|\mu\|}}{1-\rho}\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}(K_\mu)}.\) (16)

Consequently, functions in \(\mathcal{H}(K_\mu)\) are BIBO-stable impulse responses.

_Proof._ By the reproducing property and Cauchy-Schwarz,

\(|h(t)|\le\|h\|_{\mathcal{H}(K_\mu)}\sqrt{K_\mu(t,t)}.\)

Since

\(K_\mu(t,t)=\int|p|^{2t}d\mu(p)\le\|\mu\|\rho^{2t},\)

summing the resulting geometric bound over _t_ gives (16). Here \(\mu\) is the pole measure that defines \(K_\mu\) in (14). The proof uses only its total mass \(\|\mu\|\) and its support radius \(\rho\); it does not require \(\mu\) to be discrete. Thus the same bound applies to finite atomic measures, such as \(\mu=\sum_jw_j\delta_{p_j}\) arising from sampled RAF dictionaries, and to non-atomic or continuous pole distributions used to define limiting kernels.

## 4. A DISK-BOCHNER THEOREM WITH SUBNORMAL KERNEL SHIFTS

The name "Disk-Bochner" is meant to emphasize an analogy with classical Bochner and Herglotz representations: positive kernels can sometimes be represented as moments of positive measures on a spectral domain. For RAFs the spectral domain is a disk. The forward direction is elementary and is exactly what the algorithm uses. The converse requires care.

_Definition 1._ (Canonical Hilbert space and shift). Let _K_ be a Hermitian PSD kernel on N<sub>0</sub>. Introduce formal vectors _e_<sub>0</sub>_,e_<sub>1</sub>_,..._ with inner product \(\langle e_s,e_t\rangle=K(s,t)\), quotient by zero-norm vectors, and complete to obtain H_<sub>K</sub>_. The canonical shift is the operator initially defined on the linear span of the _e<sub>t</sub>_ by

\(Te_t=e_{t+1}.\) (17)

When this map is well defined and bounded, the pair (_T,e_<sub>0</sub>) is the canonical cyclic realization of _K_ because

\(K(s,t)=\langle T^se_0,T^te_0\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_K}.\) (18)

_Definition 2._ (Subnormality). A bounded operator _T_ on a Hilbert space H is subnormal if there exists a larger Hilbert space \(\mathcal{G}\supseteq\mathcal{H}\) and a normal operator _N_ on G such that H is invariant under _N_ and _T=N_|<sub>H</sub>. Normality means _N_<sup>∗</sup>_N=NN_<sup>∗</sup>.

_Proposition 2._ (Disk measures imply a radius defect). If _K_ has representation (14) with \(\text{supp}(\mu)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}_\rho}\), then _K_ is PSD and satisfies

\(\rho^2K(s,t)-K(s+1,t+1)\unknown 0.\) (19)

Moreover, the canonical shift is subnormal.

_Proof._ PSD follows from Theorem 1. For the radius defect,

\(\sum_{s,t}a_s\overline{a_t}\big(\rho^2K(s,t)-K(s+1,t+1)\big)\)<br> \(=\int_{|p|\le\rho}(\rho^2-|p|^2)\left|\sum_sa_sp^s\right|^2d\mu(p)\ge 0.\)

For subnormality, let _N_ be multiplication by _p_ on \(L^2(\mu)\). This is a normal operator with spectrum contained in \(\overline{\mathbb{D}_\rho}\).

The closed subspace generated by 1_,p,p_<sup>2</sup>_,..._ is invariant under _N_, and the restriction has moments _K_. Hence the canonical shift is unitarily equivalent to a subnormal restriction of _N_.

_Proposition 3._ (Radius defect and contractivity). For a Hermitian PSD kernel _K_, the radius defect (19) is equivalent to a bounded canonical shift satisfying \(\|T\|\le\rho\).

_Proof._ For \(x=\sum_sa_se_s\),

\(\rho^2\|x\|^2-\|Tx\|^2=\sum_{s,t}a_s\overline{a_t}\big(\rho^2K(s,t)-K(s+1,t+1)\big).\) (20)

Thus the defect is PSD precisely when \(\|Tx\|\le\rho\|x\|\) on the dense span of the canonical vectors, and hence on H_<sub>K</sub>_ by continuity.

_Theorem 4._ (Disk-Bochner). Let _K_ be a Hermitian PSD kernel on N<sub>0</sub>. Then _K_ admits

\(K(s,t)=\int_{|p|\le\rho} p^s\overline{p}^td\mu(p)\) (21)

for a finite positive Borel measure \(\mu\) on \(\overline{\mathbb{D}_\rho}\) if and only if its canonical shift is subnormal and has a normal extension _N_ with \(\sigma(N)\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{D}_\rho}\). In either case the radius defect (19) holds.

_Proof._ The forward implication follows from Proposition 2. Conversely, suppose the canonical shift _T_ has a normal extension _N_ with spectral measure \(E(\cdot)\) and spectrum in \(\overline{\mathbb{D}_\rho}\). Let _b=e_<sub>0</sub>. Since _T<sup>sb</sup>=N<sup>sb</sup>b_ for all \(s\ge 0\),

\(K(s,t)=\langle N^sb,N^tb\rangle.\) (22)

By the spectral theorem,

\(N=\int_{\sigma(N)}pdE(p).\) (23)

Define the scalar measure \(\mu(\Omega)=\langle E(\Omega)b,b\rangle\). Then \(\mu\) is finite, positive, supported in \(\overline{\mathbb{D}_\rho}\), and

\(K(s,t)=\int_{\sigma(N)}p^s\overline{p}^td\mu(p).\) (24)

_Proposition 4._ (Uniqueness). If a finite positive measure satisfying (21) exists, it is unique.

_Proof._ If two measures agree on all mixed moments \(p^s\overline{p}^t\), their difference integrates to zero against every polynomial in _p_ and <o>_p_</o>. This algebra contains constants, separates points on the compact disk, and is closed under conjugation. By Stone-Weierstrass it is dense in \(C(\overline{\mathbb{D}_\rho})\), so the measures agree on all continuous functions and are equal by the Riesz representation theorem.
