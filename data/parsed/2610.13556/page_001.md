# DISJOINT UNIONS AND FIXED-PARAMETER ALGORITHMS FOR CHROMATIC VILLAINY

Abstract. Chromatic villainy is the maximum, over rearrangements of optimal graph colorings, of the minimum Hamming distance to a proper coloring with the same color multiplicities. Weak villainy allows the repair to change these multiplicities. We give exact composition laws for disjoint unions and determine the eventual growth under repetition. Write _mG_ for the disjoint union of _m_ copies of _G_. For every graph _G_, with \(k=\chi(G)\), weak villainy satisfies _B_w(_mG_)=_mF<sub>k</sub>(G_) whenever _k|m_, where _F<sub>k</sub>(G_) is the maximum distance to a proper _k_-coloring over all assignments of _k_ color labels to vertices. Its bounded defect from _mF<sub>k</sub>(G_) is eventually periodic; we give an explicit stabilization bound and identify the least period, which divides _k_. For ordinary villainy, we obtain a computable rational slope \(\Lambda(G)\ge F_k(G)\) such that \(B(mG)=m\Lambda(G)+O_G(1)\), where the error is bounded independently of _m_ and eventually periodic, with a computable period and threshold. Consequently the difference between the two values is bounded exactly when \(\Lambda(G)=F_k(G)\), and otherwise grows linearly. The slope is determined by a finite optimization using at most _k_ source assignments. We also answer the unrestricted superadditivity questions negatively: for every _C\>_0, a connected graph can lose more than _C_ in weak villainy after union with a triangle, and some connected 3-chromatic graph _G_ satisfies \(B(G\sqcup G)<2B(G)-C\). Finally, both exact values on an _n_-vertex graph can be computed in time _f(B_w(_G_))(_n_+1)<sup>_O_(1)</sup>, without either value being supplied. Threshold recognition is fixed-parameter tractable as well.

### 1. Introduction

Chromatic villainy measures the cost of repairing a rearrangement of an optimal coloring. The cost counts vertices whose colors change. In the _villainy B(G_) of Clark et al. [2], the repair must preserve every color multiplicity; _weak villainy_<sub>w</sub>(_G_) permits different multiplicities on the same \(\chi(G)\)-color palette. Both parameters maximize the minimum repair distance over all such rearrangements. Thus \(0\le B_\mathrm{w}(G)\le B(G)\le|V(G)|\). A _word_ is any assignment of color labels to vertices, without requiring properness. Its _profile_ is the vector of its labeled color multiplicities; it is feasible if a proper coloring realizes it (Theorem 2.1). We call a rearranged source coloring a disturbance.

We study the behavior of these parameters under disjoint union and the complexity of recognizing graphs with a fixed value. Both questions were raised in the early literature; see [2, 14] and the account of Jahanbekam and Lin [12]. Clark et al. proved that weak villainy is superadditive under disjoint union when the summands have the same chromatic number. They proved the ordinary analogue under the additional assumption that every optimal coloring of one summand has equal-sized color classes. It was natural to ask whether these restrictions could be removed, and to determine when equality holds. On the algorithmic side, the classifications of graphs with villainy zero [2] and villainy two [12] give polynomial-time recognition in those cases. The complexity at other fixed values remained to be determined.

This paper continues our study in [13], where we determined odd-cycle profile radii, obtained complete multipartite formulas, and determined the extremal values at fixed order and chromatic number. Here we turn to disjoint unions and computation. The profile viewpoint remains central, but composition requires tracking transfers of color counts and, for ordinary repair, simultaneous responses to different target profiles. Our aim is to describe how global multiplicity constraints combine local repair costs, and to determine when those costs can be computed efficiently.
