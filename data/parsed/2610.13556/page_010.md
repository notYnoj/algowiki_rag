<tbc>The zero-cost multiset of length _k_ decomposes into zero-cost blocks, because all costs are nonnegative. Hence the gcd of the lengths of zero-cost blocks divides _k_. Every zero-cost multiset also decomposes into zero-cost blocks. Its possible lengths are therefore precisely the additive semigroup generated by those block lengths, that is, their nonnegative integer combinations, and their gcd is the number _h_ in the statement. Thus _H=h_Z_/k_Z. The sequence (_a<sub>r</sub>_) has period _h_. Any period _t_ must send its zero entry _a_<sub>0</sub> to another zero entry, so _t_ mod \(k\in H\); hence _h_ is its least positive period. By (4.11), it is also the least eventual period of (_d<sub>m</sub>_).

Set _c<sub>r</sub>=a<sub>r</sub>_ for \(0\le r<h\). These coefficients satisfy the stated bounds, and _c<sub>r</sub>\>_0 when \(r\neq 0\) because such a residue does not belong to _H_. Every zero-cost block has length at most _M_ and is itself a zero-cost multiset. Conversely, every zero-cost multiset of length at most _M_ decomposes into zero-cost blocks. The two collections of lengths have the same gcd, proving the finite formula for _h_.

Finally, all local pairs, their costs, and all blocks of length at most _M_ can be enumerated from the displayed local data. The shortest-path construction then computes the coefficients, and the zero-cost block lengths compute _h_. This is a finite procedure; no polynomial bound on the enumeration is asserted. □

When _k_=1, the graph is edgeless and \(F_1(G)=\beta_1(mG)=0\).

**Corollary 4.7.** _For a graph G with_ \(\chi(G)=k\)_, the following are equivalent:_

(_1)_ _B_<sub>w</sub>(_G_)=_F<sub>k</sub>(G);_<tbc>

(_2)_ _B_<sub>w</sub>(_mG_)=_mB_<sub>w</sub>(_G_) _for every positive integer m;_<tbc>

_(3)_ _B_<sub>w</sub>(_kG_)=_kB_<sub>w</sub>(_G)._

_Proof._ If the first equality holds, the error bound in Theorem 4.5 is zero for every _m_. The second statement includes the third. Finally, (4.9) with _m=k_ shows that the third implies the first. □

Thus additivity over every repetition is equivalent to a property of a single graph: an unrestricted worst source can be chosen with a feasible profile.

## 4.3. Count-preserving repairs and response functions.

For ordinary villainy, the target profiles on the summands are coupled by their prescribed sum. We retain the common palette of _k_ colors and the profile sets A_<sub>k</sub>(J),_M_<sub>k</sub>(J_) introduced in Theorem 4.1. Local source profiles range over A_<sub>k</sub>(J_), even when they are not proper profiles.

**Definition 4.8** (Repair responses)**.** The _response function_ of a word \(w:V(J)\rightarrow[k]\) of profile **a** is<tbc>

\(f_w(\mathbf{p})=min_{y\in\mathcal{P}_k(J)\mathbf{m}(y)=\mathbf{p}}d_\mathrm{H}(w,y)(\mathbf{p}\in\mathcal{M}_k(J)).\)

<tbc>Here all colorings and words use the common palette; a proper coloring of a summand need not use every color. Extend _f<sub>w</sub>_ by \(+\infty\) outside M_<sub>k</sub>(J_). Write

\(\mathcal{R}_k(J,\mathbf{a})=\mathrm{Max}\{f_w:\mathbf{m}(w)=\mathbf{a}\},\)

where Max retains the coordinatewise maximal functions and removes duplicates. Thus a response is discarded if another realizable response is at least as large at every target profile and larger at some profile. The resulting family is an _antichain_: no two distinct remaining responses are comparable coordinatewise. It records the possible tradeoffs between target profiles without retaining the source words themselves.

For two response functions define their min-plus convolution by

\((f\star g)(\mathbf{p})=min_{\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{p}}\big(f(\mathbf{u})+g(\mathbf{v})\big).\)

The profiles where its value is finite are exactly M_<sub>k</sub>(G_)+M_<sub>k</sub>(H_) when _f,g_ are responses on _G,H_, respectively. For sets of responses, put \(\mathcal{U}\star\mathcal{V}=\{f\star g:f\in\mathcal{U},g\in\mathcal{V}\}\).
