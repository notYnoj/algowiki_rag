<tbc>_The number h is the least eventual period of the defect sequence_ \(d_m=mF-\beta_k(mG)\)_: it is the least positive integer for which d_<sub>_m+h_</sub>=_d<sub>m</sub> for all sufficiently large m. In particular, for_ \(m\ge N\),

\(\beta_k(mG)=mF\Longleftrightarrow h|m.\)

_Moreover,_

\(h=gcd\{1\le\ell\le M:\beta_k(\ell G)=\ell F\}.\)

_The period and coefficients are computable from the feasible profiles and the common-palette radii of G._

_Proof._ Write _d=k_-1. We use the Steinitz lemma in the following standard form: vectors in R_<sup>d</sup>_ of \(\ell_\infty\) norm at most _n_ and with sum zero can be ordered so that every partial sum has \(\ell_\infty\) norm at most _dn_; see \[6\]. Consequently a nonempty zero-sum multiset of integer vectors of norm at most _n_ that has no proper nonempty zero-sum submultiset contains at most (2_dn_+1)_<sup>d</sup>=M_ vectors. Indeed, for a multiset of length _L_, the partial sums **s**<sub>0</sub>=**0**,**s**<sub>1</sub>_,...,_**s**<sub>_L_-1</sub> in the Steinitz ordering are distinct lattice points in \[-_dn,dn_\]_<sup>d</sup>_. An equality between two of these sums would produce a proper nonempty zero-sum submultiset.

To each pair \((\mathbf{a},\mathbf{p})\in\mathcal{A}_k(G)\times\mathcal{M}_k(G)\), associate the vector<tbc>

\(\unknown=(a_1-p_1,\dots,a_d-p_d)\)

<tbc>and nonnegative integer cost \(F-\rho_{G,k}(\mathbf{a})\). The omitted coordinate of **a**-**p** is determined by the first _d_, because its coordinate sum is zero. Thus a multiset of _m_ local pairs has vector sum zero precisely when its source profiles sum to its proper profiles. Such a choice is exactly a feasible global source profile on _mG_. For fixed local source profiles, the maximum weak repair cost is the sum of the local radii, since sources can be chosen independently and weak repairs on different copies are independent. It follows that

\(d_m:=mF-\beta_k(mG)\)

is the minimum total cost of a zero-sum multiset of _m_ local pairs. The length of a multiset is its number of pairs, counted with multiplicity, and its cost is the sum of their costs. We also allow the empty multiset and put _d_<sub>0</sub>=0.

Call a nonempty zero-sum multiset a block if it has no proper nonempty zero-sum submultiset. Every zero-sum multiset is a disjoint union of blocks, by successively taking a minimal nonempty zero-sum submultiset. Each block has length at most _M_, by the preceding Steinitz consequence. There are only finitely many blocks because the set of local pairs is finite.

Consider the directed graph with vertex set Z_/k_Z. For every block of length \(\ell\) and cost _b_, place an edge from each residue _u_ to \(u+\ell\), with weight _b_. A path starting at zero represents a concatenation of blocks, so the shortest-path distance _a<sub>r</sub>_ from zero to _r_ is

\(a_r=min_{m\ge 0,m\equiv r}_{(modk)}d_m.\)

Every residue is reachable: a local pair with **a**=**p** is itself a block of length one. Edge weights are nonnegative, so a shortest path can be chosen without repeated vertices. It has at most _k_-1 edges and therefore represents a witness of length \(m_r\le(k-1)M=N\) and cost _a<sub>r</sub>_.

The cyclic relabeling construction in Theorem 4.5 gives a zero-cost multiset of length _k_. For any \(m\ge N\) with \(m\equiv r(modk)\), append (_m-m<sub>r</sub>)/k_ copies of that multiset to the witness above. This gives \(d_m\le a_r\), while the definition of _a<sub>r</sub>_ gives the reverse inequality. Hence

\(d_m=a_{modk}(m\ge N).\) (4.11)

The original error bound also gives _a_<sub>0</sub>=0 and \(a_r\le r\varepsilon\) for \(0\le r<k\).

To identify the least period, let _H_ be the subgroup of Z_/k_Z generated by the lengths of zero-cost blocks. Nonnegative edge weights imply that _a<sub>r</sub>_=0 exactly when _r_ is reachable from zero using only zero-cost edges. In a finite group, the semigroup generated by given residues is their subgroup; thus _a<sub>r</sub>_=0 exactly when \(r\in H\). Appending a zero-cost path shows that \(a_{r+u}\le a_r\) for \(u\in H\). Applying the same argument with \(-u\in H\) proves equality. Consequently (_a<sub>r</sub>_) is constant on the cosets of _H_.
