Because the size of the children of a node _n<sub>j</sub>_ that does not satisfy (10) are bounded by \(\lfloor\mathrm{size}(n_j)/2\rfloor+\mathrm{max}(1,\mathrm{size}(n_j)/\mathrm{lg}s'_\#)\), repeating this linear recurrence down from the root to a leaf bounds the leaf size by 2_e_+1 (where _e_≈2.71 is Euler’s constant). But as _L<sub>i</sub>_ is full containing 8_\>_2_e_+1 active representatives, we know a scapegoat node that satisfies (10) must exist on the path.

The subtree rooted at the scapegoat node is then rebuilt: move all _z_ active representatives into _D_, then redistribute them evenly across the gaps in the subtree so that for every internal node _n<sub>k</sub>_ in the subtree \(0\le\mathrm{size}(n_{2k})-\mathrm{size}(n_{2k+1})\le 1\). To compute the block of active representatives for each gap, we conduct an in-order traversal of the subtree with a stack of one bit per tree level packed into _O_(1) words that allows us to recalculate the correct block for each ancestor node [App. A]. Upon reaching a leaf node, we transport the correct block into the gap. Because the sizes of the scapegoat node’s children differ by at least 2 by condition (10), at least one child is forced to fall strictly below its maximum capacity. This limits \(\mathrm{size}(n_j)\le 7\mathrm{span}(n_j)\), ensuring that an even redistribution leaves at most 7 active representatives per gap, restoring the gap invariant. The process rearranges the positions of active representatives, invalidating the location returned by previous _R_.search operations.

\(\blacktriangleright\) **Lemma 2.2.** _R.insert(x_) _requires O_(lg<sup>3</sup>_m) comparisons and O_(lg<sup>3</sup>_m) moves amortized._

**Proof.** Excluding the cost of rebalancing, insertions cost _O_(lg_m_) amortized: the initial search is _O_(lg_m_), the linear shift conducts _O_(1) representative exchanges as the size of gaps is constant, and the \(2s'_\#\) representative exchanges conducted by resizing are spread among the \(s'_\#/2\) insertions since the previous resize to _O_(1) representative exchanges per insertion.

To bound rebalancing costs, we use the accounting method. For each internal node _n<sub>k</sub>_, let \(\psi\left(n_k\right)=\mathrm{max}\left(0,\left|\mathrm{size}\left(n_{2k}\right)-\mathrm{size}\left(n_{2k+1}\right)\right|-1\right)\) denote its _imbalance_. Now consider an instance of rebalancing that rebuilds a subtree of size _z_. The traversal to find a scapegoat node costs _z_=size(_n<sub>j</sub>_) comparisons, as 1 comparison is used to count each active representative [App. A]. The rebuild then swaps each representative into then out of _z_ using 2_z_ representative exchanges costing _O(z_lg_m_), resetting imbalance in the subtree to 0. By condition (10), prior to the rebuild the subtree contained \(\Omega(z/\mathrm{lg}s'_\#)\) units of imbalance, so each unit of imbalance is repaired at \(O(z\mathrm{lg}m)/\Omega(z/\mathrm{lg}s'_\#)=O(\mathrm{lg}^2m)\) cost. Inserting a new active representative increases the size of a leaf node by 1, which may increase the imbalance of each of its \(\mathrm{lg}s'_\#\) ancestors by 1. Each unit incurred may later be repaired at _O_(lg<sup>2</sup>_m_) cost, bounding the cost of dynamic rebalancing at _O_(lg<sup>3</sup>_m_) amortized. Combining costs gives the overall bound. \(\blacktriangleleft\)

## 2.3 Sorting Small Subsequences

We now introduce a specialized _small sort_ that is designed to sort small subsequences \(A'\) of \(m'=O(\mathrm{lg}^cm)\) active elements for some constant _c_ given a _merge buffer_ \(B'\) of \(m'\) buffer elements, the hole, and _O_(1) auxiliary words of \(w\ge\mathrm{lgm}\) bits, adapted from [8]. This subroutine is used during extraction to sort segments. The algorithm is implemented as a _k_-way merge sort, where

\(k=2^{\lceil\mathrm{lg}(w/\mathrm{lg}\mathrm{lg}m)\rceil}\ge\mathrm{lgm}/\mathrm{lglgm}\)

It begins by sorting runs of size _k_ by table sort from [9] with merge sort on the pointers. Subsequently, adjacent groups of _k_ runs are merged by using a selection tree to repeatedly select the minimum element from the current front of all runs. The choice of _k_ as a power of 2 ensures the selection tree forms a perfect binary tree, minimizing overhead.
