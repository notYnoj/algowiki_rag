**Warmup: heap evaluation.** Let us start with a simpler motivating scenario in which the data structure is required to reveal information _only at the end_. Consider a sequence \(\mathcal{S}=(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\dots,\sigma_n)\) of `insert` and `delete-min` operations given upfront as input. As usual, an operation `insert`(_x_) inserts an element _x_ with a given key into the heap, and a `delete-min` operation removes the element with the smallest key from the heap. The task is to compute the final state of the heap obtained by executing S from an empty heap. We require keys to be distinct and constant-time comparable, but otherwise place no restriction on them. The sequence S can intermix `insert` and `delete-min` operations arbitrarily, even allowing for prefixes with more `delete-min` than `insert` operations. In such cases, i.e., when executing a `delete-min` on an empty heap, we say that the individual operation is _unsuccessful_, not affecting the state of the data structure.

The final state of the heap is revealed in the form of an (unordered) list containing the inserted elements that survive all deletions, staying in the heap until the end. For example, for \(\mathcal{S}=\big(\text{\texttt{insert}}(a_2),\text{\texttt{insert}}(a_4),\text{\texttt{delete-min}},\text{\texttt{insert}}(a_3),\text{\texttt{insert}}(a_5),\text{\texttt{delete-min}},\text{\texttt{delete-min}},\text{\texttt{insert}}(a_1)\big)\), where key(_a<sub>i</sub>_)=_i_, a valid output is (_a_<sub>5</sub>_,a_<sub>1</sub>). Alternatively, we could ask to reveal the unordered list of all _deleted_ elements, which would be (_a_<sub>3</sub>_,a_<sub>2</sub>_,a_<sub>4</sub>). It is essential that the heap state is revealed only at the end. If we could match each `delete-min` to the identity of the deleted element, the sorting lower bound would apply, implying a logarithmic lower bound on the cost of operations.

We call this problem _heap evaluation_; it can be seen as a restricted mode of operation of heaps, natural in applications with limited data dependency, i.e., where intermediate operations are relevant only inasmuch as they affect the final state of the data structure. The task can of course be solved in O(_n_log_n_) time by running a standard online heap. The question we ask (and answer) in this paper is whether faster, i.e., O(_n_)-time algorithms are possible by taking advantage of the offline nature of the problem. We observe that even the easier problem of reporting the _minimum_ of the heap in the end was not known to be solvable faster than O(_n_log_n_).

The heap evaluation problem can be seen as a generalization of the _selection problem_, as follows. If the two types of operations are _separated_, i.e., if the sequence consists of _n_ `insert` operations followed by _d_ `delete-min` operations, then the task amounts to finding the _d_ smallest elements in a list of length _n_. This is a classical problem, well known to be solvable in deterministic O(_n_) time, e.g., by the celebrated _median-of-medians_ algorithm [BFP<sup>+</sup>73]. Algorithms for selection, however, do not seem applicable to the general problem when `insert` and `delete-min` operations are interleaved in more intricate ways (say, if each `delete-min` is preceded by two insertions).

It can be observed that the information-theoretic lower bound for sorting does not transfer to our problem. Indeed, since the number of different outputs is at most 2_<sup>n</sup>_ (subsets of the inserted elements), the classical argument can at best yield a lower bound of log<sub>2</sub>(2_<sup>n</sup>_)=_n_ on the decision-tree complexity of the problem. For other problems of a similar flavor, e.g., for _element distinctness_ or _longest increasing subsequence_, an \(\Omega(nlogn)\) lower bound can be shown in more restricted comparison or algebraic decision-tree models [Ben83, Fre75]. These results employ adversary arguments or invoke topological properties of the solution space. Transferring such techniques to the heap evaluation problem is ruled out by our first result: an optimal, linear-time algorithm.

**Theorem 1.1** (Heap evaluation)**.** _There is a deterministic comparison-based algorithm that, given a sequence_ S _of_ `insert` _and_ `delete-min` _operations on an initially empty heap, computes the (unordered) list of elements remaining in the heap after the execution of_ S _in_ O(|S|) _total time._
