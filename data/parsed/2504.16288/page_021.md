For the supersequence relation, given the \(\varepsilon\)NFA _A_ with state set _Q_ and string _w_, we want to compute the minimum or maximum length of a supersequence of _w_ accepted by _A_. We build in time _O_(|_w|m_) the product graph _G<sub>A,w</sub>_ and for each transition \((q,b,q')\) in _A_ with \(b\in\Sigma\) and each \(i\in\{0,\dots,|w|\}\) we add a _b_-labelled edge with weight 1 from (_i,q_) to \((i,q')\). These additional edges are called _extra-edges_ in the following. Note that the extra-edges mimic the \(\varepsilon\)-transitions added in the \(\varepsilon\)NFA _A_sub defined in Section 3, relative to the original \(\varepsilon\)NFA _A_. We call this graph _G_ and note that it can be constructed in time _O_(|_w|m_). Let the source _s_ of _G_ be (0_,q_<sub>0</sub>) and let the target _t_ be (|_w|,q_<sub>f</sub>).

Let us describe the _st_-paths in _G_. For any supersequence _u_ of _w_ accepted by _A_, we can build a path of weight |_u_| from _s_ to _t_ in _G_ following an accepting run \(\rho\) over _w_ of the \(\varepsilon\)NFA _A_sub. More precisely, when \(\rho\) takes a transition \((q,x,q')\) of _A_sub and the prefix _w_\[1:_i_\] for \(i\in\{0,1,\dots,|w|\}\) has already been consumed before the transition \((q,x,q')\), then there are two possible cases. Either \((q,x,q')\) is an original transition of _A_, in which case we take the corresponding _x_-labelled edge from (_i,q_) to \((i+1,q')\) of _G_ if _x=w_\[_i_+1\], and the corresponding \(\varepsilon\)-labelled edge from (_i,q_) to \((i,q')\) of _G_ if \(x=\varepsilon\). Or \((q,x,q')\) is not a transition of _A_, which means that it has to be an \(\varepsilon\)-transition \((q,\varepsilon,q')\), in which case we take an extra-edge from (_i,q_) to \((i,q')\) of _G_. Note that for any \(\varepsilon\)-transition \((q,\varepsilon,q')\) of _A_sub that is not a transition of _A_, there are several (and at least one) transitions \((q,b,q')\) with \(b\in\Sigma\) in _A_; thus, there are also several (and at least one) extra-edges from (_i,q_) to \((i,q')\) that differ in their labels. We take just any of those extra-edges. The weight of this _st_-path corresponds to the number of edges labelled by a symbol from \(\Sigma\) (i.e., transitions in \(\rho\) that read symbols from \(\Sigma\) and that are transitions of _A_) plus the number of extra-edges (i.e., the number of \(\varepsilon\)-transitions of _A_sub in \(\rho\) that are not transitions in _A_). Thus, the weight of the _st_-path is |_u_| and its label is _u_.

Conversely, given an _st_-path in _G_, we can build an accepting run \(\rho\) of _A_sub over _w_ by taking the transitions corresponding to the edges of _G_. More precisely, for every _x_-labelled edge of _G_ from (_i,q_) to \((i+1,q')\) or from (_i,q_) to \((i,q')\) that is not an extra-edge, we take the transition \((q,x,q')\) of _A_sub, and for every extra-edge of _G_ from (_i,q_) to \((i,q')\) labelled with some \(b\in\Sigma\), we take the transition \((q,\varepsilon,q')\) of _A_sub (such a transition exists by definition of _A_sub). This witnesses that _w_ has a supersequence accepted by _A_, namely the label _u_ of the _st_-path in _G_. The number of such symbols, which is the length of _u_, is the weight of the _st_-path; and the string _u_ is the label of the _st_-path.

This means that the lengths of supersequences of _w_ accepted by _A_ correspond to the weights of _st_-paths in _G_. Thus, we conclude with Lemma 5.2 that we can solve the min- and max-variant of the matching problem for the supersequence relation, including the computation of witnessing strings, in time _O_(|_w|m_).
