1. _H is a MVCS subgraph of G_<sub>1</sub>_,...,G<sub>n</sub>._<tbc>

_2._ _K is a maximum clique within the set_ K.

Now consider Task (2), where we want to find a _connected_ MVCS of the graphs _G_<sub>1</sub>_,G_<sub>2</sub>_,...,G<sub>n</sub>_. To recall, edges \(\{(u,v),(u',v')\}\) in _G_<sub>1</sub>⋆_G_<sub>2</sub> are formed by either edges \(\{u,u'\}\in E(G_1)\) and \(\{v,v'\}\in E(G_2)\), or non-edges \(\{u,u'\}\notin E(G_1)\) and \(\{v,v'\}\notin E(G_2)\). Thus, we can additionally label the edges \(\{(u,v),(u',v')\}\) as `type-1` if they are based on edges in the factors, or as `type-0` otherwise. Assume now that we have found a clique _K_ in _G_<sub>1</sub>⋆_G_<sub>2</sub> that may consist of both `type-0` and `type-1` edges. In \[11\], such edges are also called _c_-edges (connected edges) and d- edges (disconnected edges). If there exists a _uv_-path in \(K\subseteq G_1\star G_2\) that consists only of `type-1` edges, it is easy to verify that there is a _p<sub>i</sub>(u)p<sub>i</sub>(v_)-path in _G<sub>i</sub>_ for \(i\in 1,2\). We call a clique _K `type-1` connected_ if, for all \(u,v\in V(K)\), there exists such a `type-1` edge _uv_-path. For `type-1` connected cliques \(K\subseteq G_1\star G_2\), it holds that _p<sub>i</sub>(K_) is a connected subgraph of _G<sub>i</sub>_ for \(i\in\{1,2\}\). This generalizes naturally to more than two factors. Hence, if we restrict our attention to `type-1` connected cliques, we can reuse the approach from Task (1) to obtain all _connected_ MVCS of _G_<sub>1</sub>_,...,G<sub>n</sub>_.
