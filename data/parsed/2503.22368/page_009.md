<tbc>The relationship between `type-A` connected common subgraphs of _G<sub>i</sub>_ and _G<sub>j</sub>_ and the respective similarity between _G<sub>i</sub>_ and _G<sub>j</sub>_ is shown in Figure 1. To avoid getting the plot scattered by outliers we decided to streamline the plot by adding pairs of graphs from M into buckets. To be more precise, let \(\kappa_{ij}\) be one of the four normalized similarity measures VH, WL, NSDP or minmax between \(G_i,G_j\in\mathcal{M}\). Moreover, let _m<sub>ij</sub>_ be the number of inclusion-maximal cliques in the product _G<sub>i</sub>⋆G<sub>j</sub>_ and, therefore, the number of `type-A` connected common subgraphs of _G<sub>i</sub>_ and _G<sub>j</sub>_ induced by inclusion-maximal `type-A` connected cliques in _G<sub>i</sub>⋆G<sub>j</sub>_. We now define a set of 1,000 buckets, _B_={_b_<sub>1</sub>_,...,b_<sub>1000</sub>} where each bucket \(b_\ell:=\{m_{ij}|\kappa_{ij}\in[\ell\cdot 0.001+0.005,\ell\cdot 0.001-0.005]\}\) contains the the value _m<sub>ij</sub>_ whenever the respective similarity \(\kappa_{ij}\) between _G<sub>i</sub>_ and _G<sub>j</sub>_ is contained in the prescribed interval. In other words, the buckets serve as a sliding window along the x-axis in which we collect the pairs of graphs _G<sub>i</sub>_ and _G<sub>j</sub>_ based on their values _m<sub>ij</sub>_ and \(\kappa_{i,j}\). For each nonempty bucket \(b_\ell\) we compute the pair \((x_\ell,y_\ell)=(\ell\cdot 0.001,\frac{\sum_{m\in b_\ell} m}{|b_\ell|})\). The value \(x_\ell=\ell\cdot 0.001\) is a representative of the similarity values \(\kappa_{ij}\in[\ell\cdot 0.001+0.005,\ell\cdot 0.001-0.005]\) while \(y_\ell\) is the average of the number of inclusion-maximal `type-A` connected cliques of in the product of pairs of graphs _G<sub>i</sub>_ and _G<sub>j</sub>_ whose value _m<sub>ij</sub>_ is contained the bucket \(b_\ell\). The plot showing the pairs \((x_\ell,y_\ell)\) computed over all pairs of graphs in M is provided in Figure 1. Roughly spoken, Figure 1 shows the average number of inclusion- maximal `type-A` connected cliques in the product _G⋆H_ (and, therefore, the average number of `type-A` connected common subgraphs of _G_ and _H_ induced by those cliques in _G⋆H_) and the respective similarity value between _G_ and _H_ for all pairs \(G,H\in\mathcal{M}\).

Figure 1 illustrates a clear relationship between the VH kernel, the WL kernel, the NSPD kernel, and the minmax similarity with the number of common subgraphs. Specifically, higher similarity values correspond to a greater number of common subgraphs induced by inclusion-maximal cliques in the respective products. These observations highlight that these similarity measures are indeed beneficial for the methods proposed in the paragraph _Ordering of Input Graphs_, which rely on such measures.
