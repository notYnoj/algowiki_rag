<tbc>In the inductive case, _B_ is a non-leaf bag with children _B_<sub>1</sub>_,...,B<sub>r</sub>_. Let \(\phi:G_B\mapsto H\). First, we show that the the monomial corresponding to \(\phi\) in Equation 1 is generated by the gate \(g_{B,\phi^\prime}\) as in Equation 3 where \(\phi':G[B]\mapsto H\) is the unique homomorphism such that \(\phi'\sim\phi\). Similarly, let \(\phi_i:G_{B_i}\mapsto H\) be the unique homomorphisms such that \(\phi_i\sim\phi\) for \(1\le i\le r\). Let \(\phi'_i:G[B\cap B_i]\mapsto H\) be the unique homomorphisms such that \(\phi'_i\sim\phi_i\) for \(1\le i\le r\). By the induction hypothesis, \(h_{B_i,\phi_i^\prime}\) will contain the monomial corresponding to \(\phi_i\) (notice that \(\phi|_{B\cap B_i}=\phi'_i\)). For this choice of \(\phi'_i\) for all \(1\le i\le r\), the first factor in the product in Equation 3 will include all \(y_{\phi(v)}\) where _v_ is local to some bag in _T<sub>B</sub>_ that is not _B_. The second factor will include all \(y_{\phi^\prime(v)(=\phi(v))}\) for _v_ that is local to _B_. Therefore the monomial corresponding to \(\phi'=\phi\) in the summation of Equation 1 is generated at the gate Equation 3. Observe that since the decomposition of \(\phi\) into \(\phi'\) and \(\phi_i\) for \(1\le i\le r\) in the above argument is unique, the monomial corresponding to \(\phi\) is also generated exactly once.

We now show that all monomials generated by the gate \(g_{B,\phi^\prime}\) as in Equation 3 correspond to valid homomorphisms. Observe that inductively the contribution of the first factor in all monomials generated correspond to valid homomorphisms \(\phi_i:G_{B_i}\mapsto H\). By the connectivity property of tree decompositions, any vertex _v_ appearing in both _G<sub>B<sub>i</sub></sub>_ and _G<sub>B<sub>j</sub></sub>_ for \(i\neq j\) must also appear in _B_. Therefore, \(\phi_i(v)=\phi_j(v)=\phi'(v)\) ensuring that any monomial produced at this gate corresponds to a valid homomorphism.

The gates \(h_{B,\phi}\) are correct because we compute them in exactly the same way we defined them in Equation 2.

### Time complexity of the construction:

We now prove that the running-time of the construction algorithm is \(\widetilde{O}(n^pm^q)\). Since the pattern graph _G_ is fixed, there are at most _O(n<sup>p</sup>m<sup>q</sup>_) pairs \((B,\phi)\) where \(\phi:G[B]\mapsto H\). The gates \(g_{B,\phi}\) are constructed by simply iterating over all such pairs and the amount of work done per gate is logarithmic in the size of the input. This is because give \((B,\phi)\), we can compute the indices of the _h_ gates in constant-time and then look up \(h_{B_i,\phi|_{B\cap B_i}}\) in _O_(log_n_)-time. Notice that the size of the circuit is _O(n<sup>p</sup>m<sup>q</sup>_) as each wire in the circuit is incident on some \(g_{B,\phi}\) which has only constant number of inputs and feeds its output to exactly one _h_ gate.

## 4 Using Homomorphisms for Finding Induced Subgraphs

Our algorithms are generalizations of algorithms for detecting small induced patterns given by Bläser, Komarath, and Sreenivasaiah \[1\] and Komarath, Kumar, Mishra, and Sethia \[7\]. For completeness, we describe the general method here.

Using inclusion-exclusion, the induced subgraph polynomial (Definition 8) can be expressed in terms of subgraph polynomials (Definition 9) as

\(\mathrm{Ind}_{G,H}=\sum_{G^\prime\supseteq G}(-1)^{|E(G^\prime)|-|E(G)|}\alpha_{G,G^\prime}\mathrm{Sub}_{G^\prime,H},\)

where \(\alpha_{G,G^\prime}\) denotes the number of subgraphs of \(G'\) that are isomorphic to _G_.
