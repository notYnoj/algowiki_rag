<tbc>We now establish the result stated in the lemma. Let \((\hat{\mathbf{d}},\hat{\mathbf{x}},\hat{\mathbf{y}})\) denote a feasible solution of \(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}\). The previous equations extend as follows for solutions of \(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}\):

\(\hat{d}_i^m=d(m_i,\mathbf{x}^m)-\sum_{\rho\in P}\hat{y}(\rho)\omega_i^m(\rho),\hat{d}_j^w=d(w_j,\mathbf{x}^m)-\sum_{\rho\in P}\hat{y}(\rho)\omega_j^w(\rho).\) (17)

Now consider the feasible solution (**d**,**x**,**y**) of P defined by \(y(\rho)=1\) if \(\hat{y}(\rho)\ge 0.5\), and 0 otherwise. The feasibility of (**d**,**x**,**y**) comes from the fact that \(\{\rho:\hat{y}(\rho)\ge 0.5\}\) is a closed set of rotations. Indeed, note that constraints 16 ensures that \(y(\rho')\le y(\rho)\) for all \(\rho<\rho'\). We have:

\(\hat{d}_i^m-\frac{1}{2}d_i^m=d(m_i,\mathbf{x}^m)-\sum_{\rho\in P}\hat{y}(\rho)\omega_i^m(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}(d(m_i,\mathbf{x}^m)-\sum_{\rho\in P}y(\rho)\omega_i^m(\rho))\)<br>

\(\ge\frac{1}{2}d(m_i,\mathbf{x}^m)\ge 0\)

as \(0\le(\hat{y}(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}y(\rho))\) for all \(\rho\in P\) and \(\omega_i^m(\rho)\le 0\) for all \(i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\) and \(\rho\in P\). Hence, \(\hat{d}_i^m\ge\frac{1}{2}d_i^m\) for all \(i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\).

Similarly, for women we have:

\(\hat{d}_j^w-\frac{1}{2}d_j^w=d(w_j,\mathbf{x}^m)-\sum_{\rho\in P}\hat{y}(\rho)\omega_j^w(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}(d(w_j,\mathbf{x}^m)-\sum_{\rho\in P}y(\rho)\omega_j^w(\rho))\)<br>
\(=\frac{1}{2}d(w_j,\mathbf{x}^m)-\sum_{\rho\in P}(\hat{y}(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}y(\rho))\omega_j^w(\rho)\)<br>
\(\ge\frac{1}{2}(d(w_j,\mathbf{x}^m)-\sum_{\rho\in P}\omega_j^w(\rho))\)

as \((\hat{y}(\rho)-\frac{1}{2}y(\rho))\le 0.5\) for all \(\rho\in P\) and \(\omega_j^w(\rho)\ge 0\) for all \(j\in\{1,\dots,n\}\) and \(\rho\in P\). By combining the inequalities obtained for men and women, we obtain that \(\hat{\mathbf{d}}\ge\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{d}\), which concludes the proof.
