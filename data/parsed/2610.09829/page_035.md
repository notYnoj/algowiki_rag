**Lemma 5.6** (Proved in Section 5.4)**.** _Consider we are given_ \((G,w,k,d,\ell)\), T _and a d-diverse_ \(\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}\)_, a non-leaf node_ \(\alpha\) _of_ T _, an integer h\>_1_, an_ \(\alpha\)_-lineage-free set_ \(R=\{\gamma_i|i\in[b]\}\)_, integers_ \(h_1,h_2,\dots,h_b\ge 1\) _with_ \(\sum_{i=1}^bh_i=h-1\)_, and for_ \(i\in[b]\), \(\theta_i\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,R,\gamma_i}\)_. Furthermore, we are given the values_ \(\Pi_\mathcal{P}(\alpha',h')\)_, for each_ \(\alpha'\in V(\mathcal{T}_\alpha)\backslash\{\alpha\}\) _and_ \(1\le h'\le k\)_. Then, in polynomial time, we can do the following:_

1. _correctly return_ NO_, indicating that there is no solution for_ \(\Pi_{\mathcal{P},\alpha,h}(\gamma_i,h_i,\theta_i)_{i\in[b]}\)_, or_<tbc>

_2._ _return a set_ \(\{\beta_i|i\in[b]\}\)_, such that if there is a solution for_ \(\Pi_{\mathcal{P},\alpha,h}(\gamma_i,h_i,\theta_i)_{i\in[b]}\)_, then there is an optimal solution_ C _for_ \(\Pi_{\mathcal{P},\alpha,h}(\gamma_i,h_i,\theta_i)_{i\in[b]}\) _such that for each_ \(i\in[b]\), \(\beta_i\in\mathcal{C}\) _and_ \(\gamma_i\) _is the_ \((\alpha,\mathcal{C})\)_-representative of_ \(\beta_i\).

We are ready to prove Lemma 5.5.

_Proof of Lemma 5.5._ We have an instance (_G,w,k,d,l_) of Planar DSC, its LSCT, T , rooted at _C<sub>r</sub>_, and a _d_-diverse \(\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}\). We now describe an algorithm to do the stated task in the lemma statement.

Consider a node \(\alpha\) of T and an integer _h_, where \(1\le h\le k\) (we will do the stated step for every choice of \(\alpha,h\), in bottom-up fashion). Recall our sanity check and discussed base case are correct and they can be applied in polynomial time. Therefore, we now assume that \(\alpha\) is a non-leaf node of T , \(\alpha\) is _d_-diverse from P, and _h\>_1. Also, we assume that for each descendant \(\alpha'\) of \(\alpha\) in \(\mathcal{T}\backslash\{\alpha\}\) and any \(1\le h'\le k\), we have already correctly computed \(\Pi_\mathcal{P}(\alpha',h')\). For each \(b\in[h-1]\), let \(\mathcal{H}_b=\{(h_1,h_2,\dots,h_b)\in[h-1]^b|\sum_{i\in[b]}h_i=h-1\}\), \(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,b}=\{R\subseteq\mathcal{M}_\alpha|R\text{is}\alpha\text{-lineage-free},|R|=b\}\). Furthermore, for \(R\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,b}\), let \(\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,R}=\{(\theta_1,\theta_2,\dots,\theta_b)|\theta_i\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,R,\gamma_i},i\in[b]\}\). We initialize \(M=\emptyset\).

We do the following iteration over each \(b\in[h-1]\), \((h_i)_{i\in[b]}\in\mathcal{H}_b\), \(R=\{\gamma_i|i\in[b]\}\in\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,b}\), \((\theta_i)_{i\in[b]}\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,R}\). Let \(\mathsf{ans}(\gamma_i,h_i,\theta_i)_{i\in[b]}\) be the answer returned by Lemma 5.6, for \((G,w,k,d,\ell)\), T,P, \(\alpha,h\), \(R,(h_i)_{i\in[b]},(\theta_i)_{i\in[b]}\). If \(\mathsf{ans}(\gamma_i,h_i,\theta_i)_{i\in[b]}=\mathsf{NO}\), then we do nothing (and move to the next iteration).

Else, let \(\mathsf{ans}(\gamma_i,h_i,\theta_i)_{i\in[b]}=\{\beta_i|i\in[b]\}\), and we do the following. We construct a number which essentially will help us eliminate the double counting of edges (outside of edges in P) that appear in two distinct cycles from \(\{\alpha\}\cup\{\beta_i|i\in[b]\}\). (Note that no three cycles from \(\{\alpha\}\cup\{\beta_i|i\in[b]\}\) can intersect due to Observation 5.4. Let \(E'\) be the set of edges from cycles in \(\{\beta_i|i\in[b]\}\cup\{\alpha\}\), that appear in at least two of these cycles, i.e., \(E'=\cup_{\beta,\beta^\prime\in\{\alpha\}\cup\{\beta_i|i\in[b]\},\beta\neq\beta^\prime} E(\beta)\cap E(\beta')\). Also, let \(\varkappa=w(E'\backslash E(\mathcal{P}))\). We next define a term, which is essentially counting the weight of edges of cycles in optimal solutions for \(\Pi_\mathcal{P}(\beta_i,h_i)\), \(i\in[b]\), the cycle \(\alpha\), and the paths in P. Note the weight of paths in P is accounted for, in each \(\Pi_\mathcal{P}(\beta_i,h_i)\) (and not in \(\varkappa\)). We insert the number \(w(E(\alpha)\backslash E(\mathcal{P}))-(b-1)\cdot w(\mathcal{P})+\sum_{i\in[b]}\big(\Pi_\mathcal{P}(\beta_i,h_i)\big)-\varkappa\) to _M_.

Finally, we set \(\Pi_\mathcal{P}(\alpha,h)=\mathrm{max}_{x\in M}x\) (empty maximum is \(-\infty\)).

## Running time analysis.

From Item 4 the number of nodes in T is bounded by a polynomial in _n_, and the number of distinct choices of _h_ is bounded by _k_. Now consider a fixed \(\alpha\in V(\mathcal{T})\), \(h\in[k]\), and \(b\in[h-1]\). Since the number of ordered partitions of a positive integer _x_ into non-zero integers is 2<sup>_x_-1</sup>, we have that |H_<sub>b</sub>_| is bounded by 2<sup>_O(k_)</sup>. Additionally, by Observation 5.2, \(|\mathcal{R}_{\alpha,b}|\) is bounded by 2<sup>_O(k_)</sup>. Using Proposition 3.1 and Observation 5.5 we can obtain that \(|\mathcal{S}_{\alpha,R}|\) is bounded by 2<sup>_O(k_)</sup>. Moreover, the above sets can be enumerated in the amount of time proportional to their sizes (with a multiplicative factor of a polynomial in log_n_). Also, Lemma 5.6 is a polynomial time procedure. Every other stated step in the algorithm can be executed in polynomial time. Thus, the running time is bounded by \(2^{O(k)}\cdot n^{O(1)}\).
