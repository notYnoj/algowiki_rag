There has been growing interest in finding _diverse_ collections of solutions to combinatorial optimization problems from the perspective of parameterized complexity. The paper [2] initiated this line of research, and [15] developed a general framework for diverse collections in matroids and graphs. Eiben, Koana, and Wahlström [12] developed algebraic techniques based on determinantal sieving that apply to various problems in the diverse-solutions settings, although their methods are not applicable to our problem. Other general techniques were recently proposed by Kumabe [20] and Ashok et al. [1]. However, our problem does not fit into this framework as in our case, the diversity measures and the size of the diverse objects (that is, the girth) are unbounded. In the context of cuts, the papers [7, 8] studied diverse minimum _s-t_ cuts and diverse solutions in problems with a distributive lattice structure. More recently [9] gave an FPT algorithm for finding diverse minimum _s-t_ cuts. Our problem Diverse Shortest Cycle Coverage contributes to this line of work, where the objects of interest are shortest cycles and diversity is measured by the weight of pairwise edge intersections; on planar graphs, this is equivalent to diversity among global minimum cuts in the dual graph.

## 2 Overview of our methods

### 2.1 Proof of Theorem 1.1

The reduction in [4] establishes W[1]-hardness of deciding, given a graph _G_ and integers _k_ and _L_, whether _G_ contains _k_ vertex- (or edge-) disjoint cycles of length at most _L_ each. In this reduction, _L_ may be larger than the girth of _G_, and this flexibility is crucial. In the case of shortest cycles, however, all cycles must have length exactly equal to the girth of _G_. This tight length constraint requires substantially more intricate arguments, even though we reuse some ideas from [4].

Theorem 1.1 is proved by reducing from the W[1]-hard Multicolored Clique problem. This problem asks, given a graph _G_ whose vertices are partitioned into _h_ sets _V_<sub>1</sub>_,...,V<sub>h</sub>_, called _color classes_, whether _G_ has a _multicolored_ clique of size _h_, that is, a clique containing exactly one vertex from each color class. The parameter of Multicolored Clique is the size of the solution size _h_. The key ingredients of our reduction are the constructions of _vertex_ and _edge_ selection gadgets which are used to encode the selection of exactly one vertex in each color class and exactly one edge between every pair of color classes of the input instance of Multicolored Clique. Then we combine these gadgets to encode the instance of Multicolored Clique. To construct vertex and edge selection gadgets, we select a sufficiently large (with respect to the size of _G_) integer \(L=2\ell\) which will be the girth of the constructed graph, and then build gadgets as unions of overlapping packings of cycles of length _L_.

The crucial difference between the W[1]-hardness proof in [4] and our result is that the edge selection gadget allows us to work with shortest cycles, while [4] has to relax the length constraint. For the edge selection gadget _F_, we assume that the number of edges between each pair of color classes is the same integer _m_. Then _F_ contains _m_ 4-tuples of _L_-length cycles.
