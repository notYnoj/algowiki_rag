# LYCHEE: Certified Screening of Inverted Lists for High-Recall Vector Search

Dongfang Zhao<br> University of Washington, Tacoma School of Engineering and Technology<br> dzhao@uw.edu

**Abstract**

Nearest-neighbor search over embedding vectors now matches faces against watchlists, transactions against known fraud patterns, and medical images against confirmed cases, where a missed neighbor is a missed threat, fraud, or diagnosis. Because an approximate index cannot report which queries lost a true neighbor, these applications require recall of 0.999 to 0.9999, which cuts the misses of recall 0.99 by 10 to 100 times, at a cost far below that of exact search over billions of vectors. Conventionally, inverted-file indexes reach high recall by probing more lists. However, they read and score every probed vector at full precision, so that the throughput of a query falls in inverse proportion to the number of probed lists. On the other hand, graph indexes are fast at moderate recall but saturate below the high-recall targets, whereas quantized lists must rerank their candidates with the full vectors. Our insight is to decide for almost every probed vector that it cannot enter the top _k_ of the probed lists without reading the vector, through the certified lower bound of exact search by certified exclusion. This paper turns the above insight into LYCHEE, a GPU inverted-file search that screens the probed lists on tensor cores under a threshold from anchors of the same lists, and that computes exact distances only for the few vectors that survive the screening, in increasing order of their certified bounds. We prove that LYCHEE returns exactly the _k_ nearest vectors of the probed lists, which is the answer of an inverted-file search at full precision, while it reads a few hundred full vectors per query regardless of the number of probed lists. We evaluate LYCHEE on eleven datasets from one million to one billion vectors against ten GPU and CPU baselines on two GPU servers. At equal recall with 200 probed lists, LYCHEE is 9.7 to 117 times faster than IVF-Flat of cuVS on the nine datasets of up to 100 million vectors, and 65 times faster on Deep-100M at recall 0.9997 with 1,000 probed lists. LYCHEE also reaches recall levels that CAGRA and HNSW do not reach, and it outruns exact search by certified exclusion by 5 to 18 times on the datasets of more than ten million vectors. On four GPUs, LYCHEE searches one billion vectors at recall 0.9999 and is 44.5 times faster than IVF-Flat on BIGANN-1B.

### 1 Introduction

Nearest-neighbor search over embeddings increasingly decides outcomes in which a missed neighbor has a direct impact on real-world applications. In security, face recognition [12, 50] searches a watchlist for the nearest face embedding, so that a missed neighbor allows a wanted person to pass the check. In health care, retrieval of medical images [39] supports a diagnosis with the confirmed cases nearest to the image of a patient, so that a missed case withholds evidence from the physician. In finance, fraud and anti-money-laundering screening matches transactions against known fraud patterns and sanctioned parties, where a missed match is both a loss and a compliance violation. Legal discovery [24], the deduplication of training data [1, 35], and retrieval-augmented generation [36] over dense passage retrieval [28, 33, 34] similarly need every relevant document, near-duplicate, or passage.

These applications need recall far above the 0.9 to 0.95 at which approximate indexes are usually compared, because the errors of an approximate index are silent. An index at recall 0.99 loses about one true neighbor in a hundred without reporting which queries lost one, so that no operator can detect or repair the miss. A screening service that

answers one million identification queries per day at recall 0.99 therefore misses about ten thousand true matches per day. Since the misses scale with one minus the recall, recall 0.9999 reduces them to about one hundred. Ranking quality follows the same trend: on MS MARCO [40], the mean reciprocal rank (MRR@10) of the retrieved passages rises from 0.3295 at recall 0.957 to 0.3373 at recall 0.997, against 0.3378 for the exact neighbors (Section 4.2). Exact search with CertEx [64] removes the misses, but its cost grows in proportion to the dataset, whereas the embedding datasets of these applications, such as DINOv2 [45], CLIP [47], and DataComp [17], hold millions to billions of vectors. These applications therefore need recall of 0.999 to 0.9999 at the cost of an approximate index, which lies at the expensive end of its recall and throughput trade-off.

The inverted-file (IVF) index [32, 53], as used in Faiss [14, 31] and cuVS [42], is the standard partition index for this trade-off, which splits the dataset into lists by _k_-means. A query then scans the vectors of its _np_ nearest lists, whose recall grows with _np_ because the true neighbors of a query near a list boundary lie in neighboring lists. However, IVF- Flat reads and scores every probed vector at full precision. On ImageNet DINOv2 embeddings, the throughput of IVF- Flat in cuVS falls from 562,367 to 3,450 queries per second
