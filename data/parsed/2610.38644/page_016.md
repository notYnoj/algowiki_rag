## 4.4 Singularly perturbed problem

Figure 3 illustrates the predictor-residual-corrector behavior for the singularly perturbed boundary-value problem with \(\nu=10^{-3}\). The coarse predictor captures the outer solution but under-resolves the narrow boundary layer near _x_=1. This is reflected by the independent residual indicator, which remains negligible over most of the interval and rises sharply only in the boundary-layer region. As a result, the adaptive strategy assigns 97 blocks to the low level, one to the mid level, and only two to the high level.

The predictor uses 200 PDE collocation points and yields an RMSE of \(5.95\times 10^{-3}\) with a maximum error of \(1.82\times 10^{-1}\). After residual-guided enrichment, the corrector uses 250 PDE points and reduces the RMSE to \(1.76\times 10^{-3}\) and the maximum error to \(6.50\times 10^{-2}\). Thus, the adaptive corrector improves the RMSE by a factor of 3.38 and the maximum error by a factor of 2.80 relative to a budget-matched uniform discretization using the same 250 PDE points. The uniform reference remains essentially unchanged from the coarse predictor, with an RMSE of \(5.95\times 10^{-3}\), showing that the improvement is due to where the additional constraints are placed rather than simply to their number.

The adaptive set uses only 10.42% of the fully enriched budget obtained by assigning 24 PDE points to every block. The predictor kernel assembly and spectral solve require approximately 0.047 and 0.030 s, respectively, while residual probing and level assignment require about 0.191 s in the recorded run. The adaptive corrector requires approximately 0.090 s for kernel assembly and 0.034 s for the spectral solve, giving a total CFKS corrector solve time of 0.123 s. These results show that the residual-adaptive stage concentrates additional constraints in the boundary-layer region while leaving the underlying CFKS representation unchanged.

For context, the same singularly perturbed steady advection-diffusion problem with \(\nu=10^{-3}\) was considered by De Florio et al. \[24\]. Their experiments used _N_<sub>int</sub>=10<sup>4</sup> interior collocation points for all methods, while the accurate ELM-based PIELM and X-TFC configurations also employed 10<sup>4</sup> hidden neurons at this parameter value. In contrast, the present CFKS predictor uses only 200 PDE collocation points, and the residual-adaptive corrector increases this number to 250 by concentrating additional points near the localized boundary layer. Thus, rather than increasing the global representation and collocation budgets, CFKS exploits the independently evaluated residual to allocate resolution only where it is needed.
