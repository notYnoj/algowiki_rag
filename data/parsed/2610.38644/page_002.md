A particularly relevant line of development begins with radial-basis-function (RBF) collocation. Kansa introduced meshfree PDE solvers based on finite RBF expansions with explicitly prescribed centers [2, 3]. Subsequent work provided approximation-theoretic foundations and clarified limitations of unsymmetric collocation systems [4, 5]. Symmetric and Hermite formulations subsequently incorporated differential and boundary operators directly into kernel collocation by applying the corresponding operators to both arguments of a positive-definite kernel, thereby constructing symmetric operator-based collocation matrices [6].

In parallel, Gaussian-process and neural-network theory established the correspondence between feature representations and kernels. Infinite-width neural models may converge to Gaussian processes [7], while Gaussian-process theory relates continuous basis-function representations to covariance kernels [8]. In such representations, a deterministic kernel can be constructed by averaging products of basis functions over a prescribed distribution of feature parameters. We refer to the resulting kernel as the _continuum-feature mean kernel_. Neither infinite-feature limits nor continuum Gaussian-basis representations are new in themselves. The question addressed in this work is how to evaluate the bounded Gaussian-RBF mean kernel and its differential-operator counterparts analytically and employ them directly in PDE solvers with prescribed physical constraints.

Random-feature methods exploit the converse direction by approximating this mean kernel using a finite sampled feature map [9]. This idea has been transferred to PDEs through random-feature methods that retain a finite randomized trial space and solve only for output coefficients [10]. More recent work has considered localized, high-order, and operator-aware variants, including residual-Christoffel sampling for finite operator-applied feature spaces [11] and analytic partition-of-unity constructions for high-order PDEs [12]. Kernel and Gaussian-process methods have likewise been applied directly to differential operators, probabilistic collocation, and structurally constrained boundary-value problems [13, 14, 15].

The present work connects these developments through _Continuum-Feature Kernel Solver (CFKS)_, based on the exact bounded mean kernel induced by a Gaussian RBF feature family. Unlike finite RBF representations, which require a prescribed number of features and their individual center locations, CFKS replaces the finite feature realization with analytical averaging over a prescribed distribution of centers. This produces a deterministic continuum-feature mean kernel, eliminating finite-feature sampling variability while retaining the Gaussian widths and center distributions as prescribed parameters.

An important property of this construction is that differential operators can be applied directly to the mean kernel. The resulting operator-applied kernels are equivalent to analytically averaging the products of the corresponding operator- applied Gaussian features. Consequently, the kernel matrices required for PDE collocation can be constructed without explicitly generating finite RBF features. For Gaussian RBFs with centers uniformly distributed over bounded intervals or boxes, the mean kernel admits a closed-form expression involving a squared-exponential factor and error-function terms that account for the bounded center support [8].

For linear PDEs, CFKS uses the continuum-feature mean kernel and its operator-applied counterparts to construct symmetric strong-form collocation systems. Boundary and initial conditions can be enforced through a null-space parameterization, separating the satisfaction of physical constraints from the interior PDE approximation. For nonlinear PDEs, analytical kernel derivatives enable direct evaluation of the nonlinear residual and its Jacobian. Linearized solves provide initialization for nonlinear least-squares residual minimization, while analytical trial-function parameterizations can enforce the prescribed physical conditions. Problems exhibiting localized structures can be addressed through multiscale continuum-feature kernels and residual-guided collocation enrichment. These extensions are demonstrated using viscous Burgers’ equation with a two-stage predictor–corrector strategy.

## Research gap

Despite substantial developments in RBF collocation, kernel methods, Gaussian-process formulations, and random-feature PDE solvers, two methodological gaps motivate the present work:

• Finite RBF and random-feature PDE methods generally require a prescribed number of features and an explicit realization of their centers, whereas conventional kernel collocation typically starts from a prescribed kernel. Although continuum-feature limits are well established, the direct construction of PDE solvers from analytically marginalized bounded Gaussian-RBF feature distributions, including the corresponding differential-operator kernels, remains comparatively underexplored.
