Unlike the linear transport experiments, however, the Burgers corrector combines residual-guided collocation enrichment with narrower prescribed Gaussian widths in a global-local multiscale kernel. The observed accuracy improvement therefore reflects their combined effect, and the present experiment does not separately quantify the contribution of either refinement mechanism.

The conditioning and timing results also clarify the computational characteristics of CFKS. The regularization and Gaussian-width study for the first-order ODE confirms that the accuracy and conditioning of the discrete operator-kernel system depend on these prescribed numerical parameters. In the steady linear benchmarks, the main computational costs are associated with analytical kernel assembly and dense linear algebra, while null-space construction contributes comparatively little to the measured solve times. For the slab-based transport problems, residual evaluation, dense solution prediction, and temporal transfer can require more computational time than the core kernel coefficient solves. Nonlinear problems introduce additional costs through repeated residual and Jacobian evaluations during iterative coefficient optimization. Consequently, the overall computational efficiency depends on both the kernel discretization and the numerical procedures used for refinement, diagnostics, and time advancement.

Taken together, these findings support CFKS as a deterministic computational framework connecting finite Gaussian- RBF feature representations with analytically constructed continuum-feature kernel PDE solvers. Its principal representational advantage is the elimination of the finite feature count and individual center-sampling variability, rather than the elimination of all numerical parameter choices. Gaussian widths, bounded center distributions, collocation configurations, regularization, and, where applicable, nonlinear initialization remain important aspects of the numerical formulation. The experiments demonstrate the flexibility of this approach across the considered benchmarks, while also identifying parameter selection, conditioning, and computational scalability as important considerations for broader applications.

**Remarks**

• **Scope of nonlinear validation.** The nonlinear CFKS formulation is demonstrated numerically using viscous Burgers’ equation at \(\nu=0.01\). Although the analytical kernel derivatives and nonlinear least-squares formulation are applicable to other sufficiently smooth nonlinear differential operators, their numerical accuracy, robustness, and convergence behavior have not been systematically investigated across broader classes of nonlinear PDEs. The reported Burgers corrector modifies both the collocation distribution and the prescribed multiscale kernel widths, so their individual effects cannot be distinguished from the present results. Further numerical studies involving different nonlinear operators, parameter regimes, and separate refinement strategies are needed to assess the generality and limitations of the nonlinear extension.<tbc>

• **Kernel parameters and numerical stability.** Although analytical marginalization removes finite-feature sampling variability, CFKS remains dependent on prescribed Gaussian widths, center distributions, and regularization parameters. The conditioning study illustrates the influence of Gaussian width and regularization on a representative linear problem, but does not establish a universally optimal parameter-selection rule. For problems containing localized structures, the appropriate kernel scales must also be considered in relation to the spatial and temporal resolution. In sequential space-time-slab computations, the accuracy of transferring the terminal solution between slabs provides an additional numerical consideration. Systematic parameter- selection procedures and conditioning-aware kernel refinement therefore remain worthwhile directions for further investigation.<tbc>

• **Computational scalability.** CFKS avoids explicit finite Gaussian-RBF feature realizations but retains the computational demands of dense kernel discretizations. For a linear system involving _N_ constraints, storing the full operator Gram matrix requires O(_N_<sup>2</sup>) memory, while direct dense factorization may require O(_N_<sup>3</sup>) operations. Nonlinear formulations additionally require repeated evaluation of the PDE residual and its Jacobian. Sequential space-time-slab marching limits the size of individual temporal problems, and residual- adaptive collocation can concentrate resolution in selected regions, but neither mechanism removes the general scalability limitations of dense kernel systems. Low-rank kernel approximations, matrix-free iterative methods, efficient residual evaluation, and localized or domain-decomposed formulations are potential approaches for extending CFKS to larger-scale applications.
