<tbc>Since the above recurrence relation correctly solves the offline _k_-set problem, every path in the DAG represents a _k_-set, and every possible _k_-set can be represented by a path of the DAG. We have |_M_|=|_E_|=2_k(n-k_)+_n_ edges which are the components of our new representation. The losses of the edges going from the node (_i,j_) to the nodes (_i_-1_,j_) and (_i_-1_,j_-1) are 0 and \(\ell_i\), respectively. Note that the loss associated with each edge is upper-bounded by 1. Most crucially, the sum of the losses of the predicted _k_-set is linear in the losses of the edges and the unit-flow polytope has _O(k(n-k_)) facets.

### Regret Bounds.

The number of _k_-sets is \(\mathcal{N}=\genfrac(){pt}{2}{n}{k}\). Also note that each path representing a _k_-set consists of exactly _D=n_ edges and its loss is bounded by _k_. Thus, using Theorem 10, EH achieves a regret bound of \(\mathcal{O}(k\left(logn\right)^\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{L^*})\). Moreover, since |_E_|=_O(k(n-k_)), using Theorem 14, CH achieves a regret bound of \(\mathcal{O}(n^\frac{1}{2}\left(logk(n-k)\right)^\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{L^*})\).

### Remark.

The convex hull of the _k_-sets in its original space, known as _capped probability simplex_, is well-behaved. This polytope has _n_+1 facets and the exact relative entropy projection to this polytope can be found efficiently (Warmuth and Kuzmin, 2008). Thus applying CH in the original space will result in more efficient algorithm with better bounds of \(\mathcal{O}(k^\frac{1}{2}\left(logn\right)^\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{L^*})\). Nevertheless, an efficient implementation of the EH algorithm can be obtained via our online dynamic programming framework. Interestingly, in the special case of the _k_-set, the regret bounds of EH is also \(\mathcal{O}(k^\frac{1}{2}\left(logn\right)^\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{L^*})\) (**?**).
