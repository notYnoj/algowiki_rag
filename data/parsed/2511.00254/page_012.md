**Lemma 3.7.** _Let_ (_G,H,u,d_) _be a facial multiflow instance. Let_ \((H_F:F\in\mathcal{F})\) _be a facial decomposition, and let x be a feasible flow satisfying the nested supports property. For every_ \(F\in\mathcal{F}\)_, let u<sub>F</sub> be the capacity vector defined by u<sub>F</sub>(e_)=_x<sup>F</sup>(e_) _and let d<sub>F</sub> be the demand vector d restricted to H<sub>F</sub>. If for every_ \(F\in\mathcal{F}\)_, the instance_ (_G,H<sub>F</sub>,u<sub>F</sub>,d<sub>F</sub>_) _has a feasible uncrossed solution y<sup>F</sup>, then_ \(y=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}}y^F\) _is a feasible uncrossed solution for_ (_G,H,u,d)._

_Proof._ The feasibility of _y_ is clear from the construction of the subproblems. Also observe that, by construction, _y_ still satisfies the nested supports property (since _G_\[_y,F_\] is a subgraph of _G_\[_x,F_\] for each face _F_ of _G_).

We prove that _y_ is uncrossed assuming each _y<sup>F</sup>_ is uncrossed. It remains to check for distinct faces _F_<sub>1</sub>_,F_<sub>2</sub> and \(P_1\in\mathrm{supp}(y^{F_1}),P_2\in\mathrm{supp}(y^{F_2})\), that _P_<sub>1</sub> and _P_<sub>2</sub> do not cross.

Consider two distinct faces _F_<sub>1</sub>_,F_<sub>2</sub>. Let \(P_1\in\mathrm{supp}(y^{F_1}),P_2\in\mathrm{supp}(y^{F_2})\). Let _h_<sub>1</sub>=(_s_<sub>1</sub>_,t_<sub>1</sub>)_,h_<sub>2</sub>=(_s_<sub>2</sub>_,t_<sub>2</sub>) be the demands routed by _P_<sub>1</sub>_,P_<sub>2</sub> respectively. Suppose _P_<sub>1</sub> and _P_<sub>2</sub> cross, for the sake of contradiction. Consider the (simple) cycle _C_ in _G_\[_y,F_\] obtained as \(C=P_1\bullet(s_1,v_{F_1})\bullet(v_{F_1},t_1)\), where \(\bullet\) denotes concatenation. Since _P_<sub>1</sub> and _P_<sub>2</sub> cross, _P_<sub>2</sub> has some segment in the open exterior of _C_ and some segment in the open interior of _C_. However, since _C_ is a simple cycle, these two segments of _P_<sub>2</sub> must lie in distinct faces of _G_\[_y,F_\], contradicting that _y_ satisfies the nested supports property.

Using Theorem 3.7, we are able to reduce to finding a feasible uncrossed flow for each instance (_G,H<sub>F</sub>,u<sub>F</sub>,d<sub>F</sub>_) in isolation. No matter how we do this, we can combine the flows with Theorem 3.7. Importantly, this means that to tackle the instance (_G,H<sub>F</sub>,u<sub>F</sub>,d<sub>F</sub>_), we are able to re-commit multi-faced demands that were originally committed to _F_ to other faces. Now, we give the main result.

**Theorem 3.8.** _Let_ (_G,H_) _be pairwise-planar and let_ (_G,H,u,d_) _be a congestion instance. If the instance is feasible, there exists a feasible uncrossed flow._

_Proof._ We prove the result by induction on |_E(H_)|. If |_E(H_)|=1 then the instance is single-source and the result follows, so suppose \(|E(H)|\ge 2\). Let \(\mathcal{H}=(H_F:F\in\mathcal{F})\) be an arbitrary facial decomposition.

We first consider the case that at least two faces _F_ have that _H<sub>F</sub>_ is non-empty. We obtain a feasible flow _x_ for (_G,H,u,d_) that satisfies the nested supports property for H by way of Theorem 3.6. For each face _F_, let _u<sub>F</sub>,d<sub>F</sub>_ be defined as in the statement of Theorem 3.7. Each instance (_G,H<sub>F</sub>,u<sub>F</sub>,d<sub>F</sub>_) is feasible via the flow _x<sup>F</sup>_. Additionally, since there are at least two faces _F_ such that _H<sub>F</sub>_ is non-empty, we have |_E(H<sub>F</sub>_)|_\<|E(H_)| for every face _F_. Hence by induction, there is a feasible uncrossed flow _y<sup>F</sup>_ for each instance (_G,H<sub>F</sub>,u<sub>F</sub>,d<sub>F</sub>_). Then by Theorem 3.7, the sum of these flows is a feasible uncrossed flow for (_G,H,u,d_).

Now, we consider the case where _H<sub>F</sub>_ is empty for all faces _F_ except one, call it _F_<sup>∗</sup>. That is, _E(H_)=_E(H_<sub>_F_<sup>∗</sup></sub>), and in particular every demand fits _F_<sup>∗</sup>. If none of the demands cross within _F_<sup>∗</sup>, then the layout is fully-planar and we are done. Otherwise, there exist \(h,h'\in E(H)\) such that \(h,h'\) cross when drawn in the face _F_<sup>∗</sup>. However, since (_G,H_) is pairwise-planar, there exists another face \(F'\neq F^*\) such that at least one of these two demands, without loss of generality say \(h'\), also fits \(F'\). Now we define a facial decomposition \(\widehat{\mathcal{H}}=(\widehat{H}_F:F\in\mathcal{F})\) as follows. We define \(E(\widehat{H}_{F^*})=E(H)\backslash\{h'\}\), \(E(\widehat{H}_{F^\prime})=\{h'\}\), and \(E(\widehat{H}_F)=\emptyset\) for all other faces _F_. That is, we now view \(h'\) as being associated with \(F'\) rather than _F_<sup>∗</sup>.

We obtain a feasible flow _x_ for the instance (_G,H,u,d_) satisfying the nested supports property for this new facial decomposition \(\widehat{\mathcal{H}}\) by way of Theorem 3.6. We define capacities _u<sub>F</sub>,d<sub>F</sub>_ for each face _F_ as in the statement of Theorem 3.7 using the new decomposition \(\widehat{\mathcal{H}}\). Each instance \((G,\widehat{H}_F,u_F,d_F)\) is feasible. Additionally, each satisfies that \(|E(\widehat{H}_F)|<|E(H)|\), and thus \((G,\widehat{H}_F,u_F,d_F)\) has a feasible uncrossed flow _y<sup>F</sup>_ by induction. Then the sum of these flows is a feasible uncrossed flow for (_G,H,u,d_) by Theorem 3.7.

With a little extra effort, we can modify Theorem 3.7 and Theorem 3.8 to show that pairwise-planar instances admit strongly uncrossed flows.
