## 5.1 Clockwise Orderings and Sectors

Let _f_ be a strongly uncrossed multiflow for planar congestion instance (_G,H,u,d_). Let P be the set of support paths, and let P_<sup>h</sup>_ be the set of support paths routing demand \(h\in E(H)\). Fix a strongly uncrossed parallelization \(\phi\). We parallelize the edges and perform disk-expansion at each node (see Section 2.2) so that the paths become node-disjoint. Then, for each commodity \(h=(s,t)\in E(H)\), we add a node and edges into the disk corresponding to _s_ to join the endpoints of the paths for _h_, and similarly for the disk of _t_. This can be done in a planar fashion for all demands simultaneously since the flow is strongly uncrossed (see Section 2.4 and Figure 7). We denote the new graph \(G_\phi\). For a path \(P\in\mathcal{P}\), we let \(\widehat{P}\) be its corresponding path in \(G_\phi\). For \(P,Q\in\mathcal{P}^h\), \(\widehat{P}\) and \(\widehat{Q}\) are internally node-disjoint paths that share their endpoints. For \(P,Q\in\mathcal{P}\) routing different demands, \(\widehat{P}\) and \(\widehat{Q}\) are (completely) node-disjoint.

Consider a commodity \(h=(s,t)\in E(H)\). We use the notation \(G_\phi[h]\) to mean the subgraph graph of \(G_\phi\) induced by the paths \(\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^h=\left\{\widehat{P}:P\in\mathcal{P}^h\right\}\). In \(G_\phi\), the paths of \(\widehat{P}^h\) have a clockwise cyclic ordering about _s_, say \(\widehat{P}_1,\dots,\widehat{P}_k\). Informally, a _sector_ is the region between consecutive paths in the clockwise ordering. We make this more precise as follows.

Consider two paths \(\widehat{P}_i\) and \(\widehat{P}_j\) in \(G_\phi\). Orient \(\widehat{P}_i\) from _s_ to _t_, and orient \(\widehat{P}_j\) from _t_ to _s_. The directed curve \(\widehat{P}_i\bullet\widehat{P}_j\), obtained by concatenating these oriented curves, is a simple closed (Jordan) curve, and partitions the plane into two regions. We define \(\mathrm{CW}^\circ(\widehat{P}_i,\widehat{P}_j)\) to be the set of points _q_ in \(\mathbb{R}^2\backslash(\widehat{P}_i\cup\widehat{P}_j)\) such that _q_ is "to the right" of \(\widehat{P}_i\bullet\widehat{P}_j\); that is, if we draw a directed curve from _q_ to \(\widehat{P}_i\bullet\widehat{P}_j\) (avoiding \(\widehat{P}_i\bullet\widehat{P}_j\) except at the extremity), it hits \(\widehat{P}_i\bullet\widehat{P}_j\) from the right. We let \(\mathrm{CW}(\widehat{P}_i,\widehat{P}_j)\) denote the closed region \(\mathrm{CW}^\circ(\widehat{P}_i,\widehat{P}_j)\cup\widehat{P}_i\cup\widehat{P}_j\). We analogously define \(\mathrm{ACW}^\circ(\widehat{P}_i,\widehat{P}_j)\) and \(\mathrm{ACW}(\widehat{P}_i,\widehat{P}_j)\) by taking the opposite orientation. Any curve that does not cross and is internally disjoint from \(\widehat{P}_i\) and \(\widehat{P}_j\) either stays entirely within \(\mathrm{CW}(\widehat{P}_i,\widehat{P}_j)\) or stays entirely within \(\mathrm{ACW}(\widehat{P}_i,\widehat{P}_j)\).

Now, for our clockwise-ordered family of paths \(\widehat{P}_1,\dots,\widehat{P}_k\), we refer to \(\mathrm{CW}^\circ(\widehat{P}_i,\widehat{P}_{i+1})\) for _i_=1_,...,k_ (addition modulo _k_) as the _open sectors_ of _f<sup>h</sup>_, and define the _closed sectors_ similarly using \(\mathrm{CW}(\widehat{P}_i,\widehat{P}_{i+1})\). The open sectors partition \(\mathbb{R}^2\backslash(\widehat{P}_1\cup\cdots\cup\widehat{P}_k)\). One sector contains the infinite face of \(G_\phi[h]\); we call this the _outer sector_ of _h_, and the others the _inner sectors_ of _h_. The _outer paths_ of _h_ are the two paths \(P,Q\in\mathcal{P}^h\) whose corresponding paths \(\widehat{P},\widehat{Q}\in\widehat{\mathcal{P}}^h\) define the outer sector of _h_.

Now consider an edge _e_ of the original graph _G_ that appears in P_<sup>h</sup>_. If _e_ lies in any outer path of P_<sup>h</sup>_, it is an _outer edge_ of _h_, otherwise it is an _inner edge_ of _h_. For our congestion result, we exploit the following key properties.
