_Proof._ Assume \(\mathsf{OPT}_\beta(s)\le\mathsf{opt}\le 2\mathsf{OPT}_\beta(s)\).

Let _U_<sup>∗</sup> be the subset of _U_ not covered by C. By Observation 3.2, there are at most \(R=\frac{C}{c}\mathsf{opt}\le\frac{2C}{c}\mathsf{OPT}_\beta(s)\) sets in C. It follows that _U_<sup>∗</sup> is generated by removing up to _R_ sets of elements from _U_. Let W be the collection of all valid subsets of _U_ that can be formed by removing the elements of at most _R_ sets chosen from S. The number of such configurations is bounded by<tbc>

\(|\mathcal{W}|\le\sum_{i=0}^R\genfrac(){pt}{2}{|\mathcal{S}|}{i}\le\sum_{i=0}^R\genfrac(){pt}{2}{n}{i}\le n^R\)

<tbc>Let _W_ be any such subset of _U_ where |_W|\>|U|/_2. Our goal is to show that the probability that _U_<sup>∗</sup>=_W_ is very small, as this will imply that PartialVoteCover is unlikely to have returned a C that leaves _\>|U|/_2 elements uncovered. Because _K_ consists of \(C\cdot\mathsf{opt}lnn\) independent uniform samples from _U_, the expected number of samples falling into _W_ is

\(\mathbb{E}[|W\cap K|]=\frac{|W|}{|U|}C\cdot\mathsf{opt}lnn>\frac{C}{2}\mathsf{opt}lnn\ge\frac{C}{2}\mathsf{OPT}_\beta(s)lnn\)

Since _U_<sup>∗</sup>=_W_ would necessarily imply that \(|W\cap K|=|U^*\cap K|\), the probability that _U_<sup>∗</sup>=_W_ is bounded above by the probability that \(|W\cap K|=|U^*\cap K|\). However, notice that \(U^*\cap K\) is exactly the set of samples in _K_ not covered by C. By Observation 3.4, the number of such samples must be strictly less than \(c\cdot\mathsf{OPT}_\beta(s)lnn\).

Applying a Chernoff bound, the probability that \(|W\cap K|\) deviates so far below its expectation is

\(Pr[|W\cap K|<c\cdot\mathsf{OPT}_\beta(s)lnn]\le Pr\left[|W\cap K|<\left(1-\frac{C-2c}{C}\right)\cdot E[|W\cap K|]\right]\)<br>
\(\le exp\left(-\frac{(C-2c)^2}{4C}\mathsf{OPT}_\beta(s)lnn\right)\)

We now union bound this failure probability over all possible valid configurations \(W\in\mathcal{W}\)<tbc>

\(Pr\left[\exists W\in\mathcal{W}:|W|>\frac{|U|}{2}\wedge|W\cap K|<c\cdot\mathsf{OPT}_\beta(s)lnn\right]\le n^Rexp\left(-\frac{(C-2c)^2}{4C}\mathsf{OPT}_\beta(s)lnn\right)\)

<tbc>Substituting \(R\le\frac{2C}{c}\mathsf{OPT}_\beta(s)\), this probability is bounded by

\(exp\left(\left(\frac{2C}{c}-\frac{(C-2c)^2}{4C}\right)\mathsf{OPT}_\beta(s)lnn\right)\)

We can make the exponent arbitrarily negative by choosing _c_ and _C_. For instance, let _C_=3_c_. The exponent coefficient simplifies to \(\frac{6}{1}-\frac{c^2}{12c}=6-\frac{c}{12}\). By choosing \(c\ge 84\), the coefficient is less than or equal to -1. Thus, the probability of failure is at most \(exp(-\mathsf{OPT}_\beta(s)lnn)\le 1/n\).

This proves that, with high probability, \(U^*\neq W\) for any _W_ with _\>|U|/_2, i.e., PartialVoteCover must terminate having covered at least half of the elements in _U_.
