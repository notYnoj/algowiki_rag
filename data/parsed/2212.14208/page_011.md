**5. Numerical results.** In this section we report results for several examples which have been considered in the literature. The general setup for all experiments is as follows: We report residual norm plots for both, the FMR and the FGAL method. In each iteration, the systems with the hermitian positive definite part _H_ are solved approximately using the CG method, asking for the initial \(\ell_2\)-norm of the residual to be reduced by a factor of \(\varepsilon_\mathrm{cg}\). We typically vary \(\varepsilon_\mathrm{cg}\) from 10<sup>-1</sup> up to 10<sup>-12</sup>. In light of Proposition 4.4 and the discussion preceding it, the FGAL and FMR iteration is stopped once the _H_<sup>-1</sup>-norm of the initial residual is reduced by \(\varepsilon_f=10^{-12}\). The case \(\varepsilon_\mathrm{cg}=10^{-12}\) in the approximate solves may thus actually be considered as a equivalent to exact solves in our context.

The residual norm plots report (relative) approximate _H_<sup>-1</sup>-norms of the residual, i.e. the quantity \(\sqrt{\widehat{r}^*r}\) where _r_ is the current residual and \(\widehat{r}\) is the result of CG when solving \(H\widehat{r}=r\) with initial guess 0 and a required reduction of the residual \(\ell_2\)-norm of \(\varepsilon_\mathrm{cg}\). This number can be somewhat off the exact _H_<sup>-1</sup>-norm; the advantage is that it is computed with an effort comparable with what we need to update an iterate. When the iterations stopped, we double-checked that the targeted decrease of the _H_<sup>-1</sup>-norm was indeed achieved by recomputing \(\sqrt{\widehat{r}^*r}\) for the final residual with now \(\varepsilon_\mathrm{cg}=10^{-12}\) in the CG method which computes \(\hat{r}\). Finally, as an illustration, our plots also give the bound on the _H_<sup>-1</sup> norm of the residuals of the FMR iterates according to Proposition 4.4. Let us stress that, as opposed to the approximate _H_<sup>-1</sup>-norms, these bounds can be computed at virtually no extra cost, so that in a production setting we could base a stopping criterion for the FMR iterations merely on these bounds.

We start reconsidering the example in \[27\].

_Example 1._ Consider the convection-diffusion equation

\(-\Delta u(x,y)+au_x(x,y)=b(x,y),\)

on \(\Omega=[0,1]\times[0,1]\) with Dirichlet boundary conditions. We discretize on a uniform square mesh with \(127\times 127\) interior grid points, using central finite differences in both, \(\Delta u\) and _u<sub>x</sub>_, and choose _a_=10<sup>4</sup>. The right hand side _b_ was chosen to have random entries.

Figure 1 shows convergence plots for four different choices of \(\varepsilon_\mathrm{cg}\). We see that for all \(\varepsilon_\mathrm{cg}\) the residual norms of the FGAL and the FMR iterates differ only marginally, with the FMR residuals, as is to be expected due to their (approximate) minimization property, being (slightly) smaller and decreasing in a smoother manner. We also see that the bound from Proposition 4.4 captures the actual convergence behavior quite accurately. With \(\varepsilon_\mathrm{cg}=10^{-1}\), we already need only about twice as many iterations as with "exact" solves, \(\varepsilon_\mathrm{cg}=10^{-12}\), while an approximate solve with \(\varepsilon_\mathrm{cg}=10^{-1}\) requires about 50 CG iterations on average as opposed to about 470 CG iterations on average for \(\varepsilon_\mathrm{cg}=10^{-12}\).

_Example 2._ This is the matrix treated in section 6.2 of \[9\]. It is based on a discretized Stokes problem obtained by using the _Q_<sub>1</sub>-_Q_<sub>1</sub> finite element discretization of the unsteady channel domain problem in IFISS \[22\]. This example was treated for two different grid parameters in \[9\]. We only consider the larger of the two, i.e grid parameter 10, resulting in a linear system of size _n_=3,151,875, and we took a step size of \(\tau/2=10^{-3}\) in the midpoint Euler rule.

The results are reported in Figure 2. To solve the systems with _H_ we use preconditioned CG with the ILU(0) preconditioner. In the solves for _H_, every about 12 preconditioned CG iterations reduce the norm of the residual by another factor of 10<sup>-1</sup>.
