<tbc>_Proof._ The set \(\Gamma=\{x_0\}\cup\bigcup_i\overline{W^s(s_i)}\) is a bouquet of _k_ circles embedded in _M_ (the loop number _i_ is formed by the two separatrices entering _s<sub>i</sub>_). Every point of \(M\backslash\Gamma\) lies on a trajectory with \(\omega\)-limit _y_<sub>0</sub>, so \(M\backslash\Gamma\) is the basin of attraction of the sink, which is an open disk. Hence \(\Gamma\) is a cellular embedding with a single face, and the boundary of this face, read along \(\partial U(x_0)\) and the chords, is exactly the traversal (3) with the signs defined above.

**Dependence on choices.** The diagram depends on the choice of the disk _U(x_<sub>0</sub>), of the initial point on \(\partial U(x_0)\), of the orientation of \(\partial U(x_0)\) (we do not fix an orientation of _M_), and of the numbering of the saddles. Changing them results, respectively, in an isotopy of the diagram, a cyclic shift of the code, a reversal, and a relabeling of the chords; the signs are unchanged by the first three operations and are carried along with their chords by the last one. Thus every optimal Morse flow on a closed surface induces a well-defined equivalence class of signed one-cycle chord diagrams (Section 3). Conversely, every equivalence class of one-cycle diagrams is realized by an optimal flow (Kybalko et al., 2018, Theorem 3).

## 5 Dual Chord Diagrams and Trajectory Cycles

We formalize the dualization procedure (formerly termed inverse diagrams) under the identical-label code format.

### 5.1 Tracing the chord cycle

Informally: start on a boundary arc between two chord endpoints and move counterclockwise; on reaching an endpoint of chord _i_, jump along the chord to its opposite endpoint; then continue along _S_<sup>1</sup> in the same direction if \(\sigma_i=+1\), and in the reversed direction if \(\sigma_i=-1\); repeat until the starting arc is reached again in the same direction.

Formally, for a position _p_ let \(\pi(p)\) be the position of the second endpoint of the chord _c<sub>p</sub>_. A _state_ is a pair \((p,\varepsilon)\) with \(p\in\mathbb{Z}_{2k}\) and \(\varepsilon\in\{+1,-1\}\): the walker stands at the point _p_ and is about to move along the boundary arc towards the neighbouring point \(p+\varepsilon\). One step is

\((p,\varepsilon)\longmapsto\big(\pi(q),\varepsilon\sigma_{c_q}\big),q=p+\varepsilon,\) (3)

where _q_ is called the _arrival point_. The tracing starts in the state (0,+1), i.e. on the arc right after the first element of the code, and stops when this state recurs. The arrival points _q_<sub>1</sub>_,...,q<sub>m</sub>_ define the _visit sequence_ **w**=(_c_<sub>_q_<sub>1</sub></sub>_,...,c<sub>q<sub>m</sub></sub>_).

**Algorithm 1** Tracing the chord cycle

Require: code **c**, signs \(\sigma\)<br>
\((p,\varepsilon)\leftarrow(0,+1)\); \(Q\leftarrow[]\triangleright\) list of arrival points<br>
repeat<br>
   \(q\leftarrow(p+\varepsilon)mod2k\); append q to Q<br>
   \(\varepsilon\leftarrow\varepsilon\cdot\sigma_{c_q}\); \(p\leftarrow\pi(q)\)<br>
until \((p,\varepsilon)=(0,+1)\)<br>
return Q

The map (3) is a permutation of the 4_k_ states, so the loop terminates in at most 4_k_ steps.

**Definition 5.1.** A _chord cycle_ is the closed traversal path generated by (3). A diagram is a _one-cycle diagram_ if |_Q_|=2_k_ and every label 1_,...,k_ occurs exactly twice in **w**; equivalently, a single traversal covers all chords twice and all 2_k_ boundary arcs.

For \(\sigma\equiv+1\) the cycle passes every chord once in each direction. For a chord with \(\sigma_i=-1\) both passes may arrive at the same endpoint, so the arrival points need not be pairwise distinct. For a one-vertex one-face map with _k_ edges the Euler characteristic is 2-_k_; hence one-cycle diagrams with all signs +1 exist only for _k_=2_g_ (orientable surface of genus _g_), and one-cycle diagrams with at least one sign -1 describe non-orientable surfaces of genus _k_.
