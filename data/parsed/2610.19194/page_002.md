Chord diagrams, that is, perfect matchings of points on a circle, are also a classical object of enumerative combinatorics. The numbers of chord diagrams and of gluings of polygon sides into surfaces of given genus were studied by Harer and Zagier Harer and Zagier (1986), and an analogue of their formula for unicellular bicolored maps was obtained in Adrianov (1997). Counting chord diagrams of a given genus up to rotations was done by Cori and Marcus (1998), who in particular treated the maximal case, that is, diagrams of genus _g_ with 2_g_ chords; Krasko (2017) extended this count to the full dihedral group. The orientable one-cycle diagrams of the present paper are exactly these maximal diagrams. Enumeration of chord diagrams of other types, motivated by Vassiliev knot invariants, and of diagrams up to rotation and reflection is given in Stoimenow (2000); Khruzin (2000). Chord diagrams were further used as an invariant of functions on surfaces by Kadubovsky, who classified functions with one essentially critical point on oriented surfaces with boundary by means of color chord diagrams Kadubovsky and Klimchuk (2003), studied the realization and topological equivalence of functions with a single saddle point on oriented surfaces and counted non-equivalent functions for surfaces of prime-related genus Kadubovskyi (2006), and computed the numbers of non-equivalent minimal functions on closed oriented and non-orientable surfaces Kadubovskyi (2015). In a series of papers by Kadubovskyi and his co-authors, two-color chord diagrams with a prescribed number of faces are enumerated under the action of the cyclic and the dihedral groups, with explicit formulas and tables of values Kadubovskyi and Sypchuk (2020); Kadubovskyi et al. (2021). Our one-cycle diagrams belong to the same circle of enumeration problems, but we treat the orientable and the non-orientable case, all genera and the pairing with duals within a single algorithm.

The chord diagram construction for optimal flows and the duality that corresponds to time reversal are due to Kybalko et al. (2018). The aim of the present paper is to make this theory algorithmic. In contrast to the classical notation, in which the two endpoints of a chord carry different indices, each chord here receives one label \(i\in\{1,\dots,k\}\) that occurs twice along the circle, which gives a canonical chord code of length 2_k_. We recall the connection between one-cycle chord diagrams, Reeb graphs and embeddings of one-vertex graphs, and we give an explicit cycle-tracing procedure, a canonical form of an equivalence class, a construction of the dual diagram, and an enumeration algorithm for all one-cycle signed chord diagrams with a given number of chords. The algorithms are implemented in Python and archived together with visualization tools on Zenodo Prishlyak (2026a,b).

We describe an algorithm that, for given _g_, enumerates all one-cycle chord diagrams and identifies the pairs of dual diagrams, corresponding to the flows _X_ and -_X_, together with the self-dual diagrams, whose flows are equivalent to their reverses.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 collects the necessary facts on Morse-Smale flows, optimal Morse flows and topological equivalence. Section 3 introduces signed chord diagrams, the code with identical endpoint labels and the canonical form of an equivalence class. Section 4 recalls the construction of the chord diagram of an optimal flow and shows that it is a one-cycle diagram. Section 5 treats the chord cycle and the dual diagram, and Section 6 describes the enumeration algorithm, its implementation and the numerical results.
