## 3 Signed Chord Diagrams and Canonical Encoding

We formalize chord diagrams using a unified endpoint labeling system suitable for algorithmization.

**Definition 3.1.** A _chord diagram D_ with _k_ chords consists of a boundary circle _S_<sup>1</sup>, 2_k_ distinct points on _S_<sup>1</sup>, and _k_ chords connecting these points in pairs (chords may cross when drawn in the disk).

In our encoding scheme:

• Each chord is assigned a unique identifier \(i\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}\).<tbc>

• Both endpoints of chord _i_ are assigned the same integer label _i_.<tbc>

• Traversing _S_<sup>1</sup> counterclockwise from a fixed origin yields a sequence **c**=(_c_<sub>0</sub>_,c_<sub>1</sub>_,...,c_<sub>2_k_-1</sub>) of length 2_k_ in which each integer from 1 to _k_ appears exactly twice; positions are indexed by Z<sub>2_k_</sub>.

**Definition 3.2.** A code **c** is in _Restricted Growth String (RGS) canonical form_ if _c_<sub>0</sub>=1, and for every _m_ with \(1\le m<k\) the first occurrence of _m_ precedes the first occurrence of _m_+1. For an arbitrary sequence **w** in which every label occurs twice, its _RGS normalization_ relabels the chords in order of first occurrence.

The RGS codes of length 2_k_ are in bijection with the perfect matchings of 2_k_ points; there are (2_k_-1)!! of them.

**Definition 3.3.** A _signed chord diagram_ (or marked chord diagram) assigns a sign \(\sigma_i\in\{+1,-1\}\) to each chord \(i\in\{1,\dots,k\}\).

**Definition 3.4.** Two signed chord diagrams _D_<sub>1</sub>_,D_<sub>2</sub> are _isomorphic_ if one can be transformed into the other via a cyclic shift of the code and a relabeling of the chords (signs are carried along with their chords). They are _equivalent_ if they can additionally be related by a reversal (mirror reflection) of the boundary circle. We write \(D_1\simeq D_2\) for equivalence.

### 3.1 Canonical form of an equivalence class

Let G be the dihedral group of order 4_k_ generated by the cyclic shift and the reversal of the code. For \(g\in\mathcal{G}\) the diagram \(g\cdot D\) is obtained by applying _g_ to the code, taking the RGS normalization, and carrying the signs along with their chords (shifts and reversals do not change signs; relabeling permutes them). The _canonical form_ of _D_ is the lexicographic minimum

\(\mathrm{can}(D)=\mathrm{min}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}g\cdot D.\) (2)

Two signed diagrams are equivalent iff their canonical forms coincide. It is computed in _O(k_<sup>2</sup>) time (4_k_ images, each normalized in _O(k_)). If G is replaced by its cyclic subgroup of order 2_k_ (shifts only), the same procedure yields isomorphism classes. The orbit of _D_ consists of 4_k/_|Stab(_D_)| distinct normalized codes; the stabilizer is the group of symmetries of _D_.

## 4 Construction of a Chord Diagram from an Optimal Flow

The construction of a chord diagram from an optimal Morse flow follows Kybalko et al. (2018); we recall it here in the notation of Section 4.

Let _M_ be a closed connected surface (orientable or not) and _X_ an optimal Morse vector field on _M_, i.e. a Morse flow with a single source _x_<sub>0</sub>, a single sink _y_<sub>0</sub>, and _k_ saddle points _s_<sub>1</sub>_,...,s<sub>k</sub>_. By Theorem 2.8, the number of saddles is \(k=2-\chi(M)\), that is, _k_=2_g_ if _M_ is orientable of genus _g_ and _k_ equals the non-orientable genus otherwise.

**Neighborhood of the source.** Let _U(x_<sub>0</sub>) be a small closed disk around _x_<sub>0</sub> whose boundary \(\partial U(x_0)\cong S^1\) is transversal to the trajectories of _X_ leaving _x_<sub>0</sub>; each such trajectory meets \(\partial U(x_0)\) exactly once.

**Chords.** For each saddle _s<sub>i</sub>_, the set _W<sup>s</sup>(s<sub>i</sub>_)\\{_s<sub>i</sub>_} consists of two trajectories (separatrices) issuing from _x_<sub>0</sub> and entering _s<sub>i</sub>_. Hence <o>_W<sup>s</sup>(s<sub>i</sub>_)</o> meets \(\partial U(x_0)\) in exactly two points. Stable manifolds of distinct saddles are disjoint, so these _k_ pairs of points are pairwise disjoint on \(\partial U(x_0)\), which gives 2_k_ distinct points. Joining the two points of each pair by a chord inside the circle (the chords may cross each other in the picture) and labeling it by _i_ yields a chord diagram with _k_ chords.

**Signs.** Let _V<sub>i</sub>_ be a regular neighborhood of \(U(x_0)\cup\overline{W^s(s_i)}\), i.e. the disk _U(x_<sub>0</sub>) with one band attached along the two points of the chord _i_. If _V<sub>i</sub>_ is orientable (an annulus), put \(\sigma_i=+1\); if _V<sub>i</sub>_ is a Möbius band, put \(\sigma_i=-1\). (In terms of the marking, +1 corresponds to an unmarked and -1 to a marked chord.)

**Proposition 4.1.** _The signed chord diagram D<sub>X</sub> constructed above is a one-cycle diagram. Its chord cycle is the boundary of the basin of attraction of the sink y_<sub>0</sub>.
