the authors of \[19\] proposed a polynomial-time algorithm for MWIS on _P_<sub>6</sub>-free graphs. This breakthrough strengthened the general belief that MWIS is polynomial-time solvable on _P<sub>k</sub>_- free graphs, for any constant _k_. Very recently, the authors of \[9\] designed a similar technique to tackle \(\mathrm{max}\)-(\(\mathrm{tw}\le t,\varphi\)) on _P_<sub>6</sub>-free graphs in polynomial-time for constant _t_. Furthermore, _K_<sub>2_,q_</sub>-induced-minor-free graphs were characterized thanks to PMCs, enabling MWIS to be solved in polynomial time in this graph class \[11\].

Another application of PMCs is the study of the Minimum fill-in problem: can a graph _G_ become chordal with the add-on of at most _k_ edges? This question is naturally related to PMCs as it deals with triangulations. As for Treewidth, Bouchitté and Todinca \[5\] proved that, given a graph _G_ with its lists of minimal separators \(\Delta_G\) and PMCs \(\Pi_G\), we can solve Minimum fill-in in time \(O(n^2\cdot|\Delta_G|\cdot|\Pi_G|)\). This was later improved \[12\] with a running time \(O(n^3|\Pi_G|)\), depending only on list \(\Pi_G\). Again, PMC enumeration stands as a bottleneck for solving Minimum fill-in. From a parameterized point of view, an exact subexponential parameterized algorithm was proposed \[17\], with a running time \(O(2^{O(\sqrt{k}\mathrm{log}k)}+k^2nm)\). It enumerates, as a subroutine, a list of certain PMCs called _vital_ PMCs.

From a more practical viewpoint, Korhonen presented a few years ago an experimental study of the enumeration algorithms based on PMCs in \[21\]. It confirmed that going through PMC enumeration is among the most effective methods to solve Treewidth, outperforming declarative approaches. Furthermore, the PMC-based algorithm evoked earlier for Minimum fill-in obtains the best performance on benchmarks, defeating integer-programming alternatives. The latter conclusion also holds for PMC-based approaches on other applications: computing generalized and fractional _hypertreewidth_, identifying the _perfect phylogeny_, or determining the _treelength_. As a last remark, another work proposed alternative practical techniques to compute the list of PMCs, using SAT and ASP declarative solvers \[23\].

## 1.2 Enumeration algorithms

Let us dive now into the details of PMC enumeration. Several results have been stated above, however the algorithm BT-enum \[6\] stands as the reference for enumerating PMCs in general graphs, even in practice \[21\]. We recall that its time complexity is \(\tilde{O}(|\Delta_G|^2)\), without any guarantee on the delay between two solution outputs (all PMCs might be returned to the very end of the execution of the algorithm). BT-enum uses simple but insightful observations on PMCs. It is a recursive algorithm: given a graph _G_, it begins with a recursive call on graph _G_\\{_a_} for some arbitrary vertex \(a\in V\) . Indeed, given the list of PMCs of _G_\\{_a_}, one can obtain all PMCs of _G_ following certain rules, recalled below.

On one hand, Bouchitté and Todinca \[6\] showed that the PMCs of _G_ containing _a_ can be written either \(S\cup\{a\}\) or \(\Omega'\cup\{a\}\), where _S_ is a minimal separator of _G_ and \(\Omega'\) is a PMC of _G_\\{_a_}. Since the enumeration of minimal separators is achieved in output-linear time \[1\] and that \(\Delta_G\le n|\Pi_G|\), checking whether all such sets are PMCs can be done in output-linear time after the recursive call. On the other hand, keeping identical notation, the PMCs which do not contain _a_ can be written either \(\Omega'\) or \(S\cup(C\cap T)\), where the additional variable _T_ is also a minimal separator of _G_ and _C_ is a connected component of _G_\\_S_ fully adjacent to _S_. The time complexity of BT-enum comes from this last costly case, where the check of all such sets \(S\cup(C\cap T)\) takes \(\tilde{O}(|\Delta_G|^2)=\tilde{O}(|\Pi_G|^2)\). BT-enum highlights the fact that PMCs can be enumerated in output-polynomial time, and more precisely in output-quadratic time.
