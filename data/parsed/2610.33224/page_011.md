## 6.3 State-of-the-Art Comparison

We compare Holo-NN’s encrypted backends against seven private ANN systems spanning cryptographic (FHE, 2PC, DPF, PIR, ORAM) and hardware-enclave architectures: **SANNS** \[14\], **PRECO** \[40\], **Tiptoe** \[29\], **Pacmann** \[48\], **Panther** \[32\], **Compass** \[49\], and **Onyx** \[37\] ( 3).

Two caveats govern how that table should be read. First, these systems are not all evaluated on a common benchmark or threat model: Tiptoe reports on a 364M- document Common Crawl corpus (and MS MARCO for quality), Pacmann on MS MARCO and SIFT-100M, and while SANNS, PRECO, Panther, Compass, and Onyx publish numbers on SIFT-1M, PRECO requires two non- colluding servers and Onyx relies on an AMD SEV-SNP hardware enclave. We therefore quote each system at its published scale rather than extrapolating, and focus like-for-like single-server cryptographic comparisons on SANNS, Panther, and Compass. Second, they do not share a quality metric -- Tiptoe and Pacmann report MRR@100, Compass MRR@10, SANNS, PRECO, and Panther a 10-NN recall/accuracy, and Onyx a top-10 recall -- so we score our own candidate lists under each convention rather than quoting a single number of our own choosing.

As seen above, Holo-NN backends bracket the single- server design space between stateless single-query execution and minimal wire footprint. Among server-private cryptographic protocols on SIFT-1M, **SANNS** established sublinear clustering with BFV and garbled circuits at 1.77 GB of per-query traffic (1.55-45.3s across 32 threads), which **Panther** reduced by 19\(\times\) to 92.9 MB (1.49-9.32s<tbc>

<tbc>across 32 threads) via improved batch PIR and multi- round 2PC top-_k_ selection. **Holo-NN (LinPIR)** requires zero pre-installed key state on the server and answers on a 6-core Xeon W-2135 CPU in **3.1**s at _M_=98,300 with **91.1** MB of total wire traffic (matching Panther’s 92.9 MB and cutting SANNS’s 1.77 GB by 19.4\(\times\) in a single non-interactive round trip rather than multi-round 2PC), or reaches 1-r@10=93.0% (10-r@50=92.0%) in **6.6**s at _M_=300,000 (**265.4**MB) and 1-r@10=95.6% in **8.0**s at _M_=380,100 (**334.6**MB) -- running 62\(\times\) to 94\(\times\) faster on the server CPU than 12-thread CKKS and 3.1\(\times\) to 4.8\(\times\) faster than 64-bit Phantom GPU CKKS. Conversely, when rotation keys can be registered for a session, **Holo-NN (CKKS)** is the lowest-wire single-server cryptographic system measured on SIFT-1M: **11.13** MB at _M_=300,000 (**29.7**s on a single RTX 3090 GPU via Phantom, or **9.5**s at _M_=98,300 for **4.24**MB), and **5.37** MB in **0.82**s under 32-bit RNS **HEIR-GPU** (**2.10** MB in **0.26**s at _M_=98,300, and **8.65** MB in **1.30**s at _M_=500,000) against Compass’s 12.2 MB, Panther’s 92.9 MB, and SANNS’s 1.77 GB, in _one_ round trip rather than Compass’s eight, SANNS/Panther’s multi-round 2PC, or Pacmann’s twenty to thirty-two, with no client- side index state and no preprocessing stream. While **PRECO** achieves 1.5 MB in 1 RTT via Distributed Point Functions over LSH buckets, it requires two non-colluding servers; instantiated on a single server via lattice keyword PIR, its own authors estimate ≈300 MB of wire traffic and ≈25 CPU-hours per query on SIFT-1M \[40\], against which Holo-NN’s holographic projection reduces single-server wire by 27\(\times\)-143\(\times\) (CKKS) and compute by more than four orders of magnitude. On a 100ms- latency 4G link Compass’s own authors measure 0.94s of user-perceived latency on SIFT-1M, against our single round trip; and Compass’s 36.95MB per-session cold start exceeds the entire per-query CKKS budget by 3.3\(\times\)-6.9\(\times\).

On top-10 multi-neighbor ranking Holo-NN falls behind, and buying more bandwidth does not close the gap. Compass matches brute-force embedding search on SIFT-1M, though it publishes no absolute MRR@10 value; at _M_=300,000 our backends reach MRR@10=0.702. Panther resolves 88.85% of each query’s true ten nearest neighbors inside its own top ten (matching SANNS’s ≈90%); we resolve 61.6% at _M_=300,000, 66.8% at _M_=380,100 (matching the first SIFT-1M operating tier of  1), and 73.0% at _M_=500,000 -- still 15.9 points short of Panther while spending 5.3\(\times\) less wire bandwidth under CKKS. The deficit is therefore not a bandwidth deficit. It is a property of the \(\ell_1\) recovery step rather than of the holographic measurement, as Section 6.4 shows: the very same candidate lists already contain 92.0% of the true ten nearest neighbors within the top _fifty_ at _M_=300,000, and 98.0% at _M_=500,000. What Holo-NN produces cheaply is coverage; what it does not produce is a precise ordering within that coverage.
