This is because one may have to wait at some intermediate vertex for a long time to get the proper connecting edge of the path from _p_ to _q_. Accordingly, multiple temporal-path objectives are used in modern temporal- graph formulations, including shortest-duration (fastest), earliest-arrival, and shortest-length variants [17], [16], [18].

As in-orbit networking and computing becomes a reality, central to the viability of such an infrastructure is efficient computation of up-to-date knowledge of the temporal graph topology characteristics. Accordingly, the _all-pairs shortest path algorithm_ (APSP) is an attractive candidate that can pre-calculate the shortest path between all pairs of routers. Results of APSP computation can be used to populate routing tables. As noted earlier, a temporal graph induced by satellites in LEO exhibits periodic structure at the orbit timescale; for example, representative LEO orbits such as the ISS complete one revolution in roughly ∼90 minutes, illustrating the short recurrence times that motivate orbit- window precomputation. Thus, by executing a temporal all-pairs shortest path algorithm over snapshots captured over one (or multiple) orbital periods, one can pre-calculate temporal shortest paths between any pair of satellites. The resulting temporal shortest paths or routing tables can then be uploaded to the satellites from the ground stations so that whenever a communication request is generated, the appropriate temporal shortest path can be used for near- instantaneous path determination. Moreover, in the event a satellite fails or an ISL’s properties change significantly, pre- computing all-pair temporal shortest paths for contingency scenarios can allow the network to quickly reroute traffic and avoid significant overhead.

## 2 RELATED WORK

Recent work has continued to expand the algorithmic toolkit for temporal-path problems beyond classical earliest-arrival formulations. Jin et al. [19] study enumerating temporal _s-t_ paths under multiple constraints using temporal subgraph construction and pruning, highlighting the computational challenges created by time-order constraints and large search spaces. Jain and Sahni [18] provide polynomial-time shortest-path algorithms for _interval temporal graphs_, demonstrating that stronger temporal structure can yield tractable shortest-path computation beyond general contact-sequence models. Parallel and hardware-accelerated temporal-path computation has also advanced. Mithinti et al. [20] propose GPU-based parallel algorithms for the fastest-path-duration problem in temporal graphs, indicating that matrix-/scan- like temporal primitives can be mapped effectively to massively parallel architectures. Complementing this, Yu et al. [21] address the overhead of transforming temporal graphs into DAG-style representations for execution, proposing redundancy-aware GPU transformation that improves end-to-end performance of temporal analytics pipelines. These works collectively motivate designs that (i) preserve temporal feasibility exactly (e.g., run/interval constraints) and (ii) expose regular computation patterns suitable for acceleration. Beyond shortest-path computation, recent theoretical work has revisited _periodicity_ and feasibility constraints that arise naturally in recurring systems. Klobas et al. [16] initiate the temporal-graph realization problem with respect to fastest-path durations, including the periodic setting. Their results reinforce that periodic temporal structure is both practically relevant and algorithmically nontrivial, motivating methods that exploit periodicity without collapsing dynamics into static aggregation. In parallel, broad surveys such as Longa et al. [15] summarize the state of temporal graph learning, emphasizing that temporal edges fundamentally change reachability and path semantics, and that realistic dynamic systems require time-aware modeling choices.

For LEO mega-constellations, multiple recent studies focus on coping with topology dynamics, laser-ISL operational constraints, and scalable planning. Bhattacharjee et al. [22] consider _on-demand_ routing when dynamic laser inter-satellite links incur non-negligible setup delays, presenting optimization and heuristic schemes evaluated on Starlink-like constellations. Ron et al. [14] propose a dynamic time-expanded-graph (DTEG) based approach for time-dependent topology optimization under multi-objective criteria (capacity, latency, churn), reflecting growing interest in explicitly time-dependent design objectives. At a higher planning layer, Cen et al. [2] introduce SatFlow, a scalable framework that jointly plans topology, traffic allocation, and terminal power allocation for mega- constellations, addressing both dynamics and operational cost at scale. Demand-aware topology design has also been explored in very recent work: Dehshali et al. [23] propose a traffic-driven heuristic (Starfield) to orient ISLs toward non- uniform demand patterns, reporting improvements over baseline topologies in packet-level simulation.
