Comparing Figure 8a and c, the Newton-Krylov solvers work quite well for the Brusselator, with the standard ILU preconditioner or none at all, since the problem is only mildly nonlinear and stiff. On the Cahn-Hilliard problem, whose nonlinearity and stiffness are far stronger, the generic preconditioners are much less effective, and the more robust sparse direct solvers perform best. This trend carries over to our hybrid GPU benchmark in Figure 9, where we find that on the easier problem the Newton-Krylov based SUNDIALS solver closely tracks the performance of `diff-soup` in both the work-precision and the scaling diagram (Figure 9a and b), but for the harder Cahn-Hilliard problem the Newton-Krylov based solvers fall behind `diff-soup` by orders of magnitude (Figure 9c and d). The GPU therefore accelerates a Newton-Krylov method only where the Krylov iteration already converges well, whereas the sparse direct factorization benefits from the device on both problems. Consequently, we have focused on developing `diff-soup` around sparse direct methods, as they are generally more robust across a wide range of nonlinear, stiff PDE problems. However, we note that Newton-Krylov methods are still extremely effective when using problem-specific preconditioners, especially for three-dimensional PDEs where sparse direct methods become memory bound due to the superlinear fill in (Section 4.3) [76]. As such, we plan to expand `diff-soup` in the near future to support preconditioned Newton-Krylov-methods.
