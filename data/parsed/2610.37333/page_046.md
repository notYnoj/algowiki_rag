## 5.2. Embedded Deep Learning Model

`diff-soup` provides the capability to embed large deep learning models directly into PDE simulations, owing to its foundations in `JAX` and its GPU acceleration. In this example, we demonstrate physics-constrained deep learning at scale by training a neural network through the solution of a three- dimensional packed bed reactor model, in which the network represents the heterogeneous catalyst activity _k_(**x**). The concentration _c_ of the reactant evolves according to the advection-dispersion-reaction equation

\(\frac{\partial c}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\big(u_x(y,z)c\big)=\nabla\cdot\big(\mathbf{D}\nabla c\big)-k(\mathbf{x})\frac{c}{K+c},\) (25)

where the reactant enters the bed at the concentration _c_<sub>in</sub>, _u<sub>x</sub>(y,z_) is a fixed velocity profile along the axial coordinate _x_, **D** carries the axial and radial dispersion coefficients, and _K_ is the saturation constant of the Langmuir- Hinshelwood rate law. We generate a synthetic dataset by solving Eq. (25) on a \(96\times 24\times 24\) grid with a known activity field and adding Gaussian noise. The true activity is a log-normal random field multiplied by a poisoning front and a streak of low-activity pellets, which together form a challenging heterogeneous target for the reconstruction. We parameterize the activity field _k_(**x**) with a wavelet-based neural field (WIRE) [100] consisting of \(7.5\times 10^4\) parameters. The final layer is a softplus function to ensure positivity, and we initialize the network to match the uniform nominal activity \(\bar{k}\). Since the neural field depends only on position, it enters the residual as a field and leaves the sparsity pattern of the Jacobian unchanged. We train the neural field by minimizing the mean squared error between the simulated and measured concentrations using the AdamW optimizer [101] with a weight decay of 10<sup>-4</sup> and a peak learning rate of 10<sup>-3</sup> under a linear warmup and cosine decay schedule.
