Second-order sensitivities compose both forward and reverse AD, with forward-over-reverse differentiation the default method for computing Hessian-vector products at a constant multiple of the gradient cost, and full Hessians. Additionally, events are differentiable, with tangents and cotangents carried through the crossing by the implicit-function rule at the trigger root of Section 2.3. Many independent solves are batched through `jax.vmap` into one program that advances every solve together and factorizes the batch of matrices in a single call. GPU acceleration is further exploited through a reduced precision mode to enable computations in Float64, Float32, or mixed precision mode - which factorizes in Float32 precision and does iterative refinement with the Float64 precision residual. Finally, `diff-soup` can distribute computation across multiple GPUs using both data and model parallelism to either distribute independent solves across GPUs or spread a single sparse factorization across several devices on one node or across the nodes of a cluster, as we quantify for one node in Section 4.3.
