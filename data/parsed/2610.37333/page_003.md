## 1. Introduction

The numerical simulation of nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs) is fundamental to science and engineering, providing quantitative predictions of coupled transport, reaction, and thermodynamic processes across multiple length and time scales. Important examples include numerical weather and climate prediction [1, 2], turbulent and reacting flows [3, 4], microstructure evolution in materials [5, 6, 7], electrochemical energy storage [8, 9, 10], and biological pattern formation [11, 12]. Recent advances are bridging these physical models with data-driven methods to enable the learning, design, and control of physical systems directly from data [13, 14, 15]. In weather and climate modeling, learned components are now embedded within physical solvers [16, 17], and in fluid mechanics, neural networks are trained through differentiable solvers to correct and accelerate simulations [18, 19, 20]. Governing equations and constitutive relationships have been inferred directly from spatiotemporal data [21, 22, 23], and PDE-constrained optimization has been extended from inference to control [24, 25]. At the same time, high-resolution imaging techniques such as _operando_ microscopy are making spatially resolved data increasingly available for high-dimensional PDE models [26, 27, 28]. Exploiting such datasets requires software that can simulate these models at the scale of the experiment and differentiate through the simulation.

Both requirements are difficult to meet for PDEs, because once discretized in space, they fall into the important class of stiff dynamical systems [29]. Stiff problems exhibit multiple competing time scales, such that the slowly varying solution of interest is surrounded by rapidly varying nearby solutions that force a numerical integrator to take small time steps [30].
