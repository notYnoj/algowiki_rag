All three algorithms follow a three-stage structure: Eq. (1) or (2) is discretized in time with an implicit multistep formula, the resulting nonlinear system is solved with a modified Newton iteration, and the step size and order are adapted to control the local error. Figure 1 shows the control flow of one trial step. A _k_-step formula approximates the time derivative at _t_<sub>_n_+1</sub> from the current and _k_ stored states,<tbc>

\(y'_{n+1}\approx\frac{1}{h_n\beta_0}\sum_{j=0}^k\alpha_jy_{n+1-j},\) (3)

<tbc>where _h<sub>n</sub>_ is the current step size and \(\alpha_j\), \(\beta_0\) are order-dependent coefficients normalized such that \(\alpha_0=1\). The NDF family folds a scaled predictor- corrector difference \((h_n\beta_0)^{-1}\kappa\left(\sum_{j=1}^k\frac{1}{j}\right)\left(y_{n+1}-y_{n+1}^{(0)}\right)\) and its history tan gents into \(\alpha_j\) and \(\beta_0\), where the scalar parameter \(\kappa\) trades a modest reduction in the stability angle at higher orders for a smaller truncation-error constant \[60, 35\]. Substituting Eq. (3) into Eq. (2) produces a nonlinear algebraic system _G<sub>n</sub>(y_<sub>_n_+1</sub>)=0 whose root advances the state by one step. Writing \(\gamma_n=h_n\beta_0\), each modified Newton iterate solves the linearized system

\(W_n\delta^{(m)}=-G_n\left(y_{n+1}^{(m)}\right),W_n=\frac{1}{\gamma_n}M-J_n,\) (4)

and updates \(y_{n+1}^{(m+1)}=y_{n+1}^{(m)}+\delta^{(m)}\). The overall scaling of the corrector equation is purely conventional, since applying Newton’s method to \(\gamma_nG_n=0\) produces identical iterates with the rescaled matrix \(\gamma_nW_n=M-\gamma_nJ_n\), so implementations are free to factorize whichever form of this pencil is most convenient \[61\]. For the fully implicit form of Eq. (1), the same iteration applies with the generalized matrix \(W_n=\partial F/\partial y+\frac{1}{\gamma_n}\partial F/\partial y'\) \[62, 63\]. The iteration is warm-started from a predictor extrapolated through the stored history, which is typically accurate enough that a few corrections suffice.

For large systems, the dominant computational bottlenecks are the formation of _J<sub>n</sub>_ and the subsequent factorization of the pencil matrix _W<sub>n</sub>_ during the linear solve at each iteration of Eq. (4) \[29, 61\]. The modified Newton iteration freezes both quantities as long as possible, even though reusing the old Jacobian reduces the convergence rate of Newton’s method from quadratic to linear \[61\]. As such, the development of reliable heuristics for efficient reuse of _J_ across both nonlinear solver iterations and time steps has been an intense area of research and hinges on the interplay between caching rules and the step size and order controllers, which we describe in turn \[29, 31\].
