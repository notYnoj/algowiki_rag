To both solve and take gradients, these methods require Jacobians which are either manually derived or computed through potentially-inaccurate finite-difference rules, expensive dense automatic differentiation, or performant sparse automatic differentiation. Gradients additionally require complex, bespoke rules for parameter sensitivities that do not easily compose with stiff solvers.

A central component of these algorithms is the repeated solution of large linear systems. For PDEs, the local structure of the differential operators gives rise to a discrete sparse representation, which is exploited either by sparse direct solvers, such as KLU [36], SuperLU [37], and UMFPACK [38], or by iterative Newton-Krylov methods like GMRES [39, 40]. Sparse direct solvers are robust across a wide range of problems but have traditionally been run on the CPU, with GPU-accelerated factorizations emerging only recently [41, 42]. Newton-Krylov methods, in contrast, require only Jacobian-vector products and are naturally implemented on GPUs, but their fast and stable convergence depends on a problem-specific preconditioner.

Existing GPU efforts fall into three groups: hybrid codes that keep the integrator on the CPU and offload Krylov kernels, batched solvers for many small systems, and JAX frameworks that target specific physics or rely on dense linear algebra. SUNDIALS and PETSc support hybrid execution, in which the time integrator remains on the host while large systems are solved with Newton-Krylov methods on the GPU [43, 44]. `DiffEqGPU.jl` [45] and `jaxdae` [46] efficiently batch many small problems on the GPU. Differentiable PDE solvers such as `JAX-CFD` [20], `JAX-Fluids` [47], and `JAX-FEM` [48] compose GPU-accelerated simulation with machine learning for specific physics or discretizations, while the general-purpose implicit integrators of `diffrax` [49] rely on dense linear algebra by default. What remains missing is robust GPU parallelism within a single large, stiff solve, as needed for two- and three- dimensional PDEs, without relying on a problem-specific preconditioner. To our knowledge, no existing package in the Python/JAX ecosystem natively composes GPU sparse direct solvers, variable step, variable order (VSVO) time integration, and sparse automatic differentiation for the differentiable simulation of large dynamical systems.

The recent release of `cuDSS` by NVIDIA [50], a sparse direct solver that performs the numerical factorization and the triangular solves on the GPU, removes the major bottleneck in the robust integration of large-scale PDEs.
