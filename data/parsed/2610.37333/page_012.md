<tbc>[31, 66], which is cumbersome and error-prone for all but the simplest right- hand sides; finite difference approximations, whose truncation and round-off errors degrade the convergence of the Newton iteration [31]; and dense automatic differentiation, which is exact but requires _n_ tangent or cotangent evaluations therefore scaling as _O(n_<sup>2</sup>) [67]. More recently, sparse automatic differentiation has matured in the Julia ecosystem, which pairs automatic sparsity detection with graph coloring to compress the Jacobian into a small number of directional derivatives and deploys the approach throughout its differential equation solvers [34, 68].

`diff-soup` builds on this template with the automatic differentiation machinery of `JAX` [69]. We assemble the pattern symbolically by composing the declared patterns of the operators that constitute the right-hand side and we compress the Jacobian following the Curtis-Powell-Reid (CPR) approach with a greedy, largest-first coloring of its column intersection graph [70, 71, 72]. The colors define a seed matrix \(V\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n_c}\), whose _k_-th column sums the unit vectors of the columns assigned to color _k_, and every nonzero entry of _J_ can be read off from the compressed product

\(B=JV\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n_c},\) (6)

because structurally orthogonal columns deposit their entries into disjoint rows of _B_. For the bounded stencils of FDM and FVM discretizations, _n<sub>c</sub>_ is set by the stencil width independent of the resolution, so \(n_c\checkmark n\), and we compute all _n<sub>c</sub>_ tangent propagations in parallel with the `jax.vmap` operator, reducing the cost of the Jacobian from _O(n_<sup>2</sup>) to _O(n_). The same batching applies to FEM kernels, where `jax.vmap` evaluates the element-local residuals across all elements, and the local kernels are differentiated and scattered to assemble the sparse Jacobian in _O(n_).

Because the global variables couple broadly to the state, they typically produce dense rows and columns that give _J_ an arrowhead structure. Bidirectional coloring methods can compress such patterns by combining forward and reverse passes [71, 72], but under the unidirectional column coloring we employ, a single dense row makes every pair of columns share a row, so each column needs its own color. We therefore exclude the global variables and equations from the sparsity pattern and compute the two dense coupling blocks with local kernels: the _n<sub>g</sub>_ dense rows \(\partial f_g/\partial y\) are evaluated in reverse mode with one `jax.jacrev` cotangent per global equation, and the _n<sub>g</sub>_ dense columns \(\partial f/\partial y_g\) in forward mode with one `jax.jacfwd` tangent per global variable.
