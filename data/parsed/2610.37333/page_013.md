Using this approach, for most PDE problems the remaining computational bottleneck is the factorization of the pencil matrix _W<sub>n</sub>_ during the linear solve of Eq. (4). Among the classical factorizations, we consider only the LU decomposition, as the pencil matrix _W<sub>n</sub>_ is nonsymmetric for general right-hand sides, which precludes the Cholesky factorization, while the QR factorization roughly doubles the operation count and produces denser factors, which reserves it for ill-conditioned least-squares problems [73]. An LU solver proceeds in three phases: an analysis phase that determines the pivoting strategy and the structure of the factors, a factorization phase that computes the lower and upper triangular factors _L_ and _U_, and a solve phase that applies them through forward and backward substitution [55]. For a dense matrix, the LU factorization costs _O(n_<sup>3</sup>) while each subsequent solve costs only _O(n_<sup>2</sup>), so the algorithm concentrates the expense in the analysis and factorization to make the solves fast. This front-loaded cost profile is well matched to the VSVO structure, because the setup gate freezes the pencil across Newton iterations and time steps, so a single factorization is reused for many cheap triangular solves [61, 31]. Nevertheless, the cubic cost of factorization is an immense barrier for large systems. Much research has focused on linear solvers that exploit sparsity to reduce this cubic cost, and for the purpose of our discussion, we limit ourselves to two families: iterative Newton-Krylov methods and sparse direct solvers [74, 73].

Newton-Krylov methods solve the linearized system in a reduced Krylov subspace \(\mathcal{K}_m=\mathrm{span}\{v,W_nv,W_n^2v,\dots,W_n^{m-1}v\}\) and therefore require only matrix-vector products with _W<sub>n</sub>_, and hence only Jacobian-vector products, which makes them attractive for large systems [40]. The workhorse algorithm implemented in most libraries is the generalized minimal residual (GMRES) method, which builds an orthonormal basis of K_<sub>m</sub>_ and selects the iterate that minimizes the residual norm over the subspace, imposing no symmetry requirements on _W_ [39]. However, the convergence of the Krylov iteration deteriorates on the ill-conditioned spectra that stiff discretizations produce, and much work has been devoted to preconditioners that transform the system before the iteration [74]. The best preconditioners are often problem specific, since their goal is to cluster the eigenvalues of the preconditioned system near unity, and the two most common generic choices are the incomplete LU (ILU) factorization and algebraic multigrid (AMG), for which we refer the reader to Saad [74] and Ruge and Stüben [75] for further details.

In contrast to the iterative methods, sparse direct solvers operate on the sparse representation of _W_ itself and require no problem-specific tuning.<tbc>
