SUNDIALS [31, 32], PETSc [66, 33] and MATLAB lack a native automatic differentiation library for their integrators, so they are supplied the analytical sparse Jacobian of each problem. `DifferentialEquations.jl` [34] assembles its Jacobian by sparse automatic differentiation with `ForwardDiff.jl` and graph coloring. Reported wall time is the median of the warm calls of one process after one cold call, and we employ a strict wall cap of 1800 s for each benchmark cell to minimize the computational burden of the large benchmark suite. Accuracy is the mean absolute error of the final state against the reference, and for a gradient the relative _L_<sup>2</sup> error against the reference gradient. The Brusselator references are external `CVODE` solves (SciPy `Radau` in one dimension) and the Cahn-Hilliard references are `diff-soup` solves, each at tolerances tightened beyond the plotted range and validated by agreement with a second solve at a neighbouring tolerance. The reference gradients are `diff-soup` reverse- mode gradients at tightened tolerances, validated against a second solve and against central finite differences. Independent SUNDIALS solves reproduce the `diff-soup` references, with `IDA` matching the Cahn-Hilliard reference to \(6\times 10^{-13}\) and the `CVODES` and `IDAS` adjoints matching both reference gradients well below every plotted error.

SUNDIALS integrates the Brusselator with `CVODE` and the Cahn-Hilliard problem with `IDA`, on the KLU [36] and `SuperLU\_MT` [77] direct solvers and on GMRES [39], with and without the ILU(0) preconditioner [74] written for this study. PETSc integrates with `TSBDF` at orders up to five with a lagged Jacobian and preconditioner, and solves the Newton systems with KLU, with GMRES and ILU(0), and with algebraic multigrid. For the Newton-Krylov methods, we tune the Krylov dimension above the library defaults, to 50 on the Brusselator and 60 on the Cahn-Hilliard problem in SUNDIALS and in the ILU(0) lanes of PETSc, and to 100 in the multigrid lanes of PETSc. `DifferentialEquations.jl` integrates with `FBDF` and `QNDF` on KLU and differentiates through `SciMLSensitivity` with a Gauss adjoint in reverse mode and `ForwardDiff` in forward mode.
