For the caching rules, all three solvers guard the nonlinear iteration with a two-tier setup gate that re-evaluates the cached Jacobian only when it has aged past a step budget or when a convergence failure has occurred [31]. Otherwise, when the ratio of the current \(\gamma_n\) to the value frozen into the factors of _W<sub>n</sub>_ has drifted outside a deadband, the mass-matrix engines rebuild the pencil \(\frac{1}{\gamma_n}M-J_n\) from the saved _J<sub>n</sub>_ without re-differentiation [31]. `JIDA` extends the same reuse to the fully implicit form, recomposing _W<sub>n</sub>_ from the saved \(\partial F/\partial y\) while re-evaluating only \(\partial F/\partial y'\), whereas the reference DAE integrator refreshes both partial derivatives at every setup [63, 31]. A convergence-rate memory extrapolates the contraction of the iteration and declares divergence after two iterations rather than exhausting the iteration budget, and failed steps escalate through a ladder that refreshes the Jacobian and shrinks the step [35, 31]. This cost hierarchy shapes the control layer that follows, because any change of the step size or the order moves \(\gamma_n\) and invalidates the saved factors, so the controllers are built to change both quantities only as often as accuracy demands.

For the local error control, each solver estimates the local truncation error \(\widehat{e}_{n+1}\) of the converged step from the difference between the predictor and the corrected solution, scaled by an order-dependent constant, and measures it in the weighted root-mean-square (WRMS) norm

\(\text{err}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n\big(\frac{\widehat{e}_{n+1,i}}{w_i}\big)^2\right)^{1/2},w_i=\text{atol}+\text{rtol}|y_i|,\) (5)

where the weight _w<sub>i</sub>_ blends the absolute tolerance atol and the relative tolerance rtol so that \(\text{err}\le 1\) accepts the step as accurate to the requested tolerances [61, 31]. Because the stored history spans the adjacent orders, the same difference tables furnish companion error estimates at orders _q_-1 and _q_+1 at negligible additional cost, and these three estimates drive both the step size and the order selection [56, 29].

Each controller converts the error estimate at order _q_ into a proposed growth factor for the step size, \(\eta=h_{n+1}/h_n\), through a safety-biased power law of the form \(\eta\propto\text{err}^{-1/(q+1)}\), and passes the proposal through a deadband around unity that holds the step size, and thereby the saved factors of _W<sub>n</sub>_, unless the predicted change is large enough to justify a refactorization [61]. The companion estimates at the adjacent orders enter the same way, each candidate order competing through its own estimate.
