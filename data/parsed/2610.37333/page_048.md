## 6. Conclusion

We have presented `diff-soup`, a software package for GPU-accelerated simulation and inference of large-scale dynamical systems built around the cuDSS sparse direct solver. Accelerating the linear solves of stiff implicit integration on the GPU yields up to \(50\times\) faster simulations and \(80\times\) faster gradients than established CPU libraries. Nonlinear PDEs on structured and unstructured domains are expressed in a general-purpose modeling system, integrated as stiff ODEs and DAEs in `JAX`, and differentiated end to end through the discretize-then-optimize approach. The resulting gradients enable PDE-constrained optimization for both classical and modern inference and learning problems. Our methods were evaluated in a systematic benchmark suite against SUNDIALS, PETSc, `DifferentialEquations.jl`, MATLAB, and `diffrax` on the heat equation, the Brusselator, and a chemo- mechanical Cahn-Hilliard problem, for both forward solves and gradients. We demonstrated these workflows by inferring the constitutive relationships of a phase-separating material with uncertainty quantification and by training a neural field embedded in a three-dimensional packed bed reactor model.

This work has opened up several interesting directions for future study. Specifically, as PDE solvers advance and large-scale physics foundation models emerge, there is a critical need for more rigorous benchmarking of stiff PDEs on both regular and irregular geometries. On the software side, we plan to improve the high-performance computing (HPC) compatibility and acceleration of `diff-soup` with more thorough support for large, distributed simulation and optimization problems. Developing better multi-GPU parallelism strategies for sparse matrix factorization is a promising and important direction of future research. Furthermore, we note the need for support of our algorithms on general accelerator devices and for a modular automatic differentiation library.
