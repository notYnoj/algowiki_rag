\(\|\mathcal{A}X-b\|_2/(1+\|b\|_2)\), \(r_\mathrm{d}=\|\mathcal{A}^*\lambda+S-C\|_F/(1+\|C\|_F)\), and \(r_\mathrm{g}=|\langle C,X\rangle-b^\top\lambda|/(1+|\langle C,X\rangle|+|b^\top\lambda|)\). Symmetric matrices are vectorized by the orthonormal svec, with off-diagonal entries scaled by \(\sqrt{2}\), so Euclidean distances in these coordinates equal Frobenius distances. Panels (a) and (c) of each figure report the fixed-point residual \(\|Z^{k+1}-Z^k\|_F\) of the shadow sequence of §5 on a logarithmic scale. Unique rank-one exactness and the existence of a strictly complementary certificate are verified once per instance. We solve both sides of the relaxation with an interior-point solver at tolerance 10<sup>-14</sup> and inspect the spectra of the optimal pair. Throughout, "certified" means that the Boolean minimum and its uniqueness are established by exhaustive enumeration of all 2_<sup>n</sup>_ cube points (exact up to floating-point summation); rank and strict-complementarity statements are numerical, at the stated tolerances. All rank readouts in this appendix use a 10<sup>-6</sup> relative eigenvalue tolerance. All new runs were performed on a laptop with an Apple M2 Max processor and 64GB of RAM.

## Instances.

_BQP-20_: the binary quadratic instance BQP-r1-20-1 of \[KJY25\], with _n_=20 and standard Gaussian _Q_ and _c_; Figure 1(a) is that paper’s saved run on the standard (non-quotient) second-order relaxation, with _N_=231, _m_=20,601, and \(\sigma=100\). _BQP-20q_: an independent Gaussian instance with _n_=20, used for Figure 1(b)-(c) on the quotient relaxation of §2 (_N_=211, _m_=16,171, \(\sigma=1\)). It is certified by direct enumeration of the 2<sup>20</sup> cube points and an interior-point solve. The minimizer is unique, with the second-smallest cube value 0.69 above the minimum; the relaxation value matches the minimum to relative error below 10<sup>-13</sup>; and a strictly complementary certificate of rank _N_-1=210 exists. _PBO4-20_: a dense degree-\(\le 4\) Boolean objective with standard Gaussian coefficients and _n_=20, relaxed at order _d_=2 (_N_=211, _m_=16,171, \(\sigma=1\)). It is certified like BQP-20q, with the cube scan performed by a fast Walsh-Hadamard transform. The minimizer is unique, with the second-smallest cube value 15.6 above the minimum; the relaxation value matches the minimum to relative error below 10<sup>-13</sup>; and a strictly complementary certificate of rank 210 exists. Not every Gaussian quartic is order-two exact: the first sampled seed has relative relaxation gap \(7.8\times 10^{-2}\), and the certified instance is the second seed.

## Cold start (Figure 1(a)).

The run converges in 4,583 iterations. The limiting primal iterate has numerical rank one, identified at iteration 1,113 (the dotted line in Figure 1(a)). The limiting certificate has numerical rank 210 out of _N_-1=230, with \(\lambda_\mathrm{min}(X_\mathrm{lim}+\sigma S_\mathrm{lim})\approx 4\times 10^{-15}\). So the limiting pair is not strictly complementary. The sharp linear phase begins essentially at rank identification.

## Sampling singular endpoints (Figure 1(b)).

For fixed \(\sigma\), the fixed points of the shadow recursion are \(\mathcal{Z}_\star=\{X_\star-\sigma S:S\in\mathcal{S}_\star\}\) (§3), so sampling an endpoint means sampling an optimal certificate. We draw a symmetrized standard Gaussian matrix _R_ and minimize \(\langle R,S\rangle\) over the optimal certificate slice \(\mathcal{S}_\star=(S_\mathrm{sc}+\mathcal{L})\cap\mathbb{S}_+(V)\) with an interior-point solver. For a generic _R_ the minimizer is unique and is an extreme point of S_<sub>⋆</sub>_. Extreme points of the positive-dimensional set S_<sub>⋆</sub>_ lie on its relative boundary, where certificates lose rank. One hundred independent draws on BQP-20q give certificates _S<sub>i</sub>_ of numerical rank 68 to 72 (median 69), against the strictly complementary rank _N_-1=210. The corresponding endpoints \(\bar{Z}_i=X_\star-\sigma S_i\) have numerical ranks 68 to 72 (median 70), far below the full rank _N_=211 attained at the strictly complementary endpoint; Figure 1(b) plots their distribution. Both readouts are relative to the matrix’s own largest eigenvalue; since \(\|\bar{Z}_i\|_\mathrm{op}\approx N\) while \(\|S_i\|_\mathrm{op}\) is much smaller, the numerical rank of \(\bar{Z}_i\) need not equal 1+rank(_S<sub>i</sub>_). The endpoints are also essentially different from one another: the pairwise distances \(\|\bar{Z}_i-\bar{Z}_j\|_F=\sigma\|S_i-S_j\|_F\) lie in \[15.7,16.7\], comparable to the certificate norms \(\|S_i\|_F\approx 13\) themselves.
