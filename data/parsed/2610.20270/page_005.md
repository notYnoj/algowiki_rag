## Main message: the degeneracy is benign.

Our headline result explains these observations: for exact Boolean Moment-SOS relaxations in the quotient form of Figure 1(b)-(c), constructed in §2, one strictly complementary certificate--anywhere in S_<sub>⋆</sub>_--is enough for the fast regime. The reason is a uniform _setwise_ error bound for the canonical Douglas-Rachford (DR) residual around the entire fixed-point set. It is produced by a mechanism we call _residual-field rigidity_: a positive first-order modulus of the residual field over the relative interior of the optimal set cannot vanish at its singular boundary. Equivalently, the directions along which the residual stalls to first order are exactly the directions along the optimal set, so the second-order slow regions of \[KY26b\] cannot form near it. Existing local linear-rate theory for splitting methods on SDPs and related conic programs is local to a regular KKT point. It assumes strict complementarity, nondegeneracy, or an error bound at the limit point the algorithm selects \[HSZ18, KJY25, Jia26, DLY26, LHL<sup>+</sup>26\]. Augmented Lagrangian methods are different, since one strictly complementary pair already yields the objective-level error bound they need \[LDZ24, CST16\]; the splitting residual does not inherit that bound (Theorem 4.4). Strict complementarity and nondegeneracy fail at the singular endpoints of a positive-dimensional certificate set. Rigidity replaces them by a condition that is checked at interior endpoints only; the regularity is _imported_ from there to the whole set. For the Boolean question above, unique rank-one exactness plus one strictly complementary certificate put the quotient relaxation in the fast regime of first-order methods; what remains open is the entrance time, which separates this rate statement from a complexity statement (§7). (Panel (a) uses the standard encoding; see §7.)

**Theorem 1.1** (Benign degeneracy, informal)**.** _Fix an order d at which the quotient Boolean moment SDP of §2 has a unique rank-one optimum and at least one strictly complementary optimal SOS certificate. Then, for every admissible step choice, the canonical DR residual satisfies a uniform local error bound around its entire fixed-point set. Consequently, ADMM, sGS-ADMM, and PDHG converge eventually linearly from every initialization, and the limiting KKT point they select need not be strictly complementary. Moreover, outside a lower-dimensional semialgebraic set in the_ 2_<sup>n</sup>-dimensional space of Boolean objectives, both hypotheses hold simultaneously at every uniquely rank-one exact order. The formal statements are Theorems 4.3 and 6.3 and Theorems 5.1 and 5.2._

## Contributions.

Our contributions are threefold.

(I) **Residual-field rigidity** (§3). For a semidefinite program with compact optimal sets and one strictly complementary optimal pair, where neither optimum need be unique, we prove that five properties of the Douglas-Rachford residual are equivalent. They are a uniform relative-interior derivative modulus, two finite kernel identities on the relative boundaries of the two optimal sets, a setwise error bound around the fixed-point set, a uniform modulus along every boundary normal, and exact first-order linearization at every endpoint. The proof uses exact projection secants instead of Taylor expansion at moving endpoints, and fixed anchors instead of spectral gaps. The theorem is Boolean-free. To our knowledge, it is the first error bound for a splitting residual on the semidefinite cone that holds uniformly around a positive-dimensional optimal set with singular boundary points; earlier bounds cover polyhedral, piecewise linear-quadratic, and well-posed conic feasibility problems \[PVZ21, ACL16\] or concern the objective rather than the splitting residual \[LDZ24, CST16\].<tbc>

(II) **Boolean verification** (§4). We verify the hypothesis of the rigidity theorem for exact Boolean Moment-SOS relaxations: two quotient-space identities--a constant-diagonal identity and an XOR-class identity--reduce the required relative-interior modulus to a finite-dimensional kernel computation and a compactness argument.<tbc>
