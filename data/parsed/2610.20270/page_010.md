### 3.4 Proof outline of Theorem 3.2

### Two obstacles.

A pointwise argument would differentiate the residual at the nearest endpoint; two features of the degenerate geometry defeat this. First, the _base moves_. Along a sequence \(Z_k\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_\star\) the nearest endpoint \(\bar{Z}_k\) changes with _k_, and the projection derivative is discontinuous in the endpoint exactly where a kernel grows. So a Taylor expansion at \(\bar{Z}_k\) has no uniform remainder. Second, _eigenvalues vanish_. The positive eigenvalues of the moving optima \(\bar{X}_k\), \(\bar{S}_k\) can tend to zero, so any bound whose constant contains their reciprocals degenerates along the sequence.

### Proof outline.

We prove (i)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii)\(\Rightarrow\)(iii)\(\Rightarrow\)(iv)\(\Rightarrow\)(i) and (iii)\(\Rightarrow\)(v)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii). The step (ii)\(\Rightarrow\)(iii) carries the weight, and three devices handle the two obstacles.

_Fixed anchors_. Every uniform spectral estimate comes from the anchor pair, through \(a_X:=\lambda_{min}(X_{sc}|_U)\) and \(a_S:=\lambda_{min}(S_{sc}|_V)\). The Sylvester operator \(Y\mapsto X_{sc}|_UY+\sigma Y\bar{S}_{VV}\) has inverse norm at most \(a_X^{-1}\) for every optimal \(\bar{S}\), singular or not, and symmetrically with \((\sigma a_S)^{-1}\) (Theorem C.3). Together with the optimal-set radii of (D.1), these gaps bound every decomposition of a normal vector into a \(\mathcal{D}^\perp\) part and two kernel parts (Theorem D.2). The anchors also prove (i)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii): to test the primal identity at a boundary certificate \(\bar{S}\), form the interior endpoints \(X_{sc}-\sigma\bar{S}_\varepsilon\) with \(\bar{S}_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)\bar{S}+\varepsilon S_{sc}\), whose primal factor is the anchor and whose dual factor is pushed toward its anchor. Along the push, the interior derivative of a direction _E_ from the left side of (3.8) converges to _E_ itself. Hence the residual derivative tends to zero, and (i) forces \(E\in\mathcal{D}\); the dual identity is symmetric (Appendix D.3).

_Exact secants_. For (ii)\(\Rightarrow\)(iii) we take _Z<sub>k</sub>_ with \(t_k:=dist_F(Z_k,\mathcal{Z}_\star)\rightarrow 0\) and residual _o(t<sub>k</sub>_), and we never differentiate. With \(\bar{Z}_k\) the nearest endpoint, the exact projections \(X_k=\Pi_+(Z_k)\) and \(S_k=-\sigma^{-1}\Pi_-(Z_k)\) define the secants \(E_k=(X_k-\bar{X}_k)/t_k\) and \(F_k=-\sigma(S_k-\bar{S}_k)/t_k\), which are bounded and sum to the unit normal \(H_k=(Z_k-\bar{Z}_k)/t_k\). These exact secants avoid a Taylor remainder at the moving endpoint. The small residual says \(\mathsf{P}E_k\rightarrow 0\) and \(\mathsf{P}^\perp F_k\rightarrow 0\), so \(\langle E_k,F_k\rangle\rightarrow 0\). Exact complementarity _X<sub>k</sub>S<sub>k</sub>_=0 gives the energy identity \(\langle E_k,F_k\rangle=\sigma t_k^{-2}(\langle X_k,\bar{S}_k\rangle+\langle\bar{X}_k,S_k\rangle)\ge 0\). Hence both complementarity gaps are \(o(t_k^2)\).

_The PSD product inequality_. For PSD _A,B_, \(\|AB\|_F^2\le\|A\|_{op}\|B\|_{op}\langle A,B\rangle\) (Theorem C.6). Applied to the two gaps, it gives \(\|X_k\bar{S}_k\|_F=o(t_k)\) and \(\|\bar{X}_kS_k\|_F=o(t_k)\), that is, \(E_k\bar{S}_k\rightarrow 0\) and \(\bar{X}_kF_k\rightarrow 0\). No eigenvalue bound enters here. The anchors remove the blocks on the wrong support, \((E_k)_{VV}\unknown 0\) and \((F_k)_{UU}\unknown 0\), since these pair to _o_(1) with _S_<sub>sc</sub> and _X_<sub>sc</sub>. The limits _E,F_ are therefore exactly the directions tested in (3.8), so \(E\in\mathcal{M}\), \(F\in\mathcal{L}\), and the limit _H=E+F_ of the unit normals lies in D. The anchor-controlled normal decomposition and the signs of the secants against the kernel cones then give a contradiction (Appendix D.4). The remaining implications are short (Appendix D.5). Differentiating the error bound along a normal gives (iii)\(\Rightarrow\)(iv), and the inclusion \(\mathcal{D}^\perp\subseteq N_{\mathcal{Z}_\star}\) at interior endpoints gives (iv)\(\Rightarrow\)(i). For (iii)\(\Rightarrow\)(v), a residual of order _o(t_) along \(\bar{Z}+tH\) forces \(dist_F(\bar{Z}+tH,\mathcal{Z}_\star)=o(t)\) through (3.9), so _H_ is tangent. For (v)\(\Rightarrow\)(ii), the projection derivative is computed at the two anchored endpoints, where it fixes primal test directions and annihilates dual ones. The PSD structure is essential (Theorems D.3 and D.4).
