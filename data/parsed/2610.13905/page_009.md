## 2.1 Sufficient Conditions for Rank Improvements

Suppose that an exact algorithm for matrix multiplication is represented by the tensor decomposition \(\mathcal{M}_{\langle m,k,n\rangle}=\sum_{j=1}^rU_j\otimes V_j\otimes W_j\). This algorithm is a good starting point for the approach outlined above if the following hold: (1) the product _U<sub>j</sub>V<sub>j</sub>W<sub>j</sub>_ is nilpotent for at least one _j_ (required) and (2) _r_ is small (desired but not required). The second criterion is one of practical concern; since we obtain APA algorithms by removing terms from an exact one, we stand to obtain the best result by starting from an exact algorithm that has minimal rank. The first criterion, on the other hand, guarantees that a term can be deleted at all. We verify this below.

**Theorem 2.1.** _Suppose that the product UVW of_ \(U\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times k}\), \(V\in\mathbb{C}^{k\times n}\) _and_ \(W\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times m}\) _is nilpotent. In this case, there exist_ \(R\in\mathbb{C}(\varepsilon)^{m\times m}\), \(S\in\mathbb{C}(\varepsilon)^{k\times k}\)_, and_ \(T\in\mathbb{C}(\varepsilon)^{n\times n}\)_, each invertible over_ \(\mathbb{C}(\varepsilon)\)_, such that all entries of R_<sup>-1</sup>_US, S_<sup>-1</sup>_VT, and T_<sup>-1</sup>_WR are polynomials in_ \(\varepsilon\) _with zero constant term._

_Proof._ Let _F_<sub>0</sub>=_G_<sub>0</sub>=_H_<sub>0</sub>=0 and inductively define _F_<sub>_j_+1</sub>, _G_<sub>_j_+1</sub>, and _H_<sub>_j_+1</sub> to be the pre-images of _G<sub>j</sub>_, _H<sub>j</sub>_, and _F<sub>j</sub>_, respectively, under the linear transformations represented by _W_, _V_, and _U_. For example, _F_<sub>1</sub>=ker(_W_), _G_<sub>1</sub>=ker(_V_), and _H_<sub>1</sub>=ker(_U_). We note two important facts about these subspaces:

1. They are nested - e.g., \(F_j\subseteq F_{j+1}\), \(G_j\subseteq G_{j+1}\), and \(H_j\subseteq H_{j+1}\). This can be shown inductively by first noting that \(F_0\subseteq F_1\) (since \(x\in F_0\) satisfies \(Wx=W0=0\in G_0\)), \(G_0\subseteq G_1\), and \(H_0\subseteq H_1\). Now, \(F_j\subseteq F_{j+1}\) implies \(H_{j+1}\subseteq H_{j+2}\), \(G_j\subseteq G_{j+1}\Rightarrow F_{j+1}\subseteq F_{j+2}\), and \(H_j\subseteq H_{j+1}\Rightarrow G_{j+1}\subseteq G_{j+2}\).<tbc>

2. _F_<sub>3_i_</sub>, _G_<sub>3_i_</sub>, and _H_<sub>3_i_</sub> are the null spaces of (_UVW)<sup>i</sup>_, (_WUV)<sup>i</sup>_, and (_VWU)<sup>i</sup>_, respectively.

If _UVW_ is nilpotent, then so are _VWU_ and _WUV_,<sup>6</sup> and therefore \(\bigcup_jF_j=\mathbb{C}^m\), \(\bigcup_jG_j=\mathbb{C}^n\), and \(\bigcup_jH_j=\mathbb{C}^k\). We can now define _R_, _S_, and _T_ as follows: in the case of _R_, take a basis _x_<sub>1</sub>_,x_<sub>2</sub>_,...,x<sub>m</sub>_ for C_<sup>m</sup>_ constructed successively from the filtration \(F_1\subseteq F_2\subseteq\cdots\) and define \(R\in\mathbb{C}(\varepsilon)^{m\times m}\) to be the matrix satisfying \(Rx=\varepsilon^ix\) if \(x\in F_i\backslash F_{i-1}\). Define _S_ and _T_ analogously using _H<sub>j</sub>_ and _G<sub>j</sub>_. It remains to show that _R_<sup>-1</sup>_US_, _S_<sup>-1</sup>_VT_, and _T_<sup>-1</sup>_WR_ have entries in \(\varepsilon\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]\). To do this, suppose that \(y\in\mathbb{C}^k\) is one of the basis vectors obtained from \(H_1\subseteq H_2\subseteq\cdots\) , where \(y\in H_i\backslash H_{i-1}\) for some _i_. By construction, we have \(R^{-1}USy=\varepsilon^iR^{-1}Uy\) and moreover \(Uy\in F_{i-1}\), so \(Uy=\alpha_1x_1+\cdots+\alpha_lx_l\) for the basis vectors _x_<sub>1</sub>_,...,x<sub>l</sub>_ of C_<sup>m</sup>_ coming from _F_<sub>_i_-1</sub>. But in this case,

\(R^{-1}USy=\varepsilon^iR^{-1}\left(\alpha_1x_1+\cdots+\alpha_lx_l\right)=\varepsilon^i\left(\alpha_1R^{-1}x_1+\cdots+\alpha_lR^{-1}x_l\right)=\alpha_1\varepsilon^{i-\beta_1}x_1+\cdots+\alpha_l\varepsilon^{i-\beta_l}x_l,\) (2.2)

where \(\beta_1,\dots,\beta_l\) are the exponents satisfying \(Rx_j=\varepsilon^{\beta_j}x_j\). Since each _x<sub>j</sub>_ belongs to _F_<sub>_i_-1</sub>, we have \(\beta_j\le i-1\) for all _j_. Hence, each exponent \(i-\beta_j\) in (2.2) is at least one, which means _R_<sup>-1</sup>_USy_ has entries in \(\varepsilon\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]\). Since this applies to any basis vector \(y\in\mathbb{C}^k\), and letting \(Y\in\mathbb{C}^{k\times k}\) be the matrix containing that basis, we conclude that _R_<sup>-1</sup>_USY_ has entries in \(\varepsilon\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]\), which is sufficient to show the same for the entries of _R_<sup>-1</sup>_US_, since multiplying on the right by _Y_ <sup>-1</sup>, which has constant entries, won’t introduce any constant terms. The same argument can be used to show that _S_<sup>-1</sup>_VT_ and _T_<sup>-1</sup>_WR_ also have entries in \(\varepsilon\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]\).

The key idea behind our search algorithm is now captured by the following result. The terms corresponding to \(j\le l\), which can be transformed to have entries that are polynomials in \(\varepsilon\) with no constant term - a property that carries over to the corresponding tensor product \(R^{-1}U_jS\otimes S^{-1}V_jT\otimes T^{-1}W_jR\) - are precisely those we referred to as \(O(\varepsilon)\) above.

**Theorem 2.2.** _Let_ \(\mathcal{M}_{\langle m,k,n\rangle}=\sum_{j=1}^rU_j\otimes V_j\otimes W_j\) _be a tensor decomposition obtained from an exact_ \(\langle m,k,n\rangle\) _matrix multiplication algorithm. If there exist_ \(R\in\mathbb{C}(\varepsilon)^{m\times m}\), \(S\in\mathbb{C}(\varepsilon)^{k\times k}\)_, and_ \(T\in\mathbb{C}(\varepsilon)^{n\times n}\)_, each invertible over_ \(\mathbb{C}(\varepsilon)\)_, such that R_<sup>-1</sup>_U<sub>j</sub>S, S_<sup>-1</sup>_V<sub>j</sub>T, and T_<sup>-1</sup>_W<sub>j</sub>R have entries in_ \(\varepsilon\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]\) _for all_ \(1\le j\le l\)_, then_

\(\sum_{j=l+1}^rR^{-1}U_jS\otimes S^{-1}V_jT\otimes T^{-1}W_jR\)

_is an approximate, symbolic decomposition of_ \(\mathcal{M}_{\langle m,k,n\rangle}\) _that corresponds to an APA algorithm of rank r-l. In particular, the border rank of_ \(\mathcal{M}_{\langle m,k,n\rangle}\) _is bounded by r-l._

<sup>6</sup>This follows from the fact that nilpotence is equivalent to the trace powers of _UVW_ being zero, and trace is invariant under cyclic permutations.
