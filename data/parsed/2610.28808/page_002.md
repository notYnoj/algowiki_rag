<tbc>Approaches related to deflation are limited-memory preconditioners (LMPs) [13], augmentation methods [10], balancing strategies [20], and low-rank updates [3]. When spectral information is used in the preconditioner, we refer to such methods as spectral preconditioners [5, 11, 13]. For broader overviews of preconditioning, see [2, 23, 30].

The effect of using approximate spectral information has therefore received considerable attention. The sensitivity of deflated CG to perturbations of the deflation subspace is studied in [17], while Giraud and Gratton [11] analyze how errors in approximate eigenpairs affect the spectrum of the preconditioned matrix. These results characterize the influence of inexact spectral information on the projected or preconditioned spectrum. Although deflation can significantly improve CG convergence, its construction may become costly when the coefficient matrix changes. For a deflation basis \(W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times k}\), one must form and solve a system with the matrix \(W^\top A(t)W\), which requires additional products with _A(t_). This problem generally needs to be solved accurately, since the convergence of deflated CG can be sensitive to errors in its solution [20]. To mitigate this sensitivity, related approaches avoid mapping the selected eigenvalues to zero, e.g., Erlangga and Nabben [7] shift selected eigenvalues to a nonzero part of the spectrum and use the resulting preconditioner within GMRES.

In [6], the authors introduced a scaled spectral preconditioner designed to reduce the energy-norm error after a prescribed number of preconditioned CG (PCG) iterations. The preconditioner is constructed from the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of _A_ and maps the selected eigenvalues to a common value \(\theta\). Experiments showed that an appropriate choice of the scaling parameter can reproduce much of the convergence acceleration obtained with deflation while requiring a lower initialization cost. In particular, this behavior was observed in a data assimilation problem when approximate eigenpairs were recycled. However, the theoretical analysis in [6] assumes that the spectral information used to construct the preconditioner is exact and is associated with the matrix of the system being solved. It therefore does not explain why similar behavior persists when spectral information from a nearby matrix is reused.

Our main objective in this work is to establish this connection theoretically when spectral information obtained from _A_ is reused for the perturbed system _A(t)x=b_. We focus on eigenpairs associated with the largest eigenvalues of _A_, although the analysis extends to any selected subset of eigenpairs. We compare the energy-norm errors generated by deflated CG and by PCG equipped with the scaled spectral preconditioner. Using first-order perturbation analysis, we show that, after one additional PCG iteration, the squared energy-norm error of PCG is bounded by the squared energy-norm error of deflated CG multiplied by an explicit factor, plus higher- order terms in _t_. The resulting bound clarifies how the scaling parameter \(\theta\) should be chosen relative to the nondeflated spectrum and provides a theoretical explanation for the similar convergence behavior observed numerically. We complement the theoretical analysis with numerical experiments on synthetic linear systems.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review deflated CG and derive a characterization of its energy-norm error. We formulate the perturbed problem, develop the first-order analysis, establish the comparison between PCG and deflated CG, and discuss practical choices of the scaling parameter in Section 3. We report the numerical experiments in Section 4 and present our conclusions in Section 5.
