## 1 Introduction

All-Pairs Shortest Paths (APSP), which asks given an _N_-vertex graph to compute the distance \(\delta(u,v)\) between all pairs of vertices, is one of the central problems in fine-grained complexity. Classical algorithms for APSP, such as the Floyd-Warshall algorithm, achieve a _O(N_<sup>3</sup>) running time. A long series of works (e.g. \[Fre76, Dob90, Tak92, Tak98, Han04, Pet04, Zwi04, Cha05, Han06, Cha10, HT12\]) improved sub- polynomial factors over the classic _O(N_<sup>3</sup>) running time, culminating in an algorithm by Williams \[Wil18\] with \(N^3/2^{\Theta(\sqrt{logN})}\) running time. Very recently, a major breakthrough by Anthropic (see Alman and Vassilevska Williams \[AV26\]) gave the first truly subcubic algorithm for APSP, falsifying one of the central hypotheses in Fine-Grained Complexity, obtaining a deterministic algorithm in _O(N_<sup>2.9995</sup>) time (see also the simplified exposition due to Karingula and Lovett \[KL26\] that achieves \(O(N^{3-\delta})\) for some \(\delta>0\)). \[AV26\] also obtain a Las Vegas algorithm running in expected time _O(N_<sup>2.998</sup>) for real-valued APSP. In light of this breakthrough, one might naturally expect further improvements to the APSP exponent.

In this note, we give a simple improvement to the exponent of APSP, via a faster algorithm for computing the (min,+) Product of two \(N\times N\) matrices. The (min,+) product of two matrices _A,B_, defined (_A∗B_)\[_i,j_\]=min_<sub>k</sub>A_\[_i,k_\]+_B_\[_k,j_\], is a useful primitive in algorithm design, and is known to be equivalent to APSP up to polylogarithmic factors \[VW18\]. Our first result is an improved randomized algorithm.

**Theorem 1.1.** _There is a Las Vegas algorithm computing the_ (min,+)_-product of two_ \(N\times N\) _matrices A,B with polynomially bounded integer entries in expected time_ \(\tilde{O}\left(N^{2.9963432}\right)\).

Using known reductions, this also gives faster algorithms for many problems equivalent to (min,+)- Matrix Multiplication, including APSP, Negative Triangle, Replacement Paths, Second Shortest Simple Path, Metricity, (min,+)-Verification \[VW18\], Weighted Tree Edit Distance \[BGMW20, NPS<sup>+</sup>25\], and 2D Maximum Subarray \[TT00, BDT16\].

Our algorithm uses a modular approach to tackle the (min,+) product in subcubic time, first proposed by Bringmann, Grandoni, Saha, and Vassilevska Williams \[BGSV19\], and subsequently refined and modified in \[GPVX21, CDXZ22, JPSX26\]. In \[BGSV19\], the very first step of the algorithm is to provide an additive approximation of _C_ by recursively scaling and rounding. This additive approximation is used to perturb the entries of _A_ and _B_ so that many entries of _A_ and _B_ become small. This enables computation of fast (min,+) product over those small entries via fast matrix multiplication \[AGM97\]. Surprisingly, as \[BGSV19\] shows after the perturbation, only a subcubic number of witnesses, triples which can contribute to _C_, are left: this provided a promising route towards refuting the APSP conjecture.

In order to complete this framework and obtain truly subcubic APSP, one needs a sparse certificate to find those remaining triplets. The works of \[BGSV19, GPVX21, CDXZ22, JPSX26\] show such sparse certificate exists for various structured matrices such as bounded difference and monotone matrices. Subsequent work \[GPVX21, CDXZ22, JPSX26\] combine the perturbation and sparse certification steps through modular arithmetic over primes.

We develop on the modular approach of \[BGSV19, GPVX21, CDXZ22, JPSX26\] and utilize Theorem 25 of the recent breakthrough \[AV26\] to provide a sparse certificate in truly subcubic time without any structural assumptions. This leads to a slightly better running time, and a significantly simpler, easy to understand algorithm. We state Theorem 25 of \[AV26\] below.

**Theorem 1.2** (Theorem 25 of \[AV26\])**.** _For every_ \(\varepsilon<\varepsilon^*:=(ln4)/(5ln10)=0.1204\dots\) _and every_ \(\kappa>0\) _there is a_ \(\gamma>0\) _such that the following holds. Given as input matrices_ \(X\in\mathbb{Z}^{N\times D}\) _and_ \(Y\in\mathbb{Z}^{D\times N}\)_, where_ \(2\le D\le N^\varepsilon\)_, whose entries are integers of absolute value at most N_<sup>_O_(1)</sup>_, as well as a set W of at most_ \(N^2/D^\kappa\) _positions of an_ \(N\times N\) _matrix, the entries_ \((XY)[I,J]\), \((I,J)\in W\)_, can be computed deterministically in time_ \(O(N^2log^2D/D^\gamma)\).

To invoke Theorem 1.2, we divide the matrices _A_ and _B_ into blocks and for each block, design a family of new matrices _X_ and _Y_ satisfying the properties of Theorem 1.2 such that computing relevant entries of (_XY_) helps us certify whether a given block of _A_ and _B_ is a candidate in which we ought to look for a witness. We stop whenever the first witness is obtained. Our second result shows that the same exponent can be achieved deterministically.
