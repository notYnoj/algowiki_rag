Among the disparate unfitted formulations, the Immersed Finite Element Method (IFEM) [22,23], the Extended Finite Element Method (XFEM) [29,34], and the Cut Finite Element Method (CutFEM) [7,9] have gained prominence. Furthermore, the unfitted methods have also been applied to the advection-diffusion equation [28], elliptic equations on hypersurfaces [30], elliptic interface problems [25,31,35], fluid-structure interaction problems [8], shape optimization [21] and time-harmonic wave equations [11,26]. A fundamental challenge inherent in these approaches is the _small cut-cell problem_: when the boundary intersects the background elements arbitrarily, the resulting system can become severely ill-conditioned as the measure of the intersection approaches zero.

Two primary paradigms have been established to restore numerical robustness. The first is the _cell-merging_ strategy, exemplified by the work of Chen et al. [12-14], which algebraically or geometrically aggregates small boundary elements with adjacent interior macro-elements. This approach has been shown to effectively mitigate the _p_-dependence of the condition number in _hp_-FEM settings, ensuring geometric stability. The second paradigm is the _variational stabilisation_ framework, notably the ghost penalty method developed by Burman and co- authors [6,10]. By augmenting the bilinear form with penalisation terms on the face facets of cut elements, this approach restores uniform coercivity and ensures that the condition number remains bounded independently of the interface’s relative position to the mesh. Similar minimal stabilisation techniques have also been extended to Unfitted Isogeometric Analysis (IGA) [4,5] and least-squares Nitsche formulations [20] to preserve optimal convergence rates on trimmed geometries.

Although these methods significantly reduce the need for mesh generation, they also introduce new implementation challenges. In particular, they often require numerical integration over geometrically intricate subregions induced by the intersection between the domain boundary and background elements. This typically necessitates geometric intersection algorithms and specialised quadrature rules, which are computationally expensive and difficult to implement robustly, especially in three dimensions. Moreover, such geometry-dependent quadrature procedures may introduce additional errors that are often not explicitly accounted for in existing error analyses of unfitted finite element methods.

To avoid these difficulties in both implementation and analysis, Duprez and Lozinski proposed a novel fictitious domain approach, referred to as \(\phi\)-FEM [3,17-19], where \(\phi\) denotes the level-set function defining the domain. This formulation belongs to the class of UnCut finite element methods, in which all integrals are evaluated over the full background elements using standard quadrature rules, thereby eliminating the need for geometry-dependent integration procedures. Stability and error estimates for \(\phi\)-FEM have been established for the Poisson equation under the assumption that the stabilisation parameters are chosen sufficiently large, depending on constants arising from trace inequalities, Poincaré inequalities, and inverse estimates. In practice, however, determining appropriate values for these parameters remains challenging for general elliptic problems.

To address the issue of parameter selection, Hu, Li, and Yang [24] proposed a robust, parameter-free least-squares UnCut finite element method that guarantees stability and optimal-order convergence without requiring large stabilisation parameters. However, in order to accommodate standard _C_<sup>0</sup> finite element spaces, their approach is formulated through the minimisation of a discrete _H_<sup>-1</sup>-residual of the governing PDE. While effective, this naturally necessitates a dual (mixed) formulation involving an auxiliary variable, thereby increasing both the algebraic dimension and the computational complexity of the resulting linear system.

Motivated by the need for a more computationally efficient framework, this paper introduces a novel UnCut formulation that leverages the high-order regularity of _C_<sup>1</sup>-continuous PHT-splines [15,33].
