# UnCut FEM WITH PHT-SPLINES ON UNFITTED T-MESHES

Abstract. We propose a novel unfitted finite element method based on polynomial splines over hierarchical T-meshes (PHT-splines) for elliptic partial differential equations (PDEs) posed on domains whose boundaries are described implicitly by a level-set function. Exploiting the enhanced smoothness of the spline space, we formulate the method by minimizing the _L_<sup>2</sup> residual of the PDE over an extended neighborhood of the physical domain. This formulation allows the method to be implemented entirely on the background mesh using standard element-wise quadrature. It yields a symmetric positive-definite linear system and avoids the geometric construction of curved cut subregions in boundary elements, as well as the associated nontrivial numerical integration. We establish well-posedness of the numerical scheme and derive optimal _a priori_ error estimates in the _H_<sup>1</sup> norm. In addition, we employ a residual-based _a posteriori_ error indicator to guide local adaptive refinement of the hierarchical T-meshes. Numerical experiments corroborate the theoretical results and demonstrate the accuracy and efficiency of the proposed approach.

### 1. Introduction

The numerical solution of partial differential equations (PDEs) defined on complex or evolving domains \(\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^d\) constitutes a cornerstone of contemporary computational science. Traditionally, finite element analysis relies on _body-fitted_ discretisations, where the triangulation _T<sub>h</sub>_ is constrained to conform to the physical boundary \(\partial\Omega\). While robust for static and simple geometries, this paradigm poses significant computational challenges in realistic scenarios. The construction of high-quality, shape-regular meshes that accommodate high-order approximations is often a prohibitive task, frequently dominating the total computational budget of the simulation pipeline.

This bottleneck is particularly pronounced in multi-query environments, such as shape optimisation, inverse problems, and uncertainty quantification, where the governing PDE must be evaluated over an extensive ensemble of geometric configurations. In these contexts, the overhead of repeated mesh generation and the subsequent verification of mesh validity can eclipse the cost of the linear solvers themselves, impeding the development of fully automated workflows. Furthermore, for time-dependent problems involving moving interfaces or evolving boundaries, classical Arbitrary Lagrangian–Eulerian (ALE) frameworks often suffer from progressive mesh distortion. Maintaining the integrity of the numerical scheme necessitates frequent remeshing and sophisticated field interpolation (projection) between successive grids, processes that are not only computationally intensive but also susceptible to introducing numerical dissipation and compromising stability.

To circumvent these geometric constraints, _unfitted_ finite element methods have emerged as a powerful alternative by effectively decoupling the functional discretisation from the underlying domain geometry. By employing a fixed background mesh _T<sub>h</sub>_ and representing the boundary \(\partial\Omega\) implicitly (e.g., via a level-set function), these methods eliminate the need for body-conforming mesh updates.
