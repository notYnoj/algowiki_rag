\(\blacktriangleright\) **Lemma 2.1** (Crossing Lemma)**.** _There exists a constant c\>_0_, such that every planar drawing of a graph with n vertices and_ \(m\ge 4n\) _edges contains at least_ \(c\frac{m^3}{n^2}\) _crossings._

Using the Crossing Lemma we will show that \(|E_1|=O(n^\frac{3+\alpha}{2})\), as follows. If \(|E_1|=O(n^\frac{3+\alpha}{2})\) does not hold, then by the Crossing Lemma there must be many crossings between the edges \(\pi_{ij}\in E_1\). We will show that this implies that there is a crossing of ply greater than \(\frac{1}{4}n^\alpha\), thus contradicting that \(\mathrm{ply}(\mathcal{D}_1)<\frac{1}{4}n^\alpha\). We now make this idea precise.

\(\blacktriangleright\) **Lemma 2.2.** _Let_ \(\mathcal{G}^\times(\mathcal{D}_1)=(\mathcal{D}_1,E_1)\) _be the intersection graph of a set_ D<sub>1</sub> _of disks such that_ \(\mathrm{ply}(\mathcal{D}_1)<\frac{1}{4}n^\alpha\)_. Then_ \(|E_1|\le\sqrt{\frac{4}{c}}\cdot n^\frac{3+\alpha}{2}\) _, where c is the constant appearing in the Crossing Lemma._

**Proof.** Consider the proper path set \(\Pi(\mathcal{D}_1)\) and let X be the set of crossings between the paths in \(\Pi(\mathcal{D}_1)\). Assume for a contradiction that \(|E_1|>\sqrt{\frac{4}{c}}\cdot n^\frac{3+\alpha}{2}\) . We will show that then there has to exist a crossing \(x\in\mathcal{X}\) of ply at least \(\frac{n^\alpha}{4}\) , which contradicts that \(\mathrm{ply}(\mathcal{D}_1)<\frac{1}{4}n^\alpha\).

We start by giving a lower bound on the total ply of all crossings in the drawing. To this end, we split each edge \(\pi_{ij}\in E_1\) in two _half-edges_ as follows. For two points \(x,y\in\pi_{ij}\), let \(\pi_{ij}[x,y]\) denote the subpath of \(\pi_{ij}\) between _x_ and _y_. Recall that _p<sub>i</sub>_ is the center of disk _D<sub>i</sub>_. We now pick an arbitrary point \(m_{ij}\in\pi_{ij}\cap(D_i\cap D_j)\) and split \(\pi_{ij}\) at _m<sub>ij</sub>_ into a half-edge \(\pi_{ij}[p_i,m_{ij}]\) connecting _p<sub>i</sub>_ to _m<sub>ij</sub>_ and a half-edge \(\pi_{ij}[p_j,m_{ij}]\) connecting _p<sub>j</sub>_ to _m<sub>ij</sub>_. For brevity, we will denote these two half-edges by _h<sub>ij</sub>_ and _h<sub>ji</sub>_, respectively. Clearly, each half-edge has length at most the radius of the disk it lies in, and so \(h_{ij}\subset D_i\) and \(h_{ji}\subset D_j\). We denote the resulting set of half-edges by E<sub>1</sub>.

We label each crossing \(x\in\mathcal{X}\) with an unordered pair of integers \(\{\lambda_1(x),\lambda_2(x)\}\), defined as follows: if _x_ is the crossing between the half-edges \(h_{ij},h_{k\ell}\), then \(\lambda_1(x)\) is the number of crossings contained in \(\pi_{ij}[x,m_{ij}]\) and \(\lambda_2(x)\) is the number of crossings contained in \(\pi_{k\ell}[x,m_{k\ell}]\); see Fig. 1(i).
