<tbc>The exponent 1/7 comes from balancing the comparison errors against the 1_/r_ gap; it need not describe the actual growth rate.

There is a more specific prediction suggested by the Gaussian infinite-component spin glass. Hastings [11] derived a ground-state dimension of order _n_<sup>2/5</sup>. Aspelmeier and Moore [1] supported this scaling numerically and gave an alternative argument balancing a finite-dimension energy correction of order 1_/r_ against a spectral correction of order (_r/n_)<sup>2/3</sup>. Balancing these two terms gives _r_ of order _n_<sup>2/5</sup>. Motivated by this prediction, we propose the following statement for unweighted random graphs.

**Conjecture 6.2.** If \(G\sim G(n,\frac{1}{2})\), then

\(\frac{log\rho(G)}{logn}\rightarrow^\mathbb{P}\frac{2}{5}.\)

The spin-glass calculations motivate this conjecture but do not establish it for the graph model. In particular, agreement of leading optimal values does not imply agreement of exact optimal ranks. Theorem 6.1 supplies a polynomial lower bound; even an upper bound \(\rho(G)=o(\sqrt{n})\) with probability tending to one remains open here.
