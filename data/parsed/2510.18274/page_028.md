## SFM Lower Bounds via Graph Cuts.

The current best deterministic lower bound for submodular function minimization (SFM) is due to Chakrabarty, Graur, Jiang, and Sidford \[CGJS22\], who showed that any algorithm must make at least \(\Omega(nlogn)\) value queries to solve SFM in the value oracle model. Their construction involves a carefully designed submodular function tailored to their lower bound framework. This raises the intriguing open question of whether any variants of graph cut functions can be used to establish a matching \(\Omega(nlogn)\) lower bound. In particular, obtaining an \(\Omega(n^{1.01})\) lower bound for SFM, whether via graph cuts or not, would be considered a major breakthrough.

## SFM Upper Bound and Directed/Weighted Graphs.

On the upper bound side of SFM, the best-known algorithm by Jiang \[Jia23\] makes \(\widetilde{O}(n^2)\) value queries. An obvious major open problem is to achieve, e.g., \(O(n^{2-\epsilon})\) bound for some constant \(\epsilon>0\). Towards this goal, computing the minimum _s-t_ cuts on directed/weighted graphs presents a major barrier as _no o(n_<sup>2</sup>) algorithm was known for _either_ the case of directed unweighted graphs and case of undirected weighted graphs (despite many progress in the sequential and other settings \[BGJV25; BCK+24; BCP+23; CKL+22; BGJ+22; BLL+21; BLN+20\]). We believe that presenting an \(O(n^{2-\epsilon})\) bound for one of these cases will be a major progress. In particular, for the directed case, even a more apparent barrier is to solving the following simple _reachability_ problem \[Nan24\]: Can we determine if there is a _directed s-t_ path in an unweighted directed graph in \(O(n^{2-\epsilon})\) cut queries for some constant \(\epsilon>0\)? It should be a breakthrough already to solve this reachability problem.

For the _global_ min-cut problem, the cut query complexity is essentially resolved for weighted undirected graphs \[MN20\]. While there has been progress in the standard sequential setting \[CLN+21\], we are not aware of any new developments in the cut query model. We leave this as another important open problem.

## Finding a Maximum Flow.

The duality between the minimum _s-t_ cut and the maximum _s-t_ flow is one of the most celebrated results in combinatorial optimization. While our algorithm computes (and enumerates all) minimum _s-t_ cuts, it does not immediately yield a maximum _s-t_ flow. This limitation is also shared by the algorithm of Rubinstein, Schramm, and Weinberg \[RSW18\]. On the other hand, the deterministic algorithm of Anand, Saranurak, and Wang \[ASW25\] does output a maximum _s-t_ flow using \(\widetilde{O}(n^{5/3})\) cut queries and communication. Interestingly, a closer examination of our approach reveals that it too can be extended to recover a maximum flow with complexity \(\widetilde{O}(n^{5/3})\): by applying either Lemma 3.4 or Lemma 3.5 with threshold \(\Delta=n^{2/3}\), and then augmenting to a full maximum flow using _O(n_<sup>2/3</sup>) additional iterations of the standard augmenting path method. Nevertheless, it remains an intriguing open problem whether one can compute a maximum _s-t_ flow using fewer than _o(n_<sup>5/3</sup>) cut queries or bits of communication. Doing so with _combinatorial algorithms_, even for the special case of bipartite matching \[BBE+22\] will also be interesting.

## Improving Query Complexity.

Our \(\widetilde{O}(n^{8/5})\)-query algorithm (Theorem 1.1) represents the first progress beyond the \(\widetilde{O}(n^{5/3})\) barrier established by \[RSW18\] and \[ASW25\], demonstrating that this bound is not optimal. A natural next milestone is to design an algorithm with complexity \(\widetilde{O}(n^{3/2})\). Although the support size of a minimum _s-t_ cut is at most _O(n_), it is reasonable to conjecture a lower bound of \(\widetilde{\Omega}(n^{3/2})\) for the problem, as this matches the support size of its dual--the maximum _s-t_ flow of value \(\Omega(n)\). In fact, one can show that any algorithm which outputs an exact maximum flow--or even a multiplicative \(\Omega(1)\)-approximation--must incur a communication complexity of \(\Omega(n^{3/2})\) bits, and therefore a cut-query complexity of \(\widetilde{\Omega}(n^{3/2})\).
