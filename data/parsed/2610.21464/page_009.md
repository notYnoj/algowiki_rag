<tbc>scale HNSW retrieval. As a result, high semantic similarity alone does not guarantee that poisoned documents are reached and returned by HNSW. As the knowledge base grows from 500K to 2M documents, the baseline RSR drops from as high as 17.0% at 500K to only 0.4%-6.0% at 2M.

In contrast, our Hub–Satellite design combines broad query coverage, local ranking competitiveness, and improved graph-search reachability. At 2M documents, our method maintains an RSR above 72% across both generation models and poisoning budgets. Cond.ASR and overall ASR also remain above 35.75% and 25.87%, respectively. These results demonstrate that our method preserves strong attack effectiveness as the HNSW knowledge base scales.

**Domain-specific knowledge bases.** Our method remains effective in domain-specific knowledge bases with denser topic-relevant benign documents. We construct knowledge bases from PubMed, SEC-EDGAR, and EPA-HERO for healthcare, personal finance, and household and public safety, respectively. As shown in  2, when the knowledge base grows from 500K to 2M, the average RSR decreases by 12.14-12.91 percentage points, but still reaches 63.46%-65.68% at 2M across the two poisoned-document budgets. At 2M, Cond.ASR remains between 39.95% and 41.19%, resulting in an overall ASR of 25.35%-27.05%.<tbc>

<tbc>These results indicate that, despite stronger competition from topic-relevant benign documents, our method maintains considerable graph-search reachability and generation- side effectiveness in domain-specific settings.

**Real user queries.** The preceding test queries are generated by an LLM and cover diverse semantic directions and formulations, but their distribution may still differ from that of real user queries. We therefore further evaluate our method on topic-related user queries extracted from real- world question-answering datasets. As shown in  2, even when the knowledge base grows to 2M, our method achieves RSRs of 59.23% and 61.45% and ASRs of 24.08% and 26.04% under the two poisoned-document budgets, respectively. These results show that our method not only covers diverse model-generated queries but also generalizes effectively to more naturally formulated real user queries.
