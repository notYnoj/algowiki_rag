<tbc>**search budget**: HNSW explores only a subset of graph nodes, so poisoned documents are no longer guaranteed to be visited and participate in candidate comparisons; (2) **graph-search reachability**: under a limited search budget, whether a poisoned document is visited further depends on the search paths from the entry node through the local adjacency structure; and (3) **intensified local competition**: as the knowledge base grows, more benign documents enter neighborhoods, competing for the search budget and occupying candidate-set slots.

These challenges are particularly consequential for topic-level poisoning. Unlike attacks optimized for a single known query, a topic-level attack must use a limited set of poisoned documents to cover queries with diverse semantic intents and formulations. Such broader coverage reduces each document’s specificity and ranking advantage for individual queries, compounding the retrieval constraints imposed by HNSW. This raises a central question: _can an attacker mount an effective topic-level poisoning attack against large-scale HNSW-RAG while generalizing to unseen topic queries?_

To this end, we propose a topology-aware topic-level poisoning attack against HNSW-RAG. Our key insight is that HNSW graph connectivity is influenced by embedding- space proximity and heuristic neighbor selection, while retrieval proceeds through graph traversal under a limited search budget. This property allows attackers to indirectly induce favorable graph neighborhoods by coordinating the semantic relationships among injected documents, thereby improving their reachability during graph search. On the retrieval side, we organize independently competing poisoned documents into a role-differentiated Hub–Satellite structure. Satellites target distinct semantic regions, maintaining local ranking competitiveness and providing multiple retrieval entry points for diverse unseen queries. Hubs aggregate semantics shared across these regions, serving as common anchors that encourage local cohesion among dispersed Satellites. This semantics-induced, "multiple-entry, locally cohesive" structure enables set-level cooperation: once a search reaches one poisoned document, neighbor expansion may expose additional poisoned documents, improving reachability and retrieval stability under limited budgets.

On the generation side, we introduce entity–claim promotion for diverse unseen queries within the target topic. Advice-seeking, comparison, and applicability queries differ in form but share a common decision-oriented goal: evaluating whether or which solution to recommend. We therefore make all poisoned documents support the same target entity and a shared claim about its benefits across different decision contexts. Evidence anchoring presents the claim as a conclusion supported by background, mechanism, conditions, and outcome evidence, while entity–claim coupling ties the mechanism and outcome to the target entity, encouraging the model to preserve this association and recommend the entity in its response. Finally, two-level candidate optimization balances document-level retrieval and generation utility with set-level coverage complementarity and topological cooperation.

Experiments show that our method maintains stable attack performance across datasets and knowledge-base sizes, achieves an attack success rate of up to 31.29%, substantially outperforms existing baselines, and generalizes to diverse unseen topic queries. Structural analysis confirms that the Hub–Satellite cooperative structure forms in the target HNSW index and significantly improves poisoned- document retrieval. Defense experiments show that existing methods cannot effectively mitigate the attack, revealing practical security risks in large-scale HNSW-RAG systems.

**Contributions.** To the best of our knowledge, we present the first RAG poisoning attack designed for large-scale HNSW indexes and capable of generalizing to unseen topic queries. Our contributions are as follows:

(1) We characterize and empirically quantify the impact of HNSW search on existing RAG poisoning attacks. (2) We propose a topology-aware Hub–Satellite poisoning method that improves unseen-query coverage and HNSW graph- search reachability through distributed query access and semantically induced local cohesion.

(3) We introduce an entity–claim promotion mechanism that combines evidence anchoring with entity–claim coupling to manipulate diverse unseen topic queries.

(4) We extensively evaluate the attack across datasets, knowledge-base sizes, and system settings, validating its effectiveness and key design choices while showing that existing defenses remain insufficient against this threat.

## 2. Background and Related Work

### 2.1. Background

**RAG.** An external knowledge base supplements the parametric knowledge encoded in LLMs. Let _D_={_d_<sub>1</sub>_,...,d<sub>N</sub>_} denote a knowledge base of _N_ documents and _q_ a user query. During retrieval, the system maps _q_ and each document _di_ into a shared embedding space, ranks the documents by their similarity scores _s(q,di_), and returns the _k_ most similar documents. During generation, the LLM produces an answer conditioned on the query and retrieved documents. Because retrieved content directly informs generation, the final output depends not only on the LLM but also on the relevance and trustworthiness of the retrieved documents.

**HNSW.** HNSW is a graph-based ANN index widely used in industrial vector search systems, including Azure AI Search [18], Amazon OpenSearch Service [19], Elasticsearch [20], and MongoDB Vector Search [21]. HNSW organizes document embeddings into a multilayer navigable graph, where sparse upper layers guide navigation toward relevant regions and a denser base layer supports local search. During index construction, each new node searches for nearby candidates and selects neighbors using a heuristic that considers both vector proximity and neighbor diversity.
