## A. Orbit Closedness and Finite Depth Guarantees

While expressiveness analyses often characterize the states reachable in the asymptotic limit of infinite depth, physical VQA implementations require exact state preparation using finite-depth architectures. The topological closedness of the dynamical Lie group orbit plays a pivotal role here, establishing a direct equivalence between infinite-depth asymptotic limits and exact finite-depth expressiveness.

The primary practical significance of orbit closedness is illustrated in Figure 2. Specifically, any projectivized target state \[_t_\] obtained as a limit of state sequences produced by parameterized quantum circuits of the form (5) as the circuit depth \(L\rightarrow\infty\) lies by definition in the topological closure \(\overline{\mathcal{O}_{[\xi]}}\) of the dynamical Lie group orbit \(\mathcal{O}_{[\xi]}=G\cdot[\xi]\).

When the orbit \(\mathcal{O}_{[\xi]}\) is closed--as guaranteed, for instance, when _G_ is compact under the periodic conditions of Theorem IV.1 (or Remark IV.3), the orbit coincides with its closure \((\mathcal{O}_{[\xi]}=\overline{\mathcal{O}_{[\xi]}})\). Consequently, any state \(|t\rangle\) reachable in the infinite-depth limit can in fact be represented _exactly_ as \(|t\rangle=e^{i\phi} g|\xi\rangle\) for some \(g\in G\) and \(\phi\in[0,2\pi]\). In turn, because _G_ is the connected Lie group generated by the skew-Hermitian operators \(\{iH_j\}_{j=1}^k\), every \(g\in G\) admits an exact synthesis as a finite-depth parameterized circuit of the form (5). Thus, orbit closedness closes the gap between asymptotic and exact expressiveness, ensuring that no target state requires infinite depth to be realized.
