<tbc>Specifically, let _G_ denote the connected analytic Lie subgroup of U(_W_) generated by the control Hamiltonians,

\(G:=\langle e^{tA}|A\in\{iH_1,\dots,iH_k\},t\in\mathbb{R}\rangle\subseteq\mathrm{U}(W).\) (7)

By construction, every parameterized VQA unitary \(U_L(\unknown)\) (see (5)) is an element of _G_. Thus, the true reachable set of the parameterized quantum circuits initialized in a unit norm state \(|\xi\rangle\) is given precisely by the dynamical Lie group orbit of the initial state:

\(\mathcal{O}_\xi:=G\cdot|\xi\rangle=\{U|\xi\rangle|U\in G\}\subseteq\mathbb{S}(W_{\mathfrak{g},\xi}),\) (8)

where \(\mathbb{S}(W_{\mathfrak{g},\xi}):=\{|\psi\rangle\in W_{\mathfrak{g},\xi}|\||\psi\rangle\|=1\}\) denotes the unit sphere of the cyclic subspace \(W_{\mathfrak{g},\xi}\). The containment \(\mathcal{O}_\xi\subseteq\mathbb{S}(W_{\mathfrak{g},\xi})\) is detailed in Remark II.4.

Next, we establish precise criteria for when the group orbit achieves _maximal reachability_ within the cyclic subspace (modulo global phase)--that is, when the projective orbit of the ray \([\xi]\) generated by \(|\xi\rangle\) completely saturates the projective space:

\(\mathcal{O}_{[\xi]}:=G\cdot[\xi]=\mathbb{P}(W_{\mathfrak{g},\xi}),\) (9)

where \(\mathbb{P}(W_{\mathfrak{g},\xi}):=\mathbb{S}(W_{\mathfrak{g},\xi})/\mathrm{U}(1)\) denotes the space of physical quantum states modulo global phase.

**Remark III.1.** Although the classification of Lie subalgebras yielding transitive actions on complex projective spaces is classical, we invoke it here to isolate the precise algebraic criteria ensuring projective transitivity within our framework. The detailed proof is deferred to Appendix A.

**Theorem III.2.** Let _V_ be a complex Hilbert space of dimension \(d\ge 2\), and let \(\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}(V)\) be a Lie subalgebra of skew-Hermitian operators. Let \(G=e^\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathrm{U}(V)\) be the connected Lie subgroup generated by g, and fix a normalized state \(|\xi\rangle\in V\) .

The projective orbit \(\mathcal{O}_{[\xi]}\) equals the full projective space P(_V_) if and only if, up to a unitary change of basis on _V_ , g is one of the following Lie subalgebras of u(_d_):

1. _full unitary or special unitary algebra:_ su(_d_) or u(_d_) for any \(d\ge 2\).<tbc>

2. _symplectic algebra (or its_ u(1)_-central extension):_ sp(_m_) or \(\mathfrak{sp}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)\) when \(d=2m\ge 4\).

For _d_=2, \(\mathfrak{sp}(1)\cong\mathfrak{su}(2)\), which coincides with case (1).

## IV. PERFORMANCE GUARANTEES AND LIMITATIONS

A fundamental geometric property of an orbit \(\mathcal{O}_\xi\) (resp. \(\mathcal{O}_{[\xi]}\)) is its topological closed--namely, whether the orbit coincides with its closure \(\overline{\mathcal{O}_\xi}\) in the ambient unit sphere \(\mathbb{S}(W_{\mathfrak{g},\xi})\) (resp. \(\mathcal{O}_{[\xi]}\) coincides with its closure in the ambient projective space \(\mathbb{P}(W_{\mathfrak{g},\xi})\)). Closedness is the exact orbit property that bridges the gap between asymptotic expressibility and exact reachability: it allows us to elevate convergence guarantees in the infinite depth limit (\(L\rightarrow\infty\)) to rigorous existence guarantees for finite depth parametrized circuits.

Below, we outline several practical and efficiently verifiable criteria ensuring the closedness of these dynamical group orbits. Complete proofs are deferred to Appendix A.

**Theorem IV.1.** Let _H_<sub>1</sub>_,...,H<sub>k</sub>_ be Hermitian operators on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space _W_. Let \(\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}(W)\) denote the dynamical Lie algebra as in (6) and let \(G\subseteq\mathrm{U}(W)\) be the corresponding connected analytic subgroup as in (7).

If each generator is periodic--that is, for every \(j\in\{1,\dots,k\}\) there exists \(\tau_j\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\) such that

\(e^{i\tau_jH_j}=I,\) (10)

then _G_ is a closed subgroup of U(_W_). Consequently, for any normalized state \(|\xi\rangle\in W\), the orbit \(\mathcal{O}_\xi\) is closed in S(_W_).

This periodicity framework applies directly to standard quantum approximate optimization algorithm and VQA ansätze featuring integer or rationally commensurate Hamiltonian spectra.
