### A. Relation to Existing Landscape and Circuit Extension Results

Theorem IX.1 connects naturally to existing strategies for escaping local minima in Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm landscapes, adaptive control architectures, and quantum optimization theory.

**Comparison with QAOA Layer-Extension Frameworks.** A prominent approach to breaking out of suboptimal stationary points involves inserting additional gates into an existing circuit. Sack, Medina, Kueng, and Serbyn \[24\] start from a local minimum of a depth-_p_ QAOA circuit energy function and insert two zero-angle gates in 2_p_+1 prescribed ways. The resulting depth-(_p_+1) circuits prepare the same state and act as stationary points of the enlarged loss function. When the enlarged Hessians are non-degenerate, each of these 2_p_+1 stationary points possesses exactly one negative Hessian eigenvalue, exposing a direction of strict energy improvement \[24, Theorems 1,2\]. Medina and Serbyn \[21\] subsequently refined this mechanism by constructing an explicit escape vector without needing Hessian diagonalization \[21, Sec. III.A\].

**Key Distinctions and Generality.** While sharing the operational intuition of identity-initialized circuit extensions (_V_(**0**)=_I_), Theorem IX.1 extends beyond these QAOA-specific results in several fundamental ways:

1. **Generality of States and Architectures.** Theorem IX.1 places no structural restrictions on the baseline ansatz \(U(\theta_p)\) and applies to _any_ reachable state \(\rho_p\) satisfying \(\ell_{\theta_p}(\rho_p,H_P)>\lambda_{min}(H_P)\) (i.e., not a global minimum). It does not require the baseline parameters \(\theta_p\) to form a non-degenerate local minimum; the conclusion also covers arbitrary stationary points, thereby guaranteeing that no suboptimal stationary state traps the optimization process at finite depth.<tbc>

2. **Local Parameter Isolation.** Strict local improvement \(\ell_{(x,\theta_p)}(\rho,H_P)<\ell_{\theta_p}(\rho,H_P)\) is achieved by varying _only_ the newly appended parameters **_x_** near **0**, leaving the trained baseline parameters \(\theta_p\) entirely untouched.<tbc>

3. **Extension Depth vs. Structural Scope.** Whereas QAOA extensions insert two zero-angle gates to form a single extra layer, our identity block has length \(L\le\mathcal{T}_\mathfrak{g}=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D}_\mathbf{a}dim\mathfrak{g})\) built from the full control pool. Allowing an extension block longer than a single QAOA layer buys universal applicability across arbitrary Lie-algebraic control spaces g.

## X. SUMMARY AND OUTLOOK

As this work connects quantum control theory, dynamical Lie algebras, orbit geometry, and quantum- circuit synthesis across several variational architectures, we organize the summary around the central questions addressed by our results. This question-and-answer format also clarifies their broader implications and the problems that remain open.

_What is the correct notion of reachability for a variational circuit?_ The accessible states form the dynamical group orbit of the initial state, rather than the entire cyclic invariant subspace generated by the initial state under the DLA action. This distinction can be substantial: an orbit of only polynomial dimension may contain exponentially many linearly independent optima. Orbit geometry therefore provides both the appropriate reachability criterion and a precise description of its limitations.

_When does asymptotic reachability imply exact finite-depth preparation?_ We show that this implication holds whenever the relevant projective orbit is closed. In particular, a nondegenerate eigenstate of one of the initial control operators has a closed projective orbit even when the full dynamical group is not closed inside the ambient unitary group. For QAOA employing an irreducible stoquastic mixer, this converts asymptotic convergence into exact ground state preparation at some finite depth.

_Which circuit depth is sufficient?_ Locally, the commutator filtration translates the rate at which native controls generate new Lie-algebra and orbit directions into explicit bounds on the required number of native pulses. Globally, when the restricted DLA is full or special unitary, we develop complementary constructions based on diagonal and two-level synthesis, alongside logical-gate compilation. The Pauli-generated DLA case links these global costs directly to the filtration depth, whereas the MIS toggle construction provides a fully explicit realization from the original controls. Together, these results elevate qualitative controllability into quantitative resource guarantees, while clearly distinguishing the cost of arbitrary unitary synthesis from the typically more efficient state-to-state transfer.
