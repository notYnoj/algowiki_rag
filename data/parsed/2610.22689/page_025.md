<tbc>Furthermore, any pair of normalized states \(|\psi\rangle,|\phi\rangle\in V\) can be connected, up to a global phase, with a circuit of depth

\(L_\mathrm{state}\le\left(2^{q+2}-4\right)K_{1\mathrm{q}}+\left(2^{q+2}-4q-4\right)K_\mathrm{CX}.\) (90)

## C. Application to Pauli-Generated Dynamical Lie Algebras

Next, we specialize the preceding circuit-level synthesis results to the setting where the dynamical Lie algebra is generated by a finite collection of imaginary Pauli strings. This bridges the abstract bounds with explicit, constructive gate sequences implementable within variational quantum algorithms.

**Theorem VIII.5.** Let \(W=(\mathbb{C}^2)^{\otimes n}\) be a qubit Hilbert space with _N_=dim(_W_)=2_<sup>n</sup>_, and suppose that the dynamical Lie algebra<tbc>

\(\mathfrak{g}=\langle iP_1,\dots,iP_m\rangle_\mathrm{Lie},P_r\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n},\)

<tbc>is generated by a finite collection of imaginary Pauli strings and satisfies g=u(_W_) or g=su(_W_). Let D<sub>**a**</sub> be the stabilization depth of the generating set **a**={_iP_<sub>1</sub>_,...,iP<sub>m</sub>_}. Then every nonidentity Pauli flow on _W_ can be synthesized using a variational quantum circuit of depth at most

\(K_P\le 2\mathcal{D}_\mathbf{a}-1.\) (91)

Furthermore, the following global bounds hold:

1. Any unitary operator _U_ on _W_ (or \(U\in\mathrm{SU}(W)\) when \(\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{su}(W)\)) can be synthesized with a global depth _L_<sub>glob</sub> bounded by<tbc>

\(L_\mathrm{glob}\le 3(2\mathcal{D}_\mathbf{a}-1)\left(\frac{7}{4}\cdot 4^n-3\cdot 2^{n-1}\right).\) \(\text{(92)}\)

2. Any pair of normalized states \(|\psi\rangle,|\phi\rangle\in W\) can be connected, up to a global phase, with a circuit depth _L_<sub>state</sub> bounded by<tbc>

\(L_\mathrm{state}\le 12(2\mathcal{D}_\mathbf{a}-1)\left(2^{n+1}-n-2\right).\) \(\text{(93)}\)

**Corollary VIII.6.** For the reduced multi-angle MaxCut QAOA on the complete graph _K<sub>n</sub>_ (\(n\ge 2\)), the free reduced dynamical Lie algebra constitutes the full unitary algebra on the reduced Hilbert space (see Theorem VII.5). Thus the reduced space carries _q=n_-1 logical qubits, and Theorem VIII.5 provides the explicit upper bounds:

\(L_\mathrm{glob}^\mathrm{proj}\le 3(4n-3)\left(\frac{7}{4}\cdot 4^{n-1}-3\cdot 2^{n-1}\right),\) (94)

\(L_\mathrm{state}\le 12(4n-3)\left(2^n-n-1\right).\) (95)

In particular, the corresponding asymptotic depth complexities scale as O(_n_4_<sup>n</sup>_) and O(_n_2_<sup>n</sup>_), respectively.

**Remark VIII.7.** Unlike their unreduced counterparts, symmetry-reduced free MaxCut QAOA instances often achieve full special-unitary, and hence projective operator, controllability on the reduced Hilbert space. In the unreduced framework, the free DLA for _archetypal_ graphs (connected graphs that are neither bipartite nor cyclic) is semisimple and isomorphic to \(\mathfrak{su}(2^{n-1})\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2^{n-1})\) \[17, Theorem 1\]; because it preserves the two invariant parity sectors, it fails to be transitive on the full _n_-qubit Hilbert space. By contrast, the symmetry-reduced framework eliminates this degeneracy, yielding a simple Lie algebra isomorphic to the full special unitary algebra su(2<sup>_n_-1</sup>) on the (_n_-1)-qubit reduced space whenever the reduced graph is archetypal (see \[29, Theorem IV.14\]). Consequently, Theorem VIII.5 is directly applicable to these reduced systems once the stabilization depth D<sub>**a**</sub> is determined.
