<tbc>whereas the orbit determines how many of those directions matter for a particular initial state. The resulting formulas translate abstract Lie-algebraic structure into quantitative _local_ circuit-depth guarantees.

In Section VII we evaluate these bounds across several concrete architectures. Leveraging the general classification framework of \[32\] and the explicit characterization of free Maximum Cut (MaxCut) dynamical Lie algebras \[17, Theorem 1\], we establish system-size-linear stabilization depth bounds for multi-angle QAOA applied to MaxCut on paths, cycles, and complete graphs, accompanied by explicit analytical bounds on circuit depth for local element synthesis. We proceed by establishing the corresponding bounds for reduced MaxCut, Maximum Independent Set (MIS), and Grover-mixer QAOA circuits, whose dynamical Lie algebras have been classified in \[29-31\].

As local charts fail to yield uniform depth bounds across an entire group manifold or orbit, we develop a complementary _global_ synthesis framework for invariant subspaces \(V\subseteq W\) of the ambient Hilbert space on which the DLA restricts to the full or special unitary Lie algebra, u(_V_) or su(_V_) (Theorem VIII.2). While the implications of these algebras for controllability are standard \[10\], the quantitative challenge lies in implementing the required elementary operations using the native control evolutions. To address this, we first establish depth bounds for synthesizing diagonal operations and elementary two-level rotations, and subsequently assemble them via the unitary decomposition techniques of \[27\]. This yields explicit analytic depth bounds for both arbitrary group elements and state-to-state transitions within the sector and its projectivization. Notably, preparing an arbitrary target quantum state can require substantially fewer control steps than implementing an arbitrary unitary operator over the same subspace.

Alternatively, a second global synthesis strategy applies when a fully controllable sector of dimension _D_=2_<sup>q</sup>_ encodes _q_ logical qubits and the restricted DLA is isomorphic to u(_D_) or su(_D_). By combining the quantum Shannon decomposition with the state-preparation framework of \[26\], we express the circuit depth bounds directly in terms of the primitive native implementation costs for single-qubit gates (_K_<sub>1q</sub>) and CNOT gates (_K_<sub>CX</sub>). This yields asymptotic projective unitary-synthesis and state-to-state transfer complexities of \(\mathcal{O}\left(D^2(K_{1\mathrm{q}}+K_\mathrm{CX})\right)\) and O(_D(K_<sub>1q</sub>+_K_<sub>CX</sub>)), respectively (see Theorem VIII.4).

Specifically, our linear stabilization depth estimate for reduced free MaxCut on complete graphs (Theorem VII.5) yields explicit global bounds over its 2<sup>_n_-1</sup>-dimensional reduced Hilbert space (Corollary VIII.6). More broadly, as highlighted in Remark VIII.7, symmetry-reduced ma-QAOA MaxCut instances on graphs, whose reduction is connected and neither bipartite nor cyclic, generate a simple DLA isomorphic to su(2<sup>_n_-1</sup>) on the (_n_-1)-qubit reduced space \[29, Theorem IV.14\]. Consequently, Theorem VIII.5 provides a direct route to global synthesis for these reduced architectures once the generator stabilization depth D<sub>**a**</sub> is determined.

For Maximum Independent Set circuits, whose generators comprise the local _Flip-or-Stay_ mixers introduced in \[30\] alongside individual vertex controls _Z<sub>v</sub>_, the DLA realizes the full unitary algebra on the feasible sector, as established in \[30\]. Letting _D_ denote the number of feasible configurations and \(\Delta_\Gamma\) the diameter of the associated toggle-group Cayley graph, our construction yields explicit circuit-depth bounds of \(\mathcal{O}(D^3\Delta_\Gamma)\) for synthesizing arbitrary unitaries and \(\mathcal{O}(D^2\Delta_\Gamma)\) for arbitrary state-to-state transfers (Theorem VIII.8). This concrete application exemplifies our broader methodological transition: moving past qualitative reachability criteria to rigorously bound the physical resources required to execute quantum operations across the reachable manifold.

Local synthesis bridges reachable-state geometry and variational optimization. Specifically, when the dynamical Lie group _G_ is closed, the problem generator satisfies \(iH_P\in\mathfrak{g}\), and an exact ground state of _H<sub>P</sub>_ is reachable within the orbit, the resulting energy landscape is free of suboptimal local minima. More concretely, from any suboptimal circuit output, one can append an identity-initialized block of bounded native-control depth that exposes either a nonzero energy gradient or a negative-curvature direction (Theorem IX.1). This statement complements QAOA layer-extension results \[24\] and applies to the broader Hamiltonian-control setting under its hypotheses, guaranteeing the existence of a descent direction.
