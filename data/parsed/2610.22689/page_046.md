<tbc>Since \(D\ge 4\), the alternating group A<sub>F</sub> acts transitively on ordered pairs of distinct elements in F. Hence, there exists a permutation \(P\in\mathcal{T}(\Gamma)\) such that<tbc>

\(P(a)=r,P(b)=s.\)

<tbc>Denoting by \(\widehat{P}\in U(W_\mathcal{F})\) the corresponding permutation operator, we obtain<tbc>

\(\widehat{P}X_{ab}\widehat{P}^{-1}=X_{rs},\)

<tbc>and consequently,

\(e^{-itX_{rs}}=\widehat{P}e^{-itX_{ab}}\widehat{P}^{-1}.\) (D25)

By definition of the toggle-group diameter \(\Delta_\Gamma\), both \(\widehat{P}\) and \(\widehat{P}^{-1}\) can be implemented using at most \(\Delta_\Gamma\) local mixer generators. It follows that

\(K_X\le K_X^{(v)}+2\Delta_\Gamma.\)

Combining this inequality with (D23) yields (D24).

### c. Global Synthesis Complexity and State-to-State Bounds

By substituting the explicit primitive costs _K<sub>X</sub>_ (Proposition D.2) and _K_<sub>diag</sub> (Proposition D.1) into the general pulse-complexity bounds of Eq. (86) and Theorem VIII.2, we establish the global synthesis and state-to-state transition bounds asserted in Theorem VIII.8.

## Appendix E: Finite-Depth Exposure of Descent Directions: Detailed Treatment

Let \(\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}(W)\) be the dynamical Lie algebra generated by the available skew-Hermitian control operators, and let _G_ denote the associated connected dynamical Lie group. We assume that the problem Hamiltonian satisfies \(iH_P\in\mathfrak{g}\). Working in the density operator formalism eliminates unphysical global phase ambiguities; we further assume that _G_ is a compact Lie group.

For a normalized initial state \(|\xi\rangle\), we recall that \(\rho=|\xi\rangle\langle\xi|\) is the initial density matrix. The corresponding _reachable state orbit_ under _G_ is the adjoint orbit

\(\mathcal{O}_\rho:=\{g\rho g^\dagger:g\in G\}.\) (E1)

A parameterized quantum circuit specifies a smooth map \(U(\theta)\in G\), which induces the circuit state endpoint map<tbc>

\(\Phi_\rho(\theta):=U(\theta)\rho U^\dagger(\theta)\in\mathcal{O}_\rho.\)

<tbc>The circuit loss function measures the expectation value of the problem Hamiltonian _H<sub>P</sub>_:

\(\ell_\theta(\rho,H_P):=\langle\psi(\theta)|H_P|\psi(\theta)\rangle=\text{Tr}\big(H_PU(\theta)\rho U^\dagger(\theta)\big),\) (E2)

where \(|\psi(\theta)\rangle=U(\theta)|\xi\rangle\). The loss function factors through the state orbit as the composition<tbc>

\(\ell_\theta(\rho,H_P)=(h\circ\Phi_\rho)(\theta),\)

<tbc>where \(h:\mathcal{O}_\rho\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\) is the linear height function defined by

\(h(A):=\text{Tr}(H_P\cdot A).\) (E3)
