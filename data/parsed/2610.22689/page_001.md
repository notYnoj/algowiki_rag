The analysis of Hamiltonian Variational Ansätze centers on three fundamental questions: which states are exactly reachable from a given initial state, what native control depth suffices for local and global synthesis, and whether a bounded circuit extension can expose a direction of decreasing cost at a suboptimal output. We address these challenges by leveraging the dynamical Lie algebras (DLAs) associated with the circuits and the geometry of the initial state’s orbit under the action of the corresponding dynamical Lie group _G_. We prove that an initial state has a closed projective orbit whenever it is a nondegenerate eigenstate of a control Hamiltonian, without requiring the dynamical Lie group itself to be closed. Combined with asymptotic QAOA convergence, this guarantees exact, finite depth ground state preparation for architectures employing diagonal problem Hamiltonians and irreducible stoquastic mixers. We also show that an orbit of quadratic dimension can contain exponentially many mutually orthogonal optimal states, demonstrating how a small reachable manifold can accommodate a large family of solutions. We bound the number of native control exponentials required for local unitary synthesis and state preparation using DLA commutator filtration growth alongside reachable orbit dimensions. Complementing these local results, we construct global frameworks on invariant sectors equipped with full unitary or special unitary dynamical Lie algebras. These constructions yield explicit applications to Pauli-generated systems and constrained maximum independent set multi-angle QAOA circuits; specifically, we show that their native control costs are directly determined by commutator filtration depth and independent-set toggle-group geometry, respectively. Finally, if the dynamical Lie group is compact, the observable _HP_ generates an admissible evolution (\(iHP\in\text{g}\), where g is the DLA), and the projective orbit of the initial state contains a ground state ray of _HP_, then a bounded depth, identity-initialized circuit extension exhibits either first-order descent or negative curvature at every suboptimal circuit output.

### I. INTRODUCTION

Variational circuits built from Hamiltonian evolutions employ a prescribed set of controls at every depth. For instance, the celebrated Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) employs a variational ansatz produced by alternating unitary evolutions generated by the exponentials of the problem and mixer Hamiltonians [12, 15]; related constructions appear throughout variational quantum algorithms (VQA) [8]. Although asymptotic convergence to the optimal eigenspace is established for suitable QAOA mixers [5], it remains an open question whether the optimum can be attained exactly at a finite circuit depth. Likewise, establishing that a transformation is controllable does not quantify the number of unitary evolutions in the basic Hamiltonians required to implement it. In this work, we establish general reachability and circuit synthesis theorems for Hamiltonian variational circuits. When applied to QAOA, these methods guarantee exact finite-depth ground state preparation for a broad class of mixers and yield explicit depth bounds across several standard architectures.

The dynamical Lie algebra g associated with a family of control Hamiltonians (see (2)) captures the infinitesimal reachable directions of a quantum circuit. While Lie-algebraic techniques provide foundational controllability criteria in quantum control [9] and have recently proved instrumental in analyzing the expressivity and trainability of variational quantum algorithms [13, 19, 23], standard DLA analysis leaves a gap when applied to state-dependent reachability, as it typically abstracts away both the fixed initial state and the explicit circuit-depth bounds required to synthesize operations from a finite native gate set. Specifically, the minimal g-invariant subspace containing the initial state \(|\xi\rangle\) defines a subspace whose unit sphere contains all states generated by the action of parameterized VQA circuits on \(|\xi\rangle\).
