<tbc>gives<tbc>

\(\pi_W(\mathrm{Ad}_{e^{ta_i}}(c))=t\pi_W([a_i,c])+O(t^2).\)

<tbc>Equivalently,<tbc>

\(\frac{1}{t}\pi_W(\mathrm{Ad}_{e^{ta_i}}(c))\longrightarrow\pi_W([a_i,c])\neq 0\text{as}t\rightarrow 0.\)

<tbc>It follows that there exists \(\delta>0\) such that

\(b_m:=\mathrm{Ad}_{e^{ta_i}}(c)\notin W\text{whenever}0<|t|<\mathrm{min}\{\delta,\varepsilon\}.\) (39)

Thus every sufficiently small nonzero conjugation parameter supplies a new linearly independent direction.

The same conjugation structure gives an exact synthesis of every selected flow. Indeed, if _b<sub>m</sub>_ is selected at stage _j_, then \(b_m\in\mathcal{C}_j(\varepsilon)\), so it has the form<tbc>

\(b_m=\mathrm{Ad}_{P_m}(a_{i_0}),P_m=e^{t_1a_{i_1}}\cdots e^{t_qa_{i_q}},q\le j-1.\)

<tbc>Therefore, for every \(s\in\mathbb{R}\),<tbc>

\(e^{sb_m}=e^{sP_ma_{i_0}P_m^{-1}}=P_me^{sa_{i_0}}P_m^{-1}=e^{t_1a_{i_1}}\cdots e^{t_qa_{i_q}}e^{sa_{i_0}}e^{-t_qa_{i_q}}\cdots e^{-t_1a_{i_1}}.\)

<tbc>In particular, if _b<sub>m</sub>_=Ad_<sub>e<sup>ta<sub>i</sub></sup></sub>(c_), then<tbc>

\(e^{sb_m}=e^{ta_i}e^{sc}e^{-ta_i}.\)

<tbc>Although _c_ need not be a selected basis element, its membership in \(\mathcal{C}_{j-1}(\varepsilon)\) supplies an exact native factorization of _e<sup>sc</sup>_. Hence _e<sup>sb<sub>m</sub></sup>_ is synthesized using at most

\(2q+1\le 2(j-1)+1\le 2j-1\)

native generator flows, without Trotterization or Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff approximations.

If _b<sub>m</sub>_ is selected at filtration stage _j_, then \(b_m\in\mathcal{C}_j(\varepsilon)\). Hence, as shown in Remark VI.3, the corresponding flow _e<sup>sb<sub>m</sub></sup>_ admits an exact factorization into at most 2_j_-1 native generator flows. Since precisely<tbc>

\(c_j:=\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{F}_j^\mathbf{a})-\mathrm{dim}(\mathcal{F}_{j-1}^\mathbf{a})\)

<tbc>basis elements are selected at stage _j_, concatenating these factorizations in the canonical-coordinate representation \(\varphi_\mathfrak{g}\) shows that every \(g\in U_{I,\varphi_\mathfrak{g}}\) can be represented using at most

\(\mathcal{T}_\mathbf{a}:=\sum_{j=1}^{\mathcal{D}_\mathbf{a}}(2j-1)c_j\) (40)

native exponential factors. Thus T<sub>**a**</sub> is a uniform upper bound furnished by the construction; cancellations or shorter conjugating words may reduce the number of factors required for a particular group element.

For any non-abelian Lie algebra g, this operation count satisfies the elementary bounds

\(\mathrm{dim}(\mathfrak{g})\le\mathcal{T}_\mathbf{a}<\mathrm{dim}^2(\mathfrak{g}).\) (41)

Since \(j\le\mathcal{D}_\mathbf{a}\) and \(\sum_jc_j=\mathrm{dim}(\mathfrak{g})\), (40) yields the refined upper bound

\(\mathcal{T}_\mathbf{a}\le(2\mathcal{D}_\mathbf{a}-1)\mathrm{dim}\mathfrak{g}.\) (42)

These observations establish a stabilization depth dependent bound on local circuit synthesis complexity, summarized in the following theorem.

**Theorem VI.4.** Let \(\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}(W)\) be a dynamical Lie algebra generated by {_a_<sub>1</sub>_,...,a<sub>k</sub>_} with stabilization depth D<sub>**a**</sub>. Then every element _g_ in the identity neighborhood \(U_{I,\varphi_\mathfrak{g}}\subseteq G\) (cf. (31)) admits a finite product- of-exponentials decomposition

\(g=e^{\theta_La_{j_L}}\cdots e^{\theta_1a_{j_1}},\) (43)

where \(a_{j_s}\in\{a_1,\dots,a_k\}\) for each \(s\in\{1,\dots,\mathcal{T}_\mathbf{a}\}\), and the required depth \(L\le\mathcal{T}_\mathbf{a}\) satisfies (42). In particular, this bounds the local parameterization depth to O(D<sub>**a**</sub>dimg).
