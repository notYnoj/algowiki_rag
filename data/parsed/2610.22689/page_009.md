<tbc>**Example IV.7.** The preceding corollary applies to several classes of dynamical Lie algebras arising in quantum optimization.

First, consider the free (multi-angle) MaxCut QAOA ansatz defined on a connected graph \(\Gamma=(V,E)\) with \(n:=|V|\ge 2\). Its dynamical Lie algebra is given by

\(\mathfrak{g}_{\Gamma,\mathrm{free}}=\langle iX_v,iZ_uZ_v|v\in V,(u,v)\in E\rangle_\mathrm{Lie}.\) (17)

By the structural classification in \[17, Theorem 1\], \(\mathfrak{g}_{\Gamma,\mathrm{free}}\) belongs to one of six families, each of which is either a compact simple Lie algebra or a direct sum of compact simple Lie algebras. Consequently, \(\mathfrak{g}_{\Gamma,\mathrm{free}}\) is semisimple, which implies that the corresponding dynamical Lie group is closed \[18, Proposition 7.9\]. Thus, Theorem IV.4 implies that whenever a target ray belongs to the closure of the orbit of the initial one, it is exactly reachable by a finite multi-angle circuit.

This conclusion extends directly to general (not necessarily connected) graphs. If a graph has no isolated vertices, its free DLA decomposes into a direct sum of the DLAs associated with its connected components, thereby satisfying the hypotheses of Corollary IV.6 with c=0.

More generally, let A={_iP_<sub>1</sub>_,...,iP<sub>m</sub>_} be a Lie-algebraically independent set of skew-Hermitian Pauli strings, and let C(A) be its anticommutation graph, whose vertices represent the generators _iP<sub>j</sub>_ and whose edges join pairs \((iP_j,iP_\ell)\) that anticommute \((P_jP_\ell=-P_\ell P_j)\). Suppose that C(A) is connected and contains at least two vertices.

According to the classification of Pauli-generated Lie algebras in \[1, Theorem 2\], the generated Lie algebra

\(\mathfrak{g}_\mathcal{A}:=\langle iP_1,\dots,iP_m\rangle_\mathrm{Lie}\) (18)

is isomorphic to a direct sum of classical compact simple Lie algebras of type so(_d_), sp(_d_), or su(_d_), where the specific algebra and rank are uniquely determined by the topology of C(A) and the algebraic relations among its generators. Thus, g<sub>A</sub> is semisimple, every projective orbit generated by the corresponding circuit architecture is closed, and exact finite reachability holds due to Theorem IV.4 .

**Theorem IV.8.** Let _H_<sub>1</sub>_,...,H<sub>k</sub>_ be a collection of Hamiltonians on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space _W_, let \(\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{u}(W)\) denote the corresponding dynamical Lie algebra (see (6)), and let \(G\subseteq\mathrm{U}(W)\) be the corresponding connected Lie subgroup. Suppose that the target observable _H<sub>P</sub>_ satisfies<tbc>

\(iH_P\in\mathfrak{g}.\)

## B. Finite-Depth Ground Space Reachability Guarantees in QAOA

While general state controllability concerns the reachability of arbitrary pure states, in many practical applications, particularly classical combinatorial optimization, our primary interest lies in the reachability of the optimal solution subspace rather than a specific target state.

Let \(W=(\mathbb{C}^2)^{\otimes n}\) denote the _n_-qubit Hilbert space equipped with the computational basis \(\{|s\rangle\}_{s\in\{0,1\}^n}\). Let _H<sub>P</sub>_ be the problem Hamiltonian acting on _W_, which encodes a classical objective function<tbc>

\(F:\{0,1\}^n\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\)

<tbc>via \(H_P|s\rangle=F(s)|s\rangle\). Let \(\lambda_\mathrm{min}:=\mathrm{min}_{s\in\{0,1\}^n}F(s)\) denote the ground state energy of _H<sub>P</sub>_, and let \(H_{P,\mathrm{min}}\subseteq W\) denote its corresponding ground space:

\(H_{P,\mathrm{min}}:=\mathrm{span}\left\{|s\rangle|s\in\{0,1\}^n,F(s)=\lambda_\mathrm{min}\right\}.\) (19)
