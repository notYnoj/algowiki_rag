## IX. FINITE-DEPTH EXPOSURE OF DESCENT DIRECTIONS

The finite-depth preparation results established in the preceding sections guarantee the existence of parametrized circuits that reproduce elements within a neighborhood \(\mathcal{V}_{[\psi]}\) of a ray \([\psi]\) in the projective orbit \(\mathcal{O}_{[\psi]}\) (see Theorem VI.6). However, they do not provide an explicit mechanism for determining concrete parameter values that realize specific states in this neighborhood. In this section, we take a first step toward resolving this constructive problem by connecting parameter identification to the infinitesimal framework developed in Section VI. Specifically, we show that any finite-depth circuit preparing a non-optimal state can be augmented by a bounded-length identity block, ensuring that the expanded parametrization explicitly exhibits either a first-order descent direction for the loss function or a direction of negative curvature.

Before presenting our main result, we formalize the framework for circuit extensions. Let \(|\xi\rangle\) be an initial reference state, and let

\(\rho_p=|\psi(\theta_p)\rangle\langle\psi(\theta_p)|\) (114)

be the density matrix corresponding to the state \(|\psi(\theta_p)\rangle=U(\theta_p)|\xi\rangle\) prepared by a depth-_p_ parameterized quantum circuit \(U(\theta_p)\). Throughout the remainder of this section, we assume that the observable (problem Hamiltonian) _H<sub>P</sub>_ satisfies \(iH_P\in\mathfrak{g}\), where g is the dynamical Lie algebra, and that the associated Lie group _G_=exp(g) is compact.

An _identity block V_(**_x_**) is a parameterized unitary operation satisfying _V_(**0**)=_I_, generated by a sequence of at most T<sub>g</sub> elementary evolutions under generators of g. Appending _V_(**_x_**) yields the augmented loss function

\(\ell(x,\theta_p):=\mathrm{Tr}\big[V(x)\rho_pV^\dagger(x)H_P\big],\) (115)

which recovers the baseline energy at the origin: \(\ell(0,\theta_p)=\ell(\theta_p)\).

The theorem below formalizes the finite-depth exposure guarantee for arbitrary non-optimal states. For complete derivations and technical proofs, we refer the reader to Appendix E.

**Theorem IX.1** (Finite-depth exposure of descent directions)**.** Assume that the _G_-orbit \(\mathcal{O}_\psi\) of the initial state \(|\psi\rangle\) intersects the ground eigenspace of _H<sub>P</sub>_, and suppose the energy prepared by the current circuit is strictly non-optimal:

\(\ell_{\theta_p}(\rho,H_P)>\lambda_\mathrm{min}(H_P),\)

where \(\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\) is the initial state density matrix and \(\ell_{\theta_p}(\rho,H_P)=\mathrm{Tr}\big[U(\theta_p)\rho U^\dagger(\theta_p)H_P\big]\) denotes the current expectation value. Then, there exists an identity block _V_(**_x_**) requiring at most T<sub>g</sub> elementary generator evolutions such that after appending _V_(**_x_**), exactly one of the following holds for the augmented loss function \(\ell_{(x,\theta_p)}(\rho,H_P)\) associated with the extended unitary \(U(x,\theta_p)=V(x)U(\theta_p)\):

1. **First-Order Descent:** The parameter gradient at the origin is non-vanishing,<tbc>

\(\nabla_x\ell_{(x,\theta_p)}(\rho,H_P)\big|_{x=0}\neq 0.\) \(\text{(116)}\)

2. **Second-Order Escape:** The gradient vanishes at **_x_**=**0**, but there exists a direction \(v\in\mathbb{R}^m\) exhibiting negative second-order curvature:<tbc>

\(\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\big|_{t=0}\ell_{(tv,\theta_p)}(\rho,H_P)<0.\) \(\text{(117)}\)

Consequently, every neighborhood of **0** contains parameters **_x_** yielding strict energy improvement:

\(\ell_{(x,\theta_p)}(\rho,H_P)<\ell_{\theta_p}(\rho,H_P).\) \(\text{(118)}\)

The identity block extension requires at most T<sub>g</sub> elementary generator evolutions.

**Remark IX.2** (Computational complexity of the escape block)**.** Recall that the identity block depth satisfies \(\mathcal{T}_\mathbf{a}=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D}_\mathbf{a}\mathrm{dim}\mathfrak{g})\le\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{dim}(\mathfrak{g})^2)\) (see Theorem VI.4), where D<sub>**a**</sub> is the filtration depth of the generator pool. Consequently, for Lie-algebraic architectures with polynomially bounded DLA dimension dim(g)=poly(_n_), Theorem IX.1 guarantees that any suboptimal stationary point can be escaped by appending a local identity block of depth at most poly(_n_).
