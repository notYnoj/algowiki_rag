## Appendix A: Proofs of the Results in Sections III and IV

This section collects the technical proofs for the theoretical results presented in Sections III and IV. We begin by establishing the classification of projectively transitive Lie algebras (Theorem III.2), followed by the topological closedness criteria for dynamical group orbits.

_Proof of Theorem III.2._ Projective transitivity implies that the representation of g on _V_ is irreducible: any proper nonzero invariant subspace would define a proper invariant projective subvariety containing the orbit, contradicting transitivity. Moreover, since _V V_ is an irreducible representation of a reductive Lie algebra g, Schur’s lemma implies that the center z(g) acts on _V_ via scalar operators, and thus acts trivially on the projective space \(\mathbb{P}(V)\cong\mathbb{CP}^{d-1}\). Consequently, the effective action on P(_V_) factors through the semisimple commutator ideal g<sub>ss</sub>=\[g,g\]. In turn, the connected analytic subgroup \(G_\mathrm{ss}\subset\mathrm{U}(d)\) corresponding to g<sub>ss</sub> is closed and compact (see, e.g., \[18, Proposition 7.9\]).

By the classification of connected compact linear groups acting transitively on complex projective space \[22,  1\], any connected compact subgroup of U(_d_) acting transitively on CP<sup>_d_-1</sup> has a Lie algebra that is, up to unitary conjugation, one of the following:

\(\mathfrak{su}(d),\mathfrak{u}(d),\)

or, when _d_=2_m_,

\(\mathfrak{sp}(m),\mathfrak{sp}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1),\)

with the indicated defining representations. This establishes the necessity of the listed Lie algebras.

Conversely, SU(_d_) and U(_d_) act transitively on the unit sphere in C_<sup>d</sup>_, and consequently transitively on CP<sup>_d_-1</sup>. Similarly, the compact symplectic group Sp(_m_) acts transitively on the unit sphere of its defining representation C<sup>2_m_</sup>, and thus transitively on the corresponding projective space CP<sup>2_m_-1</sup>. Adjoining the central scalar factor U(1) leaves the projective orbits unchanged. Therefore, all listed Lie algebras admit transitive actions on complex projective space.

_Proof of Theorem IV.1._ Let _K_:=<o>_G_</o> denote the topological closure of _G_ in U(_W_), and let k be its Lie algebra. Since _G_ is connected, _K_ is a compact, connected Lie group.

Since _G_ is generated by the exponentials of elements in g, we have Ad_<sub>g</sub>_(g)=g for all \(g\in G\). By the density of _G_ in _K_ and the continuity of the adjoint action, it follows that

\(\mathrm{Ad}_U(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{g}\text{for every}U\in K.\)

Differentiating this relation yields \([\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{g}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}\), establishing that g is an ideal of k.
