<tbc>While producing a collection of circuits that reproduces every operator within an identity neighborhood \(U_{I,\varphi_\mathfrak{g}}\) in the dynamical Lie group _G_ is sufficient to generate the corresponding neighborhood \(\mathcal{V}_{[\psi]}\) of the projectivization of a state \(|\psi\rangle\) in \(\mathcal{O}_{[\psi]}\), the upper bound on circuit depth appearing in (51) can be noticeably smaller, as the following example demonstrates.

**Example VI.7.** Consider an _n_-qubit Hilbert space _W_=C_<sup>d</sup>_ with _d_=2_<sup>n</sup>_. The dimensions of the full special unitary Lie algebra su(_d_) and the unit sphere of states S(C_<sup>d</sup>_) are

\(\mathrm{dim}(\mathfrak{su}(d))=d^2-1,\mathrm{dim}_\mathbb{R}(\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}^d))=2d-2.\) (53)

Hence, replacing the dimension of the full dynamical Lie algebra with the dimension of the state orbit reduces the governing dimension factor from order O(4_<sup>n</sup>_) down to O(2_<sup>n</sup>_).

## VII. STABILIZATION DEPTH BOUNDS FOR QAOA ARCHITECTURES

Next we compute explicit bounds on the stabilization depth D<sub>**a**</sub> for several prominent families of parameterized quantum circuits. Specifically, we analyze the dynamical Lie algebras associated with multi-angle QAOA ansätze applied to various combinatorial optimization problems, as well as Grover-mixer QAOA (GM-QAOA) architectures. These concrete bounds characterize the bracket depth required to exhaust the underlying Lie algebra, thereby yielding explicit upper bounds on the local circuit depth required for expressivity near the identity of the group.

In Subsection VIIE, we leverage these results to establish explicit circuit-depth upper bounds for local unitary synthesis and state preparation. A rigorous treatment and full technical proofs for these stabilization depth derivations are provided in Appendix C.

**Remark VII.1.** The stabilization depth D<sub>**a**</sub> is defined relative to a specific choice of generating set \(\mathbf{a}\subset\mathfrak{g}\) rather than the Lie algebra g itself (see Definition VI.2). However, each circuit architecture considered in this section is equipped with a canonical, predefined set of elementary gate generators (e.g., individual Pauli terms or driver/problem Hamiltonian components). Consequently, when the generating set **a** is unambiguous from context, we adopt the slight abuse of notation \(\mathcal{D}_\mathfrak{g}\equiv\mathcal{D}_\mathbf{a}\) to denote the stabilization depth of the corresponding dynamical Lie algebra.

### A. Dynamical Lie Algebras for the MaxCut Problem QAOA

The Maximum Cut problem on a finite graph \(\Gamma=(V,E)\) seeks a vertex partition maximizing the number of crossing edges. In the standard Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm framework, this combinatorial problem is mapped to an Ising spin system defined by the transverse-field mixer _B_ and problem Hamiltonian _H<sub>P</sub>_:

\(B=\sum_{w\in V}X_w,H_P=\sum_{\{v,w\}\in E}Z_vZ_w.\) (54)

The associated standard dynamical Lie algebra is generated by these two collective operators:

\(\mathfrak{g}_{\Gamma,\mathrm{std}}:=\left\langle iB,iH_P\right\rangle_\mathrm{Lie}.\) (55)

In multi-angle QAOA, each individual term in the mixer and problem Hamiltonians is assigned an independent variational parameter. The corresponding _free_ dynamical Lie algebra is formed by taking these individual operators as separate control fields:

\(\mathfrak{g}_{\Gamma,\mathrm{free}}:=\left\langle\{iX_w|w\in V\}\cup\{iZ_vZ_w|\{v,w\}\in E\}\right\rangle_\mathrm{Lie}.\) (56)
