<tbc>Since g is a reductive Lie subalgebra of the compact Lie algebra u(_W_), it decomposes into semisimple and central components:

\(\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_\mathrm{ss}\oplus\mathfrak{z},\mathfrak{g}_\mathrm{ss}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],\mathfrak{z}=Z(\mathfrak{g}).\)

Let _G_<sub>ss</sub> and _Z_<sub>0</sub> denote the connected analytic subgroups corresponding to g<sub>ss</sub> and z, respectively. At the group level,

\(G=G_\mathrm{ss}Z_0,\)

where _G_<sub>ss</sub> and _Z_<sub>0</sub> commute.

For any \(A\in\mathfrak{z}\), the centrality of _A_ together with the inclusion \(iH_r\in\mathfrak{g}\) implies<tbc>

\([A,H_r]=0.\)

<tbc>Consequently, if \(H_r|s\rangle=\lambda|s\rangle\), then<tbc>

\(H_rA|s\rangle=AH_r|s\rangle=\lambda A|s\rangle.\)

<tbc>Thus, \(A|s\rangle\) belongs to the \(\lambda\)-eigenspace of _H<sub>r</sub>_. As this eigenspace is assumed to be one-dimensional, there exists a real scalar \(\alpha_A\in\mathbb{R}\) such that

\(A|s\rangle=i\alpha_A|s\rangle,\)

which upon exponentiation yields

\(e^A|s\rangle=e^{i\alpha_A}|s\rangle.\)

Therefore, the connected central subgroup _Z_<sub>0</sub> acts on \[_s_\] purely by phase multiplication, leaving the projective class \[_s_\] invariant.

Furthermore, as _Z_<sub>0</sub> commutes with _G_<sub>ss</sub>, for every \(g_\mathrm{ss}\in G_\mathrm{ss}\) and \(z\in Z_0\) there exists a phase \(\theta(z)\in\mathbb{R}\) satisfying

\(zg_\mathrm{ss}|s\rangle=g_\mathrm{ss}z|s\rangle=e^{i\theta(z)}g_\mathrm{ss}|s\rangle.\)

It follows that the full projective orbit reduces to the semisimple orbit:

\(G\cdot[s]=G_\mathrm{ss}\cdot[s].\) (A3)

Since its Lie algebra g<sub>ss</sub> is compact and semisimple, the connected analytic subgroup _G_<sub>ss</sub> is compact \[18, Proposition 7.9\]. Being the continuous image of a compact group, the orbit \(G_\mathrm{ss}\cdot[s]\) is compact, and therefore closed in P(_W_). Together with (A3), this establishes the statement in (13).

**Corollary A.1.** Under either hypothesis of Theorem IV.4, let

\(F:\mathbb{P}(W)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\)

be a continuous function. Then both

\(\mathrm{inf}_{[v]\in G\cdot[s]}F([v])\text{and}\mathrm{sup}_{[v]\in G\cdot[s]}F([v])\)

are attained by finite parametrized circuits generated by _H_<sub>1</sub>_,...,H<sub>k</sub>_.

_Proof._ Under either hypothesis, the orbit \(G\cdot[s]\) is a closed subset of the compact space P(_W_), and is therefore compact. Consequently, the continuous function _F_ attains its minimum and maximum on this orbit. By Theorem IV.4, every point of the orbit is realized by a finite word in the generating one-parameter subgroups.
