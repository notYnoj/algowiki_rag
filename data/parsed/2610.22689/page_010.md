<tbc>Suppose that the mixer Hamiltonian _H<sub>M</sub>_ in QAOA is _stoquastic_ with respect to the computational basis, meaning its off-diagonal elements satisfy \(\langle s|H_M|t\rangle\le 0\) for all distinct \(s,t\in\{0,1\}^n\), and _irreducible_, in the sense that it has no stable proper coordinate subspaces.

By the Perron-Frobenius theorem, the ground state space of _H<sub>M</sub>_ is one-dimensional. Let \(|\xi\rangle\) denote a unit vector spanning this space.

Given _H<sub>M</sub>_ as above and _H<sub>P</sub>_ diagonal in the computational basis, it was shown in \[5\] that the QAOA ansatz, initialized at \(|\xi\rangle\), asymptotically prepares a state within the minimum-energy sector _H_<sub>_P,_min</sub> as the circuit depth \(p\rightarrow\infty\).

Since every parametrized quantum circuit representing the QAOA ansatz (see (15)) is structurally confined to the dynamical Lie group _G_, satisfying the Perron-Frobenius conditions imposes the non-trivial overlap constraint on the initial state orbit closure:

\(\overline{G\cdot[\xi]}\cap\mathbb{P}(H_{P,\mathrm{min}})\neq\varnothing.\) (20)

The following result is a particular instance of Theorem IV.4 (by setting _H<sub>r</sub>=H<sub>M</sub>_). It demonstrates that under mild conditions on the mixer, the orbit of the initial state is closed, thereby upgrading the standard infinite-depth asymptotic convergence of QAOA to an exact, finite-depth reachability guarantee.

**Theorem IV.9.** Let _W_ be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space equipped with a computational basis. Let \(H_P,H_M\in\mathrm{Herm}(W)\) be Hermitian operators such that _H<sub>P</sub>_ is diagonal in the computational basis, and _H<sub>M</sub>_ is irreducible and stoquastic in the same basis. Let \(|\xi\rangle\) be the unique normalized ground state of _H<sub>M</sub>_ .

Then, there exist a finite circuit depth \(L\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\), parameter vectors \(\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^L\), a normalized ground state \(|\zeta\rangle\in H_{P,\mathrm{min}}\), and a global phase \(\phi\in\mathbb{R}\) such that

\(U_L(\beta,\gamma)|\xi\rangle=|\zeta\rangle,\) (21)

where \(U_L(\beta,\gamma)\) is defined as in (15).

Consequently, the infinite-depth asymptotic convergence guarantee of QAOA is promoted to an exact finite-depth reachability guarantee.

**Remark IV.10.** While Theorem IV.9 establishes a qualitative existence guarantee, it is non-constructive in nature, as it neither provides explicit target-specific depth bounds nor yields concrete parameter optimization procedures.

## V. GEOMETRY VS ALGEBRA: SMALL ORBITS WITH EXPONENTIALLY MANY LINEARLY INDEPENDENT OPTIMA

A fundamental geometric feature of the dynamical Lie group orbit \(\mathcal{O}_{[\xi]}\subseteq\mathbb{P}(W)\) generated by the ray of an initial state \(|\xi\rangle\) is that it forms a smooth, connected manifold rather than a linear subspace. Consequently, a low-dimensional orbit can _curve_ through projective Hilbert space in such a way that the states inside it span a high-dimensional vector subspace.

In this section, we present an explicit example of this phenomenon, arising from a fixed-particle _XY_ ansatz, where the orbit of quadratic dimension O(_n_<sup>2</sup>) in the number of qubits _n_ contains exponentially many linearly independent ground states of the problem Hamiltonian _H<sub>P</sub>_. Although the underlying classical optimization task in this construction is intentionally simple, its primary purpose is to vividly demonstrate that low orbit dimensionality poses no intrinsic barrier to capturing exponentially many solutions or achieving exact ground space reachability in variational quantum algorithms.

Let \(n\ge 2\), \(1\le k\le n-1\), and consider the fixed-excitation (fixed-Hamming-weight) subspace<tbc>

\(W_k:=\mathrm{span}\left\{|S\rangle|S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\},|S|=k\right\}.\) (22)

<tbc>Here, _S_ records the occupied qubit sites, and \(N_j:=\frac{I-Z_j}{2}=|1\rangle\langle 1|_j\) is the occupation-number operator at site _j_. We consider the nearest-neighbor _XY_ path mixer _H<sub>M</sub>_ paired with an endpoint problem Hamiltonian _H<sub>P</sub>_:

\(H_M:=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(X_jX_{j+1}+Y_jY_{j+1}\right),H_P:=N_1.\) (23)
