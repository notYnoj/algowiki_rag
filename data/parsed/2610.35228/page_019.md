<tbc>Assume that the number of levels is subexponential in _q<sub>N</sub>_, uniformly over this parameter range, in the precise sense that<tbc>

\(max_{1\le t\le t_{max}(N)}logr(N,t)=o(q_N)\text{as}N\rightarrow\infty.\)

<tbc>For all sufficiently large _N_, every such level is at most _R<sub>N</sub>_. For each sufficiently large graph size supplied by Proposition 4.8, the associated integer _T_ must therefore satisfy \(t_{max}(N)<T\le Cq_N\): otherwise the proposed implication at _t=T_ contradicts Eq. (18). This bounds the valid parameter range for infinitely many values of _N_. In particular, it rules out a universal range \(t_{max}(N)=\omega(\sqrt{logNloglogN})\) under the stated level bound. For any fixed _a\>_0 and \(\beta>1/2\), one has

\(\frac{a(logN)^\beta}{q_N}=\frac{a(logN)^{\beta-1/2}}{\sqrt{loglogN}}\longrightarrow\infty.\)

The first step uses the definition of _q<sub>N</sub>_, and the second step follows because a positive power of log_N_ grows faster than \(\sqrt{loglogN}\). Hence a universal range \(t_{max}(N)\ge a(logN)^\beta\) for all sufficiently large _N_ is impossible under the stated level bound. This is a restriction on this implication from the Lasserre value and does not exclude algorithms using additional properties of the graph.
