To deal with the non-existence of stable matching, Biró et al. \[4\] introduce the problem of finding a matching that minimizes the number of blocking pairs, called Min- BP-SRI. Unfortunately, Min-BP-SRI is W\[1\]-hard with respect to the number \(\beta\) of blocking pairs \[6\] and NP-hard to approximate \[4\].

A third variant of SM is to allow agents to have both _incomplete_ and _indifferent_ preferences and between agents. In this case, a stable matching<sup>1</sup> still exists, but different stable matchings may have different sizes. A commonly studied goal for incomplete preferences with ties is to find a stable matching with maximum size. This gives rise to the setting of Maximum-Size Stable Marriage with Incomplete preferences and Ties (Max-SMTI). Max-SMTI is NP-hard to approximate \[22\] and W\[1\]-hard with respect to the number of ties \[24\].

The above inapproximability and parameterized intractability results beg the study of approximation algorithms combined with fixed-parameter tractable (FPT) algorithms. Given the inherent computational hardness of Min-BP-SMI, Min-BP-SRI, and Max-SMTI, parameterized complexity provides a natural framework to explore efficient algorithms when certain problem parameters are small. In this work, we investigate FPT-approximability for both minimizing the number of blocking pairs and maximizing the size of stable matchings in presence of ties.

## 1.1 Our Contributions

We show that Min-BP-SMI and Min-BP-SRI remain resistant to FPT-approximability. More precisely, both problems are W\[1\]-hard with respect to \(\beta\) to approximate to a factor \(F(\beta)\cdot n^{O(1)}\), where _n_ is the number of agents, \(\beta\) is the number of blocking pairs, _F_ is an arbitrary computable function. In other words, it is unlikely to obtain an FPT algorithm with respect to \(\beta\) that can distinguish between instances that have at most \(\beta\) or at least \(F(\beta)\) blocking pairs. This hardness result for Min-BP-SMI holds even when we require the matching to be perfect, i.e., every agent has to be matched. On the other hand, when preferences can be incomplete and may have ties, we provide a simple FPT-approximation scheme for Max-SMTI with respect to the number \(\tau\) of agents with ties, i.e., an approximation scheme that given an instance _I_ of SMTI, where \(\tau\) agents have preferences containing ties, and an approximation ratio \(\varepsilon\), outputs in \(f(\varepsilon,\tau)\cdot|I|^{O(1)}\) time a stable matching with size at least \(\varepsilon\cdot\text{OPT}(I)\), where OPT(_I_) denotes the size of a largest stable matching in _I_ and _f_ is a computable function depending only on \(\varepsilon\) and \(\tau\).

## 1.2 Related Work

Min-BP-SMI and Min-BP-SRI are particularly difficult to approximate. Biró et al. \[3\] and Hamada et al. \[14\] show that Min-BP-SMI is NP-hard to approximate to factor \(n^{1-\varepsilon}\) for any \(\varepsilon>0\), where _n_ is the number of agents. The result holds even when the desired matching size is \(k=\frac{n}{2}\) , i.e., every agent has to be matched. Hamada et al. \[15\] extend the result to the Hospital-Residents problem. Biró et al. \[3\] show that Min- BP-SMI remains NP-hard to approximate to some constant factor \(\delta>1\), even when the preference lists are of length at most three. On the positive side, when one side of the preference lists have length at most two, Min-BP-SMI becomes polynomial-time solvable. For the roommates setting, Biró et al. \[4\] show that Min-BP-SRI is NP-hard to approximate to factor \(n^\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\) for any \(\varepsilon>0\). They also consider the case when the preference lists have length at most _d_. They obtain a dichotomy result: The problem remains NP-hard to approximate to some constant factor even when _d_=3, but can be approximated within factor 2_d_-3 in general. If \(d\le 2\), then the problem becomes polynomial-time solvable.

The parameterized complexity of the canonical decision variants of Min-BP-SMI and Min-BP-SRI has attracted some study; in what follows we use Min-BP-SMI and Min-BP-SRI to refer to the decision variants for the sake of brevity. Gupta et al. \[12\] show that Min-BP-SMI remains W\[1\]-hard for the combined parameter \(\beta+t+d\), where \(\beta\) is the number of blocking pairs, _t_ the increase in size to a stable matching of the instance, and _d_ the maximum length of a preference list.
