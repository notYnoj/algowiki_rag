Establishing optimality for general _k_ is more difficult because the conditions governing how samples must be allocated among items have a more complicated structure (Zhang et al. 2023, Qin and You 2026). Russo (2020) conjectures that his top-_k_ extension of top two Thompson sampling achieves a posterior error decay rate of at least half the optimum.

Given the rich literature on this problem, our motivation for this work stems from a related project. In Goldberger and Rudi (2026), we study the top-_k_ identification problem in a _different_ setting with three key components that existing methods do not address in combination. First, the decision maker can choose among different binary sample forms (in particular, a binary rating of an item or a comparison of two items), each with a different cost and signal quality. Methods that allocate directly from the current state have no clear way to handle this, whereas methods that solve a static problem may, because the costs and signal quality of each sample form can be incorporated into the objective. Second, that paper considers "small" budget settings, so analysis in the asymptotic regime is not of primary importance. Third, samples may be collected in batches. Among the methods above, OCBA-style and elimination-based algorithms support batching (Jun et al. 2016). However, OCBA algorithms generally rely on asymptotic analysis, and elimination-based algorithms are often outperformed in simulation by other methods such as gap-e and KL-LUCB (Bubeck et al. 2013, Kaufmann and Kalyanakrishnan 2013, Zhang et al. 2023). This motivated us to develop a new method for that specific problem. In this paper, building on insights from that work, we develop a simpler, far more general method for Bayesian top-_k_ identification.

**Our approach.** We take the approach of solving a sequence of tractable static optimization problems, which we develop in two steps. First, we give an upper bound on EOC, structurally different from those in the OCBA literature on EOC, and discuss why this structure has the potential for an improved algorithm. Second, to address the intractability of minimizing this bound as a one-round static problem, we approximate the future posterior distribution after a batch via expected moment matching rather than relying on asymptotic analysis. This idea originates in the EVI and OCBA literature with Gaussian samples by Chen et al. (1997), He et al. (2007), and Zhang et al. (2023), and we generalize it to other distributions. These two steps yield a simple, interpretable per-round static surrogate objective. A key property of the surrogate is that it is separable across items, enabling efficient algorithms for solving the batch problem without relying on heuristics.

**Results and theoretical guarantees.** Similar to the top two and modified expected-improvement methods for _k_=1, our method is conceptually simple and intuitive, yet remarkably comes with strong theoretical performance guarantees for general _k_.
