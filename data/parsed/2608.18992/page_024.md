_U_<sub>∗</sub> knows, in round _r_, all earlier wake up steps of other nodes in _U_, in which they did not receive any message from \(\partial U\), which is justified due to Lemma 13. Due to the assumption that \((U,\partial U)\) is an edge cut, it is clear that a node \(u\in U_*\) cannot gain any information about _R_<sub>_\<i_<sup>∗</sup></sub> by receiving a message from other nodes in _U_ (or _V<sub>A</sub>_), as that knowledge is already part of the given conditioning. Thus, we can interpret each subsequent wake-up of _u_ in some round _k_ as a single guess of the form "_R_<sub>_i_<sup>∗</sup></sub>=_k_?". The work of Massey [40] shows that guessing the value of a random variable _X_ in this manner requires at least 2<sup>**H**[_X_]-2</sup>+1 guesses in expectation. Let _T_ be the total number of times that some node in _U_<sub>∗</sub> wakes up until one of them succeeds in guessing the correct round _R_<sub>_i_<sup>∗</sup></sub>. Applying the aforementioned result of [40], we get

\(\mathbf{E}\big[T\big|R_{<i^*}=r,\hat{\Pi}_{<i^*}=\pi,S_{V_A}=s,\mathcal{E}\big]=\Omega\big(2^{\mathbf{H}\big[R_{i^*}|R_{<i^*}=r,\hat{\Pi}_{<i^*}=\pi,S_{V_A}=s,\mathcal{E}\big]}\big).\) (25)

Let **1**<sub>E</sub> be the indicator random variable for event E, which is 1 if \(M\le\bar{M}\). To shorten the notation, we define random variable \(\mathsf{Y}=(R_{<i^*},\hat{\Pi}_{<i^*},S_{V_A})\). We have

\(\mathbf{E}[\alpha_U]\ge\mathbf{E}[T]=\mathbf{E}_{y\sim\mathsf{Y},b\sim\mathbf{1}_\mathcal{E}}\big[\mathbf{E}[T|\mathsf{Y}=y,\mathbf{1}_\mathcal{E}=b]\big]\)<br> \(\text{(since}\mathbf{Pr}[\mathcal{E}]\ge 1-\frac{1}{2c})\ge\big(1-\frac{1}{2c}\big)\mathbf{E}\left[\mathbf{E}[T|\mathsf{Y}=y,\mathcal{E}]\big|\mathcal{E}\right]\)<br> \(\text{(by (25))}\ge\big(1-\frac{1}{2c}\big)\mathbf{E}\left[2^{(\mathbf{H}[R_{i^*}|\mathsf{Y}=y,\mathcal{E}]-2)}\big|\mathcal{E}\right]\)<br> \(\text{(by Jensen’s inequality)}\ge\big(1-\frac{1}{2c}\big)2^{(\mathbf{E}[\mathbf{H}[R_{i^*}|\mathsf{Y}=y,\mathcal{E}]|\mathcal{E}]-2)}\)<br> \(\text{(by (2))}\ge\big(1-\frac{1}{2c}\big)2^{(\mathbf{H}[R_{i^*}|\mathsf{Y},\mathcal{E}]-2)}\)<br> \(\text{(by (24))}\ge\big(1-\frac{1}{2c}\big)2^{\big(\frac{C}{4c}-c_2\big)logn-2-o(1)}\)<br> \(=\Omega\big(2^{\big(\frac{C}{4c}-c_2\big)logn-3}\big)\)<br> \(\text{(by (16))}=\Omega\big(n^{c_1+2}\big).\) (26)

However, according to (14), we have \(L\le n^{c_1}\), and thus we have arrived at a contradiction to the assumed upper bound on \(\mathbf{E}[\alpha_U]\), which completes the proof of Property (i). Similarly to the above, we can conclude that Property (ii) also holds, due to the event E occurring with probability 1 for algorithms that have a (per node) worst case upper bound on the number of awake rounds. This completes the proof of Lemma 1.
