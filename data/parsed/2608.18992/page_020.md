## A Proof of Lemma 1 (Cut-based Energy Lower Bound)

Consider an algorithm A that satisfies the premise of the lemma. In our analysis, we assume that \(|\text{supp}(Z)|\le 2^{c\cdot L}\) and mainly focus on proving (i). Wherever necessary, we explain how to make the argument work for (ii) (i.e., without the upper bound on the support size).

\(\blacktriangleright\) **Definition 12.** _The_ extended transcript \(\hat{\Pi}\) _consists of the (non-empty) messages that the nodes in U receive from_ \(\partial U\)_, whereby we augment each message with the corresponding round number in which it was sent. Formally, let random variable M be the total number of messages the nodes in U receive over all rounds from their neighbors in_ \(\partial U\)_. We define_

\(\hat{\Pi}=((R_1,\hat{\Pi}_1),\dots,(R_M,\hat{\Pi}_M)).\)

_Random variables_ \(R_1\le\cdots\le R_M\) _are specific round numbers; for convenience, we define R<sub>0</sub>=0.

_Each_ \(\hat{\Pi}_i\) _corresponds to a non-empty message m that a node_ \(u\in U\) _receives from a node in_ \(\partial U\) _during round R<sub>i</sub>. Note that_ \(\hat{\Pi}_i\) _includes the source ID and destination ID._

See Figure 1b for an example of an extended transcript. Note that it is perfectly possible (and even likely for some algorithms) that a node in _U_ wakes up in some round without receiving any message from a neighbor in \(\partial U\). We define the _sequence of wasted awake rounds_ W=((_W_<sub>1</sub>_,u_<sub>1</sub>),(_W_<sub>2</sub>_,u_<sub>2</sub>)_,..._), where \(Wi\le W_{i+1}\), to be the sequence of round-node pairs, such that \(u_i\in U\) wakes up in round _Wi_ without receiving any (non-empty) message from \(\partial U\). We cannot simply ignore such rounds, as a node in _U_ may infer some information about _Z_ also from the absence of any message from \(\partial U\). Nevertheless, the reason why we do not need to explicitly include the wasted awake rounds in the extended transcript \(\hat{\Pi}\) is that the entire sequence can be computed from \(\hat{\Pi}\) and the initial state of the nodes in _V<sub>A</sub>_ (which includes the nodes in _U_). The next lemma formalizes this intuition. Its proof follows by induction over the extended transcript: \(\blacktriangleright\) **Lemma 13.** _The sequence of wasted awake rounds_ W _of the nodes in U is a deterministic function of the extended transcript_ \(\hat{\Pi}\) _and the initial state S<sub>V<sub>A</sub></sub> of the nodes in V<sub>A</sub>,_

**Proof.** The proof follows by induction over the extended transcript. To keep the notation simple, we assume that \(R_1<\cdots<R_M\); it is straightforward to extend the proof to the general case where \(R_1\le\cdots\le R_M\). Formally, we show the following claim: For all \(i\in[M]\), the prefix \((R_{\le i-1},\hat{\Pi}_{\le i-1})\) of \(\hat{\Pi}\) together with the initial state _S<sub>V<sub>A</sub></sub>_ determine the prefix \((W_{\le j_i},u_{\le j_i})\) of W, where _j<sub>i</sub>_ is the largest index such that rounds \(W_1\le\cdots\le W_{j_i}<R_i\). For the base case (_i_=1), _R_<sub>0</sub>=0 and \(\hat{\Pi}_0\) is empty by definition. Thus, it will be sufficient to show that all wasted awake rounds occurring prior to round _R_<sub>1</sub>, i.e., before the first message from \(\partial U\) is received by some node in _U_, are fully determined by the initial state _S<sub>V<sub>A</sub></sub>_. For every \(u\in U\), the timing of _u_’s next wake up step is a deterministic function of _S<sub>V<sub>A</sub></sub>_, as this determines all communication that _u_ may receive from its neighbors in \(G\backslash\partial U\), all of which are in _V<sub>A</sub>_. Thus the prefix \((W_{\le j_1},u_{\le j_1})\) of W is a function of _S<sub>V<sub>A</sub></sub>_ as well. For the induction step, assume that the statement holds for some \(i\in[M]\), i.e., the prefix \((R_{\le i-1},\hat{\Pi}_{\le i-1})\) and _S<sub>V<sub>A</sub></sub>_ already determine all wasted awake steps \((W_{\le j_i},u_{\le j_i})\) prior to round _R<sub>i</sub>_. Our goal is to show that the additional knowledge of the pair \((R_i,\hat{\Pi}_i)\) suffices to compute all wasted awake steps prior to _R_<sub>_i_+1</sub>.
