## 1.4 Universe Reduction for Min-Plus Convolution

Having stated our two main technical innovations, let us finally turn to our results involving Min- Plus Convolution. We start with our universe reduction. Here, by _universe_ \[_u_\] we refer to the range of the entries in the sequences _A_ and _B_. Recall that the Min-Plus Convolution Hypothesis postulates that the min-plus convolution _A∗B_ of two given sequences _A,B_ cannot be computed in subquadratic time. In this hypothesis, as is common in fine-grained complexity, the assumption is that the universe is polynomially bounded, _u=n_<sup>_O_(1)</sup>. A stronger hypothesis, the _Strong_ Min-Plus Convolution Hypothesis, postulates that subquadratic time is impossible even for sequences over the universe \[_n_\]:

**Hypothesis 1.5** (Strong Min-Plus Convolution)**.** _Min-Plus Convolution over the universe_ \[_n_\] _cannot be solved in time_ \(O(n^{2-\epsilon})\) _for any constant_ \(\epsilon>0\).

For even smaller universes, however, there are faster algebraic algorithms. Specifically, the Min- Plus Convolution problem can be reduced to computing the sumset of two integer sets in {0_,...,nu_}, leading to an alternative \(\widetilde{O}(nu)\)-time algorithm using the FFT. This algorithm differs substantially from the naive brute-force algorithm, suggesting that in this small-universe regime (\(u\checkmark n\)) the Min- Plus Convolution problem might behave quite differently than in the large-universe regime (\(u\gg n\)). Thus, a natural question is whether this \(\widetilde{O}(nu)\) could be improved, perhaps by some more elaborate algebraic technique? Investigating this question is also highly relevant for the Min-Plus Convolution problem as a source of hardness, as many lower bounds under the Min-Plus Convolution Hypothesis could be strengthened if we could assume hardness for small universes. We successfully resolve this question, proving that time \(\widetilde{O}(nu)\) is best-possible across all ranges of \(u\le n\):

**Theorem 1.6** (Small-Universe Reduction)**.** _Let_ \(\gamma\in[0,1]\)_. Min-Plus Convolution for length-n input sequences over a universe of size_ \(u=n^\gamma\) _cannot be solved in time_ \(O(n^{1+\gamma-\epsilon})\) _(for any constant_ \(\epsilon>0\)_), unless the Strong Min-Plus Convolution Hypothesis fails._

Even more ambitiously, can one also show a reduction from the large-universe regime? Is there a universe reduction from, say, _u=n_<sup>10</sup> to _u=n_? We _almost_ answer this question in the affirmative. Unfortunately, in analogy with \[Fis26\], there remains one key step in our proof for which the state-of-the-art additive combinatorics tools are not strong enough yet. For this reason, Fischer encapsulated the missing structural statement as the following plausible hypothesis.
