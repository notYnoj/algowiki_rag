### Complexity Analysis.

The complexity analysis of the algorithm boils down to computing the expected sizes of the list generated by the algorithm. A crucial observation is that the algorithm only depends on the random variable **a**<sup>(_pq_)</sup>. Now, if **a**<sup>(_pq_)</sup> is uniformly distributed over \(\mathbb{Z}_{pq}^n\) then \(\mathbf{a}^{(pq)}\cdot\mathbf{u}\) modulo _pq_ is uniformly distributed over Z_<sub>pq</sub>_, given \(\mathbf{u}\in\{0,1\}^n\backslash\{0^n\}\). Using similar arguments, we can say that in both the modular and the integer case, subset sums mod _p_ and _q_ are uniformly distributed over Z_<sub>p</sub>_ and Z_<sub>q</sub>_ respectively. Therefore, the probability of any subset surviving the first level is 1_/p_<sup>2</sup>=1/2<sup>_n/_2</sup> (1_/p_ for each constraint), and it reaching the second level is 1_/p_<sup>2</sup>_q_=1/2<sup>3_n/_4</sup>. And, since there are 2_<sup>n</sup>_ subsets in total, the expected size of the final list is 2<sup>_n/_4</sup>. Using similar arguments, we conclude that the expected sizes of the lists _L_<sub>12</sub> and _L_<sub>34</sub> are also 2<sup>_n/_4</sup>.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first fine-grained average-case analysis for Equal Subset Sum (with density 1) that combines two-round Wagner-style pruning with an explicit second- moment argument. We further conjecture that the same viewpoint may eventually extend to the worst-case setting, once combined with the structural ideas developed by Jin and Wu [JW24]. We also conjecture that by using the randomness available in the filtering algorithm, i.e., choosing the bins modulo _p_ and _q_ uniformly randomly rather fixed values, we can improve the success probability to 1-_o_(1).

### 2.4 Related Work

Ajtai [Ajt96] showed that an \(\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{n})\)-approximation to Minkowski’s vector problem would yield an O(_n_)-approximation to the Shortest Vector problem. Hoberg et al. [HRRY17] showed connection between Number Balancing Problem and Minkowski. In the setting of Pigeonhole Equal Subset Sum, their result implies that a polynomial-time algorithm when the sum of the integers is \(2^{n-\sqrt{n}}\) would yield a polynomial approximation to the Shortest Vector Problem in polynomial time.

There has been a significant interest in analyzing the Subset Sum in restricted regimes or under average-case assumptions. The classical result by Lagarias and Odlyzko [LO85b] shows that Subset Sum can be solved in polynomial time on average when the input lies in the low-density regime. This line of work has had cryptographic implications, particularly in attacking knapsack- based cryptosystems, and has led to further developments [CJL<sup>+</sup>92, HGJ10, BCJ11, JW26]. More recently, Agrawalet al. [ASS<sup>+</sup>24] presented a new algorithm for average-case _k_-XOR problems at low densities.

With regard to reductions from variants of the Subset Sum problem to lattice problems, van Emde Boas [vEB81] gave a reduction from Equal Sum to the Closest Vector Problem (CVP). A standard reduction from ordinary Subset Sum to CVP is explicitly presented by Micciancio [Mic02]. Krishnamoorthy, Webb, and Moyer [KWM12] reduced a decisional version of a variant of the Number Partitioning Problem to CVP. Yu and Xiao [YX18] reduced multi-dimensional modular subset sum to the Bounded Distance Decoding problem. Dutta and Rajasree [DR23] showed that Unbounded Subset Sum reduces to CVP under the \(\ell_1\) and \(\ell_\infty\) norms. More recently, Hittmeir [Hit25] gave a reduction from a constrained variant of Subset Sum to the gap variant of Shortest Vector Problem.
