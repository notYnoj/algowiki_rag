In particular, the study of which substructures can be guaranteed in all graphs of sufficiently high chromatic number has a long history in graph theory, going back at least to Hadwiger’s famous conjecture [Had43] from 1943 and the seminal constructions of Tutte and Zykov [Zyk52, Des54] from the mid- 20th century, demonstrating the existence of triangle-free graphs with arbitrarily large chromatic number. Later, in one of the first instances of the probabilistic method in graph theory, Erdős [Erd59] famously proved the existence of graphs that simultaneously have arbitrarily large chromatic number and girth<sup>1</sup>. Since then, the topic of unavoidable substructures in high-chromatic graphs has flourished into an active and diverse branch of graph theory; we refer the interested reader to [SS20, Sco23] for two recent surveys on the topic.

Amongst the most famous open problems in this direction is a conjecture due to Erdős and Hajnal [Erd69] going back to at least 1969, stating that high-chromatic graphs of large girth not only exist (as shown by Erdős), but are in fact _ubiquitous_ in the sense that they can be found within _every_ graph of sufficiently large chromatic number.

**Conjecture 1.1.** _For every integer_ \(g\ge 4\) _there exists a (smallest) function_ \(f_g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\) _such that every graph G of chromatic number at least f<sub>g</sub>(k_) _contains a subgraph with chromatic number at least k and girth at least g._

Conjecture 1.1, despite its innocent look, has seen little progress over the years. The only solved special case is when _g_=4, a famous result due to Rödl [Röd77] from 1977. Rödl’s proof for the existence of _f_<sub>4</sub>(_k_), while extremely elegant and memorable, produces an enormous upper bound on _f_<sub>4</sub>(_k_), namely a power tower of _k_-s of height \(\Theta(k^2logk)\) (cf. [PTW26]). The conjecture remains open for \(g\ge 5\), but by analyzing the so-called _Burling graphs_, Pettie, Tardos and Walczak [PTW26] recently achieved the remarkable result that _f_<sub>5</sub>(_k_), if it exists, must grow at least as fast as a power tower of height linear in _k_. The weaker problem, whether one can always pass to a subgraph of large chromatic number and large _odd-girth_<sup>2</sup>, has also remained open and was explicitly highlighted as a conjecture by Mohar and Wu in 2018 [MW22].

**Conjecture 1.2** (cf. Conjecture 7 in [MW22])**.** _For every odd integer_ \(g\ge 5\) _there exists a (smallest) function_ \(h_g:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}\) _such that every graph of chromatic number at least h<sub>g</sub>(k_) _contains a subgraph of chromatic number at least k and odd-girth at least g._

A variant of this problem was in fact already posed much earlier by Erdős and Hajna [Eo76] in 1975, who asked whether for every infinite cardinal m and every odd integer \(g\ge 5\) every infinite graph of chromatic number at least m contains a subgraph with chromatic number at least m and odd-girth at least _g_.
