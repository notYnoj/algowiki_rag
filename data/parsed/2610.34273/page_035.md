**Definition 5.2** (formal expressions and evaluation)**.** Let F_<sub>n</sub>_ be the set of finite formal expressions generated by the atoms in Definition 5.1 under \(\oplus_K\), \(\otimes_K\), and the following partial-sum constructors. For _e_=(_u,v_) with \(1\le u<v\le n\), the expression \(X\boxplus_eY\) is allowed precisely when \(X,Y\in\mathcal{F}_n\) have the same dimension on every leg outside _e_.

Extend ev recursively by evaluating \(\oplus_K\) and \(\otimes_K\) with the coordinate conventions in Definition 2.2, and by evaluating \(X\boxplus_eY\) on the already specified coordinate representatives using Definition 2.9. Thus the two sectors are disjoint and matched on legs _u,v_, and their coordinates on every other leg are shared. This recursion assigns a dimension vector and a particular coordinate representative to every expression; no new choice of representative is made when taking a partial sum. Every evaluation is nonzero. Repeated formal operations are left-associated and follow the displayed index order.

A permutation \(\pi\in S_n\) (Definition 2.11) acts on expressions by permuting the entries of every atom, distributing over the two full operations, and mapping \(X\boxplus_eY\) to \((\pi X)\boxplus_{\pi e}(\pi Y)\). Here \(\pi e\) is the pair of images of the endpoints of _e_, written in increasing order. Distinct expressions remain distinct even when their evaluations are equal or locally isomorphic.

**Definition 5.3** (edge, diagonal, and complete-graph expressions)**.** For positive integers _q,R_ and integers \(1\le i<j\le n\), define the formal edge expression<tbc>

\(\mathbf{E}_{i,j}^{(q)}=\mathsf{C}_\mathbf{x},x_s=\left\{\begin{array}{ c c }
q, & s\in\{i,j\}, \\
1, & s\notin\{i,j\},
\end{array}\right.(1\le s\le n).\)

<tbc>For the complete-graph product, order the pairs (_i,j_) lexicographically. Define the formal diagonal and complete-graph expressions by

\(\mathbf{I}_n(R)=\mathsf{C}_{(1,\dots,1)}^{\oplus_KR},\mathbf{G}_{n,q}=\otimes_{1\le i<j\le n}\mathbf{E}_{i,j}^{(q)}.\)

Their evaluations are \(E_{i,j}^{(q)}\), I_<sub>n</sub>(R_), and GT_<sub>q</sub>(K<sub>n</sub>_), respectively. For the edge expression, the two nontrivial indices sum to _q_-1, and reversing either one gives the displayed representative of \(E_{i,j}^{(q)}\). This distinction between an expression and its evaluation will let us bound the permitted derivations without imposing a claim on every tensor degeneration.
