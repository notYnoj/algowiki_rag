By construction, \(S_\phi,S_q\) are exactly the residuals of (1a),(1c) at \((\phi_\mathrm{ex},q_\mathrm{ex})\). Adding \(S_\phi,S_q\) to the right-hand sides of (1a),(1c) makes \((\phi_\mathrm{ex},q_\mathrm{ex})\) an exact solution of the resulting, forced system. At the discrete level, \(S_\phi,S_q\) are evaluated at _t_<sub>_n_+1</sub> (using the same discrete spatial operators as the scheme itself) and added to the right-hand sides of (S1),(S3) only; (S4) is left untouched, since its correctness follows once \(\phi^{n+1}\) already matches \(\phi_\mathrm{ex}\). The resulting forced scheme has a known exact solution at every \(\Delta t\), so the error \(\|\phi^n-\phi_\mathrm{ex}(t_n)\|_\infty\) can be measured directly. Because \((\phi_\mathrm{ex},q_\mathrm{ex})\) does not solve the unforced system, this forced run is not expected to, and does not, satisfy the energy identity (12). The check is purely about the truncation-error order, not about stability.
