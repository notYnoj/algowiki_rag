## D Detailed component descriptions

This appendix expands the 27 component choices of  1 into a fuller description of what each component stores, how it initializes and repairs candidates, and how it moves or ranks them. Throughout, "repair" denotes the map that turns a proposed object back into valid geometry, and only the exact verifier of Section 3 determines a reported score.

### D.1 MISR components

_Representations._ `triangle\_free\_rect` stores a list of integer rectangle coordinates under the constraint that the intersection graph stay triangle-free, that is, no three rectangles share a common point. Rectangles are added one at a time from a pool that mixes rectangles near existing ones, thin horizontal and vertical bars, and unrestricted random rectangles; a candidate is admissible only if its neighbors in the current intersection graph are pairwise non-intersecting, which is exactly what prevents a triangle. Repair deletes a member of any triangle that appears and refills to the minimum size. Because sparse conflict graphs can support large fractional-versus-integer gaps this is a strong but natu<tbc>

<tbc>ral restriction, but it cannot reach a construction whose intersection graph must contain a triangle. `quadratic\_ program\_rectangles` does not store rectangle coordinates directly; it stores four short bounded integer coefficient vectors, expands each rectangle index into a small quadratic feature vector of its binary digits, and decodes four dot products with those coefficients into a rectangle (through one of three decoders: sorted endpoints, center-and-size, or alternating thin segments). Changing a single coefficient therefore moves many rectangles at once, giving cheap, globally correlated moves. Decoded rectangles are cleaned and repaired to a triangle- free family, so many different coefficient programs collapse to the same geometry; the representation favors concise algebraic regularity but can be dominated by its repair step. `fixed\_symmetry\_rectangles` stores direct integer rectangles at a fixed object count, with no triangle-free constraint. Each family is translated to the origin and reduced to the lexicographically smallest of its eight images under the planar reflections and quarter-turns, so mirror and rotated copies collapse to a single training string. Repair clamps coordinates, restores the target cardinality, and re-canonicalizes; because the cardinality is fixed, an apparent add or delete may be undone or completed by repair.

_Local-search operations._ `sequence\_pair\_pivot` keeps a historical name (no final representation stores endpoint sequences). On the triangle-free representation it samples add, replace, nudge, resize, and delete moves with add and replace unweighted, drawing new rectangles from a large triangle-free pool; on the quadratic-program representation it uses the generic coefficient, decoder, and composition neighborhood; and on the fixed-symmetry representation it becomes direct coordinate jitter, resize, duplicate, and delete followed by fixed-cardinality repair. `program\_coeff\_pivot` is most meaningful with the quadratic-program representation, where it changes one coefficient by a small step, refreshes a short coefficient block, switches among the three decoders, or changes the active object count. Because a single coefficient enters the quadratic feature of many rectangle indices at once, such a move is global and correlated: it can reshape several rectangles together, unlike nudging one endpoint of one rectangle.
