<tbc>token patterns shared by high-scoring constructions. We deliberately keep it small and train it from scratch at every training event: two layers, four attention heads, embedding width 96, feed-forward width 384, GELU activation, no dropout, a causal attention mask, context length 128, and generated sequences of up to 512 tokens. It is optimized with AdamW at learning rate \(3\times 10^{-4}\), batch size 32, and three epochs per training call. The vocabulary is rebuilt from the current elite archive with explicit `\<BOS\>`, `\<EOS\>`, and `\<UNK\>` symbols, so there is no fixed global vocabulary size. Character- level tokenization keeps the vocabulary small at the cost of allowing malformed samples, which are discarded or repaired; when a training event yields too few parseable samples, an order-8 character _n_-gram model fitted to the same archive supplements the transformer output.

_The surrogate._ Each configuration carries a surrogate: a problem-specific function that ranks candidates within the search and selects the elite that will be exactly audited. Surrogates range from cheap structural statistics that never solve the objective, through approximations of it, to functions that invoke the exact scorer itself with additional shaping terms. A surrogate is therefore not necessarily an approximation: what separates it from the exact oracle is its role, since the oracle sits outside the search and alone determines reported scores.

_Local search and verification._ A local move makes a small, validity-preserving change to a construction, in the simplest case perturbing one coordinate by \(\pm 1\). The operations we use range from these simple coordinate perturbations to structure-aware moves and small, exactly scored hill climbs; the concrete operators considered for each problem are described in Section 5. Improved candidates are accepted greedily.

**Challenges in adapting to combinatorial geometry.** Adapting PatternBoost to combinatorial geometry is not automatic, because the geometric setting departs from the graph and sequence problems the framework was designed for in three ways. First, a construction is a set of geometric objects with large coordinate ranges and many symmetries - translation, reflection, and axis exchange - so a naive coordinate encoding represents each combinatorial structure in a great many equivalent ways and forces the transformer to spend its capacity relearning them. Second, the objective is not a closed-form count but the outcome of an exact optimization - an LP, an ILP and their ratio, or a dynamic program - which is expensive to evaluate and, being a ratio, changes only in coarse jumps, so most local moves leave it unchanged. Third, randomly generated or model-sampled coordinates rarely satisfy the validity constraints of a non-trivial instance, such<tbc>

<tbc>as disjointness, triangle-freeness, or congruence, so proposals must be repaired, and repair can silently undo a coordinated change. These difficulties are why the representation, the local-search operation, and the surrogate must be co-designed for each problem, which we take up in Section 5.

**Exact verification and certificates.** Trust rests on verification that is independent of the search. Before deduplication and scoring, candidates are canonicalized: coordinates are translated to a canonical origin, integer grids are scaled by their gcd when possible, objects are sorted by normalized coordinates or a canonical graph label, and ties are broken deterministically. Surrogates are allowed to be biased and problem-specific, but every reported value is recomputed by the exact oracle of Section 3 and saved as a standalone certificate. These oracles use only open-source solvers, with no commercial optimization license; the per-problem solvers and numerical settings are given in Section 5.2. To keep results non-trivial, accepted constructions required at least eight geometric objects, unit-square certificates required integer stabbing number at least four, and guillotine certificates required at least two destroyed rectangles. We disclose the strongest prior: one of the three guillotine representations builds its instances around a fixed seven-rectangle spiral obstruction topology, so the nine configurations using it search an obstruction family rather than neutral coordinates. It inserts neither a score nor a certificate, and our best guillotine construction does not come from it: the destroyed fraction of 1/3 is reached by the neutral `rect\_direct\_disjoint` representation, while the spiral-seeded configurations reach at most 0.27. A strict post-processing audit re-verified every construction, checking clean completion of every run, agreement among all recorded scores, certificate- hash consistency, and independent exact re-verification of every certificate. All runs terminated cleanly, and all 81 constructions passing the audit.
