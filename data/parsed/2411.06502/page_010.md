<tbc>The case where the path in \(\mathrm{sim}(L,L')\) crosses the right border of the left subrectangle, and the the path in \(\mathrm{sim}(R,R')\) crosses the upper border of the left subrectangle can be handled symmetrically. This allows us now to recurse on the left subrectangle as well.

It remains to discuss how to patch together the outputs of the two subrectangles to get the outputs of the full rectangle. For the sake of brevity, we omit discussing this here, but it is not difficult to see that by employing similar calculations to those before (border-to-border paths in an alignment graph combined with the outputs of the two subrectangles via max-plus products), we can calculate \(\mathrm{sim}((x,x'),(y,y'))\) for all \((x,x'),(y,y')\) on the lower-left border of the rectangle, ignoring the contribution arising from _c<sub>a</sub>_ or \(c'_{a^\prime}\) being mapped to other central nodes. With some additional computations (which requires redefining \(\mathrm{sim}((x,x'),(y,y'))\) to compute 2<sup>4</sup> values instead of one, depending on whether central nodes \(c_a,c_b,c'_{a^\prime},c'_{b^\prime}\) were already mapped or not), we can factor in these contributions and compute the desired output for the entire rectangle.

This shows how the divide-et-impera scheme can handle vertical cuts. By symmetry of the problem, the scheme can handle horizontal cuts as well, allowing us to split a single instance in four roughly equal parts to recurse on.

For a rough analysis, let us focus on the case when \(n=n'=2^k\) for which the scheme always recurses on squares. This results in the recurrence relation \(\mathsf{T}(n)=4\cdot\mathsf{T}(n/2)+\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{T}_\mathsf{MUL}(n))\), which implies \(\mathsf{T}(n)=\mathcal{O}(\mathsf{T}_\mathsf{MUL}(n))\).<sup>5</sup>

**Getting rid of Assumption A.** Up to this point, we argued how to determine the similarity between two caterpillar trees, **T** and \(\mathbf{T}'\), under Assumption A. In the general case, the mapping of \(\mathrm{sim}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{T}')\) (when observed from the root downwards) preserves this assumption up to some point, i.e., there exist \(a,b,a',b'\) such that nodes in _l_<sub>1</sub>_,...,l_<sub>_a_-1</sub> are only mapped to nodes in \(l'_1,\dots,l'_{a^\prime-1}\), nodes in _r_<sub>1</sub>_,...,r_<sub>_b_-1</sub> are only mapped to nodes in \(r'_1,\dots,r'_{b^\prime-1}\), nodes in _c_<sub>1</sub>_,...,c_<sub>min(_a,b_)-1</sub> are only mapped to nodes in \(c'_1,\dots,c'_{min(a^\prime,b^\prime)-1}\). Then, one of two cases can occur:

**(a)** _l<sub>a</sub>_ is mapped to \(c'_a\), nodes in _l_<sub>_a_+1</sub>_,...,l_<sub>_b_-1</sub> are exclusively mapped to nodes \(r'_{a^\prime-1},\dots,r'_{b^\prime+1}\), and _c<sub>b</sub>_ is mapped to \(r'_{b^\prime}\). The first and third conditions are optional; if they do not hold, we include _l<sub>a</sub>_, \(r'_{a^\prime}\), and _l<sub>b</sub>_, \(r'_{b^\prime}\), respectively, in the middle condition.

**(b)** _r<sub>a</sub>_ is mapped to \(c'_a\), nodes in _r_<sub>_a_-1</sub>_,...,r_<sub>_b_+1</sub> are mapped to nodes \(l'_{a^\prime+1},\dots,l'_{b^\prime-1}\), and _c<sub>b</sub>_ is mapped to \(l'_{b^\prime}\). As before, the first and third conditions are optional; if they do not hold, we include _r<sub>a</sub>_, _r<sub>b</sub>_ and \(l'_a\), \(l'_{b^\prime}\), respectively, in the middle condition.

Without loss of generality, we focus on the former case, as the two cases are symmetric.

This scenario is depicted in Figure 3 and can be visualized by overlaying an additional string similarity graph onto the existing ones in the grid (specifically, the one involved in \(\mathrm{sim}(L,\mathsf{rev}(R'))\), where \(\mathsf{rev}(R')\) denotes the reversed string \(R'\)). When overlaying this similarity graph, we ensure that the indices of \(\mathsf{rev}(R')\) align with the indexing of the grid, and the paths in this string alignment graph travel from the upper-left border to the lower-right border. The two paths that were previously observed now extend from (1,1) to \((a,a')\) and \((b,b')\), where _l<sub>a</sub>_ is mapped to \(c'_{a^\prime}\) and _c<sub>b</sub>_ is mapped to \(l'_b\). Within this new alignment graph, we search for a path connecting \((a+1,a')\) with \((b,b'+1)\). If _l<sub>a</sub>_ is not mapped to \(c'_{a^\prime}\) or _c<sub>b</sub>_ is not mapped to \(l'_{b^\prime}\), then this path starts/ends at \((a,a')\) and \((b,b')\), respectively.

The details necessary for considering this final path can be incorporated into the earlier divide-et- impera framework, although it necessitates additional inputs and outputs for the scheme. For the sake of brevity, we omit details here.
