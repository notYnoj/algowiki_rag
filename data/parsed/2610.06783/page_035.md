## 3.3 Exact Triangle in truly subcubic time

**Theorem 19** (Exact Triangle)**.** _Let_ \(\varepsilon':=0.00175\) _and_ \(\varepsilon_T:=0.0017\)_. For every constant_ \(\nu\ge 1\)_, Exact Triangle on n vertices per part with integer weights of absolute value at most_ \(n^\nu\) _can be solved by a deterministic algorithm in O(n_<sup>3-1/648</sup>log<sup>2</sup>_n_) _time using Theorem 5 (via Corollary 15), and in_ \(O(n^{3-\varepsilon^\prime} logn)\le O(n^{3-\varepsilon_T})\) _time using Corollary 26 (via Corollary 16)._

_Proof._ Assume _n_ is larger than a constant depending on \(\nu\) (smaller instances are solved by brute force). In both cases we apply Theorem 17, whose instances have at most \(n^2/\sqrt{D}\) query pairs each, and solve every instance by a corollary of Section 3.1. The values of _g_ below balance the cost of the instances against that of the scans (Remark 20).

_By Theorem 5._ Let _D_ be the largest power of four with \(D\le n^{1/18}\), so that \(D\ge n^{1/18}/4\), and let \(g:=\lceil D^{1/36}\rceil\). Solve every instance by Corollary 15, which applies because \(D^{18}\le n\). The _O(nD_<sup>1/36</sup>) instances cost _O(n_<sup>2</sup>log<sup>2</sup>_D/D_<sup>1/18</sup>) each, so _O(n_<sup>3</sup>_D_<sup>-1/36</sup>log<sup>2</sup>_n_) in all, the scans cost _O(n_<sup>3</sup>_D_<sup>-1/36</sup>log_n_), the choice of _p_ costs _O(n_<sup>log<sub>2</sub></sup><sup>7</sup>_D_<sup>3/2</sup>)=_O(n_<sup>2.9</sup>) with Strassen’s algorithm, and building the instances costs _O(n_<sup>2</sup>_D_<sup>1.03</sup>). Finally \(D^{-1/36}\le 4^{1/36}n^{-1/648}\), so the time is _O(n_<sup>3-1/648</sup>log<sup>2</sup>_n_).

_By Corollary 26._ Let \(D:=\lfloor n^{1/18}\rfloor\) and \(g:=\lceil D^{0.0315}\rceil\), and solve every instance by Corollary 16, which applies because \(D^{18}\le n\). The instances cost \(O(nD^{0.0315})\cdot O(n^2/D^{0.063})=O(n^3D^{-0.0315})\), the scans _O(n_<sup>3</sup>_D_<sup>-0.0315</sup>log_n_), the choice of _p_ costs _O(n_<sup>log<sub>2</sub></sup><sup>7</sup>_D_<sup>3/2</sup>)=_O(n_<sup>2.9</sup>) with Strassen’s algorithm, and building the instances costs _O(n_<sup>2</sup>_D_<sup>1.04</sup>). Finally \(D\ge n^{1/18}/2\) gives \(D^{-0.0315}\le 2n^{-0.0315/18}=2n^{-0.00175}\), so the time is \(O(n^{3-\varepsilon^\prime} logn)\le O(n^{3-\varepsilon_T})\).

_Remark_ 20. With \(g=D^\eta\) and a saving \(D^\gamma\) per instance, the instances cost \(n^3D^{\eta-\gamma}\) and the scans \(n^3D^{-\eta}\), up to logarithmic factors. They balance at \(\eta=\gamma/2\), that is, at \(\eta=1/36\) for the saving _D_<sup>1/18</sup> of Theorem 5 and at \(\eta=0.0315\) for the saving _D_<sup>0.063</sup> of Corollary 26. Any improvement of these savings improves the final exponent \(\eta/18\) directly, and the factor 1/18 in it comes from the assumption \(N\ge D^{18}\) of these theorems. Any other algorithm for Lop-AE-SparseTri(_n,D_) can be plugged into Theorem 17 in the same way. With the straightforward algorithm (_O(n_<sup>2</sup>_/g_) per instance, since every vertex of _A_ has \(O(\sqrt{D}/g)\) neighbors in the middle part) the reduction recovers the brute-force bound _n_<sup>3</sup> up to a logarithmic factor.
