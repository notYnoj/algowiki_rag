We select the prime with the smallest count, which is also the prime with the fewest false positives, and call it _p_. To bound _F(p_), note that a triple with \(S(a,b,c)\neq 0\) is a false positive exactly of the primes in the range that divide _S(a,b,c_). Since \(0<|S(a,b,c)|\le 3n^\nu\) and these primes are at least \(\sqrt{D}/2\), there are at most \(log_{\sqrt{D}/2}(3n^\nu)\) of them. Hence the numbers of false positives of all the primes in the range add up to at most \(n^3log_{\sqrt{D}/2}(3n^\nu)\). By the prime number theorem there are \(\Omega(\sqrt{D}/logD)\) primes in the range, and _F(p_) is at most the average over them, so, as in \[FKP24, Lemma 3.2\], \(F(p)=O(n^3log(3n^\nu)/\sqrt{D})=O(\nu n^3logn/\sqrt{D})\), since \(logD=O(log(\sqrt{D}/2))\) for \(D\ge 16\).

_The instances._ As in \[CX24\], let \(s:=\lfloor\sqrt{D}\rfloor\) and split _C_ into pieces _C_<sub>1</sub>_,...,C<sub>h</sub>_ of at most \(\lceil s/g\rceil\) vertices each, so that \(h\le\lceil ng/s\rceil\). For \(\varrho\in\mathbb{Z}_p\) let \(W_\varrho\) be the set of edges \((a,b)\in A\times B\) with \(w(a,b)\equiv\varrho(modp)\), and cut it into chunks of at most \(n^2/\sqrt{D}\) query pairs. There are at most \(p+\sqrt{D}\le 2\sqrt{D}\) chunks in all. For each chunk \(\mathcal{Q}\subseteq W_\varrho\) and each piece _C<sub>k</sub>_ form the instance of Lop-AE-SparseTri(_n,D_) with query pairs _W_:=Q and middle part \(C_k\times\mathbb{Z}_p\), of size at most \(sp\le D\), whose middle vertices are pairs (vertex, label) as in \[CX24\], with

\(a\sim(c,\sigma)\Longleftrightarrow\sigma\equiv w(a,c)+\varrho,(c,\sigma)\sim b\Longleftrightarrow\sigma\equiv-w(b,c)(modp).\)

Within the chunk, the condition \(S(a,b,c)\equiv 0(modp)\) has become the equality \(w(a,c)+\varrho\equiv-w(b,c)\) of a label of (_a,c_) and a label of (_b,c_), so a query pair \((a,b)\in\mathcal{Q}\) has a common neighbor if and only if some \(c\in C_k\) has \(S(a,b,c)\equiv 0(modp)\). There are at most \(2\sqrt{D}h\le 2\sqrt{D}(ng/s+1)\le 4ng\) instances, since \(s\ge\sqrt{D}-1\ge 3\sqrt{D}/4\) because \(D\ge 16\), and \(\sqrt{D}\le\sqrt{n}\le 2n/3\) because \(D\le n\) and we may assume \(n\ge 3\), and they are produced non-adaptively, since _p_ is chosen before any of them. Writing them down costs _O(n_<sup>2</sup>_Dg_): the two bipartite graphs of an instance have _O(nD_) entries, and the chunks are computed once and shared by the pieces.

_Witnesses._ For every query pair that the oracle accepts, scan the piece _C<sub>k</sub>_ of its instance for a _c_ with _S(a,b,c_)=0, as in the exhaustive search of \[CX24\]. A zero triangle is always found, since its _c_ lies in some piece and makes the oracle accept its pair. We stop as soon as a zero triangle is found. A failed scan, of a piece _C<sub>k</sub>_ for a pair (_a,b_), contains a \(c\in C_k\) with \(S(a,b,c)\equiv 0(modp)\) but \(S(a,b,c)\neq 0\), that is, a false positive (_a,b,c_) of _p_, and distinct scans contain distinct false positives, so there are at most _F(p_) failed scans, and the scans cost \(O((F(p)+1)\sqrt{D}/g)=O(\nu n^3logn/g)\).

_Remark_ 18 (Comparison with \[CX24\] and \[VX20\]). We emulate the instances and the witness search of Chan and Xu \[CX24, Section 3\]. The difference is in how the pairs are grouped so that the condition on _S(a,b,c_) becomes an equality of labels. Their labels are exact and there are no false positives. After hashing, _w(a,b_) mod _p_ takes only _p_ values, so we can group by it directly, in a single round of instances instead of a recursion.
