as described next; every represented walk has _O(H_) edges, with a constant that depends on _i_; and the entry for (_u,v_) is \(\delta(u,v)\) whenever \(\eta(u,v)\le H\). The products in the routine are computed with the deterministic (min,+)-product algorithm with witnesses of Zwick \[Zwi02, Lemma 3.3\], which finds the witnesses deterministically by the method of Alon and Naor \[AN96\]. A witness of an entry (_u,v_) of a product is an index _k_ at which its minimum is attained, and we store it with the entry. The walk that the entry represents is then the concatenation of the walks represented by the entries (_u,k_) and (_k,v_) of the two factors, and it is listed, when needed, by following the stored witnesses recursively down to the entries of the weight matrix, which represent single edges; this takes _O(H_) time, because the walk is a concatenation of _O(H_) such entries. For a product in which one dimension is _n_ and the other two are at most _q_, with entries of absolute value at most _W_, that algorithm takes \(\tilde{O}(Wnq^{\omega-1})\) time, by cutting the product into _n/q_ square products.

The routine for level 0 takes the weight matrix with zeroes on the diagonal and squares it _O_(log_H_) times, with the entries larger than 2_HM_ replaced by \(\infty\), and keeps the rows and columns of _T_. The routine for level \(i\ge 1\) first calls the routine for level _i_-1 with the target set \(T\cup B_i\) and the horizon _L_:=2_C<sub>i</sub>t<sub>i</sub>_. This call is okay, because \(|T\cup B_i|=\tilde{O}(n/t_i)\le\tilde{O}(n/t_{i-1})\) and _L=O(Rt_<sub>_i_-1</sub>). Let _F_ be the pair of tables that it returns; they have exact distances for the pairs with \(\eta\le L\), and they have the entries of \(V\times(T\cup B_i)\) and \((T\cup B_i)\times V\) . Let _Q_ be the smallest power of two above _H/t<sub>i</sub>_, and set the diagonal of _F_\[_B<sub>i</sub>,B<sub>i</sub>_\] to zero. The routine computes, by repeated squaring,

\(E_i(T,H)[V,T]:=min\big\{F[V,T],F[V,B_i]\star F[B_i,B_i]^{\star Q}\star F[B_i,T]\big\},\)

where ⋆ is the (min,+)-product and the minimum is entrywise, and it computes _E<sub>i</sub>(T,H_)\[_T,V_\] symmetrically.

The entry for a pair (_u,v_) with \(v\in T\) and \(\eta(u,v)\le H\) is exact. To see this, take a shortest path from _u_ to _v_ with the fewest edges, and cut it into blocks of _t<sub>i</sub>_ edges followed by a remainder of fewer than _t<sub>i</sub>_ edges. Each block is a shortest path with the fewest edges between its endpoints, so the bridge property of _B<sub>i</sub>_ replaces it by a shortest walk between the same endpoints that has at most _C<sub>i</sub>t<sub>i</sub>_ edges and passes through a vertex of _B<sub>i</sub>_; choose one such vertex in each block. The result is a shortest walk from _u_ to _v_ through at most _H/t<sub>i</sub>_ chosen vertices of _B<sub>i</sub>_, in which _u_, the chosen vertices, and _v_ are consecutively at most _L_=2_C<sub>i</sub>t<sub>i</sub>_ edges apart. The table _F_ is exact on these consecutive pairs, so the product above finds \(\delta(u,v)\). The represented walks have _O(H_) edges, since those of _F_ have _O(L_) edges and at most _Q_+2=_O(H/t<sub>i</sub>_) of them are concatenated. Every product of the routine has one dimension _n_, the two others \(\tilde{O}(n/t_i)\), and entries \(O(HM)=\tilde{O}(Rt_i)\), so it takes \(\tilde{O}(Rt_i\cdot n\cdot(n/t_i)^{\omega-1})\le\tilde{O}(n^{\omega+\gamma})\) time, and the recursion has \(O(1/\gamma)\) levels.

_The stages._ The algorithm maintains a matrix of distance estimates, initially the weight matrix with zeros on the diagonal, and runs one stage for each level _i_=0,1_,..._ until _t_<sub>_i_+1</sub>=_n_. The stage for level _i_ computes the tables _E<sub>i</sub>_:=_E<sub>i</sub>(B<sub>i</sub>,C<sub>i</sub>t_<sub>_i_+1</sub>), then the (min,+)-product

\(E_i[V,B_i]\star E_i[B_i,V],\)

and takes its entrywise minimum with the matrix of estimates. Then, if _t_<sub>_i_+1</sub>_\<n_, it constructs _B_<sub>_i_+1</sub> from the walks represented in _E<sub>i</sub>_, as described below. The product fixes every pair (_u,v_) with \(t_i\le\eta(u,v)\le t_{i+1}\): the bridge property of _B<sub>i</sub>_ gives \(b\in B_i\) with \(\delta(u,v)=\delta(u,b)+\delta(b,v)\) and \(\eta(u,b),\eta(b,v)\le C_i\eta(u,v)\le C_it_{i+1}\), so both _E<sub>i</sub>_\[_u,b_\] and _E<sub>i</sub>_\[_b,v_\] are exact. The stages thus find all distances, and every estimate is the weight of a walk, so none is too small.
