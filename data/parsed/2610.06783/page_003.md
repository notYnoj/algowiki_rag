• **Orthogonal Vectors (OV)**: Given _n_ Boolean vectors in \(d=\omega(logn)\) dimensions, are two of them orthogonal? The brute-force algorithm solves OV in _O(n_<sup>2</sup>_d_) time, and only subpolynomial improvements are known: the fastest algorithms \[AWY15, CW21\] run in _n_<sup>2-1_/O_(log(_d/_log_n_))</sup> time.

The apparent lack of progress on problems such as these led to new theories of hardness: NP-completeness \[Coo71, Kar72\] for problems with no known polynomial-time algorithm, and fine-grained complexity (FGC) \[Vas18, Bri19\] for problems whose best known algorithms were stuck at essentially the straightforward running time. Both theories relate problems via reductions. FGC focuses on improvements in the exponent: a fine-grained reduction from problem _A_ to problem _B_ with respect to running times _a(n_) and _b(n_) implies that an \(O(b(n)^{1-\varepsilon})\)-time algorithm for _B_ for some \(\varepsilon>0\) can be converted into an \(O(a(n)^{1-\delta})\)-time algorithm for _A_ for some \(\delta>0\).

Fine-grained complexity took three of the problems from our list above and postulated that the state-of-the-art algorithms cannot be beaten: in the word RAM model of computation with _O_(log_n_)-bit words, APSP with polynomially bounded integer weights requires _n_<sup>3-_o_(1)</sup> time (the "APSP hypothesis" \[RZ04, VW10, VW18, AV14\]), 3SUM on _n_ integers of polynomial size requires _n_<sup>2-_o_(1)</sup> time (the "3SUM hypothesis" \[GO95, Pˇat10\]), and there is no \(\varepsilon>0\) such that CNF-SAT on _n_ variables and _O(n_) clauses can be solved in \((2-\varepsilon)^n\) time (the "Strong Exponential Time Hypothesis," SETH \[IP01, IPZ01, CIP10\]).

Through fine-grained reductions, FGC built a web of relations among problems, and within it, classes of problems that are equivalent to each other (see the surveys \[Vas18, Bri19\]). For a large variety of problems for which no improved running times had been obtained in decades, FGC pointed to reasons: they are 3SUM-hard, APSP-hard, SETH-hard, or worse. In other words, to improve the known algorithms, one knew which obstacle needed to be overcome.

For instance, many problems are known to be equivalent to APSP under fine-grained reductions. Either all of the following problems are solvable in _truly subcubic_ time, meaning in \(O(n^{3-\varepsilon})\) time for some constant \(\varepsilon>0\) (_truly subquadratic_ is defined similarly), or none of them is: the (min,+)-product _A⋆B_ of two \(n\times n\) matrices _A_ and _B_ (defined by (_A⋆B_)\[_i,j_\]=min_<sub>k</sub>(A_\[_i,k_\]+_B_\[_k,j_\])) \[FM71, AHU74\]<sup>1</sup>, Negative Triangle and Second Shortest Simple Path \[VW10, VW18\], Graph Radius \[AGV15, AGV23\], Tree Edit Distance \[BGMW20, NPS<sup>+</sup>25\], and many other problems \[VW10, VW18, RV11, AGV15, AGV23\], more of which are listed below, after Figure 1. Perhaps surprisingly, it is also known \[CVX21\] that solving APSP in truly subcubic time would give a polynomially faster algorithm for directed unweighted APSP as well, and the corresponding hypotheses are in fact equivalent \[Fis26\] (albeit conditionally<sup>2</sup>), even though the longstanding running times of the two problems are different: ≈_n_<sup>3</sup> for APSP and ≈_n_<sup>2.5</sup> for directed unweighted APSP (if \(\omega=2\)).

Similar reductions and equivalences are also known for 3SUM: all nontrivial 3-linear degeneracy tests are equivalent to 3SUM \[DGS20\], and many other problems are 3SUM-hard (e.g., many problems in computational geometry \[GO95, BHP01, SEO03, AHP08\], and some string problems \[ACLL14, AVW14, KPP16\]).

CNF-SAT reduces to Orthogonal Vectors \[Wil05\], so that unless SETH is refuted, OV requires _n_<sup>2-_o_(1)</sup> time. SETH also implies fine-grained hardness for many exponential-time problems \[CDL<sup>+</sup>16\].

<sup>1</sup>The equivalence between APSP and (min,+)-product is extremely tight: the running times are the same up to constant factors \[FM71, AHU74\]. <sup>2</sup>The equivalence in \[Fis26\] requires both \(\omega=2\) and the resolution of a conjecture in combinatorics.
