_(b)_ 3_SUM._ Let _A,B,C_ be sets of _n_ reals, the problem asks whether _a+b=c_ for some \(a\in A\), \(b\in B\), and \(c\in C\). The 3SUM version with one set and _a+b+c_=0 reduces to this version in the standard way.

Now, sort _A_ and _B_, and cut them into _n/d_ consecutive blocks _A_<sub>1</sub>_,...,A<sub>n/d</sub>_ and _B_<sub>1</sub>_,...,B<sub>n/d</sub>_ of _d_ numbers each, writing _A<sub>i</sub>_\[_k_\] for the _k_-th number of _A<sub>i</sub>_. Here the counting problem \[CVX22, Problem 4.5\] is the following: we are given a set Q of _O(n_<sup>2</sup>_/d_) quadruples \((i,j,k,\ell)\), and we must count, for every \((i,j,k,\ell)\in\mathcal{Q}\), the pairs \((k',\ell')\in[d]^2\) with \(A_i[k']+B_j[\ell']<A_i[k]+B_j[\ell]\); again, a call may have \(\le\) in place of _\<_, and it may restrict \(k'\) and \(\ell'\) to subsets of \[_d_\]. By Section 3.3 and Lemmas 3.9 and 4.6 of \[CVX22\], 3SUM reduces, with Las Vegas randomization, to polylogarithmically many calls of this counting problem, and \(\tilde{O}(n^2/d)\) additional time. By Fredman’s trick, the condition is \(A_i[k']-A_i[k]<B_j[\ell]-B_j[\ell']\). So a call is one comparison count: the rows are the _n_ pairs (_i,k_) and the columns are the _n_ pairs \((j,\ell)\), the row list R<sub>(_i,k_)</sub> consists of the _d_ numbers \(A_i[k']-A_i[k]\), \(k'\in[d]\), the column list \(\mathcal{C}_{(j,\ell)}\) of the _d_ numbers \(B_j[\ell]-B_j[\ell']\), \(\ell'\in[d]\), all numbers have the same color, and _P_:=Q. A restriction of \(k'\) or of \(\ell'\) deletes numbers from the lists. Forming the lists can be done using _O(nd_) subtractions.

## Solving comparison counts.

In the comparison counts of Lemma 36(a), every list has at most one number of each color, so that \(\gamma(r,c)\) is the number of colors _k_ with _A_\[_r,k_\]_\<B_\[_k,c_\], where _A_\[_r,k_\] is the number of color _k_ in R_<sub>r</sub>_ and _B_\[_k,c_\] the one in C_<sub>c</sub>_ (a color missing from either list is skipped); this is the dominance product of the \(n\times d\) matrix _A_ and the \(d\times n\) matrix _B_ at the positions in _P_. In those of Lemma 36(b), all numbers have the same color, and \(\gamma(r,c)\) is the number of pairs _x\<y_ between the two lists. Merging the two sorted lists of every pair in _P_ takes _O_(|_P|d_) time. To improve on this, we utilize Matoušek’s technique for the dominance product \[Mat91\], which counts the pairs that lie in different blocks of the sorted order by one thin matrix product, and the pairs in the same block directly.

**Lemma 37** (after Matoušek \[Mat91\])**.** _Let_ \(s\ge 1\) _be an integer and_ \(D'':=\lceil 2nd/s\rceil+d\)_. Given row lists_ R_<sub>r</sub> and column lists_ C_<sub>c</sub> as above and a set_ \(P\subseteq[n]^2\)_, we can build matrices_ \(X\in\{0,\dots,d\}^{n\times D^{\prime\prime}}\) _and_ \(Y\in\{0,\dots,d\}^{D^{\prime\prime}\times n}\) _and compute integers_ \(\gamma_2(r,c)\), \((r,c)\in P\)_, with_

\(\gamma(r,c)=(XY)[r,c]+\gamma_2(r,c)\text{\textit{for every}}(r,c)\in P,\)

_deterministically in_ \(O(nD''+(|P|+nds+ds^2)logn)\) _time. The only operations on real numbers are the comparisons made when sorting the numbers of each color. The same holds with_ \(\le\) _in place of \< in the definition of_ \(\gamma\).

_Proof._ For each color _k_, sort the numbers of color _k_ of all the lists, and break ties so that, among equal numbers, those from column lists precede those from row lists. Let pos(_x_) be the position of _x_ in this order. For _x_ from a row list and _y_ from a column list of the same color, _x\<y_ holds if and only if pos(_x)\<_pos(_y_). (For \(\le\), we reverse the tie-breaking rule.) Cut the order into _blocks_ of _s_ consecutive positions, and let \(\text{blk}(x):=\lfloor\text{pos}(x)/s\rfloor\). Then _x\<y_ holds if and only if either blk(_x)\<_blk(_y_), or blk(_x_)=blk(_y_) and pos(_x)\<_pos(_y_). As in Matoušek’s algorithm, we count the pairs of the first kind, which lie in different blocks, by a matrix product, and the pairs of the second kind, which lie in the same block, by brute force.

_Different blocks._ The lists contain at most 2_nd_ numbers in all, so there are at most \(2nd/s+d\le D''\) pairs (color, block), and we index the columns of _X_ and the rows of _Y_ by the pairs, padding with zeros if needed. Let \(X[r,(k,\beta)]\) be the number of numbers of color _k_ in R_<sub>r</sub>_ that lie in block \(\beta\), and let \(Y[(k,\beta),c]\) be the number of numbers of color _k_ in C_<sub>c</sub>_ that lie in a block after \(\beta\).
