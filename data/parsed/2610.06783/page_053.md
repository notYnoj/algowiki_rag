_The cost of the stages._ Let _s_:=_t_<sub>_i_+1</sub>. The product of the stage for level _i_ has middle dimension \(|B_i|=\tilde{O}(n/t_i)=\tilde{O}(Rn/s)\) and entries \(\tilde{O}(s)\). Cutting _B<sub>i</sub>_ into \(\tilde{O}(R)\) groups of \(\lceil n/s\rceil\) vertices turns it into \(\tilde{O}(n^\gamma)\) products of an \(n\times\lceil n/s\rceil\) matrix by an \(\lceil n/s\rceil\times n\) matrix with entries \(\tilde{O}(s)\). Up to this grouping, these are the products of the randomized reduction, and the analysis of \[CVX21, Theorem 3.1\] applies to them. Fix a small constant _a\>_0. When \(s\le n^{1-\mu-a}\), the middle dimension \(n^r:=\lceil n/s\rceil\) has \(r\ge\mu+a\), and fast rectangular matrix multiplication computes the product in \(\tilde{O}(sn^{\omega(1,r,1)})=O(n^{2+\mu-ca})\) time \[Zwi02, Lemma 3.3\], for a constant _c\>_0, by the convexity of \(\omega(1,\cdot,1)\), since \(\omega(1,\mu,1)=1+2\mu\) and \(\omega<2+\mu\). When \(s\ge n^{1-\mu+a}\), the naive algorithm computes it in \(O(n^2\cdot n/s)=O(n^{2+\mu-a})\) time. In between, the middle dimension is at most \(n^{\mu+a}\) and the entries are \(\tilde{O}(n^{1-\mu+a})\); we cut the middle dimension into at most _n<sup>a</sup>_ pieces of \(n^\mu\) columns and pad each piece to an instance with _n_<sup>1+_O(a_)</sup> rows, so that its entries fit the range of the hypothesis, and the hypothesized algorithm computes it in \(O(n^{2+\mu-\varepsilon_1+O(a)})\) time. With _a_ small enough, the product of every stage takes \(O(n^{2+\mu+\gamma-\varepsilon_2})\) time, for a constant \(\varepsilon_2>0\) that depends on \(\varepsilon_1\) and \(\mu\).

_The next bridge._ Let _s_:=_t_<sub>_i_+1</sub>_\<n_. The bridge _B_<sub>_i_+1</sub> is read off the walks represented in _E<sub>i</sub>=E<sub>i</sub>(B<sub>i</sub>,C<sub>i</sub>s_), and this is where the small target set pays off. These are 2_n|B<sub>i</sub>_| walks with _O(s_) edges each, so listing them all takes \(\tilde{O}(n|B_i|s)=\tilde{O}(n^2R)\le\tilde{O}(n^{2+\gamma})\) time, whereas listing a path for every pair of vertices, as Zwick’s construction does, would take _O(n_<sup>2</sup>_s_) time, which exceeds our budget at the large scales. From each listed walk, erase the cycles, and keep the resulting path if it has at least _s/_2 edges. Let _B_<sub>_i_+1</sub> be a greedy hitting set of the vertex sets of the kept paths, each of which has more than _s/_2 vertices. It has _O_((_n/s_)log_n_) vertices by the analysis of the greedy heuristic \[Lov75, Chv79\], and it is computed in time linear in the total size of the sets, up to a logarithmic factor.

This _B_<sub>_i_+1</sub> is an (_s,C_<sub>_i_+1</sub>)-bridge for a constant _C_<sub>_i_+1</sub> that depends only on _i_. Consider first a pair (_x,y_) with \(\eta(x,y)=s\). The bridge _B<sub>i</sub>_ gives _b_ with \(\delta(x,y)=\delta(x,b)+\delta(b,y)\) and \(\eta(x,b)+\eta(b,y)\le C_is\), so the entries of _E<sub>i</sub>_ for (_x,b_) and (_b,y_) are exact, and the two walks that represent them are shortest walks. A cycle on a shortest walk has weight zero, so the two paths obtained by erasing the cycles are still shortest, and their concatenation is a shortest walk from _x_ to _y_ with _O(s_) edges. This walk has at least _s_ edges, since otherwise erasing its cycles would give a shortest path from _x_ to _y_ with fewer than \(\eta(x,y)\) edges. So one of the two paths has at least _s/_2 edges and was kept, and _B_<sub>_i_+1</sub> contains one of its vertices, which lies on the concatenated walk. For a pair with \(\eta(x,y)>s\), apply this to the first _s_ edges of a shortest path from _x_ to _y_ with the fewest edges, and keep the rest of that path; the walk obtained has at most \(C_{i+1}s+\eta(x,y)-s\le C_{i+1}\eta(x,y)\) edges.

_Running time._ There are \(O(1/\gamma)\) stages. Each computes its tables in \(\tilde{O}(n^{\omega+\gamma})\) time, its product in \(O(n^{2+\mu+\gamma-\varepsilon_2})\) time, and its bridge in \(\tilde{O}(n^{2+\gamma})\) time. Choosing \(\gamma\) smaller than both \(\varepsilon_2/2\) and \((2+\mu-\omega)/2\) gives the running time \(O(n^{2+\mu-\varepsilon^{\prime\prime}})\) for a constant \(\varepsilon''>0\), and every step is deterministic.

We compose the known reduction with the deterministic (min,+)-product algorithm of Theorem 22.
