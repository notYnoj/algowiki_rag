The next lemma will help us decide when the union of two forests _G_<sub>1</sub> and _G_<sub>2</sub> is acyclic. Intuitively, this is the case if there is no sequence \((w_1,w_2,\dots,w_{2\ell})\) of vertices such that _w_<sub>2_i_-1</sub> and _w_<sub>2_i_</sub> are connected in _G_<sub>1</sub> for each \(i\in[\ell]\) and _w_<sub>1</sub> and \(w_{2\ell}\) as well as _w_<sub>2_i_</sub> and _w_<sub>2_i_+1</sub> for each \(i\in[\ell-1]\) are connected in _G_<sub>2</sub>. We will next define a notion of acyclic pairs of partitions that captures this intuition. Unfortunately, the intuition does not quite work when _G_<sub>1</sub> and _G_<sub>2</sub> are not edge-disjoint. As a simple example, consider just two vertices _u_ and _v_. If they are connected by the same path in _G_<sub>1</sub> and _G_<sub>2</sub>, then the union of the two is just a path and therefore acyclic. If however, they are connected by two paths that differ by at least one edge, then the union is not acyclic. We circumvent this issue by keeping track of the set of edges in the intersection and ignoring those for one of the two graphs. We next define our notion of acyclic pairs of partitions. A pair (_P,Q_) of partitions is **_acyclic_** if the following graph _F<sub>P,Q</sub>_ is acyclic. Start with a vertex _v<sub>x</sub>_ for each element _x_ and a vertex _u<sub>p</sub>_ for each block \(p\in P\cup Q\). Then, add an edge between _v<sub>x</sub>_ and _u<sub>p</sub>_ if \(x\in p\). Note that deciding whether a pair (_P,Q_) is acyclic can be done in O(_n_) time, where _n_ is the number of elements partitioned by _P_ and _Q_, as we can build the bipartite graph in O(_n_) time and then check for a cycle in linear time. Since each element vertex is incident to at most 2 edges, the number of edges is at most 2_n_ and thus this can also be done in O(_n_) time. We next show that acyclic partitions accurately describes when the union of two partial solutions is acyclic.
