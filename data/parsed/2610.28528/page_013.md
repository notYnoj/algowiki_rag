<tbc>two-replica system is conserved, so pairing sub-optimal solutions should trade optimality between the two members of a pair: as one improves the other degrades, and retaining only the better member would drive a pool of sub-optimal solutions towards optimality. We note two caveats. The MIS repair step (Section IID) changes set sizes and therefore breaks the exact energy conservation the argument relies on, and no experiment in this work seeds the algorithm with a deliberately sub-optimal pool. We therefore offer this as a direction for future work rather than an established mechanism.

Classical post-processing thus provides a computationally cheap step that can partially mitigate poor quantum hardware performance.

### C. Limitations, Non-Ergodicity, and Future Directions

There are, however, limitations to cluster-update- based post-processing approaches. First, the Houdayer algorithm is fundamentally non-ergodic: it can only reach configurations obtainable by a single cluster exchange between two seeds. If the quantum seeds are all confined to a single pocket of the solution space, the algorithm will map only that disconnected pocket and leave other regions entirely unexplored. Coverage rises with the size of the seed pool and falls with the size of the manifold (Section IIIE). Increasing the number of quantum shots or diversifying the annealing schedule, for which systematic design methods now exist [21], would enlarge and diversify the seed pool and, in turn, improve manifold coverage. A further possibility for future work is to pool seeds from solvers with different sampling biases, for example quantum measurements together with a cheap classical heuristic, so that the sources populate complementary regions of the manifold. Alternatively, including less optimal solutions can allow the algorithm to climb over small barriers in the energy landscape by successively applying cluster updates. Second, we have not run the converse control of seeding the algorithm with cheap classical near-optima, either in place of the quantum seeds or mixed with them, so the marginal value of the quantum seeds specifically, as opposed to any sufficiently diverse near-optimal pool, is not isolated here. Ref. [11] addresses the bias-correction question for classical optimizers but not this comparison. Third, the approach is heuristic, i.e., there is no _a priori_ guarantee that all optima are found. Where a reference degeneracy is available this can be checked rather than assumed, and Section IIIE does so: coverage of the ground-state manifold ranges from 0.001% to 100%, and on six instances the recovered pool is provably complete. Completeness is therefore attainable, but only for manifolds small enough that a fixed seed budget can enumerate them. For applications that require solution diversity rather than completeness, a finite number of uncorrelated solutions is often sufficient. Fourth, the exploration scales as O(|S<sub>0</sub>|<sup>2</sup>)<tbc>

<tbc>in the size of the _seed_ pool, which becomes costly for very large seed sets. However, our saturation-based stopping criterion mitigates this in practice. Finally, all instances in this study are unit disk graphs arising from Rydberg atom array geometries. The effectiveness of Houdayer updates on graphs where clusters percolate (e.g., power- law distribution of nodes as in scale-free graphs or higher dimensions) degrades rapidly. However, some improvement might be achievable even in these cases for sections of the ground-state manifold.

Houdayer cluster updates are not specific to MIS. The core mechanism of symmetric difference decomposition into independent clusters, rejection-free exchange applies to any combinatorial problem where solutions can be represented as binary assignments and pairs of solutions generate a well-defined overlap structure. It carries over without a new repair step to maximum clique and minimum vertex cover, through their equivalence to the MIS problem, and to weighted MIS, where the exchange still preserves independence and changes the weight by the difference between the two sides of each cluster. Other candidates, such as constraint satisfaction problems with degenerate solution spaces, would need a repair step adapted to their feasibility structure.

## V. CONCLUSION

We have measured how much ground-state information a neutral atom MIS measurement record contains beyond the solutions the device reports. Across 43 instances from QuEra’s Aquila Rydberg device, classical post-processing via Houdayer cluster updates recovers between 1.0\(\times\) and 11923\(\times\) as many distinct configurations at the certified-or-best-found size (median 24.3\(\times\), and 8.9\(\times\) over the 35 instances whose seeds were already optimal), turning pools of tens to hundreds of configurations into collections with a median of 463 and as many as nearly two million. The entire dataset required 18 minutes of computation on a single laptop core, measured end to end, compared with about 100s of device time for each 1000-shot hardware run.

Two features of this data set make the measurement possible. First, exact ground-state degeneracies are available for these instances, so the recovered fraction is a number rather than a bound: a median of 21% of the true manifold, and on six instances provably all of it. The non- ergodicity of the method is thus quantified rather than disclaimed, and its practical boundary identified, namely that completeness is reachable when the manifold is small enough for a fixed seed budget to enumerate, and that coverage falls steeply as the manifold grows. Second, the same exact references reveal that the quantum seed pool sat below the true optimum on 8 of the 43 instances. Amplification and solution quality are therefore separate axes: a run can be amplified a thousandfold and still describe a suboptimal manifold, and only an exact reference distinguishes the two cases.
