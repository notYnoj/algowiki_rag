To quantify the difference, we ran a local search from the same seed pools that moves a selected vertex to a free site with exactly one selected neighbor, keeping the set size, and whenever such a move frees a vertex adds it and continues from the larger set, with the same number of candidate evaluations as the cluster updates used on each instance. For the MIS on unit disk graphs, the sparse and geometrically constrained edge structure causes the induced subgraph \(G[\Delta_{ij}]\) to break into several components, so a single pair generates 2_<sup>c</sup>_ candidates: over the 135202 seed pairs of the 30 instances with optimal seeds (at most 20000 per instance), the median is _c_=3 and the maximum _c_=13. The components are not restricted to the two-vertex form drawn in Figure 1: 57% contain one vertex from each parent and the remainder are larger, up to 44 vertices. The fan-out is unaffected by component size, since each component contributes one binary choice however many vertices it holds. Between maximum independent sets every component is balanced, and none of these pairs contains an imbalanced one. Between the suboptimal seeds of the other 7 instances with at least two of them, 0.16% of components are imbalanced, all but one of odd size, and these are the source of the size improvements in Section IIID. The exponential fan-out per pair is what drives the rapid manifold expansion observed across all instance types.

The complementarity with quantum sampling is also important. Quantum annealing explores the solution space through coherent tunneling and tends to cluster samples near local energy minima. Houdayer updates operate orthogonally: they systematically recombine structurally distinct solutions to reach regions of the ground- state manifold that neither single-solution perturbation nor further quantum sampling would easily access. The two methods are thus complementary. Quantum provides high-quality seeds, and classical cluster updates provide systematic coverage.

## B. Two Regimes: Amplification and Quality Improvement

Our results reveal two qualitatively distinct operating regimes for hybrid Houdayer cluster-update post- processing, determined by the relationship between quantum hardware capability and ground-state manifold structure.

In the **large manifold regime**, quantum hardware reaches the true ground state (verified against the reference optima, Section IIF) and produces an initial pool of diverse optimal solutions. Classical post-processing then systematically expands this pool by recombining seeds to reach structurally distant manifold regions. This regime dominates for the standard test bed and for those benchmark instances that combine a large degenerate manifold with a diverse seed pool, where amplification factors can reach 10<sup>3</sup>-10<sup>4</sup> times the original pool.

Amplification and solution quality are, however, largely decoupled, and the clearest illustration is instance `HP1435`. It is the hardest Benchmark instance (_H_=1435, QAA success rate 0% [7]), its quantum seeds sit one atom below the true optimum of 45, and Houdayer post-processing nonetheless amplified them \(1104\times\) into 118153 configurations, all at the suboptimal size 44. A large, diverse seed pool therefore guarantees a large final pool but says nothing about whether that pool sits at the optimum. Amplification quantity scales with the accessible manifold size and seed diversity rather than with hardness, and independently of whether the seeds are optimal: larger degenerate spaces offer more recombination opportunities, and high initial diversity (Figure 4) seeds broader exploration.

In the **difficult landscape regime**, quantum hardware does not reliably find the true ground state. The initial solution pool sits at a suboptimal energy level, and the Houdayer algorithm by operating on these seeds can construct recombinations that repair into higher-quality configurations. This regime characterizes the three hard instances where classical post-processing discovered strictly larger independent sets. Amplification in this regime is variable (from \(10\times\) for instance `L17\_seed424` up to \(1139\times\) for instance `L17\_seed46`). Both figures refer to the first-level pass, which expands the quantum seed pool at the seed size and is where the larger configurations are first discovered. The subsequent pass seeded by those configurations amplifies a different, smaller starting set, so ratios across the two levels are not comparable. The spread reflects how many configurations exist at the improved size, but the qualitative outcome, improvement in solution quality, is of greater practical interest than the raw count. It demonstrates that classical recombination can partially compensate for systematic biases in quantum sampling, and in one case close the gap to the true optimum.
