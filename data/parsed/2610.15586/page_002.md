Sample-based Krylov quantum diagonalization (SKQD) uses time-evolved states \(e^{-iHT}|\psi\rangle\) as the source of the bitstrings and comes with convergence guarantees \[8\]. The same idea applies to linear systems: one measures quantum Krylov states _e_<sup>-_iAT_</sup>|_b_〉 generated by the Hermitian matrix _A_ itself and solves the system classically on the observed basis states. A related approach was developed concurrently by Park _et al._ \[9\], who apply sample-based Krylov diagonalization to general Hermitian linear systems with a variational reconstruction based on the cost Hamiltonian and prove support-discovery guarantees for that setting. The present paper is self-contained. We treat positive-definite systems from elliptic partial differential equations and reconstruct the solution by a Galerkin solve. The energy-norm bounds of Sec. 3 are derived directly from the outcome law and do not rely on the sampling theorems of Ref. \[9\].

In this paper we develop the method for linear systems that arise from elliptic partial differential equations. These systems are positive definite, and their solutions are smooth. Two observations guide the work. First, the solution of such a system is not concentrated in the grid-point basis, but it is strongly concentrated in a sine or cosine basis matched to the boundary conditions. We therefore measure in such a basis. Second, for a positive-definite system a Galerkin solve on the sampled set is simpler than a variational reconstruction and is optimal in the energy norm. We derive error bounds for this reconstruction and relate them to the sampling probabilities.

Elliptic problems on curved domains are naturally formulated on a Riemannian manifold \[10, 11\]. A cross section with coordinates _u<sup>i</sup>_ carries a metric _g<sub>ij</sub>_, and the physical operator becomes a weighted Laplace-Beltrami operator. In this language a change of coordinates is a diffeomorphism, and the weak form of the problem is invariant under it. The metric then enters the discrete matrix only through the combinations \(\sqrt{g}g^{ij}\) and \(\sqrt{g}\). In two dimensions the first combination is invariant under conformal changes of the metric, which makes the stiffness matrix independent of the geometry. Symmetries of the cross section are isometries generated by Killing fields _X_ with L_<sub>X</sub>g_=0, where L_<sub>X</sub>_ is the Lie derivative. They give selection rules that tell us which measurement outcomes can be discarded. Deformations of the cross section are generated by vector fields _V_ , and the Lie derivative L_<sub>V</sub>g_ gives their first-order effect on the matrix. We use these notions throughout Sec. 4 and summarize them in Appendix A.

We then formulate the method for elliptic problems on a Riemannian cross section. We use the formulation for the implicit step of pulsatile flow in elliptic, lens-shaped, and curved ducts. These ducts are models of compressed and eccentrically narrowed coronary arteries \[12\]. We compare the quantum sampler with classical mode selection, and we test the outcome laws on six- and eight-qubit IonQ and IBM processors. We do not claim a quantum advantage for these examples. The theory and the numerical results instead identify where the sampling is efficient and where it is not.

The contributions of this paper are as follows. (1) We formulate Krylov-state sampling in any fixed basis with an efficient transform (Sec. 2.2). This follows from unitary equivalence, but it is what makes the method useful for partial differential equations. (2) For positive-definite systems we replace the variational reconstruction by a Galerkin solve and derive expected and high-probability energy-norm bounds, a sample complexity, a sampling efficiency, a computable residual certificate, and a support- completeness theorem for the dephased outcome law (Sec. 3). The optimality of the Galerkin solution is the classical Céa property; the new elements are the bounds that connect the energy-norm error to the outcome law. (3) We formulate the method on Riemannian cross sections (Sec. 4). The conformal invariance of the Dirichlet energy, Killing symmetries, and first-order shape calculus are standard tools. Here we use them to identify the matrix class, to construct measurement filters with a guarantee, and to bound the effect of geometric uncertainty. (4) We test the method on elliptic, lens-shaped, and curved ducts in simulation and on two quantum processors (Secs. 5 and 6), and we compare it with classical mode selection at equal subspace size.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 describes the sampling protocol, measurement in a general basis, and the Galerkin reconstruction. Section 3 derives the energy-norm guarantees, the support-completeness theorem, and the effect of finite time averaging.
