For \(\ell>1\), some level-\(\ell\) constituent tensors \(T_{i,j,k}^{(\ell)}\) are no longer matrix multiplication tensors, so each independent copy of \(\mathcal{T}=\otimes_{i,j,k}\big(T_{i,j,k}^{(\ell)}\big)^{\otimes\alpha(i,j,k)\cdot n}\) may also no longer be a matrix multiplication tensor. To resolve this issue, prior works \[CW90, DS13, Vas12, LG14, AV21\] use the laser method recursively to analyze _T<sub>i,j,k</sub>_’s that are not matrix multiplication tensors.

## The work of \[DWZ23\].

Consider the analysis on the tensor \(T^{(\ell)}\) of the laser method. In previous approaches prior to the work of Duan, Wu and Zhou \[DWZ23\], one would first apply the laser method on \(T^{(\ell)}\) to obtain multiple copies of \(\mathcal{T}=\otimes_{i,j,k}\big(T_{i,j,k}^{(\ell)}\big)^{\otimes\alpha(i,j,k)\cdot n}\) which consists of level-\(\ell\) constituent tensors \(T_{i,j,k}^{(\ell)}\) and do not share level-\(\ell\) variable blocks. Then for each \(T_{i,j,k}^{(\ell)}\) that is not a matrix multiplication tensor, one would recursively apply the laser method to obtain multiple copies of some other tensors that are independent over level-\((\ell-1)\) variable blocks.

Recall that for a level-\(\ell\) constituent tensor \(T_{i,j,k}^{(\ell)}\), we can partition its variable set \(X_i^{(\ell)},Y_j^{(\ell)},Z_k^{(\ell)}\) re cursively into \(\sqcup_{i^\prime} X_{i^\prime}^{(\ell-1)}\otimes X_{i-i^\prime}^{(\ell-1)}\), \(\sqcup_{j^\prime} Y_{j^\prime}^{(\ell-1)}\otimes Y_{j-j^\prime}^{(\ell-1)}\) and \(\sqcup_{k^\prime} Z_{k^\prime}^{(\ell-1)}\otimes Z_{k-k^\prime}^{(\ell-1)}\) respectively. In the first recursive step, when applying the laser method on \(T_{i,j,k}^{(\ell)}\), we take the \(n'\)-th tensor power \(\big(T_{i,j,k}^{(\ell)}\big)^{\otimes n^\prime}\) of \(T_{i,j,k}^{(\ell)}\) for some large \(n'\) and specify a distribution \(\beta\) over triples \(((i',i-i'),(j',j-j'),(k',k-k'))\) where \(0\le i'\le i\), \(0\le j'\le j\), \(0\le k'\le k\), and zero out all variables blocks that are not consistent with the marginal distributions induced by \(\beta\). Therefore, in \(T_{i,j,k}^{\otimes n^\prime}\), only a subset of the level-\((\ell-1)\) variable blocks survive the above zeroing-out.

Now suppose we can move the above zeroing-out step earlier, say before we have independent copies of T when we first apply the laser method on \(T^{(\ell)}\), then instead of keeping independent copies of T , we only need to keep a subtensor \(\mathcal{T}'\) of it, where \(\mathcal{T}'\) is T after applying the above zeroing-out step. This leads to one of the key observations in \[DWZ23\]: we do not need to have copies of \(\mathcal{T}'\) that are fully independent over the level-\(\ell\) variable blocks. Instead, any two copies can share the same level-\(\ell\) variable block as long as they do not share the same level-\((\ell-1)\) variable blocks that would survive the first zeroing-out in the recursive application of the laser method on the level-\(\ell\) constituent tensors. As a result, we can potentially keep more independent copies of \(\mathcal{T}'\), because of the relaxed constraints, and each copy \(\mathcal{T}'\) would still be essentially as good as T for the purpose of the analysis because we are merely moving a later zeroing-out earlier. Because we are keeping more copies, by the asymptotic sum inequality, we will achieve a better bound for \(\omega\).

In subfigure (a), works prior to \[DWZ23\] including \[Vas12, AV21, LG14\] zero out level-3 blocks according to \(\alpha\) and obtain level-3-independent<sup>2</sup> copies of \(\mathcal{T}'\) before zeroing out level-2 blocks. It is not obvious how one can accomplish the above modification. Duan et al. \[DWZ23\] considered the notion of _split distributions_, which roughly measures how a level-\(\ell\) block "splits" into level-\((\ell-1)\) blocks with respect to the recursive leveled partition. By observing the split distribution of a level-\(\ell\) block, one gains some partial information about the level-\((\ell-1)\) blocks that allows the modification of zeroing out level-\((\ell-1)\) blocks inconsistent with \(\beta\) earlier. Ideally, one would hope to achieve this modification symmetrically over the _X_-, _Y_-, and _Z_-variables, i.e., allow the sharing of level-\(\ell\) variable blocks in all three dimensions, but the method in \[DWZ23\] did not achieve that.
