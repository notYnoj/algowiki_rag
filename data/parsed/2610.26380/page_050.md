## D.3. Supporting lemmas

LEMMA D.1. _Fix a set_ \(\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{D}\)_, and let M_:=span(E)_. Let_ \(\mathcal{H}_\infty:=\sigma\left(\bigcup_{t\ge 1}\mathcal{H}_t\right)\) _denote the_ \(\sigma\)_-algebra generated by the full infinite history. Let_ \(\Omega\in\mathcal{H}_\infty\)_. Suppose that every_ \(L\in\mathcal{E}\) _is sampled infinitely often on_ \(\Omega\)_. Then, for every_ \(v\in M\),

\(v^\top\mu_{t-1}^*\rightarrow v^\top\theta,v^\top\Sigma_{t-1}^*v\rightarrow 0\text{\textit{a.s. on}}\Omega.\)

_Moreover,_ \(\unknown_\Omega v^\top\theta\) _is_ \(\mathcal{H}_\infty\)_-measurable._

_Proof._ For evaluation _t_, let \(L_t:=e_{i_t}-e_{j_t}\in\mathcal{D}\) be its chosen direction and let \(X_t\in\{-1,1\}\) be the observed outcome. Let \((\mathcal{F}_t)_{t\ge 0}\) be the filtration defined by \(\mathcal{F}_{t-1}:=\sigma(\theta,\mathcal{H}_t,L_t)\) for \(t\ge 1\). Then F<sub>_t_-1</sub> contains all previously observed outcomes and the direction chosen before observing _X<sub>t</sub>_. In particular, _L<sub>t</sub>_ is F<sub>_t_-1</sub>-measurable.

Fix \(L\in\mathcal{E}\), and define<tbc>

\(N_t(L):=\sum_{s=1}^t\unknown\{L_s=L\},\widehat{X}_t:=\unknown\{X_t=+1\}.\)

<tbc>Set<tbc>

\(\xi_t(L):=\unknown\{L_t=L\}\left(\widehat{X}_t-\mathbb{P}(X_L=+1|\theta)\right).\)

<tbc>Since \(\unknown\{L_t=L\}\) is F<sub>_t_-1</sub>-measurable, and since conditional on F<sub>_t_-1</sub> the success probability on the event {_L<sub>t</sub>=L_} is \(\mathbb{P}(X_L=+1|\theta)\), we have \(\mathbb{E}[\xi_t(L)|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}]=0\). Thus \((\xi_t(L))_{t\ge 1}\) is a martingale difference sequence with respect to \((\mathcal{F}_t)_{t\ge 0}\). Now define<tbc>

\(M_t(L):=\sum_{s=1}^t\frac{\xi_s(L)}{max\{N_s(L),1\}}.\)

<tbc>Then \((M_t(L))_{t\ge 1}\) is a martingale, and<tbc>

\(\mathbb{E}\big[\{M_t(L)-M_{t-1}(L)\}^2\big|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\big]\le\frac{\unknown\{L_t=L\}}{max\{N_t(L),1\}^2}.\)

<tbc>Hence<tbc>

\(sup_{t\ge 1}\mathbb{E}[M_t(L)^2]\le\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{s=1}^\infty\frac{\unknown\{L_s=L\}}{max\{N_s(L),1\}^2}\right]\le\sum_{j=1}^\infty\frac{1}{j^2}<\infty.\)

<tbc>By the square-integrable martingale convergence theorem, _M<sub>t</sub>(L_) converges almost surely. On \(\Omega\), \(N_t(L)\rightarrow\infty\), so Kronecker’s lemma gives<tbc>

\(\frac{1}{max\{N_t(L),1\}}\sum_{s=1}^t\xi_s(L)\rightarrow 0\text{a.s. on}\Omega.\)

<tbc>Therefore, on \(\Omega\),

\(\frac{1}{N_t(L)}\sum_{s=1}^t\unknown\{L_s=L\}\widehat{X}_s\rightarrow\mathbb{P}(X_L=+1|\theta)\text{a.s.}\)

The left-hand side is \(\mathcal{H}_\infty\)-measurable, so on \(\Omega\) the limit \(\mathbb{P}(X_L=+1|\theta)\) is \(\mathcal{H}_\infty\)-measurable. Since the link is strictly increasing, \(L^\top\theta\) is also \(\mathcal{H}_\infty\)-measurable on \(\Omega\). Since this holds for every \(L\in\mathcal{E}\), the same holds for every \(v\in M=\mathrm{span}(\mathcal{E})\).
