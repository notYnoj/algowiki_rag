## 7.1. Setup

_Configurations._ We consider \(I\in\{10,20,40,80,160\}\) and selection sizes \(k\in\{1,\sqrt{0.1I},0.1I\}\) (rounded). The prior is exchangeable with \(\mu_0=\mathbf{0},\Sigma_0=10\cdot\mathrm{Id}_I\), and the rating threshold is \(\theta_0=0\) (in expectation half of the items get thumbs-up ratings). Evaluator accuracies are 75% for ratings and 85% for comparisons, calibrated through Remark 1 to \(\beta_0=0.316\) and \(\beta_1=0.439\). We fix the cost of a rating at _r_<sub>0</sub>=1 and consider comparison costs \(r_1\in\{2,3\}\). The total budget is _R_=4_I_. These are the base configurations. Later subsections also test modifications of them to demonstrate that the results are not brittle to these choices.

_Endgame._ In each configuration, we run the switching procedure of §5.2 with _M_=1,000 to choose the switching point from SA to KG. The candidate switching points are \(\nu=0,0.05,\dots,0.95,1\).

_Policies._ Alongside SE, we run modified versions of it to gain insight into the algorithm’s behavior as discussed above. To keep track of the modifications of SE, we specify each aspect changed by an optional argument. The first optional argument is the switching point. \(\text{SE}(\mathrm{sw}=\nu)\) switches from SA to KG once the share \(\nu\) of the budget has been spent, so for example, SE(sw=1) is SA. The second optional argument is which evaluation type is allowed at each point of the budget: rc (rate then compare). SE itself may mix the two types throughout, whereas SE(rc=_u_) is restricted to only ratings until a proportion _u_ of the budget is spent and only comparisons after, so for example, SE(rc=1) is ratings only for the entire budget.

To measure performance, we compare against three benchmarks: a slightly modified RATERANK of Li et al. (2018), which allocates based on myopic information gain about the top-_k_ set per unit cost, the KG policy with random fallback when it gets "stuck", and KG with SA as the fallback, KG(SA), which corresponds to SE(sw=0).

_Simulation protocol._ For each configuration, we run 1,000 independent replicates where \(\unknown\) is drawn from the prior and for each \(\unknown\) the same stream of evaluation outcomes is drawn across policies. We report the average realized opportunity cost, which we refer to as the EOC (technically this is an estimator of the EOC). Posterior updates use variational inference with a multivariate Gaussian variational family. RATERANK is run for \(I\le 40\) only, due to its computational cost. Figures that demonstrate the algorithm’s behavior are presented on the _I_=40_,k_=2_,r_<sub>1</sub>=3 configuration. The corresponding figures for all other configurations are provided in the Appendix.
