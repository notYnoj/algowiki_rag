As discussed in the introduction, ratings are not effective in separating items whose utilities are close together, which is key for narrowing in on the terminal selection. Conversely, when _r_<sub>1</sub>=2, SE and SE(rc=0) are quite comparable, that is, ratings do not add much value. Thus, to see the value in combining the two input types, one of the main premises of this paper, we look at the change in EOC of SE and SE(rc=0) as the cost of a comparison goes from _r_<sub>1</sub>=2 to _r_<sub>1</sub>=3, shown in Figure 4. SE can adapt to this, while SE(rc=0) must still rely on comparisons alone. We see that having the ability to rate can be a major improvement. The EOC of SE(rc=0) increases by between 57% and 167%, whereas that of SE increases by only between 43% and 88%, since it can substitute the now more expensive comparisons with ratings.
