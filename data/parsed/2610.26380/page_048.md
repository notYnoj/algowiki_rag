## Appendix D: Proof of Theorem 2

All probabilities and expectations below are with respect to the prior, the evaluation model, and the policy. Until the final convergence argument, consider SA run continuously without a budget constraint.

## D.1. Notation

Let

\(P:=Id_I-\frac{1}{I}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top,\mathbf{1}^\perp:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^I:\mathbf{1}^\top x=0\}.\)

Let D denote the finite set of evaluation directions available to the policy. A rating of item _i_ has direction **_e_**_<sub>i</sub>_, and a pairwise comparison of items _i_ and _j_ has direction **_e_**_<sub>i</sub>_-**_e_**_<sub>j</sub>_. Thus,<tbc>

\(\mathcal{D}=\{e_i-e_j:(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}\},\)

<tbc>where **_e_**<sub>0</sub>=**0**, and \(\mathbf{1}^\perp\subseteq span(\mathcal{D})\). For \(L\in\mathcal{D}\), define<tbc>

\(\beta_L:=\left\{\begin{array}{ c c }
\beta_0, & L\text{is a rating direction,} \\
\beta_1, & L\text{is a comparison direction,}
\end{array}\right.\theta_0(L):=\left\{\begin{array}{ c c }
\theta_0, & L\text{is a rating direction,} \\
0, & L\text{is a comparison direction,}
\end{array}\right.\)

<tbc>and define _r<sub>L</sub>_ analogously as _r_<sub>0</sub> or _r_<sub>1</sub>. Conditional on \(\theta\) and the history before an evaluation, its success probability depends only on the chosen direction. For every \(L\in\mathcal{D}\),

\(\mathbb{P}(X_L=+1|\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\epsilon_j}\big[g\big(L^\top\theta-\theta_0(L)+\epsilon_j\big)\big],\)

where _j=_0 for a rating direction and _j=_1 for a comparison direction. This probability is continuous and strictly increasing in \(L^\top\theta\).

Let H_<sub>t</sub>_ be the history available at the start of round _t_, i.e., the \(\sigma\)-algebra generated by all evaluation choices and outcomes through round _t_-1, and let

\(\mu_{t-1}^*:=\mathbb{E}[\theta|\mathcal{H}_t],\Sigma_{t-1}^*:=Cov(\theta|\mathcal{H}_t)\)

be the exact posterior moments at the start of round _t_. SA uses the approximate moments \((\mu_{t-1},\Sigma_{t-1})\).

For notational convenience, write<tbc>

\(g_{t,i}:=e_i-b_t,\sigma_{t-1,i}^2:=g_{t,i}^\top\Sigma_{t-1}g_{t,i},z_{t-1,i}:=\frac{\mu_{t-1,i}-q_t}{\sigma_{t-1,i}},w_{t-1,i}:=\frac{\phi(z_{t-1,i})}{\sigma_{t-1,i}}.\)

<tbc>Here _z_<sub>_t_-1_,i_</sub> and _w_<sub>_t_-1_,i_</sub> are defined only when \(\sigma_{t-1,i}>0\). By Theorem 1 and the proof of Corollary 1, either

(i) \((q_t,b_t)=(\mu_{t-1,j},e_j)\) for some _j_. Then \(\sigma_{t-1,j}=0\), while \(\sigma_{t-1,i}>0\) for \(i\neq j\). Since \(a_{q_t,b_t}(\theta)=\theta_j\), the _j_th loss term is zero before and after every evaluation, so its contribution to the marginal score is zero.<tbc>

(ii) \(\sigma_{t-1,i}>0\) for every _i_, and<tbc>

<tbc>\(\sum_{i=1}^I\Phi(z_{t-1,i})=k,b_{t,i}=\frac{w_{t-1,i}}{\sum_{\ell=1}^Iw_{t-1,\ell}},i=1,\dots,I.\)<tbc>
