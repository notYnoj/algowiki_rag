At the instance level the raw converged WS layer is still ahead of the raw contour-head U-Net by 0.042 F1 and of the tuned watershed by 0.009 F1 / 0.005 PQ (intervals clear of zero, but small, and the 20-step layer that was actually trained is 0.005 IoU and 0.008 BF<sub>2</sub> _below_ the watershed output while 0.008 F1 above it); with the 8-px min-area filter that sections 7 and 10 apply to every method, the order flips: the watershed U-Net reaches F1 0.757 / PQ 0.618 against the WS layer's 0.726 / 0.589, -0.031 F1 paired, and the filtered contour-head logits alone are level with the WS layer (+0.004 F1, +0.001 PQ). So the two things the WS layer was doing in section 10, removing small spurious components and benefiting from a contour map, are each done at least as well by a min-area filter and an extra head; what is left to the WS layer is the best count error of any output (0.21 raw, 0.20 filtered, against the watershed's 0.34 / 0.23, which over-segments: 27 components per patch for 23.7 nuclei), a clear advantage on sparse patches (fewer than 10 nuclei, F1 0.670 vs 0.596, count error 0.50 vs 0.81) and on HeadNeck and Adrenal, and the fact that it is a single differentiable module with no post-processing hyper-parameter, where the watershed’s marker spacing had to be tuned on a third fold and moves PQ by 0.17 across its range. On crowded patches (more than 30 nuclei) the watershed is the better instance segmenter, which is consistent with the seam statistics: the watershed cuts 31 % of the seam pixels where the converged WS layer cuts 23 %, no more than the U-Net's own logits.<tbc>

- **Removing the predicted contour is not a usable post-processing.** The contour head's 2-3 px band costs 0.064 IoU and 0.163 BF<sub>1</sub> when subtracted, although it cuts 55 % of the seams; a 1-px contour target, or a thinning step, would be needed for (a) to compete.<tbc>

**-** **What this establishes.** With a fair U-Net the finite-p layer with the edge target is a competitive instance segmenter on PanNuke, not a better one: level at the pixel level, slightly ahead on raw components, behind once both sides are filtered, better on sparse patches and on counts, worse on crowded ones. The remaining arguments for the WS layer are therefore architectural (one module, trained end to end, a limit that section 10 showed is usable again) rather than a metric margin on this dataset. The same caveats as section 10 apply, one seed and one fold pair, plus the baseline's own: the simplest marker recipe (distance-transform maxima) with one tuned spacing; a HoVer-Net-style distance or gradient head would be a stronger classical baseline still.
