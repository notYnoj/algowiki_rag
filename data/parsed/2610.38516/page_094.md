<tbc>them. The strengths therefore act as a per-nucleus correction of the unary at the rim, which the foreground head already provides per pixel. The one ranking that matters for the output, primaries above duplicates (the duplicates do win pixels), is not learned either (AUC 0.34-0.41). The partition cross-entropy rewards a marker for the pixels it takes, not for being a nucleus. Saturation (gradient 1e-11 for a candidate that wins or loses its pixels outright, section 14) leaves exactly the decisive cases unsupervised.<tbc>

**-** **The limit collapses again.** The exact Power Watershed with the learned strengths gives F1 0.10-0.16 on the 307 limit patches: any \(\sigma\text{\_}k<1\) is a hard veto at p \(\rightarrow\infty\) (section 14), and here every candidate sits at \(\sigma\approx 0.75\)-0.83. Strengths of this kind are incompatible with deploying the limit (the control, at \(\sigma=1\), keeps 0.611 / 0.502). The scorers’ oracles, 0.917-0.927 against 0.856 for the control, are inflated the same way as section 14’s: ground-truth markers enter at \(\sigma=1\), stronger than any marker the trained solve has seen.<tbc>

**-** **For the paper.** The section 14 sentence stands and can be sharpened: _making marker selection differentiable did not help. Strengths computed from the marker head degraded its detections (-0.063 F1), and scorers isolated from it, a strength channel or a detached per-candidate network, were still 0.006-0.013 F1 behind training the solve on the extracted markers alone, because the partition loss rewards a marker for the pixels it takes, not for being a nucleus._ The marker extraction stays a discrete step inside the training loop (paper commit 8988128).
