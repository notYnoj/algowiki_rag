- **The detection objective recovers the missed nuclei and loses more elsewhere.** The pooled peak loss halves the nuclei without a marker (14.5 \(\rightarrow\) 7.0 % for the WS layer, 21.8 \(\rightarrow\) 8.9 % for the baseline) but it does so by raising the map wherever something nucleus-like sits: markers in background go from 3.6 to 10.0 per patch for the WS layer (1.6 \(\rightarrow\) 6.8 for the baseline) and nuclei split by several markers from 1.6 to 7.4 % (1.6 \(\rightarrow\) 4.8 %), and no threshold on fold 3 trades these back (the chosen thr 0.3 against 0.2). The net effect is -0.090 F1 / -0.083 PQ for the WS layer and -0.028 / -0.029 for the baseline, with the oracle unchanged (the costs are the same): under the WS layer every spurious marker becomes an instance, since the solve has no mechanism to discard a label, while the watershed’s foreground mask and minimum area absorb part of them, which is why the baseline loses a third as much. The raw-maximum form was worse still (needle peaks, hundreds of markers). So the lever that the oracle rows pointed at, the markers, does not move with a per-nucleus objective of this kind: what is needed is a marker head that is precise as well as sensitive (a detection loss with explicit negatives, or HoVer-Net’s gradient term that penalises spurious maxima), and that improvement would go to the baseline’s watershed as much as to the WS layer.<tbc>

**- **What this says about the plan.** With the one-seed caveat lifted, the record on PanNuke is: the multi-marker WS layer with the distance head is a strong instance segmenter (F1 0.787-0.791, PQ 0.636-0.642 on three fold pairs), the plain three-head U-Net with a watershed on the same head is at least as good on F1 and 0.012-0.023 PQ better, the WS layer’s costs are 0.09-0.10 F1 better than the baseline’s with perfect markers, and the first attempt at better markers helped neither. The WS layer’s case therefore rests on what the oracle measures, the quality of its learned costs given the markers, which only pays off with a marker head that does not exist yet, and on its structural properties (one differentiable module, the exact limit at 0.7 s per patch, no post-processing beyond three scalars). The ruche cluster makes the next sweep cheap: a 3 \(\times\) 3 seeds \(\times\) fold-pairs grid of the two models is 18 jobs, one afternoon on the A100 partition.

### P.4 A marker head that is precise as well as sensitive? Hard negatives and the gradient term

Section 11.3 ended with the peak loss raising sensitivity and precision together in the wrong direction: fewer nuclei without a marker, many more markers in background and inside nuclei.
