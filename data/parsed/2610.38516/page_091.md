- **The exact limit does not benefit, it suffers.** On the 307 limit patches the limit of the implicit-trained costs loses 0.107-0.108 IoU against their converged solve (0.60 against 0.71) and has the worst count error of the four runs (0.39-0.42), where the unrolled runs’ limits lose 0.039 and 0.083 IoU and vary a lot between seeds (0.668 / 0.620). Deploy-at-the-limit therefore needs its own training consideration (higher final p, or a sharper cost parametrisation) whichever differentiation mode is used.<tbc>

- **What this says for the paper.** The three efficiency claims hold, measured: memory independent of depth for the implicit mode (92 MB for a batch of 16 at any tolerance, against 24 MB per unrolled step), cost affine in K at a fixed number of PCG steps (20.3 ms per marker per batch of 16, 4.8 MB per marker per image) with fewer steps needed as K grows, and an exact limit flat in K (29-51 ms on one core with Numba for K = 1-128). The implicit mode also wins on gradient accuracy (1e-3 against 0.9 relative error at the training p, K = 20) and on trained accuracy (+0.02 BF<sub>2</sub>, +0.03-0.05 F1 and PQ on two seeds), at twice the training time. Caveats to state: the affine law needs a batch large enough to fill the GPU (about 150 systems on an A100; below that the cost per step is flat), the converged adjoint of the multi-marker WS layer takes about twice the forward’s steps, and the limit of implicit-trained binary costs is worse than that of unrolled-trained ones.
