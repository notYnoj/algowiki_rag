<tbc>outputs. Boundaries come out rounded, output potentials are rarely binary, and the number of labels is fixed by a one-hot channel layout decided at design time. Watersheds have the opposite profile: results are crisp and directly explainable through labelling and the topographical nature of the input data. As a result, boundaries appear on the strongest edges of a cost map, regions are piecewise constant, and the number of regions is equal to the number of markers, not a property of the algorithm. An interesting combination is thus to add a watershed at the end of a segmenting network and train through it. The obstacle is that a watershed is a combinatorial procedure on a graph, with a gradient that is zero almost everywhere.

This article makes the case that the Power Watershed framework of Couprie et al. [13,14] can be used in this context. It provides its own relaxation, the Random Walker [21] at finite _p_, and we evaluate what the resulting watershed layer (WS layer) can be used for. The framework minimises \(\sum_ew_e^p|x_u-x_v|^q\) over potentials _x_ fixed on seeds; _q_=2 with finite _p_ is the Random Walker (RW), and \(p\rightarrow\infty\) is the Power Watershed (PW), a maximum spanning forest with a small linear solve on each plateau. Reading _p_ as an inverse temperature of the Gibbs distribution over spanning forests, as the probabilistic watershed of Fita Sanmartín et al. [16] suggests (the \(p\rightarrow\infty\) collapse is that of softmax\(\rightarrow\)argmax [24]), every finite-_p_ member is a convex problem whose solution is exactly differentiable, and the path ends at the PW, a true watershed rather than a surrogate as in learned watersheds [32] or post-hoc watershed transforms [5,22].

Our contributions are the following. (i) A differentiable RW/PW layer with unaries, written with dense shifted-grid operators so that it is batched, running on the GPU and can be differentiated in either of two ways: by unrolling a few preconditioned conjugate-gradient steps or implicitly, by one adjoint solve (Sect. 3). Its multi-label form costs one right-hand side per label, and its limit one spanning forest whatever the number of labels, whereas graph cuts are exact only for two labels. (ii) A conditioning study of solver cost, distance to the limit and gradient support as functions of _p_ (Sect. 4.1). (iii) An experimental account on synthetic scenes and PanNuke nuclei [18,17] that evaluates where the WS layer helps. With correct edge costs it recovers exact boundaries; as a binary last layer on real images it is level with a thresholded U-Net; with the instances as labels it is level with a tuned marker watershed on the same network, and ahead of it when training data are scarce (Sect. 4).
