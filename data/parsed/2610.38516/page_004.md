<tbc>a maximum spanning forest flooded from the confident pixels, i.e. a marker- controlled watershed whose markers are decided by the unaries. As the last layer of a network, \(c_i^1=-\alpha logs_i\) and \(c_i^0=-\alpha log(1-s_i)\) with _s_ the network’s soft foreground probability; \(\alpha\) is Grady’s \(\gamma\) and sets how strongly the unaries compete with the edges.

### Many labels.

With _K_ markers (classes or instances), the multi-label RW solves (3) with the shared matrix \(L(w)+\mathrm{diag}(\sum_ka^k)\) and one right-hand side _a<sup>k</sup>_ per label; summing the equations and using _L_**1**=0 shows that the _x<sup>k</sup>_ sum to one, so the output is a per-pixel distribution over this image’s markers. The cost is therefore affine in _K_ and the problem is exact and convex for every _K_. The limit is one spanning forest irrespective of _K_. This is where the family differs most from graph cuts, exact for two labels but NP-hard beyond, where \(\alpha\)-expansion \[8\] is the usual approximation; in the limit the multi-label problem becomes tractable \[3\].

## 3.2 WS layer differentiation

Let _A=L(w_)+diag(_a_<sup>0</sup>+_a_<sup>1</sup>) and let \(\ell(x)\) be the loss. With \(\lambda=A^{-1}\partial\ell/\partial x\) one obtains, for every edge and pixel,

\(\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial w_e}=-(x_u-x_v)(\lambda_u-\lambda_v),\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial a_i^1}=\lambda_i(1-x_i),\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial a_i^0}=-\lambda_ix_i,\) (4)

and the chain rule through _w<sub>e</sub>=e_<sup>-_pc<sub>e</sub>_</sup> contributes a factor -_pw<sub>e</sub>_. This _adjoint solve_ therefore yields the gradient with respect to all costs, with memory independent of the solver’s depth. We term this the _implicit mode_. Alternatively, _T_ steps of Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradients are unrolled and differentiated by automatic differentiation. We term this the _unrolled mode_; this is what we deploy when the truncated solver is the WS layer, and _T_ then caps how far a boundary can move, since _T_ iterations propagate information at most in a radius of _T_ pixels. The binary experiments below use the unrolled mode with _T_=20 unless stated; the instance experiments use the implicit mode, solving to a relative residual of 10<sup>-4</sup> (at most 100 iterations) in training and 10<sup>-5</sup> at evaluation, so the radius argument does not restrict them. The two modes do not compute the same thing: at \(p\ge 20\), twenty unrolled steps give the exact gradient of the truncated solver, far from that of the RW (cosine 0.67-0.75 on the edge costs), while the implicit mode matches it to 10<sup>-3</sup> with 30-90 iterations and a constant 0.09GB per batch of 16. On an A100 the multi-marker solve costs 41+20_K_ms per batch of 16 256<sup>2</sup> images with _K_ markers each, and the exact limit 29-51ms per patch on one CPU core for _K_ up to 128 (Appendix F). On a regular grid all operators are dense shifted differences, so the WS layer is batched and can run on a GPU without sparse matrices; the markers of the multi-label form go in the batch dimension. The architectures of the trained models are drawn in Appendix C.The exact limit is evaluated on the CPU by the plateau algorithm of \[14\] with per-label solves on ambiguous plateaus; our implementation agrees with the authors’ reference code to single precision \[1, §B\].
