- **The instance loss did not teach either model to separate nuclei.** The seam is 0.09 % of the pixels; even at \(\times\)21 it is 2 % of the loss, and the U-Net’s output is indistinguishable from its BCE output at the pixel level (paired differences within \(\pm\)0.002), with a marginal instance gain (F1 0.663 vs 0.656 raw, 0.696 vs 0.683 filtered) and more spurious small components (count error 0.315 vs 0.275 raw). For the WS layer the effect was negative on everything: 0.01 IoU below its BCE self, 18 % soft pixels, and the learned edge head stopped being a contour detector: under this loss its costs are diffuse blobs inside the nuclei rather than lines along their boundaries (last column of `instance/figures/examples\_rw\_learned\_crop.png`), so the exact limit floods whole clusters (0.59 IoU, -0.108 against the converged solve). Asking the WS layer to put a 2-px background seam between two touching nuclei at p \(\le\) 50 is asking a diffusion to produce a line; the cost head would need a cost of order cmax across the seam and nothing elsewhere, and the gradient through 20 PCG steps did not find that solution. A seam loss on the cost head directly (an edge target), rather than on the WS layer output, is the obvious next thing to try, and was not tried here.<tbc>

- **Where the limit stands.** Under the boundary loss the exact PW limit improves on its BCE self (0.673 vs 0.661 IoU, 0.755 vs 0.711 BF<sub>2</sub>) and its price against the converged solve drops from 0.046 to 0.034 IoU and from 0.034 to 0.013 BF<sub>2</sub>, i.e. the sharper the trained solution, the less the limit loses. Its raw instance numbers are the speckle problem again (161 components per patch); filtered, they are no better than under BCE (F1 0.598 vs 0.618, count error 0.275 vs 0.246), so the boundary loss improved the limit’s pixels but not its objects.<tbc>

**- **Pets is the one place a gap opened, and it is small.** With the boundary loss both models gain 0.011-0.012 IoU and 0.045-0.054 BF<sub>2</sub> over BCE, and the converged WS layer is now above the U-Net on every metric (0.867 vs 0.864 IoU, 0.782 vs 0.769 BF<sub>2</sub>, 0.643 vs 0.629 BF<sub>1</sub>); the paired BF<sub>2</sub> advantage of the 20-step layer, +0.007, is significant but tiny, and the exact limit now matches the U-Net’s boundary scores (0.776 vs 0.769 BF<sub>2</sub>) at a 0.013 IoU cost, down from 0.03 under BCE. On large objects a loss that pays for boundary placement does let a spatial prior earn its keep; on 25-px nuclei it does not, which is consistent with section 6’s diagnosis (the WS layer is a smoother at p \(\le\) 50) and leaves the p / cost-scale question open.<tbc>
