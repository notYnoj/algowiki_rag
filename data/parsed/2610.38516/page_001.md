# The Power Watershed as a Differentiable Segmentation Layer

**Abstract.** Can a Watershed transform, the classical tool of morphological segmentation, be placed at the end of a deep network and trained through? The Power Watershed (PW) is the \(p\rightarrow\infty\) limit of the seeded Random Walker (RW) with edge weights raised to the power _p_. At every finite _p_ the RW solves a sparse positive-definite linear system and is exactly differentiable with respect to the edge weights and the unaries through one adjoint solve; its multi-label form costs one right-hand side per label, and its limit is the PW. We study this family as the last layer of a segmentation network, the watershed layer (WS layer). A conditioning study measures how the solve, the gradient and the distance to the limit evolve with _p_, and the implicit gradient is checked against unrolling. On synthetic data with correct edge costs the WS layer transforms a soft prediction into an exact boundary. On nuclei segmentation (PanNuke), a binary WS layer is level with a thresholded U-Net. With the instances as labels, the WS layer turns the U-Net into an instance segmenter whose number of labels is set per image by the markers rather than by the output layout, and the network is trained through that partition: instance _F_<sub>1</sub> rises from 0.656 to 0.795 over a U-Net with connected components, level with the same network followed by a tuned marker watershed. At the training temperature the partition is a watershed of the learned costs, and the finite temperature lets the solve hedge, discounting weak markers and sharing contested pixels. The hedging puts the WS layer 0.015-0.020 _F_<sub>1</sub> ahead of the baseline when trained on a tenth of the data, at the price of having to deploy the finite-p solve rather than its exact limit at inference.  and the full experimental record are released. **Keywords:** Morphological segmentation \(\cdot\) Random Walker \(\cdot\) differentiable segmentation \(\cdot\) instance segmentation \(\cdot\) deep unrolling.
