_What is the "diagonal rescaling" of the reference solve, and is it an estimate of the largest eigenvalue?_ No; the two uses of the diagonal are different. The reference solve of Sect. 4.1 factorises the Jacobi-scaled matrix _SAS_ with _S_=diag(_A_)<sup>-1/2</sup>, so that every diagonal entry of the scaled matrix is one, solves, maps back with _S_, and then runs two steps of iterative refinement on the original system. It is needed because at large _p_ the diagonal entries of _A_ are sums of weights _e_<sup>-_pc_</sup> that span many orders of magnitude from one pixel to the next, and a sparse LU of the raw matrix loses its pivots to underflow or cancellation; scaling each row and column by the inverse square root of its diagonal equalises the magnitudes, and it is the near-optimal diagonal scaling for the condition number (van der Sluis). The largest diagonal entry appears instead in the bound of Sect. 3: for a symmetric positive definite matrix, \(\lambda_\mathrm{max}\) is at least every diagonal entry (the Rayleigh quotient of a unit vector), which with the upper bound on \(\lambda_\mathrm{min}\) from a pixel whose four edges carry the highest cost gives \(\kappa(A)\unknown e^{p\Delta c}/4\). So the diagonal bounds the condition number in one place and improves it in the other.

_Why does the binary WS layer not beat a thresholded U-Net?_ Because a U-Net trained with cross-entropy for 30 epochs learns a similar sharpening by itself. Inside the trained model the layer does what it should (its own logits are speckle, 350 components per patch, which the layer cleans up to the true 24), but the two- stage system is not better than the one-stage one, on Pet or on PanNuke, under a plain cross-entropy or under boundary-weighted, seam-weighted or Dice-band losses (the record’s Pet and PanNuke binary sections). Two things did matter: an edge target on the cost head (Sect. 4.3) and training in the implicit mode, i.e. with the true Random Walker gradient, which lifts the binary layer to the U-Net’s pixel scores (Appendix F). Twenty unrolled conjugate-gradient steps give the exact gradient of the truncated solver, not of the Random Walker, at \(p\ge 20\).
