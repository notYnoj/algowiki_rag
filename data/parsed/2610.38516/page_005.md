#### Two difficulties.

The limit \(x^\infty\), seen as a function of the weight vector _w_, is locally constant: it changes only when the order of two edge weights flips, which happens on the finitely many hyperplanes where two weights are equal. Away from those ties \(\partial x^\infty/\partial w\) exists and is zero, and on them it is undefined. As _p_ grows, _x<sup>p</sup>_ inherits this behaviour: its gradient is well defined for essentially every weight configuration, but it concentrates on fewer and fewer edges and carries less and less information about which direction would improve the loss. At the same time the solve degrades: \(\lambda_\mathrm{max}(A)\) is at least the largest diagonal entry, hence at least _w_<sub>max</sub>, while \(\lambda_\mathrm{min}(A)\) is at most the weighted degree plus the unary of any pixel (the Rayleigh quotient of its indicator); if some pixel has its four edges at the highest cost, the condition number \(\kappa(A)=\lambda_\mathrm{max}(A)/\lambda_\mathrm{min}(A)\) satisfies \(\kappa(A)\unknown e^{p\Delta c}/4\), with \(\Delta c\) the spread of the edge costs. What matters is therefore \(p\Delta c\), not _p_ alone. With continuous learned costs ties are rare, and the limit reduces to a maximum spanning forest: the plateau solves that distinguish the PW matter only for quantised costs. Section 4.1 measures both these effects.

### 4 Experiments

All code, raw results and the extended version of this article [1] are publicly available<sup>6</sup>. The metrics we use are: intersection over union (IoU), boundary F-score at a pixel tolerance (BF<sub>_t_</sub>), the fraction of pixels with potential in (0.1,0.9) (soft pixels), and, for instance segmentation, F<sub>1</sub> of the matches at IoU 0.5, panoptic quality (PQ) [25], aggregated Jaccard index (AJI) and the relative object-count error. On PanNuke we also report a label-free edge-alignment score, the fraction of predicted boundary pixels within one pixel of a strong colour edge, since the dataset’s semi-automatic contours bound what a boundary score can show. PQ is the product of the segmentation quality SQ, the mean IoU of the matched objects, and the detection quality DQ, an object-level F<sub>1</sub>; it penalises merged and split objects alike.
