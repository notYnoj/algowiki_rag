**The variant.** From the scheduled epochs on (`--mk-sched 10 15`, as in 11.5) the training markers are not the fold-3 markers of section 11.1 but permissive candidates: every h-maximum of the predicted distance map with prominence 0.1 above 0.1, grown down to 0.1, area 1 (`--mk-train-thr 0.1 --mk-train-h 0.1 --mk-train-min-area 1`; about 40 candidates per training image, of which 17-21 in background, against 25 markers per patch in 11.5). Every candidate k gets a strength \(\sigma\text{\_}k=\text{sigmoid}((z\text{\_}k-\theta)/\mathrm{T})\), z\_k a score of the marker logit over the candidate and \(\theta\), T two learned scalars of the model (initialised at -1 and 1), and the strength multiplies the foreground probability inside the candidate's attachment:

`a\_k,i = ($\backslashsigma$\text{\_}k\text{\_}s\text{\_}i)^p` `on the candidate’s pixels, i.e. c1\_i $\backslashto$ c1\_i $-$ log $\backslashsigma$\text{\_}k`.

The strength enters in cost units, like every other cost of the WS layer. A plain multiplier \(\sigma\text{\_}k\) on a\_k would shift the cost by only \(-log\sigma\text{\_}k/\mathrm{p}\), which at p = 20 cannot veto a marker inside a nucleus, where the background attachment (1 - s)^p is about e^-160. The cross-entropy target of a candidate follows the owner rule of 11.5 (the nucleus holding the majority of its pixels) with one change, `--ds-multi primary`: of the candidates a nucleus owns, only the one with the largest overlap is its target. A duplicate is then wrong wherever it wins and is pushed towards zero strength, exactly like a candidate in background, instead of sharing the nucleus as the 11.5 sum rule allowed. Unmarked nuclei go to the background label (`--mk-unmarked bg`), the ground-truth markers of the early epochs carry no strength (\(\sigma=1\)), and the rest of the recipe is 11.5's (`--mk-head dist --w-mk 2`, p 5 \(\rightarrow\) 20, edge target, 30 epochs, seed 0, fold 1 \(\rightarrow\) fold 2).
