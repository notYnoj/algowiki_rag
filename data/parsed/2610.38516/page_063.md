**–** **The solver is a watershed.** With the WS layer's own markers and the WS layer's own output foreground, a marker watershed of the WS layer's contour map reproduces the converged solve on every metric to three digits (differences +0.000 with intervals of width 0.000 on 2523 patches): at p = 20 with the edge target's sharp costs, the solve assigns every pixel to the marker that the steepest path of the contour map reaches first, which is what the watershed does, as section 11.1's 0.008 F1 gap between the solve and the exact Power-Watershed limit already suggested. With the WS layer's markers inside this model's foreground the watershed is -0.010 F1 / -0.002 PQ and +0.019 IoU / +0.018 AJI against the solve: the markers decide the detections, the foreground decides the pixels, and the assignment step is immaterial. The reverse cell confirms it from the other side: this model's markers (compact cores above d = 0.5) through the WS layer's solve lose 0.058 F1 / 0.075 PQ to the same markers through the watershed, because the WS layer's degenerate foreground head gives the background label a strong attachment inside the nuclei and the compact markers do not reach far enough to win the rim; the WS layer only works with markers grown down to d = 0.2 that already cover most of each nucleus. The relief of the watershed is immaterial too (the contour map, -d and their sum agree to 0.001): the markers grown by a watershed of the distance map already decide every split between touching nuclei, and the final watershed only distributes the rim below the threshold.<tbc>
