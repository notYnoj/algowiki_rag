The disagreement is the fraction of all pixels whose label differs from the limit’s, and in brackets the same count divided by the pixels that are foreground in either output.

**The WS layer’s PCG does not reach large p, and neither does sparse LU, so the finite-p curve needs its own solver.** On 256 evenly spaced patches the WS layer’s own converged solve (Jacobi-PCG, tol 1e-5, at most 1000 iterations, as in every evaluation) was run in fp32 and fp64 and compared with the exact solve. For the instance model it is exact up to the training p: 0 pixels differ at p \(\le\) 10, 0.015 % at p = 20, where it takes 189 iterations on average (435 at most), and every fp64 system converges. In fp32 at p = 20 the true residual stalls around 1e-3 (rounding: the recursive residual meets the tolerance, the true one cannot), with no effect on the labels. From p = 35 it degrades (0.5 % of the pixels differ), and from p = 100 it fails in both precisions: it stops at the iteration cap, true residuals reach 1e5-1e8, 5-12 % of the pixels are wrong, and F1 is 0.27-0.53 instead of 0.72 at p = 200-500. fp32 additionally floors 6-20 % of the edge weights at 1e-30. The binary models fare better because their edge costs are \(\le\) 1: `rw\_learned`’s PCG is exact to 0.001 % of the pixels up to p = 200 and wrong on 0.44 % at p = 500. For `edge\_bce`, 0.8-3.3 % of the pixels are wrong at p = 100-500, and since the thresholded output is then speckled, its F1 collapses (0.24 at p = 100 against 0.53). A diagonally rescaled sparse LU (scipy, fp64) agrees with the exact solve up to p = 100 (4 pixels over 32 patches differ there), but beyond that it loses the pivots to cancellation (d\_a-w\_ai<sup>2</sup>/d\_i of two nearly equal numbers): at p = 500 it returns negative probabilities and labels that depend on the ordering. `rw\_exact.ExactRW` solves the same system by Gaussian elimination in the Grassmann-Taksar-Heyman form, on a nested-dissection order of the grid: eliminating node i adds w\_ai w\_ib / d\_i to the remaining weights and w\_ai e\_i / d\_i to the attachment (excess) of a, and the pivot is never updated by subtraction but recomputed as the sum of the current weights and excess. Every operation is between positive numbers, so the result is accurate to rounding whatever the conditioning. The weights are held as logarithms (logadexp), and so are the label vectors of the substitution (a log scale per node), so nothing underflows. The symbolic factorisation depends on the grid only (2.6 M entries at 256<sup>2</sup>, 12.5 M at 512<sup>2</sup>), and a solve takes 1.3-1.9 s on one core at any p. Checks: \(\Sigma\)\_k x\_k = 1 to 1e-13 at every p. Its labels are identical to the WS layer’s PCG wherever the PCG converges (0 pixels at p \(\le\) 10 on the 256 patches, 0.015 % at p = 20), and to the sparse LU on 32 patches per model at every p \(\le\) 100 (4 pixels over 32 patches differ at p = 100 for the instance model, where the LU is at the edge of its accuracy). The disagreement with `pw\_limit\_multi\_numpy` falls to 0 pixels at p = 3000 on the three patches tried, which also confirms the limit code independently of section 12.
