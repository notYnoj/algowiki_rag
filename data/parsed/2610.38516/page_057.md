### P.1 Better markers: a tuned threshold, a distance head, and a higher solver cap

Added 18-19 October 2026 (side session). Section 11 ended with the markers as the bottleneck: the same trained costs give F1 0.959 from the ground- truth markers and 0.681 from the marker head’s. This section tries the two things it named, as options of `experiments/exp\_instance\_pannuke.py` (the defaults reproduce the section 11 run): `--mk-head dist` (a HoVer-Net-style distance head), `--tune-fold F` (the marker threshold, minimum marker area and, for the distance head, the prominence of the maxima chosen by PQ on a held-out fold, never on fold 2), `--load` (re-evaluate a saved model) and the existing `--maxit` of the training solve. `run\_instances\_11\_1.sh` launches the runs, `plot\_instances.py`\\-\\/-ref adds the section 11 run to every table, `compare\_instances.py` (numpy only) makes the cross-run tables. Results: `results/pannuke\_instances/{bce\_tuned, dist, dist\_ext, maxit300, maxit300\_tuned}/` (each with `figures/summary.md` holding all the tables of section 11 plus the tuning table, `run.log`, the per-image npz; the examples npz is kept for `dist` only), `results/pannuke\_instances/compare\_11\_1.md`.

**Choosing the post-processing on fold 3. --tune-fold 3** runs the trained model on 300 evenly spaced patches of fold 3 (neither the training nor the test fold, as the marker spacing of section 10.1), builds the markers for every setting of a grid, runs the converged WS layer from them and keeps the setting with the best PQ of the WS layer’s output; the whole table goes to the json and to the top of `summary.md`. For the BCE head of section 11 the grid is thr \(\in\{0.2\dots 0.7\}\times\) min area \(\in\{1,4,9,16\}\) px (24 settings; 50 min while sharing the GPU with a training run); for the distance head the same \(\times\) h \(\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2\}\) (72 settings, 41 min on a free GPU). The section 11 model was re-tuned this way without retraining (`bce\_tuned`: its saved `.pt` through `--eval-only --load`), to separate the effect of the threshold from the effect of the head.

**The distance head** (`dist`). Channel 3 of the U-Net predicts, through a sigmoid, the normalised distance transform of every instance: the Chebyshev distance to the instance boundary (the `label\_core\_distance` already used for the training markers, capped at 12 px) divided by the instance’s own maximum, so every nucleus is 1 at its centre and 0 at its border whatever its size, background 0; MSE loss with weight 2 in place of the BCE on eroded masks. Everything else is section 11’s recipe: the WS layer’s cross-entropy from the ground-truth markers eroded by 2-3 px, p 5 \(\rightarrow\) 20, the edge target, 30 epochs, the 100-iteration training solve. Test markers are the regional maxima of the predicted map with prominence at least h (`skimage.morphology.h\_maxima`, one seed per plateau, so saturation at 1 is harmless), grown by a watershed of the map down to the threshold thr, basins below the minimum area dropped: two touching nuclei whose maxima are separated by a valley deeper than h get two markers even when the valley is above thr.
