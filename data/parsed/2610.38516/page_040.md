with the sharp parametrisation moves: -0.005 IoU and -0.009 BF<sub>2</sub> instead of -0.012 and -0.021, and instance F1 0.678, PQ 0.548, count error 0.225 against the gap-target U-Net's 0.667 / 0.541 / 0.296 at the same threshold and without any post-processing (section 7's min-area-filtered, boundary-trained U-Net, F1 0.714, remains better). But the seam statistics say the gain is not the mechanism we wanted: the WS layer cuts no more seam pixels than the U-Net and a third fewer than its own logits, the network _lowered_ the edge-cost scale it was given rather than raising it, and the inner logits at p = 10 are far closer to a plain U-Net's output than at p = 50. At p ≈ 10 the unaries s^p are strong enough to anchor every moderately confident pixel, so the WS layer degenerates toward a mild, edge-aware smoothing of a U-Net that now does most of the work itself, and the result is a system that behaves like the U-Net plus a small denoiser: fewer spurious components (hence the instance numbers), slightly blurrier boundaries (hence BF<sub>1</sub>-BF<sub>3</sub> still below).<tbc>

**-** **The limit is lost at low p.** Costs learned at p = 10 or 20 mean nothing at p = \(\infty\): the exact PW on them scores 0.44-0.51 IoU and about 10 components per patch, because the network relied on unaries whose p-th power no longer saturates and on contours with holes that a maximum-spanning-forest path walks through. The train-at-finite-p / deploy-at-the-limit scheme of section 6 (-0.04 IoU at p = 50) and the finite-p gains above therefore pull in opposite directions on this data; one cannot have both with a single schedule. What is left untried, and would be needed before concluding that a cut-capable last layer cannot help on nuclei: supervising the cost head directly with the seam mask (an edge target rather than hoping the segmentation loss finds it), a loss that pays for the separation of instances rather than for pixels near seams, and a dataset with pixel-accurate boundaries where a 1-px seam is a real image feature.

## L Does a boundary- or instance-level loss open a gap? (PanNuke, Pets)

: `--loss {bce,boundary,instance}` in `experiments/exp\_unet\_seg.py`, `experiments/run\_losses.sh`, `experiments/compare\_losses.py` (tables, paired statistics, `losses.png`). Raw results, per-image metrics and figures: `results/pannuke\_losses/{boundary,inst` and `results/pets\_losses/boundary/`.

**Hypothesis.** Sections 3 and 6 trained everything with a per-pixel BCE, and under that loss the plain U-Net learned to sharpen its own output as well as the WS layer does. A loss that pays for boundary placement or for separating neighbouring objects should favour a model with an explicit spatial prior, if the WS layer is to be worth anything. Both losses are applied to the final output of _both_ variants (for `rw\_learned` the same loss on the WS layer output, plus 0.5 times the same loss on the logits, as the aux BCE before), so the comparison stays fair; everything else is the recipe of section 6 (fold \(1\rightarrow\) fold 2, 30 epochs, p annealed \(5\rightarrow 50\), K = 20).
