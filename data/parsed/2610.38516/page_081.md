- **Where the 10 % gain comes from: detection under noisy markers, not boundaries.** The matched nuclei are segmented equally well (SQ 0.811 against 0.813); the whole difference is DQ, i.e. F1. The marker columns say why. The baseline’s distance head is weaker at 10 % and its fold-3 tuning answers by raising the marker threshold to 0.5 (minimum area 9, h 0.3), which leaves 24.5 % of the nuclei of fold 2 unmarked (21.8 % at full data): on fold 3 the best setting at the WS layer’s threshold 0.2 marks more nuclei (19 % unmarked) but costs 0.014 PQ, because every extra marker the watershed is given becomes an instance, and keeping the unmarked components as instances (`ws\_fb`) is worse still (F1 0.725). The WS layer keeps threshold 0.2 at every size, marks 84-86 % of the nuclei with 4.3-4.9 background markers per patch, and the solve trained on its own markers (section 11.5) absorbs the spurious ones through the unary costs instead of growing them, so its recall does not collapse with the head.
