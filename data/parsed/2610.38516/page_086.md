12.7 % for 11.5 and 21.8 % for the watershed baseline. At 10 % of the fold it is level with the 11.5 recipe (F1 -0.001, PQ 0.000, intervals across zero) and keeps that recipe's +0.019 F1 over its baseline: neither gain nor loss where the head is weak anyway.<tbc>

- **The selection was not learned; the detector was damaged.** Three facts. (1) The learned strengths barely discriminate: on fold 2 every candidate has \(\sigma\ge 0.5\) (background 0.64, primary 0.69, duplicate 0.68 on average), so the cut \(\tau\le 0.5\) removes nothing (`layer\_all` equals `layer` to every digit and the fold-3 table is flat in \(\tau\) up to 0.5, then collapses at 0.7). The mean background strength is weaker than the mean primary's by only a factor (0.64 / 0.69)^20≈0.2 in the attachment. (2) The ranking the strengths could use is no better than that of a head trained on its own target: AUC 0.876 against 0.867 for `dist\_sched10\_bg` and 0.874 for `unet\_dist` (primary vs background), 0.666 against 0.707 and 0.715 (primary vs duplicate). (3) The gradient that reaches the marker logits through the solve made the head a worse detector: three times as many background candidates (19.9 against 6.6 per patch at thr 0.1), a fifth fewer primaries (17.7 against 21.5), MSE to its distance target 0.022 against 0.008. What pushes the true nuclei's maps down was not isolated. A plausible route, unverified: a candidate grown down to 0.1 over two touching nuclei is the primary of one and wrong over the other, so the loss pushes the whole merged candidate down. Three of these facts point the same way. The partition cross-entropy is a weak and partly misdirected detection signal. It can tell the solve to give a spurious marker fewer pixels, which the 11.5 recipe already does through the unary costs (the variant's solve absorbs 12.6 background candidates per patch at a count error of 0.22). It cannot tell the marker head where a nucleus is that no candidate covers, since an unmarked nucleus sends no gradient to the head.<tbc>

- **The strengths live in the finite-p softness, which the limit removes.** The exact Power Watershed of the same costs and strengths gives F1 0.115 on the 307 limit patches (11.5: 0.700). At p \(\rightarrow\infty\) the shift -log \(\sigma\text{\_}\mathrm{k}\approx 0.4\) cost units turns into a hard threshold: a marker loses its whole nucleus to the background seed as soon as a path through the nucleus reaches a pixel whose background cost is below c1 + 0.4 without crossing a higher edge cost, where at p = 20 the shift only rescales the attachment by \(\sigma^\text{^}\mathrm{p}\). Deploying this variant at the limit is not possible. The oracle with ground-truth markers rises to 0.931 (11.5: 0.872), for a related reason: ground-truth markers carry \(\sigma=1\), i.e. attachments e^7 times stronger than any marker the trained solve has seen, so they override the background as the section 11 oracle did. It is not comparable with the other oracles.<tbc>

**- **What this says about the plan.** Within this design (attachment-level strengths fed by the marker head's score, trained only by the partition cross- entropy), putting marker selection in the gradient path does not let end-to-end training through the WS layer beat the U-Net + watershed pipeline; it hurts at full data and is neutral at 10 %. The bottleneck stays detection (which nuclei get a marker at all), and the WS layer's gradient is the wrong signal for it. For the paper, the statement it supports: _making marker selection_
