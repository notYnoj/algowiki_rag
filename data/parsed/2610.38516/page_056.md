- **The learned costs are what the WS layer contributes, and a classical watershed cannot use them as well.** The same predicted markers through the skimage marker watershed of the learned contour map inside s \> 0.5 give F1 0.597 (-0.084 paired), partly because that mask is the degenerate foreground head (723 components per patch, F1 0.024, as in section 6’s division of labour: with the background attachment taking c0 =-log(1-s) the network only needs s as a soft prior and the 0.5 auxiliary BCE did not stop it). The marker-controlled watershed with image-gradient costs, exact (0.335 F1) or at finite p (0.355), fails on H&E as `rw\_img` did in section 6: the colour gradient is not the nucleus boundary. So the classical control is far below, and the margin over it is the learned cost head plus the soft background seed, not the solver.<tbc>

- **The limit is usable again, at p = 20.** The exact K-label Power Watershed of the learned costs is within 0.008 IoU and 0.013 BF<sub>2</sub> of the converged finite- p solve on the 307 patches, with +0.007 F1 and -0.003 PQ, where section 6.1’s binary model trained at p \(\le\) 20 lost 0.2 IoU at the limit. Two reasons: the cross-entropy over markers trains the costs to put a cut between every pair of neighbouring markers, which is exactly what a maximum spanning forest needs, and the background is a soft seed (a\_0 = (1-s)^p) rather than a competing unary that has to saturate. So deploy-at-the-limit costs 0.7 s per patch on one CPU core against 224 ms per image on the GPU for the converged solve, and gives the same instances. One training caveat: the training solve hit its 100-iteration cap from epoch 9 (p ≈ 8) on, so two thirds of the training ran on a forward solve above the 1e-4 residual and an implicit gradient of an approximately solved system; the validation curves are flat from epoch 10, which is consistent with a converged model but was not checked with a higher cap. The edge scale went from 8 to 2.0 again (section 6.1).<tbc>

**- **What this says about the plan.** The multi-marker WS layer is the right object: one solve for any number of nuclei, exact limit, no post-processing hyper-parameter, and a margin of +0.02 F1 / PQ and +0.16 AJI over the pipeline a practitioner would build from the same U-Net. It does not beat a U-Net that was given a contour head and a tuned watershed, and the oracle says the reason is marker quality, which is a detection problem the WS layer does not touch.
