<tbc>smaller than average (median 107 px against 190). Under the pixel MSE a small nucleus hardly weighs, so `--w-peak` adds a per-nucleus term: the maximum of the predicted map over every ground-truth instance should reach the target’s, loss = mean over instances of (max target - max prediction)\(_+^2\), weight 1, the MSE kept. The first form, the raw maximum (`dist\_peak`), is a failure of a kind worth recording: its gradient reaches one pixel per nucleus per step, and after 30 epochs the map carries needle-shaped maxima everywhere (on 16 fold-3 patches 200 regional maxima per patch at h 0.2 against 24 for `dist`, 144 at h 0.3; the mean map and its area above 0.2 are unchanged), so the h-maxima rule produces hundreds of markers, the tuning solves spent 1516 s on their first batch and the run was stopped (`results/pannuke\_instances/\\dist\_peak/README.md`). The second form (`dist\_peak5`, `--peak-pool 5`) takes both maxima on the \(5\times 5\) mean-pooled maps, so a nucleus must carry a plateau; it trains normally (peak term 0.0034 at the end, 283 s per epoch) and its untuned validation markers looked like the answer: 89 % of the nuclei with exactly one marker and 4 % with none at epoch 29, against 80 / 18 % for `dist`. The baseline got the same term (`unet\_dist\_peak5`, peak 0.0030). Both were tuned on fold 3 as before (the WS layer’s tuning took 6605 s against 2438 s: more markers per solve).

**Results on fold 2 for the peak-loss runs** (seed 0, the rows of section 11.1 / 11.2 for reference):

Paired on fold 2: `dist\_peak5` - `dist` -0.090 F1 \[-0.095, -0.085\], -0.083 PQ, -0.033 IoU; `unet\_dist\_peak5` - `unet\_dist` -0.028 F1 \[-0.031, -0.025\], -0.029 PQ, +0.006 IoU; `dist\_peak5` - `unet\_dist\_peak5` -0.065 F1, -0.067 PQ, -0.051 IoU; the oracles are unchanged (`dist\_peak5` oracle - `dist` oracle -0.001 F1). On fold 3 the tuning already said so: the best PQ of the `dist\_peak5` layer over the 72 settings is 0.560 against 0.646 for `dist`, and of `unet\_dist\_peak5` 0.632 against 0.662.

**- **Honest outcome: section 11.2 holds on every seed and fold pair.** Across five configurations the baseline is level or ahead of the WS layer on F1 (+0.003 to +0.011, every interval at or above zero), ahead on PQ (+0.012 to +0.023) and IoU (+0.009 to +0.020), and the WS layer’s advantage with ground-truth markers is a stable +0.09 to +0.10 F1. The spread between seeds (0.002 F1, 0.001 PQ for the WS layer; 0.006 F1, 0.002 PQ for the baseline) and between fold pairs (0.004 F1, 0.005 PQ) is smaller than the baseline-minus-layer difference on every configuration, so the one-seed caveat of sections 11.1 and 11.2 is lifted: the distance-head U-Net with a watershed is at least as good a nucleus instance segmenter as the WS layer with the same head, and slightly better on PQ, and the WS layer’s instances remain 0.01-0.02 IoU short of the thresholded foreground on every fold.
