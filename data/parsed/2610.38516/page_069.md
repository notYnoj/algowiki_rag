- **Hard negatives fail for a reason that is specific to the pooled design, and instructive.** They do what they are asked, background markers fall from 10.0 to 1.5 per patch, but the nuclei without a marker come back (25 %) and the WS layer’s foreground IoU falls to 0.600, the lowest of any full-set output since section 6. The negatives are taken on the \(5\times 5\) mean-pooled map over the pixels outside every instance, so a background pixel next to a nucleus carries nuclear pixels in its window and is pushed down together with the nucleus’s edge, in exactly the band the WS layer’s markers are grown into (thr 0.2); the peak term pulls the other way inside, and the fold-3 tuning ends at thr 0.7 with small markers and a shrunken foreground. A background eroded by the pooling radius would remove the conflict; whether it would also remove the false markers the peak loss creates (which sit on nucleus-like stroma, not next to nuclei) is open, and was not run.<tbc>

**-** **The WS layer pays for every spurious marker, the watershed does not.** The spread between the two models is the same lesson as section 11.3: with the same head and the same loss, the baseline loses a third to a quarter of what the WS layer loses (-0.026 against -0.074 with the gradient term, -0.019 against -0.132 with all three), because the watershed’s foreground mask and minimum area absorb markers that the solve must turn into instances. A marker head good enough to help the WS layer would have to be more precise than the watershed needs.<tbc>

**-** **What this says about the plan.** Two rounds (11.3, 11.4) of objectives on the distance head, six variants, have not moved the WS layer’s markers past the MSE-trained map of section 11.1, and the one neutral change (the gradient term) is neutral for the baseline too. The lever is not the loss on the map but what the costs are trained with: the solve has only ever been trained from ground-truth markers (section 11’s recipe), so it has never learned to confine a spurious label or to do without a missing one, and at test time it meets both. Training the solve from the predicted markers for part of the schedule, with the cross-entropy still against the true instances, is the experiment that addresses the mismatch directly; it is the next one if this thread continues. Everything here remains one seed on one fold pair.
