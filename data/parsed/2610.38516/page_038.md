**- The label-noise caveat, measured.** The ground-truth boundaries lie within 1 px of a strong colour edge only 55.9 % of the time; every method’s output does so at least as often (U-Net 57.7 %, layer 57.9 %, limit 57.7 %). Either the labels are indeed a pixel or two off the visible edge, or nucleus boundaries on H&E are often not strong colour edges at all (cytoplasm and nucleus of similar hue). In both cases boundary F-scores at 1 px against these labels cannot separate a method that follows the image from one that follows the label noise, which is part of the reason the BF<sub>1</sub> gap (-0.023) is larger than the BF<sub>3</sub> gap (-0.012). The `rw\_img` limit shows the other side: it follows image edges 80 % of the time and is useless. A dataset with pixel-accurate manual boundaries (CoNSeP, MoNuSeg) is needed for the pixel-level boundary claim; the instance metrics above do not depend on the exact boundary and already tell the same story.<tbc>

**- **What the WS layer would need to help here.** Edge costs that are sharp and large where nuclei touch (a cost head trained with an explicit boundary target, or costs from the instance interfaces that the data provide), a target that leaves the interface pixels as background so that separating nuclei is rewarded, and a lower p, or a p-dependent cost scale, so that the WS layer stops being a smoother before it is a cutter. Section 6.1 tries the target, the lower p and the sharper parametrisation.

## K.1 Letting the WS layer cut: gap target, lower p, sharper edge costs

**What was changed.** Three of the four things section 6 asked for, together. (i) _Gap target_: the binary mask with the 2-px seam between touching nuclei set to background, for training, validation and test, for every variant, so that separating nuclei is rewarded instead of punished (the seam is 0.09 % of the pixels, so pixel metrics against the gap mask and against the binary mask are comparable; the U-Net of section 6 scores 0.717 against the gap mask instead of 0.719). The instance metrics are unchanged, and the "ground truth" ceiling row becomes the gap mask itself, F1 0.998. (ii) _Lower p_: \(5\rightarrow 20\) and \(2\rightarrow 10\) instead of \(5\rightarrow 50\), so that the unaries s^p stay soft and anchor the solution while a contour can still be a hard cut. (iii) _Sharper edge costs_: \(8\cdot\sigma(\mathrm{h})\) instead of \(\sigma(\mathrm{h})\), with the scale 8 learnable (initialised at the unary clamp c\_max = 8), so that the cost head can make a contour as expensive as a confident unary. Everything else is section 6's recipe (fold \(1\rightarrow\) fold 2, 30 epochs, K = 20, BCE + 0.5 aux BCE). Five runs: the U-Net under the gap target, and the learned-cost layer under the gap target at \(5\rightarrow 50\), at \(5\rightarrow 20\), at \(5\rightarrow 20\) with sharp edges and at \(2\rightarrow 10\) with sharp edges.
