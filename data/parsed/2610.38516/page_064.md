76.6 % of the nuclei with one marker, 21.8 % with none, 1.6 in background per patch, against the WS layer’s 83.9 / 14.5 % and 3.6. The detections come out the same (DQ 0.794 against 0.791) because the WS layer’s extra markers are mostly spurious ones; swapping the markers under the same watershed moves F1 by -0.014 in favour of this model’s set. Keeping the unmarked foreground components as instances recovers IoU (0.725) and AJI (0.666) but costs 0.014 F1 / 0.017 PQ, as the WS layer’s fallback markers did (-0.015 F1): the unmarked components are mostly merged clumps and fragments, not missed nuclei. On the sparse patches (fewer than 10 nuclei) the baseline is clearly ahead (F1 0.727 / count error 0.35 against 0.689 / 0.64: fewer background markers), on the crowded ones (more than 60) slightly behind (0.754 / 0.23 against 0.763 / 0.15, with 29 % of the nuclei unmarked against 22 %).<tbc>

**-** **The oracles are not comparable, and the remaining gap is still the markers.** With the ground-truth markers the watershed reaches F1 0.867 / PQ 0.708, +0.073 F1 over its predicted markers, where the WS layer’s oracle reaches 0.959: the watershed is confined to the predicted foreground, so a marker in a nucleus that s \> 0.5 missed yields nothing (count error 0.066 against the WS layer oracle’s 0.020), whereas the WS layer’s marker attachment overrides its foreground head. The 0.073 of this model’s oracle gap is the 21.8 % of the nuclei without a marker, as the 0.17 of the WS layer’s was its 14.5 %: the next step on either side is a detection objective for the distance head, not the assignment step.<tbc>

**-** **What this says about the plan.** On PanNuke, with a fair baseline on every count (the same backbone, the same three targets, post-processing tuned on a held-out fold like the WS layer’s markers), the multi-marker WS layer is not a better nucleus instance segmenter than a U-Net with a HoVer-Net-style head and a 16-ms watershed: it is 0.003 F1 behind, 0.013 PQ behind, six times slower at inference, and its foreground head degenerates. What the WS layer still offers is architectural (one differentiable module trained end to end from the instance labels, no post-processing threshold on the foreground, an exact limit within 0.01 F1), plus the one empirical fact this section adds, that at the p it trains to its solve _is_ the marker watershed of its learned contour map. The remaining caveats are those of sections 10.1 and 11.1: one seed, one fold pair, the baseline’s thresholds chosen on 300 patches of fold 3, and the foreground target of the two models identical here but different from section 10.1’s gap target (not re-run with `--gap`).
