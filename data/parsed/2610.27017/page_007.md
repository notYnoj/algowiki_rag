Combining the two gives (2.6).<br>
The Proof is completed.

With the above preparations, we obtain the generating function of the BTTB matrix sequence \(\{A_N\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\).

**Theorem 2.1.** Let \(\gamma_1,\gamma_2\in(1,2)\). Then _A<sub>N</sub>_ is the BTTB matrix given by \(A_N=T_n^{(2)}(f_{\gamma_1,\gamma_2})\) with

\(f_{\gamma_1,\gamma_2}(\theta_1,\theta_2)=f_{\gamma_1}(\theta_1)+\frac{\eta_2}{\eta_1}f_{\gamma_2}(\theta_2),\) (2.7)

where \(\eta_1=\sigma_{\gamma_1}\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x^{\gamma_1}},\eta_2=\sigma_{\gamma_2}\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta y^{\gamma_2}}\) , and \(f_\gamma(\theta)\) is defined in (2.6).

_Proof._ By the definition of _A<sub>N</sub>_, we have<tbc>

\(A_N=I_N\otimes T_{\gamma_1}+\frac{\eta_2}{\eta_1}T_{\gamma_2}\otimes I_N,\)

<tbc>where<tbc>

\(I_N\otimes T_{\gamma_1}=T_N(1)\otimes T_N(f_{\gamma_1})=T_n^{(2)}(\widetilde{f}_{\gamma_1}),\)<br>
\(T_{\gamma_2}\otimes I_N=T_N(f_{\gamma_2})\otimes T_N(1)=T_n^{(2)}(\widetilde{f}_{\gamma_2}).\)

<tbc>Moreover, the Fourier coefficients of \(\widetilde{f}_{\gamma_1}\) and \(\widetilde{f}_{\gamma_2}\) are given by<tbc>

\(\left(\widetilde{f}_{\gamma_1}\right)_{[k_1,k_2]}=\left\{\begin{array}{ c c }
(f_{\gamma_1})_{k_1}, & k_2=0, \\
0, & k_2\neq 0,
\end{array}\right.\text{and}\left(\widetilde{f}_{\gamma_2}\right)_{[k_1,k_2]}=\left\{\begin{array}{ c c }
(f_{\gamma_2})_{k_2}, & k_1=0, \\
0, & k_1\neq 0.
\end{array}\right.\)

<tbc>Hence, \(\widetilde{f}_{\gamma_1}(\theta_1,\theta_2)=f_{\gamma_1}(\theta_1)\) and \(\widetilde{f}_{\gamma_2}(\theta_1,\theta_2)=f_{\gamma_2}(\theta_2)\). Therefore, the generating function of \(\{A_N\}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\) is

\(f_{\gamma_1,\gamma_2}(\theta_1,\theta_2)=f_{\gamma_1}(\theta_1)+\frac{\eta_2}{\eta_1}f_{\gamma_2}(\theta_2).\)

The proof is completed.

**Remark 2.1.** If a bivariate function \(f(\theta_1,\theta_2)\) satisfies \(f(\theta_1,\theta_2)=f(|\theta_1|,|\theta_2|)\), then \(f(\theta_1,\theta_2)\) is called an even function. It is easy to verify that \(f_{\gamma_1,\gamma_2}(\theta_1,\theta_2)\) is a bivariate continuous real-valued even function.

For a real-valued continuous even function \(f(\theta_1,\theta_2)\), the associated BTTB matrix \(T_n^{(2)}(f)\) is real symmetric. To characterize the spectrum of the matrices generated by \(f_{\gamma_1,\gamma_2}(\theta_1,\theta_2)\), we recall the following standard result, which bounds the eigenvalues in terms of the minimum and maximum of the generating function:

**Lemma 2.3.** \[28\] Let \(f(\theta_1,\theta_2)\in C^1([-\pi,\pi]^2)\) be a real-valued even function , with its maximum and minimum denoted by _f_<sub>max</sub> and _f_<sub>min</sub>, respectively. Then \(T_n^{(2)}(f)\) is a real symmetric matrix, and its eigenvalues are bounded by the range of \(f(\theta_1,\theta_2)\) in the sense that

\(f_\mathrm{min}\le\lambda_\mathrm{min}\left(T_n^{(2)}(f)\right)\le\lambda_\mathrm{max}\left(T_n^{(2)}(f)\right)\le f_\mathrm{max}.\) (2.8)
