_Proof._ The existence of such a partition \((X',X'')\) is a basic fact in discrepancy theory \[Cha01\]. (A random partition works with high probability by a simple application of the Chernoff bound.) A deterministic construction of \((X',X'')\) can be obtained by the method of conditional expectation; see \[Cha01, Theorem 1.2\] for a good description.<sup>5</sup>

The above lemma implies the existence of a \((k,\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{k}))\)-halver: for each \(y\in[ku]\), we simply fix one subset \(S\subseteq X\) with \(|S|\le k\) and \(\sigma(S)=y\), if exists, and add it to the collection C, and then apply Lemma 2.3 to this collection. However, the construction time is too large (we would have to solve the subset sum problem in order to construct the collection C in the first place!). To improve the construction time, we worsen the additive error of our halver from \(\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{k})\) to \(\widetilde{O}(k/\sqrt{b})\) for a parameter \(b\le k\). We accomplish this by building a collection of small "canonical" subsets of sizes at most _b_, generated from Koiliaris and Xu’s algorithm, and then apply Lemma 2.3 to construct a halver for this collection:

**Lemma 2.4** (Canonical subset generation)**.** _Given a set X of integers in_ \[_u_\] _and a number b, we can generate a collection_ C _of_ \(\widetilde{O}(b^2u)\) _"canonical" subsets of X each of size at most b, in_ \(\widetilde{O}(b^3u)\) _deterministic time, satisfying the following property:_

_For every subset_ \(S\subseteq X\)_, there exists a subset_ \(\hat{S}\subseteq X\) _such that_ \(|\hat{S}|=|S|\), \(\sigma(\hat{S})=\sigma(S)\)_, and_ \(\hat{S}\) _is expressible as a union of O_((|_S|/b_+1)log_u_) _disjoint canonical subsets in_ C.

_Proof._ Run the algorithm from the proof of Lemma 2.1 with _k=b_. For each dyadic interval \(I=(a,a+\ell]\), \(i\in[b]\), and \(y\in\Sigma_i(X\cap I)\), find one subset \(S_I[i,y]\subseteq X\cap I\) with |_S<sub>I</sub>_\[_i,y_\]|=_i_ and \(\sigma(S_I[i,y])=y\), and add the subset to the collection C. The number of such subsets is \(O(b^2\ell)\) per _I_ (since there are _b_ choices of _i_ and \(O(\ell b)\) choices of \(y\in(ai,(a+\ell)i]\)), each of which can be generated in \(\widetilde{O}(b)\) time. Over all dyadic intervals _I_ (which have total length _O(u_log_u_)), the total number of subsets is \(\widetilde{O}(b^2u)\).

To prove that C satisfies the stated property, consider an arbitrary subset \(S\subseteq X\). First partition (0_,u_\] into a collection I(_S_) of disjoint dyadic intervals, each containing at most _b_ points of _S_: namely, we start by visiting the root dyadic interval, and when we visit a dyadic interval containing more than _b_ points of _S_, we recursively visit its two child intervals. The number of dyadic intervals visited per level is _O_(|_S|/b_+1). Thus, the number of dyadic intervals generated is \(|\mathcal{I}(S)|\le O((|S|/b+1)logu)\). For each interval \(I\in\mathcal{I}(S)\), let \(i_I=|S\cap I|\in[b]\) and \(y_I=\sigma(S\cap I)\in\Sigma_i(X\cap I)\). Define \(\hat{S}\) to be the union of _S<sub>I</sub>_\[_i<sub>I</sub>,y<sub>I</sub>_\] over all \(I\in\mathcal{I}(S)\); these sets \(S_I[i_I,y_I]\subseteq X\cap I\) are obviously disjoint because the intervals in I(_S_) are disjoint; and \(|S_I[i_I,y_I]|=|S\cap I|\) and \(\sigma(S_I[i_I,y_I])=\sigma(S\cap I)\). Therefore, \(|\hat{S}|=|S|\) and \(\sigma(\hat{S})=\sigma(S)\).

**Lemma 2.5** (Fast halver construction)**.** _Given a set X of integers in_ \[_u_\] _and_ \(b\le k\)_, we can compute a_ \((k,\widetilde{O}(k/\sqrt{b}))\)_-halver of X in_ \(\widetilde{O}(b^3u)\) _deterministic time._

_Proof._ Apply Lemma 2.4 to construct a collection C of \(\widetilde{O}(b^2u)\) canonical subsets in \(\widetilde{O}(b^3u)\) time. Then apply Lemma 2.3 to this collection C, with _k_ replaced by _b_, to compute a partition \((X',X'')\) in \(\widetilde{O}(b^3u)\) time.

To prove that \((X',X'')\) satisfies the \((k,\widetilde{O}(k/\sqrt{b}))\)-halver property, consider an arbitrary subset \(S\subseteq X\) with \(|S|\le k\). Let \(\hat{S}\subseteq X\) be such that \(|\hat{S}|=|S|\le k\), \(\sigma(\hat{S})=\sigma(S)\), and \(\hat{S}\) is expressible as a union of _O_((_k/b_)log_u_) disjoint canonical subsets \(S_j\in\mathcal{C}\). We have \(|S_j\cap X'|,|S_j\cap X''|\le|S_j|/2+\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{b})\) for each such subset _S<sub>j</sub>_. Summing over _S<sub>j</sub>_ gives \(|\hat{S}\cap X'|,|\hat{S}\cap X''|\le|\hat{S}|/2+\widetilde{O}(k/b\cdot\sqrt{b})\).

<sup>5</sup>The time bound stated there was _O_(|_X_||C|), but because all our subsets have sizes at most _k_, the running time of the algorithm is actually _O(k_|C|+|_X_|).
