_Proof._ The proposition holds trivially for _n_=1, since then the sole solution point must lie either in the top-left or in the bottom-right corner of the sole demand (_p_<sub>0</sub>_,p_<sub>1</sub>). For the case of a contradiction, assume the proposition is not true for all \(n\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{1\}\) and fix the smallest _n_ for which this is the case. So there is a triangular instance \((P_\Delta,D_\Delta)\) of size _n_ as well as a corresponding solution \(Q_\Delta\) of size _n_ that contains a non-grid point \(q\in Q_\Delta\backslash G_\Delta\). Without loss of generality, assume _p_<sub>0</sub>=(0,0).

Depending on the position of _q_, transform \((P_\Delta,D_\Delta)\) into a suitable triangular instance \((P'_\Delta,D'_\Delta)\) of size _n_-1 with a corresponding solution \(Q'_\Delta\) of size at most _n_-1 containing a non-grid point. Once this is achieved, we immediately get a contradiction to the minimality of _n_.

Consider first the case \(x(q)\unknown x(p_1)\). We transform \((P_\Delta,D_\Delta)\) into \((P'_\Delta,D'_\Delta)\) by removing both the input point _p_<sub>1</sub> and the demand (_p_<sub>0</sub>_,p_<sub>1</sub>) and by projecting _p_<sub>0</sub> to the _x_-coordinate _x(p_<sub>1</sub>) (not changing its _y_-coordinate). To construct \(Q'_\Delta\) from \(Q_\Delta\) we project all points with _x_-coordinate at most _x(p_<sub>1</sub>) to _x_-coordinate _x(p_<sub>1</sub>) and remove any points with _y_-coordinate _\>y(p_<sub>2</sub>) (which cannot help to connect _p_<sub>0</sub> to any of _p_<sub>2</sub>_,p_<sub>3</sub>_,...,p<sub>n</sub>_). One can easily check that \(Q'_\Delta\) is a solution for \((P'_\Delta,D'_\Delta)\): Any M-path going through one of the projected points remains intact, as the projected point is still reachable from the projection of _p_<sub>0</sub> (by going straight up). Moreover, \(|Q'_\Delta|<|Q_\Delta|\), since we either removed the top-left grid point if it was in \(Q_\Delta\) (since it has _y_-coordinate _y(p_<sub>1</sub>)_\>y(p_<sub>2</sub>)) or at least two of the projected points had the same _y_-coordinate (or there could not be a M-path from _p_<sub>0</sub> to _p_<sub>1</sub> in \(Q_\Delta\)), causing them to get merged during the projection. Note that \(q\in Q'_\Delta\), since this case assumes \(x(q)\unknown x(p_1)\), such that _q_ is not affected by the projection. As stated above, we get a contradiction to the minimality of _n_.

The case \(y(q)\unknown y(p_n)\) yields the same contradiction via a symmetrical argument. Thus, it remains to consider the case that _q_ and all other non-grid points have _x_-coordinate _\<x(p_<sub>1</sub>) and _y_- coordinate _\<y(p<sub>n</sub>_). Then there must we some M-path that leaves either the _x_- or the _y_-axis at some non-grid point \(q_1\in Q_\Delta\) to reach another non-grid point \(q_2\in Q_\Delta\). We consider only the case that _q_<sub>1</sub> lies on the _y_-axis; the other case is proven symmetrically. We transform \((P_\Delta,D_\Delta)\) into \((P'_\Delta,D'_\Delta)\) using the same construction as above (projecting everything left of _p_<sub>1</sub> onto _x(p_<sub>1</sub>)). This causes _q_<sub>1</sub> and _q_<sub>2</sub> to merge (giving \(|Q'_\Delta|<|Q_\Delta|\)) and ensures the existence of a non-grid point in \(Q'_\Delta\) (the projection of _q_<sub>1</sub> and _q_<sub>2</sub>). Again, as stated above, this yields a contradiction to the minimality of _n_.
