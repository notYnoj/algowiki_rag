As an immediate consequence, any M-path that satisfies an _SC_ demand (_S,c<sub>j</sub>_) must go through one of the variable points _x<sub>i</sub>_ (note that no \(q\in Q_\phi\backslash Q_{SX}\) can share an _x_- or _y_-coordinate with _S_, since none of the demands \(d\in D_\overline{SC}\backslash D_{SX}\) would cover such a _q_).

Next, remember that Proposition 17 restricts _Q<sub>con</sub>_ to the corners of _D<sub>con</sub>_. This implies that a M-path entering _GX<sub>i</sub>_ through _x<sub>i</sub>_ cannot immediately leave _GX<sub>i</sub>_ but must first go through the inside of _GX<sub>i</sub>_. Otherwise, there would be some \(q\in Q_\phi\) outside of _GX<sub>i</sub>_ either with _x(q_)=_x(x<sub>i</sub>_) and _y(q)\>y(x<sub>i</sub>_) or with _y(q_)=_y(x<sub>i</sub>_) and _x(q)\>x(x<sub>i</sub>_). This _q_ must be covered by some demand \(d\in D_\overline{SC}\), and our construction is such that this can only be done for a \(d\in D_{con}\) (see Figure 2). That is \(q\in Q_{con}\), and Proposition 17 then implies that _q_ lies in the corner of _d_. But no corner of a connection demand shares an _x_- or _y_-coordinate with _x<sub>i</sub>_ (see Figure 2), such that _q_ cannot exist.

A simple consequence of this is that any M-path entering variable _GX<sub>i</sub>_ (through _x<sub>i</sub>_) can leave _GX<sub>i</sub>_ only by entering one of the connection demands \((x_{ik}^+,p_{ij}^+)\) or \((x_{ik}^-,p_{ij}^-)\) (see Figure 3b) and must then follow the corresponding variable-clause path to _GC<sub>j</sub>_ (see Figure 2).

So in summary, any M-path from _S_ to a clause point _c<sub>j</sub>_ must enter some variable gadget _GX<sub>i</sub>_ at _x<sub>i</sub>_, which it then must leave either through a positive or negative variable-clause path to reach a connected clause gadget _GC<sub>j</sub>_. Note that _Q<sub>cl</sub>_ guarantees that this M-path actually reaches _c<sub>j</sub>_ (in fact, Proposition 16 implies a strict structure on _Q<sub>cl</sub>_, but we do not need this for our proof). In the next and final step, we argue about the structure of _Q<sub>var</sub>_, showing that _x<sub>i</sub>_ cannot be connected to both a positive and negative variable-clause path. Once this is shown, we can identify \(Q_\phi\) with the variable assignment that assigns _X<sub>i</sub>_=1 if and only if _x<sub>i</sub>_ is connected to positive variable-clause paths and _X<sub>i</sub>_=0 otherwise. This implies that \(Q_\phi\) is a boolean solution and, since \(Q_\phi\) satisfies all _SC_ demands, that \(\phi\) is satisfiable.

So consider _Q<sub>var</sub>_. For any \(i\in[n]\) there must be a dedicated \(q_i\in Q_{var}\) on the left or bottom side of the _XD_ demand \((x_i,d_i)\in D_{var}\). We consider only the case that _q<sub>i</sub>_ lies on the bottom side; the other case is symmetrical. Since (_x<sub>i</sub>,d<sub>i</sub>_) is the only non-_SC_ demand that covers _q<sub>i</sub>_, Corollary 7 implies that the remaining |_Q<sub>var</sub>_|-_n_ solution points from _Q<sub>var</sub>_ lie in _R(D<sub>var</sub>_\\_D<sub>XD</sub>_), where \(D_{XD}:=\{(x_i,d_i)|i\in[n]\}\). Thus, the inside of the rectangle formed by _x<sub>i</sub>_ and the point \((x(x_i^-),y(x_i^+))\) is empty (see Figure 3b). But then, the next point \(q'_i\) on the M-path coming from _x<sub>i</sub>_ through _q<sub>i</sub>_ must have \(y(q'_i)\ge y(x_i^+)\) or \(x(q'_i)\ge x(x_i^-)\). In the former case, \(q'_i\) (which is used by any M-path coming from _x<sub>i</sub>_) can reach only positive variable-clause paths, while in the latter case only negative variable clause-paths are reachable. As argued above, this finishes the lemma’s statement.

## E Sublogarithmic Approximation for MinGMConn

In this section, we present the algorithm underlying Theorem 3, that is, an algorithm with approximation ratio \(O(\sqrt{logn})\). The idea is to divide the plane into \(s\checkmark n\) many vertical strips, where _s_ can be thought of as a function that is sublinear in n. We call demands between points lying within the same strip _intra-strip demands_ and between points in different strips _inter-strip demands_. In order to satisfy the intra-strip demands, we recursively execute the algorithm (with fixed recursion parameter _s_) and exploit that demand pairs in different strips are independent. To satisfy the inter-strip demands, we project points to the adjacent strip boundaries. This results in an instance with \(s\checkmark n\) many distinct _x_ coordinates for which we can apply the algorithm HorizontalManhattan presented in the previous section.
