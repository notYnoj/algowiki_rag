If _n_ is odd,<tbc>

\(\kappa_n^{(+)}U^+(x_n)-\kappa_n^{(-)}U^-(x_n)=-K_0\Pi_O(x_n)+o(N^{-q}),\)

<tbc>and<tbc>

\(\kappa_n^{(+)}V^+(x_n)-\kappa_n^{(-)}V^-(x_n)=-K_0\Pi_O(x_n)+o(N^{-q}).\)

<tbc>Hence

\(\mathcal{Q}_n=\left\{\begin{array}{ c c }
-K_0(g_q^++g_q^-)\Pi_E(x_n)+o(N^{-q}), & n\text{even}, \\
K_0(g_q^++g_q^-)\Pi_O(x_n)+o(N^{-q}), & n\text{odd}.
\end{array}\right.\) (54)

Combining (53) and (54), and using (37), we obtain

\(\widetilde{F}_n=\left\{\begin{array}{ c c }
K_0(g_q^++g_q^-)x_n^{q+1}\prod_{s\in E}\left(1-\frac{n}{n_s}\right)+x_n\sigma_{n,N}, & n\text{even}, \\
-K_0(g_q^++g_q^-)x_n^{q+1}\prod_{s\in O}\left(1-\frac{n}{n_s}\right)+x_n\sigma_{n,N}, & n\text{odd},
\end{array}\right.\) (55)

where<tbc>

\(sup_{|n|>N}|\sigma_{n,N}|=o(N^{-q}).\)

<tbc>Here we used (37) and (5). Indeed,<tbc>

\(nx_n=\frac{2}{\pi}+O\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),\)

<tbc>and hence, uniformly for |_n|\>N_,<tbc>

\((x_n-x_{n_s})-x_n\left(1-\frac{n}{n_s}\right)=\frac{nx_n-n_sx_{n_s}}{n_s}=O(N^{-2}).\)

<tbc>It follows that

\(x_n\Pi_E(x_n)=x_n^{q+1}\prod_{s\in E}\left(1-\frac{n}{n_s}\right)+x_nO(N^{-q-1}),\)

and analogously for \(\Pi_O\).

The remainders in (55) are of the form \(x_n\sigma_{n,N}\), where<tbc>

\(sup_{|n|>N}|\sigma_{n,N}|=o(N^{-q}).\)

<tbc>Since<tbc>

\(\sum_{|n|>N}|x_n|^2=O(N^{-1}),\)

<tbc>we have

\(\sum_{|n|>N}|x_n\sigma_{n,N}|^2=o(N^{-2q-1}).\)

The squared sum of the leading terms in (55) is _O(N_<sup>-2_q_-1</sup>), so the corresponding cross terms are also _o(N_<sup>-2_q_-1</sup>) by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Thus only the leading terms in (55) contribute to the limit.

For the even indices, the Riemann-sum argument gives

\(N^{2q+1}\sum_{|n|>Nn\text{even}}|\widetilde{F}_n|^2\longrightarrow K_0^2(g_q^+-g_q^-)^2\frac{(2/\pi)^{2q+2}}{2}\int_{|u|>1}u^{-2q-2}\prod_{s\in E}\left(1-\frac{u}{c_s}\right)^2du.\)
