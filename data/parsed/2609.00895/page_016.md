and since \(\mathrm{deg}\Delta^\pm\le q-1\), the coefficients of _x<sup>q</sup>_ in \(\Phi^\pm\) are<tbc>

\(\Phi_q^+=-g_q^+,\Phi_q^-=-g_q^-.\)

<tbc>Hence the kernel element having the same leading coefficients as the error pair \((\Phi^+,\Phi^-)\) is<tbc>

\(-g_q^+(U^+,U^-)-g_q^-(V^+,V^-).\)

<tbc>This suggests the approximation

\(\Phi^\pm(x)\approx-g_q^+U^\pm(x)-g_q^-V^\pm(x),\)

which is made precise in Lemma 3.3 and Theorem 3.4 below.

The marginal distributions<tbc>

\((q_e,q_o)=(q+1,q-1)\text{or}(q_e,q_o)=(q-1,q+1)\)

<tbc>also give

\(\mathrm{dimker}\mathcal{E}=2,\)

but they do not satisfy the leading-order non-singularity condition (35). They will therefore be considered separately in Case B below.

**Case A: strict balance** _q<sub>e</sub>=q<sub>o</sub>=q_

**Lemma 3.2.** _Under the strict-balance condition q<sub>e</sub>=q<sub>o</sub>=q,_<tbc>

\(U^+(x)=\frac{1}{2}\big[\Pi_E(x)+\Pi_O(x)\big],U^-(x)=-\frac{1}{2}\big[\Pi_E(x)-\Pi_O(x)\big],V^+(x)=-\frac{1}{2}\big[\Pi_E(x)-\Pi_O(x)\big],V^-(x)=\frac{1}{2}\big[\Pi_E(x)+\Pi_O(x)\big].\text{(38)}\)

<tbc>_Proof._ For a kernel element with coefficients of _x<sup>q</sup>_ given by (_g_<sup>+</sup>_,g_<sup>-</sup>), the preceding characterization of kerE gives<tbc>

\(W_E=(g^+-g^-)\Pi_E,W_O=(-g^+-g^-)\Pi_O.\)

<tbc>For (_U_<sup>+</sup>_,U_<sup>-</sup>), we have (_g_<sup>+</sup>_,g_<sup>-</sup>)=(1,0), and hence<tbc>

\(W_E=\Pi_E,W_O=-\Pi_O.\)

<tbc>For (_V_<sup>+</sup>_,V_<sup>-</sup>), we have (_g_<sup>+</sup>_,g_<sup>-</sup>)=(0,1), and hence<tbc>

\(W_E=-\Pi_E,W_O=-\Pi_O.\)

<tbc>Using (36), equivalently

\(\Phi^+=\frac{W_E-W_O}{2},\Phi^-=-\frac{W_E+W_O}{2},\)

gives (38).
