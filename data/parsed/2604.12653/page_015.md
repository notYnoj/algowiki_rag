**Lemma 19.** _The amortized running time of_ access(_i_) _is O(d(i_)).

_Proof._ We first analyze the time spent updating the main tree and the number of coins created. We generalize the notion of bills to larger height differences: A pair _u,v_ of consecutive leaders has max(0,|height(_u_)-height(_v_)|-1) bills. By Lemma 14, the existing leader of leaf _i_ has height _O(d(i_)). Thus, deactivating the nodes on the root-to-leaf path of leaf _i_ takes _O(d(i_)) time and creates _O(d(i_)) bills and coins. Consider now three consecutive leaders _t,u,v_ with height(_u_)-height\((v)\ge 3\). We deactivate _u_ and the right child of _u_. This creates three new leaders _u_<sub>1</sub>_,u_<sub>2</sub>_,u_<sub>3</sub> with height(_u_<sub>1</sub>)=height(_u_)-1 and height(_u_<sub>2</sub>)=height(_u_<sub>3</sub>)=height(_u_)-2. Overall, the number of bills decreases by at least one. Indeed, the pair _u_<sub>3</sub>_,v_ has two fewer bills compared to the pair _u,v_, but the pair _t,u_<sub>1</sub> might have one more bill compared to _t,u_. We use this one bill to pay for the _O_(1) coins of _u_<sub>1</sub>_,u_<sub>2</sub>_,u_<sub>3</sub> and for the _O_(1) time spent.

This shows that the time spent updating the main tree and the number of coins created have an amortized cost of _O(d(i_)). Next, we show that the time spent updating the index trees is _O(k_) where _k_ is the number of newly created leaders. Then, this time is dominated by the time spent updating the main tree, and the lemma follows. Building the linked lists _N<sub>i</sub>_ takes constant time per newly created leader. To analyze the time spent splitting or joining the index trees, let _h_ be the maximum height of any newly created leader. On each of the index trees _L_<sub>0</sub>_,...,L_<sub>_O_(log_h_)</sub>, we perform a bulk deletion and a bulk insertion, involving _O(k_) leaders in total. By Lemma 15, each of these trees has height _O(h_). Moreover, the heights of these trees are bounded by a geometric series. Thus, the bulk operations take _O(k+h_) time in total. By invariant (3) and Lemma 16, there are at least _h/_2 newly created leaders, i.e. \(h\in O(k)\). This shows the lemma.
