• _if_ \(\{\{v_i^T,v_i^2\},\{v_i^F,v_i^1\}\}\subseteq M\)_, then_ \(bp(M)|_{G_{V_i}}=\{\{v_i^T,v_i^1\}\}\cup bp(M)|_{G_{F(v_i^1)}}\cup bp(M)|_{G_{F(v_i^2)}}\).

_Proof of Claim 2._ For the first part of the statement, suppose that \(\mathrm{max}_{a_r\in A[G_{V_i}]}|bp_{a_r}(M)\cap E[G_{V_i}]|\le 1\). By the contrapositive of Claim 1, the use of forcing gadgets attached to \(v_i^1\) and \(v_i^2\) implies that we must have \(M(v_i^1)\in\{v_i^T,v_i^F\}\) and \(M(v_i^2)\in\{v_i^T,v_i^F\}\). Given that _M_ is a matching, it immediately follows that either \(\{\{v_i^T,v_i^1\},\{v_i^F,v_i^2\}\}\subseteq M\) or \(\{\{v_i^T,v_i^2\},\{v_i^F,v_i^1\}\}\subseteq M\). The strict subset notation of the claim follows from the fact that if equality held, the agents within each forcing gadget would all block with each other, therefore violating that \(\mathrm{max}_{a_r\in A[G_{V_i}]}|bp_{a_r}(M)\cap E[G_{V_i}]|\le 1\).

For the second part of the statement, if it is the case that neither \(\{\{v_i^T,v_i^1\},\{v_i^F,v_i^2\}\}\subset M\) nor \(\{\{v_i^T,v_i^2\},\{v_i^F,v_i^1\}\}\subset M\), then it must be the case that at least one of \(v_i^1,v_i^2\) is matched to some agent within their respective copy of the forcing gadget or is unmatched, in which case Claim 1 states that \(\mathrm{max}_{a_r\in A[G_{V_i}]}|bp_{a_r}(M)\cap E[G_{V_i}]|>1\).

The third part of the statement follows trivially: \(v_i^T\) and \(v_i^1\) are each other’s first choices, and they are matched to each other, and \(v_i^2\) is matched to \(v_i^F\) whom they prefer to any agent in their forcing gadget. Hence, the only blocking pairs within the _G<sub>V<sub>i</sub></sub>_ gadget are those admitted by _M_ within the copies of the forcing gadgets. The fourth part of the statement follows from a similar argument, with the additional blocking pair \(\{v_i^T,v_i^1\}\) incurred as these agents are each other’s first choices.

We will now establish how agents in the clause gadget must be matched for the matching to have desirable properties, and vice versa.

**Claim 3.** _Let G<sub>C<sub>j</sub></sub> be an occurrence of the clause gadget, and let M be a matching of I. If it is true that_ \(\mathrm{max}_{a_r\in A[G_{C_j}]}|bp_{a_r}(M)\cap(E[G_{C_j}]\cup\{\{x_j^s,v(x_j^s)\}:1\le s\le 3\})|\le 1\)_, then there exists at least one_ \(x_j^d\) _in G<sub>C<sub>j</sub></sub> such that_ \(M(v(x_j^d))\unknown_{v(x_j^d)}x_j^d\)_. Conversely, if there exists at least one_ \(x_j^d\) _in G<sub>C<sub>j</sub></sub> such that_ \(M(v(x_j^d))\unknown_{v(x_j^d)}x_j^d\) _and, furthermore,_ \(\{x_j^{d-1},x_j^{d+1}\}\in M\) _(addition modulo 3), then_ \(\mathrm{max}_{a_r\in A[G_{C_j}]}|bp_{a_r}(M)\cap(E[G_{C_j}]\cup\{\{x_j^s,v(x_j^s)\}:1\le s\le 3\})|\le 1\).

_Proof of Claim 3._ For the first part of the statement, notice that for at least one \(x_j^d\) agent in _G<sub>C<sub>j</sub></sub>_, it must be the case that \(M(x_j^d)\in\{v(x_j^d),x_j^d\}\). Then due to the preference cycle, it must necessarily be the case that \(\{x_j^d,x_j^{d-1}\}\in bp(M)\). Now suppose that \(\mathrm{max}_{a_r\in A[G_{C_j}]}|bp_{a_r}(M)\cap(E[G_{C_j}]\cup\{\{x_j^s,v(x_j^s)\}:1\le s\le 3\})|\le 1\). If \(M(x_j^d)=v(x_j^d)\), then \(v(x_j^d)\) is matched to someone exactly as good as \(x_j^d\). If \(M(x_j^d)=x_j^d\) (i.e., \(x_j^d\) remains unmatched in _M_), then \(v(x_j^d)\) must be matched to someone better than \(x_j^d\), otherwise \(\{x_j^d,v(x_j^d)\}\in bp(M)\) and so \(|bp_{x_j^d}(M)|>1\).

For the second part of the statement, notice from the preference construction within _G<sub>C<sub>j</sub></sub>_ that if \(\{x_j^{d-1},x_j^{d+1}\}\in M\), then _M_ admits the blocking pair \(\{x_j^d,x_j^{d-1}\}\) but \(\{x_j^d,x_j^{d+1}\}\) is not a blocking pair because \(x_j^{d+1}\) prefers \(x_j^{d-1}\) to \(x_j^d\). Similarly, both of \(x_j^{d-1}\) and \(x_j^{d+1}\) prefer each other to \(v(x_j^{d-1})\) and \(v(x_j^{d+1})\), respectively. Lastly, if \(v(x_j^d)\) is matched to someone at least as good as \(x_j^d\), then \(\{v(x_j^d),x_j^d\}\) is not a blocking pair. Hence, no agent within _G<sub>C<sub>j</sub></sub>_ is contained in more than one blocking pair.
