Apart from considering restricted cases, another standard technique of dealing with NP-hard problems of practical relevance and theoretical interest is to design efficient approximation algorithms \[44\]. However, we can immediately state the following negative observation based on our intractability results.

**Corollary 6.1.** _The problems_ Minimax-AlmostStable-sri _and_ Minimax-AlmostStable- Max-smi _admit no polynomial-time approximation algorithms with a guarantee better than 2, unless_ P=NP.

_Proof._ Notice that an efficient algorithm with a guarantee _c\<_2 would contradict that the problems 1-Max-AlmostStable-sri and 1-Max-AlmostStable-Perfect-smi are NP-hard (as we showed in Theorems 4.1 and 4.4, respectively), as we could distinguish in polynomial time between instances that admit a matching (respectively perfect matching) in which no agent is in more than \(c\cdot\mathrm{OPT}<2\) blocking pairs, i.e., every agent is in at most one blocking pair, and instances where this is not true.

A trivial positive observation is the following: both Minimax-AlmostStable-sri and Minimax-AlmostStable-Max-smi can be efficiently approximated within the maximum preference list length _d_max, as we can efficiently decide whether the optimal (minimum) maximum number OPT of blocking pairs that any agent is contained in is 0 or at least 1 (see our discussion at the beginning of Section 4). Hence, we can either return a stable matching (which is optimal) or the empty matching (which admits at most \(d_\mathrm{max}\cdot\mathrm{OPT}\) blocking pairs per agent). Can we do better than this?

In Section 6.1, we will give a natural algorithm for Minimax-AlmostStable-sri that gives a more desirable guarantee of \(\left\lceil\frac{d_\mathrm{max}}{2}\right\rceil\) (in fact, we provide a stronger guarantee: no agent blocks with more than half of their preference list, regardless of the optimal value). Then, in Section 6.2, we will strengthen the inapproximability result from Corollary 6.1 to rule out (subject to \(\mathsf{P}\neq\mathsf{NP}\)) many classes of desirable approximation algorithms for Minimax-AlmostStable- Max-smi.

## 6.1 Approximating Minimax Matchings in General Graphs

Beyond the trivial upper bound \(\mathrm{OPT}\le d_\mathrm{max}\le n-1\) that we stated at the beginning of the section, we can make the following observation. Let \(I=(A,\succ)\) be an sri instance with complete preference lists and _n_ agents. Then, for any cut of _A_ (i.e., a partition of _A_ into two sets) consisting of (roughly) equally sized sets _A_<sub>1</sub> and _A_<sub>2</sub> (if _n_ is odd, let |_A_<sub>1</sub>|=|_A_<sub>2</sub>|+1), the smi instance \(I'=(A,\succ')\), where \(\succ'\) is constructed from \(\succ\) by making all agents within _A_<sub>1</sub> and all agents within _A_<sub>2</sub> mutually unacceptable, admits a stable matching _M_. Clearly, \(\mathrm{max}_{a_i\in A}|bp_i^{I^\prime}(M)|=0\le\mathrm{max}_{a_i\in A}|bp_i^I(M)|\le|A_1|-1=\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil-1\).

It turns out that we can generalise this observation to arbitrary sri instances with bounded preference lists of length _d_max by connecting this problem to a restricted local-search version of the classical Max-Cut problem \[22\]. In particular, we highlight in advance that our algorithm does not really depend on the preference rankings at all, other than an initial step that checks whether the instance is solvable, but rather exploits the acceptability graph in a purely graph-theoretical way, which allows us to make desirable approximation guarantee deductions.
