**Lemma 6** _Assume that_ \(\alpha\) _and_ \(\beta\) _are not orthogonal for all pairs of_ \(\alpha\in A\) _and_ \(\beta\in B\)_. If T<sub>A</sub> has a substring S such that P<sub>B</sub> is a CT-subsequence of S, then the frontier of CT(S_) _contains at most one_ 1.

_Proof_ Let _S_\[_p_\]=1 be a character which belongs to the frontier of _CT(S_). By the definition of Cartesian trees, the left subtree _L_ of _S_\[_p_\] consists only of 2 and 3. Since _L_ must be isomorphic to \(CT(V_2(\beta))\) for some \(\beta\in B\), the frontier of _L_ consists only of 2 (similar to Claim 1). Again, by the definition of Cartesian trees, the left subtrees of 2’s in _L_ consist only of 3, i.e., _CT_(333). Thus, _L_ is isomorphic to \(CT(V_2(\tilde{\beta}))\) where \(\tilde{\beta}=(1,1,\dots,1)\). Since there are at most one such vector \(\tilde{\beta}\in B\), the frontier of _CT(S_) can contain at most one 1.

Now, we start to prove the following lemma.

**Lemma 7** _If_ \(\alpha\) _and_ \(\beta\) _are not orthogonal for all pairs of_ \(\alpha\in A\) _and_ \(\beta\in B\)_, then T<sub>A</sub> dose not have a substring S such that_ \(|S|\le(n_B-1)(10d+4)+1\) _and P<sub>B</sub> is a CT-subsequence of S._

_Proof_ By Lemma 6, any substring of _T<sub>A</sub>_ which CT-matches _P<sub>B</sub>_ contains at most one 1, in turn, it contains at least _n<sub>B</sub>_ 0’s. That is, the first _n<sub>B</sub>_ 0’s in _P<sub>B</sub>_ should be aligned to some 0’s in _T<sub>A</sub>_. Recall that, for any \(\alpha\in A\) and \(\beta\in B\), \(V_2(\beta)\) is not a CT-subsequence of \(V_2(\alpha)\), but it is a CT-subsequence of _V_<sub>1</sub>(**0**). Thus, the shortest substring of _T<sub>A</sub>_ which contains prefix \(\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n_B-2}\mathtt{0}V_2(\beta_k)\right)\cdot\mathtt{0}V_2(\beta_{n_B-1})\mathtt{0}\) of _P<sub>B</sub>_ as a CT-subsequence is \(\left(\prod_{k=s}^{n_B-1+s}\mathtt{0}V_1(\mathtt{0})\mathtt{0}V_1(\alpha_k)\right)\cdot\mathtt{0}V_1(\mathtt{0})\mathtt{0}\) for some integer \(s\ge 1\). The remaining part of _P<sub>B</sub>_ is \(V_2(\beta_{n_B})\mathtt{0}\). Since \(V_2(\beta_{n_B})\) is not a CT-subsequence of \(V_1(\alpha_{n_B+s})\), the shortest substring of _T<sub>A</sub>_ that starts with \(V_1(\alpha_{n_B+s})\) and contains \(V_2(\beta_{n_B})\mathtt{0}\) as a CT-subsequence ends within 0_V_<sub>1</sub>(**0**)0=01(23231)_<sup>d</sup>_0. Furthermore, the ending position within 01(23231)_<sup>d</sup>_ of such substring is greater than two since the length-2 suffix of _P<sub>B</sub>_ is 10, which does not CT-match 01. Therefore, the length of a shortest substring of _T<sub>A</sub>_ that contains _P<sub>B</sub>_ as a CT-subsequence is at least (_n<sub>B</sub>_-2)(10_d_+4)+(5_d_+3)+5_d_+3=(_n<sub>B</sub>_-1)(10_d_+4)+2_\>(n<sub>B</sub>_-1)(10_d_+4)+1.
