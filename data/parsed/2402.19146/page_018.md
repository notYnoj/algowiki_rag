• Assume that _q_=1. In this case, we need to show C_<sub>L</sub>_={0}. Suppose on the contrary that there exists \((i',j')\) such that \(S[i']>S[i]\), \(T[j']>T[j]\), \(\ell_1\le i'\le i-1\), and \(\ell_2\le j'\le j-1\). Let \(I^*=(i',i_1,\dots,i_{|Q|})\), \(J^*=(j',j_1,\dots,j_{|Q|})\), and \(Q^*=\alpha\cdot Q\) where \(\alpha\) is a character in \(\Sigma\) that satisfies \(\alpha>Q[q]\). Also, CT(_Q_<sup>∗</sup>)=CT(_S_<sub>_I_<sup>∗</sup></sub>)=CT(_T_<sub>_J_<sup>∗</sup></sub>) holds since CT(_Q_)=CT(_S<sub>I</sub>_)=CT(_T<sub>J</sub>_), \(\alpha>Q[q]\), \(S[i']>S[i]\), and \(T[j']>T[j]\) hold. Moreover, _Q_<sup>∗</sup> is an f-CT-CS of \(S[\ell_1..r_1]\) and \(T[\ell_2..r_2]\) with (_i,j_), since _S_\[_i_\]=min(_S_<sub>_I_<sup>∗</sup></sub>) and \(T[j]=\mathsf{min}(T_{J^*})\). This contradicts the fact that _Q_ is the f-CT-LCS of \(S[\ell_1..r_1]\) and \(T[\ell_2..r_2]\) with (_i,j_) (from |_Q_<sup>∗</sup>|_\>|Q_|). Thus, C_<sub>L</sub>_={0} and maxC_<sub>L</sub>_=0.<tbc>

• Assume that _q\>_1. Let _p_<sub>1</sub> (resp., _p_<sub>2</sub>) be the predecessor of _i_ in _I_ (resp., the predecessor of _j_ in _J_). By the definition of the Cartesian-tree, CT(_Q_\[1.._q_-1\])=CT(_S_<sub>_I_\[_i_<sub>1</sub>:_p_<sub>1</sub>\]</sub>)=CT(_T_<sub>_J_\[_j_<sub>1</sub>:_p_<sub>2</sub>\]</sub>) holds, and there exist _i_<sup>∗</sup> and _j_<sup>∗</sup> such that _S_\[_i_<sup>∗</sup>\]=min(_S_<sub>_I_\[_i_<sub>1</sub>:_p_<sub>1</sub>\]</sub>)(_\>S_\[_i_\]) and _T_\[_j_<sup>∗</sup>\]=min(_T_<sub>_J_\[_j_<sub>1</sub>:_p_<sub>2</sub>\]</sub>)(_\>T_\[_j_\]). This implies that _Q_\[1.._q_-1\] is an f-CT-CS of \(S[\ell_1..i-1]\) and \(T[\ell_2..j-1]\) with (_i_<sup>∗</sup>_,j_<sup>∗</sup>). Thus, \(|Q[1..q-1]|=q-1\le\mathrm{max}\mathcal{C}_L\) holds. In the rest of this case, we show \(q-1\ge\mathrm{max}\mathcal{C}_L\) to prove the equality. Suppose on the contrary that _q_-1_\<_maxC_<sub>L</sub>_. Since 0_\<q_-1_\<_maxC_<sub>L</sub>_ (from assumptions), there exists \((i'',j'')\in[n]^2\) such that \(C(i'',j'',\ell_1,i-1,\ell_2,j-1)=\mathrm{max}\mathcal{C}_L\). Let \(Q''\) be an f-CT-LCS of \(S[\ell_1..i-1]\) and \(T[\ell_2..j-1]\) with \((i'',j'')\). Then there exist subscript sequences \(I''\) and \(J''\) over \(\{\ell_1,\dots,i-1\}\) and \(\{\ell_2,\dots,j-1\}\), respectively, such that \(\mathsf{CT}(Q'')=\mathsf{CT}(S_{I^{\prime\prime}})=\mathsf{CT}(T_{J^{\prime\prime}})\). Let \(\hat{I}\) denote the subscript sequence that is the concatenation of \(I''\) and \(I[i:i_{|Q|}]\), and \(\hat{J}\) denote the subscript sequence that is the concatenation of \(J''\) and \(J[j:j_{|Q|}]\). Then \(\mathsf{CT}(Q''\cdot Q[q..|Q|])=\mathsf{CT}(S_\hat{I})=\mathsf{CT}(T_\hat{j})\), \(S[i]=\mathsf{min}(S_\hat{I})\), and \(T[j]=\mathsf{min}(T_\hat{j})\) hold. This implies that \(Q''\cdot Q[q..|Q|]\) is an f-CT-LCS of \(S[\ell_1..i-1]\) and \(T[\ell_2..j-1]\) with (_i,j_). However, \(|Q''\cdot Q[q..|Q|]|=|Q''|+|Q[q..|Q|]|=\mathrm{max}\mathcal{C}_L+|Q|-q+1>q-1+|Q|-q+1=|Q|\) holds. This contradicts to the fact that _Q_ is the f-CT-LCS of \(S[\ell_1..i-1]\) and \(T[\ell_2..j-1]\) with (_i,j_). Thus _q_-1=maxC_<sub>L</sub>_ also holds for _q\>_1.

We can also prove |_Q_|-_q_=maxC_<sub>R</sub>_ by a symmetric manner. Therefore, |_Q_|=_q_-1+|_Q_|-_q_+1=maxC_<sub>L</sub>_+maxC_<sub>R</sub>_+1 holds.

Then we can obtain an _O(n_<sup>8</sup>)-time and _O(n_<sup>6</sup>)-space algorithm for solving CT-LCS based on Lemma 8 (see Algorithm 1). Our algorithm computes a six-dimensional table _C_ for any \((i,j,\ell_1,r_1,\ell_2,r_2)\in[n]^6\) that satisfies \(\ell_1\le i\le r_1\), \(\ell_2\le j\le r_2\). Notice that the processing order _i_<sub>1</sub>_,i_<sub>2</sub>_,...,i<sub>n</sub>_ (resp., _j_<sub>1</sub>_,j_<sub>2</sub>_,...,j<sub>n</sub>_) w.r.t. index _i_ (resp., _j_) has to satisfy \(S[i_1]>S[i_2]>\cdots>S[i_n]\) (resp., \(T[j_1]>T[j_2]>\cdots>T[j_n]\)). The algorithm finally returns \(\mathsf{ct}\text{\_}\mathsf{lcs}(S,T)=\mathrm{max}\{C(i,j,1,n,1,n)|1\le i\le n,1\le j\le n\}\). For each fixed \((i,j,\ell_1,r_1,\ell_2,r_2)\in[n]^6\) (i.e., _O(n_<sup>6</sup>) iterations), we can compute C_<sub>L</sub>_ and C_<sub>R</sub>_ in _O(n_<sup>2</sup>) time. Therefore, we can compute table _C_ in _O(n_<sup>8</sup>) time and _O(n_<sup>6</sup>) space.

**Theorem 3** _CT-LCS can be solved in O(n_<sup>8</sup>) _time and O(n_<sup>6</sup>) _space._

## 5.1.2 _O(n_<sup>6</sup>)-time _O(n_<sup>4</sup>)-space algorithm

In the sequel, we propose an improved algorithm that is based on the previous algorithm and runs in _O(n_<sup>6</sup>) time and _O(n_<sup>4</sup>) space. The key observation is that C_<sub>L</sub>_ and C_<sub>R</sub>_ actually depend on only four variables. Namely, C_<sub>L</sub>_ depends on \((i,j,\ell_1,\ell_2)\), and C_<sub>R</sub>_ depends on (_i,j,r_<sub>1</sub>_,r_<sub>2</sub>). Let \(L(i,j,\ell_1,\ell_2)=\mathrm{max}\mathcal{C}_L\) and _R(i,j,r_<sub>1</sub>_,r_<sub>2</sub>)=maxC_<sub>R</sub>_.<tbc>
