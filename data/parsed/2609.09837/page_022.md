Let _G_<sub>1</sub>_,...,G<sub>s</sub>_ be the connected components of _G_ in an arbitrary fixed order, choose a connector \(v_i\in V(G_i)\), and put \(r(i)=\sum_{h\le i}|V(G_h)|\). Join _v<sub>i</sub>_ to the color-(_q_+1) vertex of _C_<sub>_r(i_)</sub>. The initial coloring on _G_ is \(\varphi\) constructed as above, and no vertex of _G_ has color _q_+1. Let _N_=|_V(H_)|=_n(q_+2) and set the budget to 2_N_<sup>2</sup>.

If _G_ has an equitable coloring \(\psi\), rename its colors to match the histogram of \(\varphi\) and leave every clique unchanged. The resulting coloring of _H_ is proper: each clique is rainbow, consecutive clique endpoints have colors _q_+1 and 1, and every connector in _G_ uses a color in \[_q_\]. It has the same global histogram as the initial coloring. The graph _H_ is connected, so by Lemma 1 it can be reached with at most (_N_-1)(2diam(_H_)-1)_\<_2_N_<sup>2</sup> slides.

Conversely, slides preserve the global histogram, and because _H_ is connected every target with equal histogram is slide-reachable by Lemma 1. In every proper target, each (_q_+1)-clique uses all _q_+1 colors once. The _n_ cliques therefore consume exactly _n_ copies of every color. What remains on _G_ is exactly the initial equitable histogram on \[_q_\] and no copy of color _q_+1. Restriction to _G_ is consequently an equitable _q_-coloring.

We finish by bounding bandwidth. Let _B_=bw(_G_). For each component, take the relative order induced by a bandwidth-_B_ layout and concatenate the component orders in the fixed order _G_<sub>1</sub>_,...,G<sub>s</sub>_. This does not increase the length of any edge. Fold the order of _G<sub>i</sub>_ so that its connector _v<sub>i</sub>_ becomes last. Write the old component order as _x_<sub>1</sub>_,...,x<sub>a</sub>,v<sub>i</sub>,y_<sub>1</sub>_,...,y<sub>d</sub>_. If \(a\ge d\), first output _x_<sub>1</sub>_,...,x_<sub>_a-d_</sub>, then, for _h_=1_,...,d_, output _x_<sub>_a-d+h_</sub>_,y_<sub>_d-h_+1</sub>, and finally output _v<sub>i</sub>_. If _d\>a_, first output _y<sub>d</sub>,...,y_<sub>_a_+1</sub>, then, for _h_=1_,...,a_, output _y_<sub>_a-h_+1</sub>_,x<sub>h</sub>_, and finally output _v<sub>i</sub>_. This also covers _a_=0 or _d_=0. Every two consecutive vertices in the old order that lie within the same connected component are now at distance at most two, so every pairwise order distance, and in particular every edge length, grows by at most a factor of two.

Number the resulting order of _G_ by 1_,...,n_. Put its _j_th vertex at position (_q_+2)_j_. Put the _q_+1 vertices of _C<sub>j</sub>_ in positions (_q_+2)(_j_-1)+1_,...,(q_+2)_j_-1, with its color-(_q_+1) vertex last and its color-1 vertex first. Clique edges have length at most _q_, chain edges have length two, and a component connector is adjacent to the clique vertex immediately before it. An original edge has length at most 2(_q_+2)_B_. Therefore \(\text{bw}(H)\le 2(q+2)(B+1)\), which completes the parameterized reduction.
