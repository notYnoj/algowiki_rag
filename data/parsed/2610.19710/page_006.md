because an online-LOCAL algorithm always sees the neighborhood around the node that is being labeled. The pre-committed labeling is always maintained such that it imposes a unique 2-coloring on the uncommitted nodes of each connected component.

Whenever a node _v<sub>i</sub>_ and its neighborhood is revealed, it first checks whether the newly revealed neighborhood connects multiple regions that were previously disconnected components in the graph _G_<sub>_i_-1</sub>. If there exist pairs of such regions that impose incompatible 2-colorings, then the conflict is broken by extending the pre-committed labeling to flip the 2-coloring on one of the components. This works by expanding the boundary between committed and uncommitted nodes by a cycle of 3 colors: first, all the uncommitted nodes neighboring a node with color 0 are committed to color 1, then nodes neighboring color 1 are committed to color 2, then nodes neighboring color 2 are committed to color 0. Once conflicts between all pairs have been broken, then there is again a consistent 2-coloring of the uncommitted nodes. The label of node _v<sub>i</sub>_ is then either set to the pre-committed value, or if not pre-committed, assigned according to the 2-coloring of the uncommitted nodes of the component.

In order to pre-commit the color of a node, it must lie within the neighborhood of radius equal to the algorithm’s locality. To keep the radius small, the algorithm maintains a so-called border count _b_ per component. Whenever a conflict between two regions occurs, the boundary of the component with smaller _b_-value is expanded, and its _b_-value is incremented by 1. The new connected component takes the maximum of its regions as its _b_-value. This balancing leads to the locality of _O_(log_n_).

The fact that this online-LOCAL algorithm requires pre-committing around the boundary of an entire region means that it cannot easily be modified into a dynamic-LOCAL algorithm. A dynamic-LOCAL algorithm can only modify the labeling within its locality radius. The “boundary” cannot even be clearly determined in a dynamic-LOCAL algorithm, since the neighborhood of any node can always keep changing.

## 4 A new dynamic-LOCAL algorithm

We will now present our approach to the problem of 3-coloring bipartite graphs with optimal dynamic locality. In Section 4.1, we first introduce a potential function with constraints such that it describes a proper 3-coloring. Based on this framework, we describe our algorithm in Section 4.2. Then, in Section 4.3, we prove that Algorithm 1 maintains the constraints on the potential function as invariants, which proves that it is a correct coloring algorithm. Finally, in Section 4.4 we bound the radius in which the coloring is modified by _O_(log_n_). Together, this lets us conclude the main result in Theorem 1.

### 4.1 Potential framework for 3-coloring

Our method is based on a potential function over the nodes with constraints that ensure that it is possible to retrieve a proper 3-coloring from the potential values. Essentially, we require that the potential difference between neighboring nodes is exactly 1. However, the constraints that we define are based on the distance between nodes, allowing us to make statements about any pair of connected nodes, even if there are uncolored nodes between them. This gives us stronger constraints on partial labelings.
