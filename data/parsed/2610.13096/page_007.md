## 4. Boundary structure and the projection theorem

### 4.1. Boundary structure

By Theorem 1, the only case requiring further analysis is

\(\delta=0,|I|=\frac{n}{2}.\)

Let _J=V_\\_I_.

**Theorem 2.** _If_ \(\delta=0\) _and_ |_I_|=_n/_2_, then_<tbc>

\(N(I)=J,|I|=|J|,\)

<tbc>_and the bipartite graph H_=(_I,J;E<sub>H</sub>_) _has a perfect matching, where_<tbc>

\(E_H=\{xy\in E:x\in I,y\in J\}.\)

<tbc>_Proof._ Since _d(I_)=0, we have |_N(I_)|=|_I_|=_n/_2. Independence of _I_ implies that _I_ and _N(I_) are disjoint, and their cardinalities sum to _n_. Hence

\(N(I)=V\backslash I=J.\)

For any \(X\subseteq I\), independence of _X_ and _id<sub>c</sub>(G_)=0 imply \(|N(X)|\ge|X|\). Moreover, all neighbors of _X_ lie in _J_ because _I_ is independent. Therefore _N<sub>H</sub>(X_)=_N(X_). Thus, _H_ satisfies Hall’s condition and has a matching saturating _I_. Since |_I_|=|_J_|, this matching is perfect.

### 4.2. Projection of small blocking sets

**Theorem 3** (Projection theorem)**.** _Let I be any maximum-cardinality critical independent set of G such that_<tbc>

\(id_c(G)=0,|I|=\frac{n}{2}.\)

<tbc>_Then G contains a small blocking set if and only if I contains a nonempty proper subset_ \(T\unknown I\) _satisfying_

\(|T|=|N(T)|.\)

_Such a subset is called a_ tight subset _of I._
