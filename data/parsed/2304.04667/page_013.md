**Theorem 1.10.** _Suppose P is a total function with nondeterministic time complexity T(m). If for some_ \(\delta>0\)_, there is a deterministic fine-grained reduction from k-SAT with time-bound_ 2_<sup>n</sup> to P with time bound_ \(T(m)^{1+\delta}\)_, i.e. from_ (_k-SAT,_2_<sup>n</sup>_) _to_ \((P,T(m)^{1+\delta})\) _then NSETH is false._

### Non-reducibility for deterministic vs. randomized reductions.

Our results, along with the framework for non-reducibility in \[CGI<sup>+</sup>16\], do not address the possibility of proving lower bounds based on SETH using a randomized fine-grained reduction. The reason is that NSETH, upon which this framework is built, does not remain plausible when facing randomization (see \[CGI<sup>+</sup>16, Wil16\]). That said, we are not aware of any instances where this barrier has been successfully bypassed with randomization. Furthermore, one can use a non-uniform variant of NSETH called NUNSETH, under which our results also hold for randomized reductions with bounded error.

## 2 Non-Reducibility Results

In the absence of _n_<sup>4-_o_(1)</sup> conditional lower bounds for general algorithms for All-Pairs Max-Flow, it may be tempting to utilize the commonly used SETH as a hardness assumption, and design a reduction from SAT to prove that the problem requires a running time of _n_<sup>4-_o_(1)</sup>. However, we demonstrate here that in most settings of the problem, such (deterministic) reductions are not possible under NSETH, thus proving our Theorems 1.3, 1.5, and 1.6. Technically, our main results in this section are fast _nondeterministic_ algorithms for solving All-Pairs Max-Flow in various settings. In fact, our nondeterministic framework is even more general, as it is applicable to Min-Cut data structures. This could be significant because in some settings, a data structure for All-Pairs Max-Flow could potentially be much smaller than _n_<sup>2</sup> - similar to cut-equivalent trees in the case of undirected graphs with edge capacities, and we aim to capture these scenarios as well with our framework.

Then, we reach our non-reducibility conclusions under NSETH by an argument first made in \[CGI<sup>+</sup>16\] to rule out SETH-based reductions to various problems, including 3SUM and All- Pairs Shortest-Path. The argument was later rewritten \[AKT20b\] to adapt from decision problems or functions (where each input has exactly one output) to total functions as every graph has at least one Gomory-Hu tree, which was the focus of \[AKT20b\]. This adaptation is detailed in Section 1.4, with Theorem 1.10. We use the same total function definition more generally for any Min-Cut data structure with \(t_{mc}=\hat{O}(1)\), which is a total function since every graph has at least one such data structure: just compute the _O(n_<sup>2</sup>) cuts naively in _O(n_<sup>2</sup>_m_) time (so in this case, _t<sub>pq</sub>(m),t<sub>po</sub>(m_)=_n_<sup>2</sup>). Next is our main framework, which is central to achieving our nondeterministic algorithms across all settings.

**Theorem 2.1.** _Suppose there is a Min-Cut data structure_ D _that given a graph_ \(G\in\mathcal{F}\) _on m edges and n nodes, after a preprocessing phase which, with high probability, makes t<sub>pq</sub>(m_) _Max-Flow calls on G and spends t<sub>po</sub>(m_) _time outside of these calls, can answer queries correctly in t<sub>mc</sub>(m_) _output sensitive time._

_Then, the nondeterministic time complexity for constructing a Min-Cut data structure for G is_ \(\tilde{t}_p(m)=O(t_{po}(m)+n^{\omega(log_n(|Q|),1,1)}+\sum_{(u,v)\in Q}volume(f_{uv}))\) _with_ \(\tilde{t}_{mc}(m)=t_{mc}(m)\)_, where_ \(Q\subseteq V\times V\) _is the set of pairs for which the t<sub>pq</sub>(m_) _Max-Flow calls are made, and f<sub>uv</sub> is any maximum uv-flow in G._

The main idea in the proof of Theorem 2.1 is to first verify all the minimum cuts that D will use on _G_ in hindsight. Then, we simulate D and whenever we encounter a Max-Flow query, we assert the corresponding guess. The correctness follows immediately from the correctness of D. Before proving Theorem 2.1, we state a technical lemma regarding the nondeterministic time it takes to verify maximum flows for a given set of node pairs _Q_.
