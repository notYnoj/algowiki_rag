Second, learning from experimental experience. An agent needs to turn experimental observations into knowledge that guides subsequent decisions. This requires identifying which choices produced a result, under what conditions the method applies and which evidence supports the lesson. The agent must retain this knowledge and retrieve it for relevant tasks. MemoHarness addresses experience reuse by building a memory bank during training and retrieving execution diagnoses and reusable patterns to adapt the harness at test time (Huang et al., 2026). In an ongoing research investigation, the agent must also update its memory as new experiments arrive, linking each lesson to the conditions and evidence that support it.

Third, exploring alternatives and backtracking. A single research direction explores only part of the available methods. The AI Scientist-v2 uses agentic tree search to develop experimental alternatives, and ScienceFlow can resume from archived executable states (Yamada et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2026). These systems demonstrate the value of branching and backtracking. For research with checkable outputs, the agent must preserve each alternative’s implementation and evidence, compare candidates under a common evaluation rule and recover earlier work when new findings make it useful.

To address these challenges, we introduce Épi, a persistent and evolving autonomous research agent. Épi uses hierarchical memory to resume investigations and retrieve experience from earlier work. It maintains versioned branches to develop alternative methods and backtrack to saved implementations, and incorporates independent review when assessing evidence and choosing its next experiment. The agent operates in verifiable research environments that evolve between campaigns, where a campaign is a complete investigation that may span multiple model sessions. Within a campaign, every branch uses the same feasibility checks, objective and comparison value. A verified finding can then establish a new reference or motivate a new task for the next campaign. Persistent execution and storage carry the research state across sessions.

Mathematical discovery provides a concrete demonstration of this research process: the agent develops methods whose outputs can be checked as constructions, bounds or proofs. We also evaluate its applicability to algorithmic coding, where the agent must design and implement a solver that handles new problem instances efficiently. Our lightweight configuration, Épi-turbo, achieves a 5.4% higher total benchmark score than x on 30 FrontierOR tasks at 64% of its model cost, using the same underlying model and reasoning effort (Section 6). These tasks include routing, scheduling, production planning and graph optimization.

Our contributions are an autonomous research agent that integrates hierarchical memory, branching and independent review to sustain and refine its investigations; a verification protocol that evaluates changing research methods under a fixed ruler and gain floor; and a collection of 120 record-setting results, comprising 100 improved constructions and 20 numerical bounds. Each record improves a documented literature or registry reference for the same problem and normalization; we count one final result per instance or parameter setting, as detailed in Section 4. These results include an approximately 84-fold narrower interval for the _n_-queens constant and a more than 3.6-million-fold narrower rigorous interval for the quartic Feigenbaum attractor dimension. Exact certificates establish that the Cohn–Elkies bound is not sharp in dimensions 10 and 11. Épi also proves Tuza’s conjecture for split graphs with an eight-vertex clique part and at most three triangle-active neighborhood types, with arbitrary multiplicities. We release the constructions and supporting certificates (Epi Team, 2026).

Our goal is to connect self-improvement with human understanding: researchers should be able to inspect how a method evolves, explain why a result holds, and use that knowledge to guide further work. Épi retains implementations, experimental artifacts, and research notes across branches and sessions, allowing researchers to inspect the development of a method and assess its supporting evidence. Its constructions, certificates, and mathematical arguments further connect computational outputs to findings that researchers can examine and build upon (Section 8).
