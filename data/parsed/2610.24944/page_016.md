These systems share Épi’s need to revise a research direction in response to feedback. AIRA<sub>2</sub> addresses a related execution challenge through asynchronous experiments and a consistent evaluation protocol (Hambardzumyan et al., 2026). In Épi, the verifier stays fixed within a campaign as the agent changes its approach.

Continuing research also depends on how previous work is represented. Kosmos uses a shared world model to connect literature search and data analysis (Mitchener et al., 2025). ScienceFlow preserves executable research states that can be resumed or revisited (Zhao et al., 2026). Knowledge can also pass between investigations through reports, as in AgentRxiv (Schmidgall and Moor, 2025), or through AutoKD’s persistent graph of accepted findings (Ge et al., 2026). Épi retains both the experimental state needed to resume a campaign and lessons intended for reuse on another problem. Branches preserve alternative histories within that campaign.

For tasks with executable evaluation, FunSearch and AlphaEvolve guide program generation using measured performance (Romera-Paredes et al., 2024; Novikov et al., 2025). SimpleTES scales search through independent trajectories and reuse of evaluated histories (Ye et al., 2026). Dream-RSI turns recorded discovery trees into replay environments for evaluating and revising exploration policies before deploying them in further searches (Zheng et al., 2026). TTT-Discover adapts model weights at test time (Yuksekgonul et al., 2026). Épi keeps model weights fixed and uses stored lessons and branch histories to guide its next experiment. Released Hyra constructions offer starting points for further refinement (Hyra Team, 2026). Épi’s investigations can also extend beyond refinement to finding a certificate or proving a statement, as in the Tuza case study.

## 8 Autonomous Research for Human Understanding

Our goal with Épi is to make autonomous research contribute to human understanding: researchers should be able to explain why a result holds, identify what it reveals about the problem, and use that knowledge to guide further work. Achieving this goal requires connecting computational artifacts to explicit formulations, mathematical arguments, and the scientific questions they resolve. Épi builds on existing theory and retains the implementations, experiments, and assessments through which its methods develop. The case studies illustrate how this process can produce knowledge that researchers can use: the Cohn–Elkies separation identifies a structural limit of an established method; the MAX-4-CUT formulation provides a reusable procedure for optimizing gadget weights; and the Tuza proof makes explicit how finite certificates establish a statement about an infinite graph family. These examples express our broader aim for self-improvement: an agent should accumulate both the ability to obtain better results and knowledge that helps people understand and extend those results.

## 9 Conclusion

Épi is an autonomous research agent that sustains investigations across model sessions and improves its methods through memory, branching and independent review. Its campaigns produced 120 record-setting results and a computer-assisted proof of a new case of Tuza’s conjecture. The released constructions and certificates provide concrete, checkable evidence for these advances. Épi-turbo extends this research process to algorithmic coding across routing, scheduling, planning and graph optimization. On 30 FrontierOR tasks, it achieves a 5.4% higher total benchmark score than x with the same model while using 64% of its model cost.

Épi couples method improvement with a persistent record of implementations, experiments, and review. This record supports inspection of the research process, while explicit constructions and proof arguments make the resulting findings available for further scientific work. Together, these evaluations demonstrate the applicability of persistent, feedback-driven method development to mathematical discovery and efficient algorithm design.
