We first show that if \(uv\in\tilde{\mu}\), then both _u_ and _v_ do not belong to any component in _Z_(C), because if _u_ or _v_ belong to a component _C_ in _Z_(C), then as argued above it contradicts the fact that we have deleted _C_ in Phase I. Thus, by the construction of \(\eta\), \(\tilde{\mu}\subseteq\eta\). Furthermore, \(\eta=\unknown_{C_i\in Z(\mathscr{C})}(E(C_i)\backslash\mu_i)\unknown\tilde{\mu}\). Since every \(C_i\in Z(\mathscr{C})\) is a \(\mu\)- alternating path due to Observation 1, we have that \(|\mu\triangleup\eta|=\sum_{C_i\in Z(\mathscr{C})}q_i\le q\) as _Z_ is a solution to \((\chi,k,q,t)\). **Claim 33** _There are at most k blocking edges with respect to_ \(\eta\).

_Proof._ For a component _C<sub>i</sub>_ in _G<sup>⋆</sup>_, recall the definition of \(\eta_i\) in Phase III. \(\eta_i\) contains all the edges in _C<sub>i</sub>_ which are not in \(\mu\) and also the edges which are in \(\mu\) but not in _C<sub>i</sub>_ and whose at least one of the endpoint is a neighbor of a vertex in _C<sub>i</sub>_. We first prove that every blocking edge with respect to the matching \(\eta\) is also a blocking edge with respect to matching \(\eta_i\), for some \(C_i\in Z(\mathscr{C})\). Let _uv_ be a blocking edge with respect to \(\eta\). Due to Proposition 1 and by the construction of \(\eta\), either _u_ or _v_ belongs to a component in _Z_(C). Without loss of generality, let _u_ belongs to a component \(C_i\in Z(\mathscr{C})\). Thus, \(\eta(u)=\eta_i(u)\), by the construction of \(\eta\) and \(\eta_i\). If _v_ is also in _C<sub>i</sub>_, then \(\eta(v)=\eta_i(v)\), and hence _uv_ is a blocking edge with respect to \(\eta_i\). Suppose that \(v\notin C_i\). Since \(uv\in E(G)\), by the construction of the graph _G_<sub>1</sub>, _v_ does not belong to any other component of _G_<sub>1</sub>. Thus, by the construction of \(\eta\) and \(\eta_i\), \(\eta(v)=\mu(v)\) and \(\eta_i(v)=\mu(v)\). Therefore, _uv_ is also a blocking edge with respect to \(\eta_i\). Recall that _k<sub>i</sub>_ is the number of blocking edges with respect to \(\eta_i\). Therefore, the number of blocking edges with respect to \(\eta\) is at most \(\sum_{i\in Z}k_i\le k\).

Due to Claims 31, 32, and 33, we can infer that \(\eta\) is a solution to \((G,\mathcal{L},\mu,k,q,t)\).

### 5.1 Deterministic FPT algorithm

To make our algorithm deterministic we first introduce the notion of an _n-p-q- lopsided universal_ family. Given a universe _U_ and an integer \(\ell\), we denote all the \(\ell\)-sized subsets of _U_ by \(\genfrac(){pt}{2}{U}{\ell}\). We say that a family F of sets over a universe _U_ with |_U_|=_n_, is an _n-p-q-lopsided universal_ family if for every \(A\in\genfrac(){pt}{2}{U}{p}\) and \(B\in\genfrac(){pt}{2}{U\backslash A}{q}\), there is an \(F\in\mathcal{F}\) such that \(A\subseteq F\) and \(B\cap F=\emptyset\).
