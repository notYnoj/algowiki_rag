## 3.2. Variation checkpoints.

Smoothness alone need not make the truncation regulated, let alone piecewise constant. The \(1\times 1\) family \(H(t)=2^{-k}+\frac{1}{4}2^{-k}e^{-1/t^2}sin(1/t)\), with _H_(0)=2<sup>-_k_</sup>, is smooth, since every derivative of _e_<sup>-1_/t_<sup>2</sup></sup>sin(1_/t_) tends to 0 at 0, yet its truncation takes both values 2<sup>-_k_</sup> and 0 on every interval (0_,b_) with _b\>_0.

For such paths, we hold truncated midpoint samples on chosen intervals (Section 3.3), and finite total variation lets us count these intervals.

**Definition 3.5.** We measure how much _H_ changes by its total variation up to time _t_,

\(\ell(t)=sup\sum_r\big\|H(s_{r+1})-H(s_r)\big\|,\)

the supremum over partitions \(0=s_0<s_1<\cdots<s_m=t\), and write \(\ell=\ell(T)\). We define _checkpoints_ as any nondecreasing list of times \(0=t_0\le\cdots\le t_n=T\).

The variation is nondecreasing, and \(\|H(t)-H(s)\|\le\ell(t)-\ell(s)\) for \(s\le t\) when \(\ell<\infty\). Finite variation also gives _H_ one-sided limits, so _H_ is regulated. For an _L_-Lipschitz family, \(\ell\le LT\), and for a continuously differentiable family, \(\ell(t)\le\int_0^t\|\dot{H}\|\).

**Lemma 3.6.** _Suppose_ \(\ell<\infty\)_, and let_ \(n\ge 1\) _be an integer. Set t_<sub>0</sub>=0_, t<sub>n</sub>=T, and_<tbc>

\(t_j=inf\big\{t\in[0,T]:\ell(t)\ge j\ell/n\big\}(0<j<n).\)

<tbc>_These equal-variation checkpoints are nondecreasing, and on each interval the variation stays between two consecutive levels: for_ \(t_j<x\le y<t_{j+1}\),

\(j\ell/n\le\ell(x)\le\ell(y)\le(j+1)\ell/n,\)

_so that_ \(\|H(y)-H(x)\|\le\ell/n\).

_Proof._ The sets defining the infima contain _T_ and shrink as _j_ increases, so their infima are nondecreasing. For _j\>_0, _x\>t<sub>j</sub>_ puts a point of the _j_th set below _x_, and for _j_+1_\<n_, _y\<t_<sub>_j_+1</sub> keeps _y_ outside the next set, so monotonicity gives the displayed bounds, with endpoint cases given by \(0\le\ell(t)\le\ell\). Subtracting gives \(\|H(y)-H(x)\|\le\ell(y)-\ell(x)\le\ell/n\). □

## 3.3. Midpoint products.

We turn checkpoints into products by holding truncated midpoint samples constant at one extra bit of precision, which halves the truncation error \(\sqrt{2}\sigma 2^{-k}\) and leaves half for variation within each interval.

**Definition 3.7.** For nondecreasing checkpoints \(0=t_0\le t_1\le\cdots\le t_n=T\) and a nonnegative integer _k_, the _midpoint product_ is generated by the (_k_+1)-bit truncation of each interval’s midpoint sample:

\(W=e^{-i(t_n-t_{n-1})G_{n-1}}\cdots e^{-i(t_1-t_0)G_0},\)<br>
\(G_j=H\big(\frac{t_j+t_{j+1}}{2}\big)^{(k+1)},\)

where an interval of length zero contributes the identity.

**Lemma 3.8.** _Let H be_ \(\sigma\)_-sparse and write U for its evolution, which exists in each case below (Proposi tion 2.5). Let_ \(0<\varepsilon\le\sqrt{2}\sigma T\)_, and let k be the least nonnegative integer with_ \(2^k\ge\sqrt{2}\sigma T/\varepsilon\).

(1) _If H is regulated, there are checkpoints whose midpoint product W (Definition 3.7) satisfies_ \(\|W-U(T)\|\le\sqrt{2}\sigma 2^{-k}T\le\varepsilon\).<tbc>

(2) _(Equal-variation steps.) If_ \(0<\ell<\infty\)_, W satisfies the same bound for_ \(n=\lceil 2^{k+1}\ell/(\sqrt{2}\sigma)\rceil\) _and the checkpoints of Lemma 3.6, and_ \(n\le 1+4T\ell/\varepsilon\).<tbc>

(3) _(Uniform steps_ \[28\]_.) If L\>_0 _and_ \(\|H(t)-H(u)\|\le L\left|t-u\right|\)_, W satisfies the same bound for t<sub>j</sub>=jT/n with_ \(n=\lceil 2^{k+1}LT/(\sqrt{2}\sigma)\rceil\)_, and_ \(n\le 1+4LT^2/\varepsilon\).

_Proof._ In (1), take a step approximation within \(\sqrt{2}\sigma 2^{-k-2}\) of _H_ \[11, (7.6.1)\], with its piece endpoints as checkpoints. The triangle inequality through its constant value bounds each open piece’s oscillation by \(\sqrt{2}\sigma 2^{-k-1}\). In (2), Lemma 3.6 bounds it by \(\ell/n\le\sqrt{2}\sigma 2^{-k-1}\), and in (3), the Lipschitz bound on length _T/n_ gives \(LT/n\le\sqrt{2}\sigma 2^{-k-1}\), both by the ceilings, which are positive since \(\ell,LT>0\).
