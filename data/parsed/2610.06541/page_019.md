and outgrows every power of _T_ for these bounds. Can polynomially many factors retain the same leakage decay? At unit steps, the correction 1-_iY_ of Lemma 6.2 cancels only the linear drift, so the question becomes which additional corrections or endpoint hypotheses would let faster-decaying bounds transfer.

A second question concerns growth in _T_. The time-discretization error of a sampled adiabatic product need not grow with _T_ [30, 19, 1, 23], and I would like the same for entry truncation. At fixed precision _k_, when is its contribution to adiabatic leakage bounded independently of _T_? Theorem 6.4 rounds the clock but keeps exact samples, so it does not answer this. Derivative bounds on _H_ alone need not answer it either, because truncating a linearly moving coordinate produces a sawtooth error that could resonate with the spectral gap. A third question is whether the unit-step comparison extends to contracting evolutions [3], which Cunningham and Roland obtain by applying unitaries at Poisson-distributed times and averaging over realizations [9]. The obstacle is that the correction 1-_iY_ of Lemma 6.2 need not be a contraction.

Finally, some costs lie outside this paper. Direct truncation needs the times at which entries cross truncation levels, which an oracle for _H(t_) does not return. Lemma 3.8(2) avoids this by computing checkpoints from the variation \(\ell(t)\), or from any computable nondecreasing \(\varphi\) with \(\|H(t)-H(s)\|\le\varphi(t)-\varphi(s)\) for \(s\le t\). For adiabatic applications, preparing a state in the initial spectral subspace and testing for the final one are further tasks.
