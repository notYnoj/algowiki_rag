_Proof._ The argument of Appendix D applies with _r=m_-1=3 in Step 1, which gives the constant _C_<sub>4</sub>=9 in (D.1).

_Remark_ 4.7 (Further results at order four). The overlap identity (3.4), the disjoint-subset unbiased estimator of Theorem 4.8 below, and its consistency are results for fixed-order U-statistics and apply verbatim to the fourth L-comoment, with \(n\ge 8\) required for the order-four/order-four block. When _X_ and _Y_ are independent, Theorem 3.4 extends with \(\mathrm{Var}(\widehat{\lambda}_4^{[XY]})=c_n\mathrm{Var}(X)\), where _c<sub>n</sub>_=(_n_+1)(_n_+2)(_n_+3)/{7_n(n_-1)(_n_-2)(_n_-3)}; the same- direction covariances between different orders vanish, and the opposite-direction covariances are \(\ell_s^X\ell_m^Y/(n-1)\) as before. The projection estimator of order four is directly computable by Corollary 4.6. What does not extend directly is the _O(n_<sup>2</sup>) algorithm for the exactly unbiased estimator: for the order-four/order-four block, the overlap terms with _c_=3,4 require sums over triples and quadruples, and an efficient evaluation is left for future work.

## 4.3 An exactly unbiased estimator of the finite-sample covariance

The projection estimator of Theorem 4.4 targets only the leading term \(\Omega/n\). It omits the corrections \(B_2/\genfrac(){pt}{2}{n}{2}\) and \(B_3/\genfrac(){pt}{2}{n}{3}\), which are not negligible for the sample sizes of 20 to 50 that are common in applications; in Section 6.3, the projection estimator understates the variances by up to about 14% at _n_=20. When standard errors should not be understated, an estimator of the exact finite-sample covariance is needed. We now construct an exactly unbiased one. It applies the standard U-statistic disjoint-subset principle (Hoeffding, 1948; Serfling, 1980), which is stated in Appendix A (paragraph "Disjoint-subset principle"). Unlike the projection estimator, it need not be PSD in a given sample; Remark 5.4 below gives PSD and PD corrections. In the univariate L-moment setting, a related exact unbiased construction was given by Elamir and Seheult (2004). The present result concerns the joint sampling covariance of the L-comoments; it is not a new general unbiasedness theorem.

### Disjoint pairs of index sets.

Each sample statistic is an average of kernel values over index sets. In the product of two statistics, a kernel value of the first statistic, computed on an index set _I_, is multiplied by a kernel value of the second statistic, computed on an index set _J_. We call (_I,J_) an ordered pair of index sets: _I_ belongs to the first kernel and _J_ to the second. The pair is _disjoint_ if \(I\cap J=\varnothing\), that is, if the two kernel values share no observation. This is the case _c_=0 in the overlap identity (3.4); the other pairs share \(c=|I\cap J|\ge 1\) observations. Because the pairs _Z_<sub>1</sub>_,...,Z<sub>n</sub>_ are independent, the two kernel values of a disjoint pair are independent, and the expectation of their product is the product of their means. Averaging the products over all disjoint pairs (_I,J_) therefore estimates the product of the means without bias. Subtracting this average from the product of the two statistics gives an unbiased estimator of their covariance.<tbc>
