<tbc>Downstream inference based on multivariate L-moment estimation also requires identification and appropriate moment Jacobians. Inverse covariance weighting (Shin et al., 2026) further requires a nonsingular limiting covariance.

The results assume independent observation pairs and continuous margins, with unrestricted dependence within each pair. Higher-order L-comoments, L-comoment matrices of more than two variables, and trimmed L-comoments (Elamir and Seheult, 2003; Hosking, 2007) are again fixed-order U-statistics. The overlap formula, exact unbiasedness, and consistency therefore extend directly; only the conditional kernels and the rank-prefix tables must be redeveloped. Ties can be handled with a kernel convention, such as sgn(0)=0 and averaging over tied orderings. This convention keeps the above properties but changes the targets, the closed forms, and the algorithm. For the _r_ largest observations of blocks (Tawn, 1988; Busabodhin et al., 2025, 2026), the present results apply when the blocks are independent, because the dependence among the order statistics within a block is allowed.

## 8 Conclusion

We derived the exact finite-sample covariance structure of sample L-comoments in both directions, for bivariate samples with unrestricted dependence within each pair. Explicit conditional kernels give every entry of the exact \(4\times 4\) sampling covariance matrix and its \(6\times 6\) extension that includes the sample means. They also yield a canonical Hoeffding decomposition of this covariance. When _X_ and _Y_ are independent, the covariance matrix is fully explicit and shows that the estimators of the two directions are correlated even when both population L-comoments vanish. We considered two estimators of this sampling covariance matrix. The first is exactly unbiased for the finite-sample covariance, is first-order consistent, and can be corrected to be PSD or PD. The second is a projection estimator of the leading asymptotic covariance, which is consistent and PSD for any observed data set.

An overlap inclusion-exclusion identity combined with rank-prefix sums evaluates the unbiased estimator in _O(n_<sup>2</sup>) time instead of _O(n_<sup>6</sup>). The numerical experiments confirmed the exact formulas and the agreement of the fast and direct algorithms. For samples of size 20-50, the unbiased estimator had no detectable bias, whereas the projection estimator understated the covariance by several percent. The projection estimator nevertheless had the smaller root mean squared error, so the choice between the two estimators is a bias-variance trade-off. The first-order approximation for L-comoment ratios was accurate except for \(\widehat{\tau}_3\) under heavy-tailed margins, where it overstated the variance. These results may be useful in subsequent studies of inference based on L-comoments, for example in tests of independence or exchangeability, in the discordancy and homogeneity tests of regional frequency analysis, and in multivariate L-moment estimation with inverse-covariance weighting.
