_Second,_ when _X_ and _Y_ are independent, we obtain a fully explicit covariance matrix.

_Third,_ we show that the exactly unbiased covariance estimator can be computed in _O(n_<sup>2</sup>) time, much faster than its direct evaluation, which requires _O(n_<sup>6</sup>) operations. The fast algorithm combines an overlap inclusion-exclusion identity with rank-prefix sums.

We also study a consistent positive-semidefinite (PSD) estimator of the leading asymptotic covariance; it also covers fourth-order L-comoments and is computed in _O(n_log_n_) time. In addition, we establish the first-order consistency of the unbiased estimator and give its positive-definite (PD) regularization. Numerical experiments confirm that the fast and direct algorithms agree and compare their computing times. The experiments also show that individual unbiased covariance estimates need not be PSD. These results are practically relevant because L-comoment-based regional and bivariate frequency analyses often rely on short records, for example a few decades of annual data (Hosking and Wallis, 1997; Chebana and Ouarda, 2007). For such sample sizes, asymptotic standard errors can be noticeably biased. Section 6 quantifies this bias and the accompanying bias-variance trade-off between unbiased and asymptotic covariance estimators.

The exact covariances of the L-comoments can also be used to approximate the covariances of L-comoment ratios by a first-order Taylor expansion, as in the delta method (Oehlert, 1992; van der Vaart, 1998); these approximations are given in Appendix E. Parameter estimation using L-comoments is not developed here. Related work uses L-moment methods for copula parameter estimation (Brahimi et al., 2015), but copula modeling and parameter estimation are beyond the scope of the present study. Sample L-comoments can already be computed using functions such as `Lcomoment.Lk12` and `Lcomoment.matrix` in the R package `lmomco` (Asquith, 2026).
