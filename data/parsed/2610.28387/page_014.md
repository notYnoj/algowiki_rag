A pair with large values of both _X_ and _Y_ therefore increases both estimators, and a pair with a large _X_ value and a small _Y_ value decreases both. Hence the chance association in the sample moves the two estimators together, and their covariance is positive even though _X_ and _Y_ are independent. For the proof of Theorem 3.4, Appendix B derives each matrix entry from the weighted-sum form (2.8) and the concomitant representation (2.4) (Hosking, 1990; Serfling and Xiao, 2007).

The opposite-direction covariances are not a small-sample artifact. By Theorem 3.4, the correlation of the two second-order estimators is

\(\mathrm{Corr}\big(\widehat{\lambda}_2^{[XY]},\widehat{\lambda}_2^{[YX]}\big)=\frac{\ell_2^X\ell_2^Y/(n-1)}{a_n\{\mathrm{Var}(X)\mathrm{Var}(Y)\}^{1/2}}=\frac{3n}{n+1}\frac{\ell_2^X\ell_2^Y}{\{\mathrm{Var}(X)\mathrm{Var}(Y)\}^{1/2}},\)

which converges to \(3\ell_2^X\ell_2^Y/\{\mathrm{Var}(X)\mathrm{Var}(Y)\}^{1/2}\) rather than to zero; for normal margins, where \(\ell_2=\sigma/\sqrt{\pi}\) with \(\sigma\) the standard deviation of the margin, the limit is \(3/\pi\approx 0.95\). Hence, even when _X_ and _Y_ are independent, the estimators of the two directions carry largely overlapping information, and their covariance matrix has substantial off-diagonal entries.

This has three practical implications. First, joint inference on both directions, for example a test of \(\lambda_2^{[XY]}=\lambda_2^{[YX]}=0\) or of \(\lambda_2^{[XY]}=\lambda_2^{[YX]}\), must use the joint covariance. Since the two estimators are positively correlated, treating them as independent overstates the variance of \(\widehat{\lambda}_2^{[XY]}-\widehat{\lambda}_2^{[YX]}\) and understates that of \(\widehat{\lambda}_2^{[XY]}+\widehat{\lambda}_2^{[YX]}\). Second, covariance-weighted procedures, such as generalized L-moment estimation with inverse covariance weighting (Shin et al., 2026) and minimum-distance fitting of L-comoments, should use the full covariance matrix. A diagonal weight matrix ignores this correlation and leads to inefficient estimators and incorrect standard errors. The full matrix, however, can be nearly singular, which motivates the positive-definite (PD) regularization in Remark 5.4. Third, the estimators of the two directions carry largely the same information. Suppose that both \(\lambda_2^{[XY]}\) and \(\lambda_2^{[YX]}\) are used as moment conditions, for example when a bivariate model is fitted by matching L-comoments. Then the second condition adds little to the first. Under independence with normal margins, for instance, the correlation of the two estimators is close to 0.95. This redundancy should be taken into account when the moment conditions are chosen.
