<tbc>L-moments are generally less sensitive to outlying observations and sampling variability. In some distributional settings, L-moment estimators are computationally efficient and can also be competitive with likelihood-based estimators, particularly for small samples (for example, Hosking, 1990; Sankarasubramanian and Srinivasan, 1999; Hosking and Wallis, 1997; Murshed et al., 2014; Shin et al., 2025). These properties make an understanding of their sampling distributions and finite-sample uncertainty especially valuable.

For univariate L-moments, Elamir and Seheult (2004) derived exact finite-sample variance- covariance formulas and distribution-free unbiased estimators, including a weighted order- statistic implementation. They also considered approximate standard errors for ratios of sample L-moments. Their findings provide a useful basis for studying the finite-sample behavior of L- moment statistics, which cannot in general be fully characterized by asymptotic approximations alone.

As a multivariate generalization, Serfling and Xiao (2007) introduced directional L-comoments and L-comoment matrices, developed concomitant and U-statistic representations, and investigated asymptotic properties. They also noted that general U-statistic theory provides a route to exact variances and unbiased covariance estimation. However, explicit joint finite-sample covariance formulas for second and third L-comoments, together with an efficient unbiased covariance estimator, were not developed there. To the best of our knowledge, a comparable explicit treatment has not subsequently been available. Motivated by the univariate work of Elamir and Seheult (2004), we address this gap. The general U-statistic overlap principle that we use is standard; our contribution is its explicit application to L-comoments.

L-comoments and related multivariate L-moment methods have been used in regional flood analysis (Chebana and Ouarda, 2007; Akinsoji et al., 2026), drought regionalization (Zhang et al., 2012), bivariate drought modeling (Abdi et al., 2017), and rainfall-frequency homogeneity studies (Shin et al., 2018). Related multivariate methods arise in homogeneity testing under spatial dependence (Šimková, 2017), joint marine extremes (Bai et al., 2023), and process monitoring (Arslan et al., 2025). In these applications, the sample L-comoment matrix is used to summarize the dependence between the measured variables. In regional frequency analysis, for example, sample L-comoments and their ratios are compared across sites with short records, and their sampling variability is typically assessed by simulation from fitted parametric models (Chebana and Ouarda, 2007; Šimková, 2017). Our object of study is different: it is the _sampling covariance matrix_ of these estimated L-comoments, which quantifies their uncertainty. Throughout the paper, "L-comoment" refers to the population quantity or its sample estimate, whereas "sampling covariance matrix" refers only to the covariance of the estimators.
