New approaches to the Erdős Distinct Subset Sums Problem

**Abstract**

We present a new method of reproving the best known lower bound of the Erdős distinct subset sums problem. Although our lower bound is asymptotically the same as in [4], it is slightly better for the case when _n_ is odd. Besides, we also provide a new lower bound on the second largest element _a_<sub>_n_-1</sub>.

## 1 Introduction

Let

\(A=\{a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_n\}\)

be a set of positive integers whose subset sums are all distinct. The Erdős distinct subset sums problem asks how small the largest element _a<sub>n</sub>_ can be. In particular, Erdős asked whether there exists an absolute constant _c\>_0 such that \(a_n\ge c2^n\) and offered 500$ for proving or disproving [6], which was claimed to be disproved by GPT-6 Astra in 2026. The classical variance method gives a lower bound of order \(2^n/\sqrt{n}\), with subsequent improvements by Elkies [5], Bae [2], and Aliev [1]. Dubroff, Fox, and Xu obtained the asymptotic lower bound

\(a_n\ge\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}-o(1)\right)\frac{2^n}{\sqrt{n}}\)

and provided two short proofs of this estimate [4]. In the opposite direction, before GPT-6 Astra, the best known upper bound was by Bohman, who constructed sets with

\(a_n<0.220022^n\)

for all sufficiently large _n_ [3].
