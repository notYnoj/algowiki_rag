**Lemma 12.** _Let_ \(i\in\{1,2\}\)_. There is a matching N<sub>i</sub> of size_ |_B_<sub>3-_i_</sub>| _between A_\\_B and B_<sub>3-_i_</sub> _such that the following hold for every_ \(uv\in N_i\)_, where_ \(u\in A\) _and_ \(v\in B\):

1. _u is of type x or y if i_=1_, and of type y or z if i_=2<tbc>

2. \(u\succ_iv\) _if u is of type y_<tbc>

_3._ _either_ \(v\in C_B^i(u)\) _or_ \(B+u\in\mathcal{I}_i\).

_Proof._ Let \(M'=(S',\mathcal{I}')\) be the matroid obtained from _M<sub>i</sub>_ by deleting \(S\backslash(A\cup B)\), contracting \((A\cap B)\cup B_i\), and truncating to the size of _A_\\_B_. That is, \(S'=(A\backslash B)\cup(B\backslash(A\cup B_i))\) and \(\mathcal{I}'=\{I\subseteq S':I\subseteq A\cup B,I\cup(A\cap B)\cup B_i\in\mathcal{I}_i,|I|\le|A\backslash B|\}\). In \(M'\), the sets \(A':=A\backslash B\) and \(B':=B\backslash(A\cup B_i)\) are bases that are complements of each other.

We define a total order \(\succ'\) on \(S'\) as follows. The elements of \(B\backslash(A\cup B_i\cup B_{3-i})\) are worst (in arbitrary order). On the remaining elements, i.e., on the elements of \((A\backslash B)\cup B_{3-i}\), we define the preferences based on the total order \(\succ_i^*\) on _S_<sup>∗</sup>. To do this, we assign an element \(u^*\in S^*\) to each \(u\in(A\backslash B)\cup B_{3-i}\) as follows. Let \(u^*=\{x_u,y_u,z_u\}\cap A^*\) if \(u\in A\backslash B\), let _u_<sup>∗</sup>=_x<sub>u</sub>_ if _i_=1 and \(u\in B_2\), and let _u_<sup>∗</sup>=_z<sub>u</sub>_ if _i_=2 and \(u\in B_1\). We then let \(u\succ'v\) if and only if \(u^*\succ_i^*v^*\). In the totally ordered matroid \(M'=(S',\mathcal{I}',\succ')\), \(A'\) is an optimal base. Indeed, _v_ is the worst element of \(C_{A^\prime}(v)\) for every \(v\in B'\). It is clear for the elements in \(B'\backslash B_{3-i}\) by the definition of \(\succ'\). As for each \(v\in B_{3-i}\), since \(A^*+v^*\in\mathcal{I}_{3-i}^*\) holds and _A_<sup>∗</sup> is an \((M_1^*,M_2^*)\)-kernel, _v_<sup>∗</sup> must be the worst element of its fundamental circuit for _A_<sup>∗</sup>. By Theorem 5, there is a perfect matching \(N'\) between \(A'\) and \(B'\) such that \(u\succ'v\) and \(v\in C'_{B^\prime}(u)\) for every \(uv\in N'\), where \(u\in A'\) and \(v\in B'\).

Let _N<sub>i</sub>_ be the subset of \(N'\) induced by \(A\cup B_{3-i}\). Then |_N<sub>i</sub>_|=|_B_<sub>3-_i_</sub>|, and every \(uv\in N_i\) satisfies \(u\succ'v\), which implies \(u^*\succ^*v^*\), which in turn implies \((v^*,u^*)\unknown\in\mathcal{R}_i^*\). In case _i_=1, as \(v\in B_{3-i}=B_2\), we have _v_<sup>∗</sup>=_x<sub>v</sub>_, and hence \((x_v,u^*)\unknown\in\mathcal{R}_1^*\). By the definition of \(\mathcal{R}_1^*\), this implies the first two properties of the lemma. We can similarly obtain these two in the case _i_=2.

Now we show that for every \(uv\in N_i\), either \(v\in C_B^i(u)\) or \(B+u\in\mathcal{I}_i\). Since \(v\in C'_{B^\prime}(u)\), _v_ is in the fundamental circuit of _u_ for \(B'\) in the matroid obtained by truncating _M<sub>i</sub>_ to the size of _A_\\_B_. This means that it is either in the fundamental circuit also in _M<sub>i</sub>_, or _B+u_ is independent in _M<sub>i</sub>_, as required.

We are now ready to prove the theorem by obtaining a contradiction. Let _N_<sub>1</sub> and _N_<sub>2</sub> be matchings described in Lemma 12. Since |_B|\>_1.5|_A_| implies |_N<sub>i</sub>_|=|_B_<sub>3-_i_</sub>|_\>|A_\\_B|/_2 for \(i\in\{1,2\}\), there is an element \(u\in A\backslash B\) that is covered by both _N_<sub>1</sub> and _N_<sub>2</sub>. Let \(uv_1\in N_1\) and \(uv_2\in N_2\). Since the first two properties of Lemma 12 hold for \(i\in\{1,2\}\), the element _u_ must be of type _y_, and \(u\succ_iv_i\) for \(i\in\{1,2\}\). But this means that _u_ blocks _B_ because of the third property of Lemma 12, a contradiction.

Thus, we have completed the proof of the 1.5-approximability of max-kernel-po with interval orders.

We conclude this section by observing that Proposition 2, i.e., the UGC-hardness of \((1.5-\delta)\)- approximation, follows from the result of Askalidis et al. \[2\] on the _maximum stable marriage problem with incomplete lists under social stability_ (max-smiss). An input of the problem is a graph _G_=(_U,W;E_), a total order \(\succ_v\) on \(\delta_G(v)\) for each \(v\in U\cup W\), and a set \(A\subseteq E\) whose elements are called _acquainted pairs_. For a matching \(M\subseteq E\), an edge \(e\in E\) _socially blocks M_ if it blocks _M_ in the classical sense and belongs to _A_. A matching is _socially stable_ if there is no social blocking pair. The task of max-smiss is to find a maximum socially stable matching. Askalidis et al. \[2\] proved that, assuming UGC, it is NP-hard to approximate max-smiss within \(\frac{3}{2}-\delta\) for any \(\delta>0\).
